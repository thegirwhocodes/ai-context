I've found comprehensive information about the European Deposit Insurance Scheme (EDIS) funding mechanism and reinsurance backstop. Here are the key findings:

## Overview
The Commission proposed to set up a European deposit insurance scheme (EDIS) in November 2015 as the third pillar of the banking union.

## Phased Implementation and Funding Structure

The establishment of EDIS is envisaged in three successive stages: a reinsurance scheme for participating national DGSs in a first period of three years, a co-insurance scheme for participating national DGSs in a second period of four years, and full insurance for participating national DGSs in the steady state.

### Reinsurance Phase
In the reinsurance phase, EDIS would provide national DGSs with liquidity in the case of a bank failure, which would have to be paid back by the national DGS. Liquidity support is the most essential element to ensure that depositors are paid out.

### Co-Insurance and Full Insurance Phases
In the coinsurance phase, EDIS would also cover losses, without recouping them from the national DGS. This would further reduce the link between banks and their Member States.

## Funding Mechanism
The national DGSs would remain in existence and, as from 2017, will provide half of the total funds in the EDIS. Their financial means would rise continuously and reach the target funding level of 0.4% of covered deposits in 2024. In addition to the national DGSs, a euro area-wide DIF, made up of individual sub-funds and a joint sub-fund, would be set up to provide the other half of the deposit insurance funding.

## Backstop Framework
A backstop framework was advocated, to be established through the European Stability Mechanism (ESM).

This comprehensive structure ensures liquidity support and eventual loss sharing while maintaining a connection to national deposit guarantee schemes during the transition phases.
