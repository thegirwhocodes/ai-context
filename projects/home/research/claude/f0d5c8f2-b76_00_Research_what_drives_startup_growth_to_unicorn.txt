## 1. Base Rates and What Separates Winners

### Conversion funnel (CB Insights, Pitchbook, Crunchbase 2018-2024 data)

- **Seed to Series A**: ~38-48% of seed-funded startups (CB Insights "Venture Capital Funnel"). The number has tightened post-2022 to ~25-30% (Carta "State of Private Markets" 2023-24).
- **Series A to B**: ~28-31%.
- **Series B to C**: ~24-28%.
- **Seed to unicorn ($1B+)**: ~1.07% (CB Insights, 2018 cohort tracked through 2024). Roughly 1 in 100 seed-funded.
- **Any-stage VC-backed to unicorn**: ~2.5%.
- **First Round's "State of Startups"** (most recent: 2023): 80% of founders cite "go-to-market" as harder than product; 70%+ of breakout companies had a founder with prior operating experience in the same domain.

### Trait separators by stage (the empirical signal)

**At Series A** (Sequoia "Arc," a16z "growth-stage memos"):
- ARR growth >3x year-over-year for SaaS
- Net dollar retention (NDR) >110% even at sub-$5M ARR
- Organic/inbound contributing >30% of pipeline (signals demand pull)
- 6-month logo retention >85% for SMB SaaS, >95% for enterprise
- Hiring velocity: doubled headcount in trailing 12 months without ICP drift

**At Series B**:
- T2D3 on track (triple, triple, double, double, double from $1M-2M ARR base) — Neeraj Agrawal, Battery Ventures, 2015
- Magic Number >0.75 (sales efficiency)
- Burn multiple <2 (David Sacks, Craft Ventures, 2020): net burn / net new ARR
- CAC payback <18 months SMB, <24 months enterprise

**At Series C**:
- "Mendoza Line" (Bessemer): minimum YoY growth to remain venture-fundable. ~30% at $100M ARR scaling down to ~20% at $300M+.
- Rule of 40 (growth% + FCF margin%) >40
- Gross margin >70% for software; >25% for marketplaces; >50% for fintech with embedded software

### Sources to anchor the agent's priors
- Christoph Janz "5 Ways to Build a $100M Business" (Point Nine): elephants, deer, rabbits, mice, flies — ARR/customer determines GTM motion mathematically.
- Tom Tunguz / Theory Ventures: median best-in-class SaaS growth at Series B in 2024 was 100% YoY (down from 150% in 2021).
- Jamin Ball "Clouded Judgement": tracks public SaaS multiples, NDR medians (~110% top quartile in 2024, down from 120%+ in 2021), Rule of 40 distributions.
- David Skok (forentrepreneurs.com): canonical CAC, LTV, payback period frameworks; "SaaS Metrics 2.0."

## 2. Quantitative Growth Playbooks by Stage

### 2A. Pre-Product-Market-Fit

**Leading indicators (the agent should monitor these continuously):**

- **Sean Ellis 40% test**: ≥40% of users say they would be "very disappointed" without your product. Threshold validated across 100+ companies (Superhuman's Rahul Vohra refined it into a segmentation methodology, First Round Review 2018).
- **Retention curves flattening**: weekly or monthly cohort retention forms an asymptote, not a decay-to-zero. Andrew Chen / a16z: "the smile curve" (retention rises after initial dip) is the strongest PMF signal.
- **Organic growth rate**: ≥15-20% MoM organic (no paid) for consumer; ≥10% MoM inbound for B2B SaaS.
- **NPS**: >50 strong, >70 outstanding (consumer); >40 strong for B2B SaaS.
- **Category-specific cohort shapes** (Lenny Rachitsky's compilation, Sacra benchmarks):
  - **SaaS**: D30 retention >70% (SMB), >85% (enterprise); month-12 logo retention >80%.
  - **Consumer social**: D1 >50%, D7 >25%, D30 >15%, asymptote ≥10%.
  - **Consumer subscription** (e.g., Headspace, Calm): M3 >50%, M12 >30%.
  - **Marketplace**: 6-month buyer repeat rate >50% (Sarah Tavel, Benchmark).

**Machine-readable PMF signals:**
```
pmf_score = weighted_avg(
  sean_ellis_pct >= 40,           # 0.30 weight
  d30_retention_above_benchmark,  # 0.25 weight
  organic_growth_mom >= 0.15,     # 0.20 weight
  nps >= 40,                      # 0.10 weight
  cohort_curve_flattening,        # 0.15 weight
)
```

**Standard interventions pre-PMF:**
- Force narrowing of ICP if Sean Ellis varies wildly across segments (Superhuman playbook).
- Kill features that the "very disappointed" segment doesn't use.
- If organic <10% MoM after 12 months, recommend pivot or wedge change.

### 2B. Post-PMF Growth

**T2D3 (Battery, Agrawal 2015):** Triple ARR for two years, then double for three. From $2M to $100M ARR in five years. Sub-trajectory = signal for intervention.

**Mendoza Line (Bessemer 2017, updated 2023):** Below this growth rate, you are not venture-fundable at the next round.
- $1M ARR: must grow >300% to be on track
- $10M ARR: >150%
- $30M ARR: >100%
- $100M ARR: ~60-70%

**Rule of 40** (Brad Feld, popularized by Bessemer): growth% + FCF margin% ≥ 40. Public SaaS median in 2024: ~25 (Jamin Ball). Top quartile >40.

**Magic Number** (Scale Ventures, Lemkin/SaaStr): (Net new ARR × 4) / S&M spend prior quarter.
- >1.0: pour fuel on the fire
- 0.5-1.0: fundable, optimize
- <0.5: stop spending, fix funnel

**NDR / GRR by ARR stage** (Bessemer State of the Cloud 2024):
- $1-10M ARR: NDR >110%, GRR >85% top-quartile
- $10-50M: NDR >115%, GRR >90%
- $50M+: NDR >120%, GRR >92%

**CAC Payback** (Skok):
- SMB: <12 months excellent, <18 months acceptable
- Mid-market: <18-24 months
- Enterprise: <24-30 months

**LTV/CAC** (Skok): >3 healthy, >5 great. Below 3 = unsustainable economics.

**Burn Multiple** (David Sacks, 2020):
- <1: amazing
- 1-1.5: great
- 1.5-2: ok
- 2-3: suspect
- >3: bad

### 2C. Marketplaces

- **Liquidity** (Andrew Chen, Bill Gurley): % of listings that transact within X days. Threshold varies — eBay historically ~50%, Airbnb tracked search-to-book conversion.
- **Sarah Tavel's "Hierarchy of Marketplaces"** (Benchmark, 2019): (1) minimum viable happiness on both sides, (2) tipping toward winner-take-most, (3) "happy GMV retention," (4) widening the moat.
- **Take rate**: 10-30% typical; software-enabled marketplaces (Faire, Shopify partners) can sustain 15%+; transactional marketplaces (Uber, DoorDash) often ~20-30%.
- **Supply/demand balance**: Lenny Rachitsky (Airbnb growth) — chase whichever side is constrained; "constrained side" rule.
- **Cohort GMV retention** (Tavel): 12-month buyer GMV retention >100% on a per-buyer basis is the unicorn signal.

### 2D. Consumer

- **DAU/MAU ratio**: Facebook standard ~50%+; "good enough" >20% for utility, >50% for social.
- **k-factor (viral coefficient)**: k = invitations × conversion. k>1 = exponential. Sustainable consumer apps usually have k 0.15-0.5 with payback through retention.
- **Retention asymptote**: must be non-zero. "Power user curve" (Chen) — % of users active 6+ days/week shows core formation.
- **L28 metric** (Facebook) — number of days active in trailing 28; bimodal distribution = healthy.

### 2E. Enterprise

- **ACV growth**: should rise 20-50% YoY as you move upmarket.
- **Pipeline coverage**: 3-4x quota in pipeline (Sales Hacker, Winning by Design).
- **Win rate**: >20% qualified-to-close healthy; >30% excellent.
- **Sales cycle length**: <90 days SMB, 90-180 mid-market, 180-365 enterprise. Lengthening = signal of buyer hesitation or ICP drift.
- **Pipeline-to-bookings conversion**: stable or improving = healthy GTM; deteriorating = leading indicator of revenue miss.

## 3. Non-Quant Winning Factors

These show up in retrospectives (Stripe, Airbnb, Snowflake, Datadog, Figma case studies; Elad Gil's *High Growth Handbook*; *Founders at Work*).

- **Founder-market fit**: domain expertise + obsession. First Round data: founders with 10+ years in their domain are 1.6x more likely to reach Series B. Detect via founder background parsing.
- **Distribution advantage**: Peter Thiel's *Zero to One* — most startups die because they have no distribution channel that scales. Look for one of: (1) virality, (2) cheap paid CAC under LTV, (3) sales motion that becomes more efficient with scale, (4) bundling/embedding into existing channels.
- **Timing/wedge**: Bill Gross (Idealab) study — timing was the #1 factor across 200+ startups, more than team or idea. Detect via category growth rate (gov data, Crunchbase category funding velocity).
- **Hiring velocity at key stages**: Stripe famously hired ahead of the curve at infrastructure layers. Signal: time-to-fill VP roles after Series B should be <6 months; senior engineering hires should keep eng:revenue ratios in band (Gergely Orosz benchmarks).
- **Capital efficiency**: ARR per dollar raised. Best-in-class >$1 ARR per $1 raised at $100M ARR (Atlassian, Veeva benchmarks).
- **Second-act decision**: Reid Hoffman / Elad Gil — wait until the first product is dominant in its segment before launching the second. Slack, Notion, Figma all had multi-year single-product runs before expansion.
- **Pricing power**: Buffett's test — can you raise prices 10% without losing >5% of customers? Signal: NDR stays >110% during price increase windows.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

**Premature scaling** (Startup Genome Report, 2011, n=3,200 startups): 70% of startup failures attributed to scaling one dimension (team, product, customer acquisition) ahead of others. Specific signals:
- Hiring growing faster than revenue (>1.5x ratio for 2+ quarters)
- Marketing spend growing while CAC payback worsens
- New geos opened before saturating home market

**Bad first sales hire**: Mark Roberge (HubSpot, *The Sales Acceleration Formula*) — first sales hire should be a player-coach, not a VP. Companies that hire a $300K+ OTE VP Sales pre-$2M ARR fail 60%+ of the time (Lemkin/SaaStr).

**Hiring too senior too early**: "rocket fuel" hires from Google/Meta often fail at <50-person companies. Signal: senior hires (>L7 equivalent) joining pre-$10M ARR with <12-month tenure.

**Expanding to second product prematurely**: Look for: launching v2 before product-1 reaches >$30M ARR, or before NDR on product-1 stabilizes >110%.

**Raising too high at too high a price (down-round trap)**: Companies raising at >50x ARR multiples in 2021 had ~70% down-round rate by 2023 (Carta data). Signal: post-money valuation / forward ARR > 30x with growth <100%.

**Founder conflict**: Noam Wasserman (*The Founder's Dilemmas*, HBS) — 65% of high-potential startups fail due to interpersonal tensions among founders. Detectable signals: equity restructuring events, sudden title changes, LinkedIn updates of cofounders.

**Other detectable failure signals:**
- Accounts receivable days lengthening (cash crunch)
- Burn multiple climbing 3 quarters in a row
- Net new logos declining while expansion masks it
- Glassdoor ratings dropping >0.5 in 6 months
- Engineering attrition >20% annualized

## 5. What "Intervention" Looks Like from Top Operators

Distilled from Elad Gil (*High Growth Handbook*), Reid Hoffman (*Blitzscaling*), Frank Slootman (*Amp It Up*), Keith Rabois (Khosla, ex-PayPal/Square), Gokul Rajaram (Square, DoorDash), Bill Campbell (*Trillion Dollar Coach*).

### Slootman's "Amp It Up" interventions
- **Raise standards**: replace "good" with "great"; demand specificity in targets
- **Sharpen focus**: kill the bottom 30% of initiatives
- **Pick up the pace**: weekly cadence over monthly; ship dates non-negotiable
- **Transform the business**: change motion (e.g., bottom-up to top-down) when growth tapers

### Rabois interventions (from his lectures, Khosla writings)
- "Barrels and ammunition": find the one person who can own a P&L (a "barrel"). Promote barrels, multiply ammunition under them.
- Hire the best in their function in the world for any role >$5M ARR-relevant.
- Force ranking on every team quarterly.

### Gokul Rajaram interventions
- Operating cadence: weekly metrics review with 5 numbers max. Anything more is noise.
- "Build the dashboard before the product" — instrument first.
- Shorten meeting cadence during turbulence; lengthen during steady state.

### Elad Gil's *High Growth Handbook* interventions
- **Org redesign every 6-12 months** post-Series B. Companies that don't reorg ossify.
- **Executive team upgrades**: by Series C, half the original team typically can't scale.
- **Force functional separation**: separate VPs of Marketing, Sales, CS by $20M ARR.

### Reid Hoffman's *Blitzscaling*
- Prioritize speed over efficiency in winner-take-most markets.
- Transition stages (Tribe → Village → City → Nation): each requires different management; signals to retool include >2x headcount growth in 12 months, missed quarterly targets two quarters in a row.

### Bill Campbell pattern
- 1:1s focused on "what's not working"
- Force trade-offs, never let a leader "do everything"

### Concrete board-level moves that move the needle (from interviews and a16z/Sequoia case studies):
- Recruiting: top board members open 5-10 senior candidate doors per quarter
- Customer intros: warm intros to 2-3 top-50 logos in target ICP per quarter
- Pricing studies: hire McKinsey/Simon-Kucher when NDR drops below 105%
- Forced narrowing: cut SKUs, kill segments, refocus on ICP
- Capital strategy: trigger bridge round at 9-12 month runway, not 6
- CEO coaching: pair founders with Reboot/Hoffman-style coaches at growth inflection points
