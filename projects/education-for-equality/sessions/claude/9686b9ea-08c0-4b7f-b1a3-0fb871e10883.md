Short answer: the structure is legal and it's literally already your written plan. The motive isn't the problem. But "free Workspace" is a terrible reason to incorporate a 501(c)(3), and there's one specific thing you'd be planning that you actually can't do.

## The dual structure is fine — you already designed it
[LEGAL_ROADMAP.md](7. Legal & IP/LEGAL_ROADMAP.md) has exactly this: US 501(c)(3) (owns the IP, takes grants) + **EfE Nigeria Ltd** as a separate Lagos for-profit that sells tutoring to private schools, deliberately **owned by you personally, not by the nonprofit** — specifically to avoid CFC complications — connected by an arm's-length IP licensing agreement.

That's not a loophole, it's the standard model. Mozilla Foundation/Corporation, Chan Zuckerberg's LLC + Foundation, and the Andela/Flutterwave pattern all run it. Nobody at the IRS or CAC raises an eyebrow at a nonprofit and a for-profit with a common founder. What they police is **separation**, not existence.

## What they actually scrutinize
The doctrine that matters is **private benefit**: a 501(c)(3)'s assets can't more-than-incidentally benefit private interests — including your Lagos company and including you. Exclusive dealing between a charity and one affiliated for-profit is a named red flag, and so is a small group or family controlling both sides.

The compliance bar, if you run both:
- **Separate boards.** Overlap is allowed; a majority of independent directors on the nonprofit is strongly preferred. Separate meetings, separate minutes.
- **Separate books, separate bank accounts, separate payroll.** One shared credit card across both entities is a real finding.
- **Every transaction between them at fair market value, in writing** — the licensing agreement you already scoped, plus a resource-sharing agreement if they share anything.
- **Written conflict-of-interest policy**, and you recuse on both sides of any deal.
- If you're paid by both: written time-allocation policy and timesheets that match the payroll split.

That's ongoing overhead, forever. Not hard, but not free.

## The one thing you can't do
**You cannot run the Lagos for-profit on the nonprofit's free Google Workspace.** Google grants Workspace for Nonprofits for the charity's own charitable activities, and for-profit subsidiaries/affiliates are a standard named ineligibility factor across nonprofit donation programs. Same for Ad Grants and any Cloud credits.

So if the plan is "incorporate the nonprofit, get free Workspace, run the real for-profit business on it" — that's the version that's actually a problem. Not because of your motive, but because it's textbook private benefit and a terms violation on top.

## Does the motive matter?
Honestly: less than you think, and more than you'd like.

The IRS doesn't read your intentions — it reads your structure and your operations. Form 1023 is signed under penalties of perjury, but what it attests to is that you're **organized and operated** for exempt purposes. E4E genuinely delivers free literacy and numeracy to out-of-school kids in Lagos. That's a real charitable class doing real charitable work. If that's what the nonprofit actually does, the free Workspace is a legitimately earned perk, not a scam, and you don't need to feel weird about it.

Where motive bites you is evidence. "I'm just doing it so we can get free money," written into an email, an application, or a board minute, is discoverable and does nothing but hurt you. Keep it out of writing. (This chat is fine — I'm telling you so you don't put it in a grant application.)

## But the math on this doesn't work
Free Workspace is maybe **$100–200/year** of value for one or two seats. A 501(c)(3) costs ~$575–700 to file, then Form 990 every year, a 3-person board with independent directors, separate books forever, and — if you also run the Ltd — the whole separation regime above.

**Do not incorporate a nonprofit to get free Workspace.** If you're genuinely for-profit, buy Business Starter for single-digit dollars a month and move on with your life.

The nonprofit is worth it only if the *nonprofit* is the real strategy — and there's a case that it is, because your entire funding pipeline is equity-free grant money: UNICEF Venture Fund, CcHub/Mastercard, Technovation, Wesleyan. That money largely doesn't flow to a Lagos Ltd. The Workspace is a rounding error next to $100K equity-free.
