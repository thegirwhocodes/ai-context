**Paper:** "Education and Economic Growth: Empirical Evidence from Nigeria" — published in *Managing Global Transitions* 16(1) and republished as **AERC Research Paper No. 407** (Nov 2020). The AERC version lists author order as Irughe, Eregha, Edafe.

## 1. Dependent Variable(s) — There are THREE separate models

The study runs three parallel regressions, each with a different dependent variable (all in **per capita** terms, all logged):

| Variable name in paper | What it is | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Real GDP per capita** (logged) | Overall economic growth proxy | `log(RGDPPC)` |
| **Non-oil GDP per capita** (logged) | Non-oil sector growth | `log(NOGDPPC)` |
| **Oil GDP per capita** (logged) | Oil sector growth | `log(OILGDPPC)` |

So the answer is **NOT** real GDP growth rate — it's the **log of real GDP per capita** (and per-capita versions of oil/non-oil GDP). The paper somewhat confusingly calls this "growth," but the regressors are levels of log per-capita GDP.

## 2. Independent Variables and How They Were Measured

From the model specification (Equations 8 and 9, p. 25) and the data description (p. 26):

| Variable | Code | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Primary school enrolment rate | `PRYENRL` | Enrolment rate (gross) |
| Secondary school enrolment rate | `SECENRL` | Enrolment rate (gross) |
| Tertiary school enrolment rate | `TERENRL` | Enrolment rate (gross) |
| Secondary school completion rate | `SECCOMP` | Completion rate (used as alternative model — replaces enrolment rates) |
| Capital formation (logged) | `log(KFM)` | Used as proxy for capital stock because **no capital-stock data exist for Nigeria** (Note 4 of the paper) |
| Time trend | `t` | Linear time trend, included to capture productivity/TFP |

Note: They could not get **primary or tertiary completion rates** for Nigeria, so completion-rate models use only secondary completion (Note 3, p. 34).

Two equations:

- **Eq. 9:** log(Y) = α₀ + α₂·SECCOMP + Xβ + μ (completion-rate model)

## 3. EXACT Data Sources (this is the critical part)

The paper's data-source statement is on **page 26** (and reaffirmed in the abstract). It is brief but explicit:

> *"The data cover the period 1970–2013 and were extracted from the **CBN Statistical Bulletin** and the **Nigerian Bureau of Statistics**."*

The abstract adds:

> *"Data for the study were sourced from **Central Bank of Nigeria Annual Bulletins** and the **Nigerian Bureau of Statistics**, as well as from the **World Bank** for the period 1970–2013."*

**The paper does NOT provide a variable-by-variable source table.** This is a real weakness for replication. The references list confirms three statistical-source citations:

- Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN). 2010. *Statistical Bulletin*, various issues. Abuja: Central Bank of Nigeria.
- National Bureau of Statistics (NBS). 2010. *National Literacy Survey*. Abuja: NBS.
- World Bank. 2013. *World Development Indicators, 2013*. Washington, D.C.

So the three sources are **CBN Statistical Bulletin**, **NBS**, and **World Bank WDI (2013 edition)** — but the paper does not pin specific variables to specific sources.

**Best inference for a replicator** (based on standard Nigerian-empirics practice and what each agency publishes):

| Variable | Most likely source |
|---|---|
| Real GDP per capita, oil GDP, non-oil GDP | **CBN Statistical Bulletin** (CBN reports oil vs. non-oil GDP disaggregation; WDI does not) |
| Capital formation (gross fixed capital formation) | **CBN Statistical Bulletin** or **World Bank WDI** |
| Primary / secondary / tertiary enrolment rates | **World Bank WDI** (Table 1 on p. 8 of the paper sources enrolment data explicitly to "World Bank, World Development Indicators, 2013") |
| Secondary completion rate | **NBS** or **WDI** (paper does not specify) |
| Population (for per-capita conversion) | CBN or WDI |

The Table 1 source line ("Source: World Bank, World Development Indicators, 2013") is the only explicit per-table attribution — and it covers enrolment.

**1970–2013** (44 annual observations, though see below).

## 5. Number of Observations

The paper does **not** explicitly state N. With 1970–2013 annual data, that's **44 observations** maximum, reduced to ~43 after first-differencing for unit-root tests. The paper does not report a sample size in any results table — another replication gap.

## 6. Econometric Method

**Fully Modified OLS (FMOLS)** and **Dynamic OLS (DOLS)** as the primary estimators (p. 26, Eq. 10 and 11). The paper says FMOLS and DOLS results were similar, so **only FMOLS is reported** in the results tables (Note 6, p. 34).

**Why these methods?** From p. 5 and p. 26:
- The variables are I(1) (non-stationary in levels, stationary in first differences) — confirmed by ADF and Phillips-Perron unit-root tests (Table 6, p. 27)
- Engle-Granger residual cointegration test confirmed long-run relationships in all six model specifications (Table 7, p. 28)
- FMOLS/DOLS correct for serial correlation and endogeneity of regressors that standard OLS cannot handle in cointegrated long-run relationships (citing Pedroni 1996, 2004)
- The authors explicitly criticize prior Nigerian studies for using only error-correction OLS

**Pre-tests used:**
- ADF unit-root test (with and without trend)
- Phillips-Perron unit-root test (with and without trend)
- Engle-Granger residual-based single-equation cointegration test

**Software:** EViews 7 (noted under Table 6).

## Replication Notes for a Student

1. **CBN Statistical Bulletin** — free, downloadable from cbn.gov.ng. The 2018 or later edition will give 1970-onward time series for real GDP, oil GDP, non-oil GDP, gross fixed capital formation, and population.
2. **World Bank WDI** — free at data.worldbank.org. Search "Nigeria" for: `SE.PRM.ENRR` (primary enrolment), `SE.SEC.ENRR` (secondary), `SE.TER.ENRR` (tertiary), `SE.SEC.CMPT.ZS` (lower-secondary completion).
3. **NBS** — nigerianstat.gov.ng. Used for the 2010 National Literacy Survey (which doesn't appear to feed into the regressions directly — it's cited in background discussion).
4. **Gap to improve on:** the paper never says which data point came from where. A student who builds an explicit variable-source-vintage table immediately advances the methodology.
5. **Other improvement avenues:** They use only secondary completion (no primary/tertiary completion); they have no capital-stock series; they use a linear time trend as a TFP proxy; they don't report N or any standard-error robustness; sample ends at 2013 (extending to 2023 with structural-break tests around the 2014 oil-price collapse and 2016 recession is a natural extension).
