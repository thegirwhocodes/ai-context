## PART 2: WEB SEARCH FINDINGS — CONSENSUS RULES

**On voice:** First person wins for personal sites and one-person businesses; third person reserved for press kits and corporate / multi-person pages. A **hybrid** is common: first-person About narrative on the site, with a third-person short bio sidebar for press.

**On photos:** Strong consensus is **yes, include a face**, especially for founders. Reason: trust and "know-like-trust." Should be high-quality, can be warm/casual, doesn't have to be a corporate headshot. For mission-driven female founders specifically, "visual storytelling—content that shows the person behind the brand—adds authenticity and builds connection."

**On bio length:** "2–4 sentences tops." Start with role + what you do, then experience/background, end with a personal touch (hobby, location, fun fact). Mobile is 64%+ of traffic so paragraphs should be 3–4 lines max and sentences under 20 words.

**On the personal brand statement format:** "Founder and CEO at FinTech X | Forbes 30 Under 30 | Passionate about Financial Inclusion" — pipe-separated, top of page.

**On a founder-specific bio template** (from research):
> "James is the Founder & CEO of SolarX. On a mission to democratize energy access for the developing world. Previously founded and exited EcoTech (acquired by Tesla)."

**On the /now page (Derek Sivers movement):** Created 2015. A page that "shares what you'd tell a friend you hadn't seen in a year." 2,300+ people have one as of latest count. Helps the writer say no to off-priority requests and helps visitors see what's current vs. legacy.

**On flat hierarchy:** Important pages within 3 clicks of homepage. Mobile-first. Navigation labels must match user mental models, not internal taxonomy.

**On About page must-haves:** Introduction · Mission · Origin story · Differentiators · Awards/testimonials · Value proposition. Press mentions/awards build trust as "milestones in the middle of your story."

## PART 3: CROSS-SITE SYNTHESIS

### The four sub-genres of founder personal sites

1. **Minimalist Founder** (Sahil Lavingia, Daniel Gross): Identity triplet or 1-paragraph bio + links to companies. Zero adornment. Works only after you're already famous in your niche.

2. **Encyclopedic Operator** (Patrick Collison, Brian Lovin): Personal site is a public index of interests, books, projects, advice, lists. Many sub-pages. Bio itself is tiny (Patrick: 20 words). Works when you have *years* of writing/curation built up.

3. **Engineer-Builder Résumé** (Brittany Chiang, Steve Krouse, Lee Robinson): About → Experience → Projects → Writing. Reverse-chronological. Metrics. CTA to résumé. Most relevant blueprint for someone with shipped products + need to be hireable/fundable.

4. **Investor / Operator-with-an-ask** (Julian Shapiro, Daniel Gross again): Hero is a question or stated need. Site exists to channel inbound to a specific kind of conversation. Bio is short. Writing supports credibility.

### What virtually NO top founder does
- Long CV-style "Education" or "Work History" tables
- Testimonials carousel
- Contact form (they show an email instead)
- Calendar booking links on the personal site (those live on company pages)
- A "Skills" list
- A "What clients say" section
- Logos of awards in a trophy wall (awards mentioned inline in prose only)
- Loading animations / video heroes

### What MOST top founders do
- First-person voice
- Email address visible (no contact form)
- 1–4 sentence bio at most
- One or two social links (typically Twitter/X + GitHub for builders, just Twitter/X + email for non-coders)
- Reverse-chronological work
- Inline mentions of recognition rather than a dedicated awards section
- Plain typography, no over-design
- A writing section (essays/blog) that doubles as proof-of-thinking

### Photo verdict
**Split.** Top male tech founders (Friedman, Collison-on-some-pages, Krouse, Lee, Krouse, Gross, Lavingia) often skip the headshot — they don't need facial recognition because their name is the brand. But the consensus expert guidance for **someone still building their public reputation** is to include a high-quality, warm-but-professional headshot, ideally on the About page (not as a hero). Brittany Chiang doesn't show one, but she's an established voice already. **For Naomi — first-time founder, still establishing public identity, mission-driven work that benefits from a human face — yes, include a photo.** Not as a giant hero image; as a portrait on the About section. A warm candid (not a corporate headshot, not a "founder in front of a window" cliché).

### Awards handling
**Inline, not in a trophy wall.** Daniel Gross doesn't list his Forbes 30 Under 30 or his exits — he just says "Cofounder of Cue, a search engine acquired by Apple in 2013." Julian doesn't list "Stripe grant" in a sidebar — it's one phrase next to the Velocity description. Brittany doesn't list "Halcyon Theme winner of X" — she lists "100k+ Installs" inline.

**For Naomi, since she has many distinct prizes (MIT, Wesleyan, UNICEF, Morgan, Rhodes/Schmidt finalist, Patricelli), a one-line "Recognized by:" row of names/logos at the bottom of the hero or as a thin band is probably better than a dedicated awards section.** Or thread them into Sabi's description — "MIT Africa Business Challenge 2nd place · Wesleyan New Venture Grand Prize winner · UNICEF AI Ventures inaugural cohort."

## PART 4: PROPOSED STRUCTURE FOR NAOMI

Given:
- 21yo Nigerian female student-founder
- Sabi is the flagship (mission-driven, voice AI for Nigerian children)
- 4 other shipped projects (Class on Time, Cortex, Bethel, Adjutant) at various stages
- Many prizes (MIT 2nd, Wesleyan Grand Prize, UNICEF, Morgan, Rhodes/Schmidt finalist, Patricelli)
- Wesleyan '27, Tuck Bridge, ALA valedictorian
- Sole technical builder

She is squarely in **Sub-genre 3: Engineer-Builder, with mission-driven framing borrowed from Sub-genre 1.** I'd loosely model on Brittany Chiang's IA but with a Sahil-Lavingia-style identity line at the top and a Julian-Shapiro-style ask at the bottom.

### Recommended sections, in order

1. **Hero / identity (≤25 words)**
   - Name on top.
   - Identity line, pipe-separated, single sentence:
     - "Founder, Sabi · Building voice AI for African children · Wesleyan '27"
   - Optional second line, a one-sentence mission statement.

2. **About / bio (60–90 words, two short paragraphs)**
   - Paragraph 1: What you build + why.
   - Paragraph 2: Background + a small human detail.
   - Photo here, not in hero. Warm candid, head-and-shoulders, looking at camera.

3. **Work (the main section, ~300 words total across 5 cards)**
   - Reverse-chronological. One card per project. Each card has: name + one-line tagline + 2-sentence description + status/metric + links (Live · Code/GitHub if applicable).
   - Sabi at top (mission-flagship), then Adjutant, Cortex, Class on Time, Bethel.
   - **Hackathon/student projects framed identically to "real" companies.** Don't apologize for them. State the prize where relevant.

4. **Recognition (one band, ~30 words)**
   - A simple horizontal row: "Recognized by: MIT Africa Business Challenge · Wesleyan New Venture · UNICEF AI Ventures · Schmidt × Rhodes Trust Rise · Morgan Hacks · Patricelli Center"
   - Logos optional but pretty hard to source consistently — text is fine.

5. **Writing (optional v1, recommended v2)**
   - Reverse-chronological list, Krouse-style: `2026 Mar - Title`. 3–5 items minimum to justify the section. If you don't have 3 essays yet, skip this for v1.

6. **Contact / CTA (≤30 words)**
   - Email visible (not a form): `nivie@wesleyan.edu`
   - Social: Twitter/X, LinkedIn, GitHub.
   - One-line "Open to" statement: "Open to partnerships in African edtech and to talking with funders backing voice AI for emerging markets."

### What to skip in v1
- A separate /writing if you only have 1 piece
- A /now page until you actually maintain it (a stale /now is worse than no /now)
- Testimonials
- Headshot at billboard size
- Resume PDF (link it in the About bio instead: "Full résumé →")
- A Bookshelf page (Patrick Collison can; you'd be padding)

### Navigation
- **Single-page scroll** with anchor links in a sticky header: About · Work · Recognition · Writing · Contact. This is Brittany Chiang's pattern and works for Naomi's volume of content.
- Multi-page (Patrick / Brian Lovin style) is too much surface area for what's currently shipped — it'll look thin.

### Target word counts per section
| Section | Target |
|---|---|
| Hero/identity line | 15–25 words |
| About/bio | 60–90 words |
| Work (5 projects × ~50 words) | ~250 words |
| Recognition band | ~30 words |
| Writing (if included) | titles only |
| Contact | ~30 words |
| **Total visible copy** | **~400–500 words** |

That's the right zone. Patrick Collison's About is 20 words. Steve Krouse's whole homepage is under 300. Brittany Chiang's full single-page site is around 500. Keep yours in that band.

## KEY DECISIONS FOR NAOMI

1. **First person, not third.** Every relevant founder under 35 uses first person on their personal site. Third person is for press packets.
2. **Yes, a photo — but not in the hero.** Warm portrait on the About section, not a full-bleed face.
3. **Tagline: identity line + mission line.** "Founder, Sabi · Building voice AI for African children · Wesleyan '27" + one mission sentence.
4. **Single-page scroll, sticky anchor nav.** About · Work · Recognition · Writing · Contact.
5. **Awards as an inline band, not a trophy wall.** Single horizontal row of names.
6. **Email visible, not a contact form.** Twitter, LinkedIn, GitHub.
7. **Hackathon projects framed identically to companies.** No apologies, no "side projects" header.
8. **No /now page in v1.** Add it later when she's willing to maintain it.
9. **No /writing section unless she has ≥3 pieces.** Otherwise it'll look stub-y.
10. **Target total visible copy: 400–500 words.** That puts her in the same density as Brittany Chiang and tighter than Patrick Collison.
