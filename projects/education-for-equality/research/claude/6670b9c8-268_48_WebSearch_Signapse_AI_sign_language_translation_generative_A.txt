## Technology Overview

Signapse uses Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to create photo-realistic sign language videos. Their photo-realistic digital signer uses world-leading Computer Vision technology to generate a BSL video that is indistinguishable from a human signer.

## Technical Pipeline

Signapse's translation process operates in two main steps:

1. **Text to Gloss**: Signapse performs automatic Sign Language translation using two steps; 1) Text to Gloss translates from Written Text (e.g. English) to Sign Language grammar and syntax (e.g. BSL). This step uses a written form of sign language grammar called Gloss, which lists the signs used in BSL order and utilises BSL linguistic constructs of Directional Verbs and Non-Manual Features.

2. **Gloss to Video**: Signapse produces sign language videos by blending between different glosses, with the digital signer calculating the positioning and direction of the glosses to ensure the video is smooth and understandable. This approach allows it to create seamless and realistic videos, converting diverse recordings into a consistent high-quality appearance.

## Data Infrastructure

The company collects examples of each gloss using a high-quality capture studio with ~30 cameras. Their Deaf BSL-native translators have recorded ~12,000 glosses, complete with Directional Verbs (~40 versions of each verb!) and Non-Manual Features.

## Key Advantages

GANs make the videos more appealing to Deaf users by generating videos that look like they were made by real people signing, rather than relying on Motion Capture-based graphical avatars.
