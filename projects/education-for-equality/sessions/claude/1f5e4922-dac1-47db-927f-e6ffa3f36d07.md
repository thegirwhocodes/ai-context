## The sidecar

On each numeric turn of a Gemini Live call, both engines re-transcribe the **same** canonical 16 kHz clip (SHA-256 recorded, so a disagreement is always attributable). It's **shadow only** — it cannot change Sabi's reply, grade, or timing.

Rules it enforces, all covered by 39 offline checks:
- neither engine ever sees the question, operands, or expected answer (that bias is what the old turn-based path did wrong)
- only agreement on a **single** numeric value counts; "two mangoes" = "two fries" = 2
- 13-vs-30, "is it twenty or thirty", and silence all **abstain** — never a guessed number
- one engine alone never decides; Groq 429s abstain instead of falling back to local (that would make two "independent" votes one engine)
- late, dropped, or crashed votes are recorded and marked stale, never applied

Code: [numeric_sidecar.py](sabi-server/numeric_sidecar.py), vote engines in [stt.py](sabi-server/stt.py), attached at [gemini_live.py](sabi-server/gemini_live.py) turn finalization. Commit `96710a6`, pushed.

## What the 1.5-second deadline is

A child is on the phone. When they finish saying an answer, Sabi has to react. If grading waits for both engines to transcribe, that wait is dead air. The research set the hard ceiling at ~1.2–1.5s because past that the child thinks the call broke and starts repeating themselves.

**Shadow mode has no deadline** — that's why it's running fine right now. Gemini answers instantly; the sidecar transcribes *afterwards* and writes a record. 14 seconds late is irrelevant when nobody's waiting on it.

**Phase 3 does have one** — that's the step where the two engines' agreement actually decides whether the answer is correct. That answer must exist within ~1.5s of the child finishing.

## Why local Whisper takes 11–14s

Three separate causes, all verified on the server just now:

1. **The `sabi` container has no GPU reservation.** Compose gives one to `ollama` only — the sabi service never asked for the GPU.
2. **The image is a CPU build.** Inside the container, `torch 2.5.1+cpu`, `cuda available: False`, `SABI_LOCAL_WHISPER_DEVICE=cpu`. Whisper large-v3 on CPU is simply that slow.
3. **The host GPU is currently unusable anyway.** `nvidia-smi` fails with a driver/library version mismatch: the *loaded kernel module* is 535.309.01 but the installed libraries are 580.173.02 — a driver upgrade landed and the machine hasn't rebooted since. Until it does, no container can use that GPU.

Groq, by contrast, came back in **0.31s** — the cloud vote is not the problem.

## Why that blocks Phase 3 specifically

Consensus needs **two** votes. If the local vote is never ready in time, the gate abstains on every single turn — Phase 3 would ship and do literally nothing. And we can't just let Groq decide alone: one engine confidently grading a misheard number is the exact failure the whole design exists to prevent.

So Phase 3 has two independent gates, not one: the bake-off precision result **and** a local engine that can answer in about a second.

## Options, cheapest first

- **Smaller/faster local model.** `SABI_LOCAL_WHISPER_MODEL_SIZE=small` or `medium`, or INT8 quantization. Already supported — one env var. For a 0.9-second number clip this can land near 1s on CPU. Costs some accuracy; the gold clips will tell us how much.
- **A genuinely different second engine** (Azure `en-NG`, Parakeet/SBPN). Worth noting Groq and local Whisper are *the same model weights* — their mistakes correlate, so their agreement is weaker evidence than it looks. A different family would be a better vote and possibly faster.
- **Fix the GPU:** reboot the host to clear the driver mismatch, add a GPU reservation for `sabi`, rebuild on a CUDA image. Best accuracy, most work — and the reboot drops any live call, so it needs a scheduled window.
- **Leave it in shadow permanently.** Still genuinely useful: it tells you *when* Sabi mis-hears children, without ever risking a wrong grade.

Fair question — and partly we already do: **Groq *is* cloud Whisper.** That vote comes back in 0.31s. The slow one is the *second* opinion.

So the real question is "why not make the second vote cloud Whisper too," and there are three reasons.

**1. Two Whispers aren't two opinions.** Groq runs `whisper-large-v3`. If the second vote is also Whisper — Groq again, OpenAI's API, anywhere — it's the same architecture and near-identical weights, so it makes the *same mistakes*. Two engines agreeing on "Tati" doesn't tell you they're right; it tells you they're the same model. Agreement is only evidence when the engines can fail differently. That's the whole point of the consensus design — otherwise we're paying twice for one vote and calling it verification.

**2. Cost at your scale.** Our own research puts Groq large-v3 at ~$0.0003 per turn because of the 10-second billing minimum. Fine for you testing. At 200k children × ~20 numeric turns a day that's roughly **$1,200/day** — and a second cloud engine doubles it. The path-to-scale plan already says shift to self-hosted for exactly this. Local Whisper is slow, but it's free forever.

**3. Children's audio.** Every extra vendor is another company holding recordings of Nigerian kids' voices — more consent surface, more to put in the DPIA you sent Sonia, more to explain to Lagos State. Not a blocker, but it's a real cost that doesn't show up on an invoice.

## What I'd actually do instead

Use a cloud second vote — just not another Whisper. **Azure Speech `en-NG`** is the standout:

- it has an actual **Nigerian English locale**, which neither Whisper has
- it's a completely different model family, so its errors are genuinely independent
- cloud latency, so the 1.5s deadline stops being a problem entirely
- free tier is 5 audio hours/month — plenty for the bake-off
- and if it beats Whisper on your gold clips, it's not just the second vote, it's a candidate to *replace* the first

That's exactly what the research doc listed as "best independent cloud comparator." The local Whisper vote then becomes the free offline third opinion where latency doesn't matter.
