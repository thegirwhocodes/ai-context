## Technology Developments

2025 is bringing groundbreaking ASL technologies to life, from AI translation and live captioning to wearable fingerspelling tools. Several notable innovations are emerging:

**AI Translation Tools:** Google plans to release SignGemma, which translates signed ASL into English text. However, it has faced criticism from the deaf community, as many argue that it does not help deaf people understand the hearing community.

**Wearable Technology:** Cornell researchers have developed SpellRing, a groundbreaking wearable device that leverages micro-sonar technology to recognize fingerspelling in American Sign Language in real time, with a proprietary deep-learning algorithm predicting fingerspelled letters with an impressive accuracy of 82% to 92%.

**Improved Captioning:** Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and Google Meet have all introduced significant improvements to their live captioning technology, making virtual meetings more accessible for Deaf and hard-of-hearing participants through faster and more accurate captions.

## Impact on Employment and Education

Access to healthcare, education, legal services and employment opportunities are often limited or entirely absent if interpreters are not provided. The need for sign language interpreters in higher education is at an all-time high, but a shortage makes it hard to give deaf students the support they need.

## Important Considerations

The most successful initiatives involve Deaf community members from the earliest stages of development, ensuring that solutions address real needs rather than perceived ones. Additionally, fingerspelling is only a small part of ASL—the language is rich and complex, and current devices do not yet capture the full picture.

Technology should empower the Deaf community and support interpreters, not attempt to replace them.
