# OOSC Data Sources for Nigeria: Compiled Research

## Bottom line up front

The **most efficient path** is to combine: (1) **UIS/GEM Report VIEW modeled estimates** (annual 2000-2023, downloadable CSV) as the primary continuous series, then triangulate with (2) **DHS Nigeria** (1990, 1999, 2003, 2008, 2013, 2018, 2023-24 = 7 survey points) and (3) **MICS Nigeria** (1995, 1999, 2007, 2011, 2016-17, 2021 = 6 survey points). Together these give ~24 annual modeled points + 13 directly-measured survey points spanning 1990-2024 - far more than the World Bank's 10 administrative observations.

---

## 1. UNESCO Institute for Statistics (UIS)

**URL:** https://databrowser.uis.unesco.org/ (replaced the old http://data.uis.unesco.org/)
**Bulk download:** https://databrowser.uis.unesco.org/resources

**Key details:**
- The UIS is the **original source** for the World Bank's `SE.PRM.UNER.ZS` indicator - so direct UIS download will have the same gap problem for the raw administrative series. However, UIS publishes more granular indicators than the World Bank surfaces.
- Available indicators include: **Out-of-school rate (1 year before primary, primary, lower secondary, upper secondary)**, **Number of out-of-school children of primary age**, **Adjusted net enrollment rate (ANER)**, gross/net enrollment rates.
- **Three downloadable CSV datasets** (updated Feb 2026):
  1. SDG 4 Education - Global and Thematic Indicators
  2. Background Information on Education Statistics
  3. Other Policy Relevant Indicators (OPRI, formerly NATMON)
- **Caveat:** UIS warns Excel may struggle with the full bulk file - use Python/R/CSV reader.

---

## 2. UNESCO GEM Report VIEW (out-of-school estimates) - **HIGHEST VALUE FOR YOUR PAPER**

**URL:** https://www.unesco.org/en/education/view/outofschool/estimates (formerly https://education-estimates.org/out-of-school/)

**Why this is the key source:** UIS + GEM Report built a **statistical model in 2022** that fills gaps by combining administrative data, DHS, MICS, and other household surveys into a **complete annual time series from 2000 onwards** - with 90% uncertainty intervals around the median estimate. This raised data coverage from 62% to 98% of countries.

**Variables for Nigeria:**
- Out-of-school rate, primary
- Out-of-school rate, lower secondary
- Out-of-school rate, upper secondary
- Disaggregated by sex
- Annual coverage 2000-2023 (~24 observations, no gaps)

**Downloadable:** Yes - the data explorer lets users "view and download model estimates directly."

**Verified Nigeria values from this source:**
- 2000: 33% OOSC rate primary | 6.4 million children
- 2010: 7.5 million children
- 2012: 27% OOSC rate
- 2020: 9.6 million children
- 2024 estimate: 10.2 million primary-age, 18.3 million total including secondary

## 3. DHS (Demographic and Health Survey) Nigeria

**URL:** https://dhsprogram.com/ (look up "Nigeria: Standard DHS")
**Microdata catalog:** https://microdata.worldbank.org/ (catalog ID 8029 for 2024)

**Years available:** 1990, 1999, 2003, 2008, 2013, 2018, 2023-24 (**7 survey waves**)

**Variables:** Net Attendance Ratio (NAR) and Gross Attendance Ratio (GAR) for primary and secondary, by sex, region, wealth quintile. School attendance is collected for all household members.

**Format:** Microdata (Stata, SPSS, SAS) requiring registration; PDF reports tabulate the headline NAR/GAR. NAR provides a survey-based proxy for school attendance from which OOSC rate ≈ 100% - NAR.

**Bonus:** Nigeria also has two **NEDS (Nigeria Education Data Survey)** - 2004 and 2010 - which are DHS-EdData supplements that drill specifically into education for ages 4-16. A third NEDS-like survey was conducted in 2015. URL: https://catalog.ihsn.org/index.php/catalog/3344

---

## 4. MICS (Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey) Nigeria

**URL:** https://mics.unicef.org/country-profiles/nigeria/4131 (country profile)
**Surveys list:** https://mics.unicef.org/surveys

**Years available:** 1995 (MICS1), 1999 (MICS2), 2007 (MICS3), 2011 (MICS4), 2016-17 (MICS5), 2021 (MICS6) - **6 waves**. Plus MICS Plus 2023-24 (three waves Feb 2023 - Jan 2025).

**Variables:** Education attendance module - same NAR/GAR primary and secondary, by sex/wealth/location. MICS6 (2021) explicitly tracks SDG4 indicators including learning outcomes, ECCDE attendance, parental involvement.

**Format:** Microdata (SPSS) downloadable after registration; published reports include tabulated attendance rates.

---

## 5. UNICEF Data Warehouse

**URL:** https://data.unicef.org/topic/education/

**For Nigeria specifically:**
- Adjusted Net Attendance Rate (ANAR) primary: dataset on HDX https://data.humdata.org/dataset/unicef-ed-anar-l1
- These are derived from the underlying DHS/MICS waves above (so it's not new data, but a clean pre-processed time series)
- **Not annual** - it inherits the survey-year structure (so ~6-7 points for Nigeria).
- Format: CSV via HDX

---

## 6. Nigeria Living Standards Survey (NLSS)

**URL:** https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/

**Years available:** 2003-04, 2009-10 (HNLSS, harmonized version), 2018-19, 2023 (**4 waves**)

**Variables:** Education module collects school attendance for household members. Use as additional triangulation, especially for wealth/expenditure interactions with OOSC.

**Format:** Microdata downloadable after registration on Nigeria NBS or World Bank LSMS catalog.

---

## 7. UBEC (Universal Basic Education Commission) Personnel Audit / Digest of Statistics

**URL:** https://ubec.gov.ng/digest-of-statistics/

**Years available:**
- 2019 & 2020 Digest of Statistics (Volumes I, II, III) - PDF
- 2022 Digest (Public, Private, and Combined) - PDF
- Earlier National Personnel Audits referenced for 2010 (~10.5M OOSC) and 2018-19 (10,193,918 OOSC)

**Variables:** Total OOSC count by state and gender (ages 6-11), classroom counts, teacher counts. State-level disaggregation is the unique value-add here.

**Format:** PDF only - **not** machine-readable CSV. Caveat: UBEC itself has stated that getting accurate OOSC figures is impossible without an updated census, since population denominators are outdated.

---

## 8. Federal Ministry of Education - Nigeria Digest of Education Statistics

**URL:** https://education.gov.ng/nigeria-digest-of-education-statistics/

**Years available:** 2011-2016 covered, with detailed 2013/14 - 2015/16 breakdowns; 2019 Digest also published (https://education.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/2019-Nigeria-Digest-of-Education-Statistics.pdf); 2017 Digest at https://nemis.education.gov.ng/2017-Nigeria%20Digest%20of%20Education%20Statistics%202017.pdf

**Variables:** Includes specifically "Out-of-School Children (age 6-11 years) by State and Gender, 2015-2016" plus enrollment by class/gender/public-private/state.

**Format:** PDF only.

## 9. EPDC (Education Policy and Data Center)

**URL:** https://www.epdc.org/country/nigeria
**Nigeria National Education Profile (2018):** https://www.epdc.org/sites/default/files/documents/EPDC_NEP_2018_Nigeria.pdf
**Nigeria OOSC Profile:** https://www.epdc.org/sites/default/files/documents/Nigeria_OOSC_Profile.pdf

**Variables:** Attendance at primary/lower secondary/upper secondary, primary completion, literacy. Pulls from DHS/MICS/UIS.

**Format:** Mostly PDF profiles - aggregator rather than primary source. Useful as cross-reference, not for new time-series.

---

## 10. WIDE - World Inequality Database on Education

**URL:** https://www.education-inequalities.org/countries/nigeria

**Variables:** OOSC rate, completion rates, attendance - all disaggregated by **wealth quintile, gender, urban/rural, region, ethnicity, language**. Built on DHS/MICS/national household survey microdata.

**Years available for Nigeria:** Tied to the underlying DHS/MICS waves (so 1999, 2003, 2008, 2011, 2013, 2016-17, 2018, 2021 windows).

**Format:** Online interactive viewer; can export filtered tables. Best use: equity dimension for the term paper.

# Recommended stitching strategy for Naomi's empirical paper

**Primary continuous series (for regression Y-variable):**
- **GEM Report VIEW modeled annual estimates 2000-2023** (CSV download) - this is the only source giving you ~24 continuous annual points without survey gaps. Methodology is documented and peer-reviewed.

**Validation / robustness checks:**
- Overlay DHS NAR (7 points: 1990, 1999, 2003, 2008, 2013, 2018, 2023-24)
- Overlay MICS NAR (6 points: 1995, 1999, 2007, 2011, 2016-17, 2021)
- Cross-check with UBEC National Personnel Audit numbers for 2010, 2018-19, 2022 (administrative)

**For Milan & Burnett (2014) replication / Eregha et al. (2018) comparison:** Those papers most likely combined UIS administrative data + DHS/MICS survey points - the same triangulation strategy. Cite the specific waves they used and whether they used OOSC rate or absolute count as the dependent variable.

**Caveat for the methods section:** The administrative UIS series and the survey-based DHS/MICS series will not match exactly because they measure different things (administrative enrollment / population vs. household-reported attendance). The GEM Report VIEW model reconciles these into one estimate with uncertainty bands - which is why it's the cleanest choice for a regression.
