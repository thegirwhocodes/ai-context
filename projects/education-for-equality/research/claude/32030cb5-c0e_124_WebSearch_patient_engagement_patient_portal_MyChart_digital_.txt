## Patient Portal Engagement Disparities

Among over 250,000 adults, 61% activated their MyChart account and 54% logged in at least once, with significantly lower engagement among patients 65 years and older, Black patients, and non-English speakers compared to younger, White, and English-speaking patients.

## Digital Divide and Health Equity Challenges

Obstacles limiting the use and benefit of digital healthcare tools include poor engagement of patients in design, lack of digital literacy, and lack of broadband access—collectively known as the "digital divide." The digital divide is defined as a multifactorial phenomenon encompassing unequal access to internet connectivity, digital devices, and digital literacy, often driven by socioeconomic status, geography, age, education, language, and disability.

## Implementation Solutions

Research highlights several promising interventions:

- Training patients in portal skills improves enrollment and engagement, and a "digital health navigator" (DHN)—a dedicated, trained staff member focused on digital literacy—is a promising approach that could support chronic disease management among underserved patients.

- Healthcare systems should expand MyChart's multilingual interface and content to ensure accessibility for diverse populations, with targeted outreach using advertisements and educational materials in different languages.

- Frameworks emphasize addressing "digital determinants of health" including infrastructure challenges like broadband access and individual barriers like digital literacy, while highlighting the importance of participatory design and community engagement.

These research findings underscore the importance of intentional, equity-focused strategies when implementing patient engagement technologies.
