## The one thing to get straight first
"The Gemini plan that got Oluremi off the bat" and "Gemini 3.5 speech-to-speech" are **two different products**, and conflating them would be an expensive mistake:

| | **Gemini-as-STT** (what got Oluremi) | **Gemini native audio / Live** (3.1 Flash Live) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Audio → **text**, with a text context prompt | Audio → **audio**, end-to-end (no transcript step) |
| Where it fits | **Drop-in STT** inside your *existing* cascade | **Replaces your entire pipeline** (STT+LLM+TTS) |
| Keeps your curriculum/grading/guardrails? | **Yes** — everything downstream is unchanged | **No** — you lose deterministic answer-checking |
| Latency | ~590ms transcribe (fine for turn-based) | Lowest possible + natural prosody/barge-in |
| Telephony | Works today (you already send WAVs to an STT) | **No SIP endpoint** — needs a Pipecat/LiveKit bridge + 8kHz→16kHz resample = re-architecting Asterisk |

**The Oluremi win was the first column — Gemini 2.5 Flash used as a transcriber with a lesson-context prompt.** That's the one you want. It is *not* the same as ripping out your pipeline for speech-to-speech.

## How everyone does low latency *before* training their own model
This is a settled playbook, and you're already on it:

1. **Cascaded pipeline (STT → LLM → TTS), but streamed.** The trick to sub-second isn't a magic model — it's **overlapping the stages**: the LLM starts generating before STT finishes, TTS starts speaking before the LLM finishes. A vanilla cascade is ~1.2–1.4s p95; a *streamed, tuned* one is **~500–650ms p95**. Human turn-taking gap is ~200–300ms, so that's the target.
2. **Hosted APIs first, self-host later.** Nobody trains their own STT/TTS on day one. They ship on Deepgram/ElevenLabs/OpenAI/Gemini (Deepgram STT ~150ms, ElevenLabs TTS ~75ms), get to market, and **collect real call audio + human corrections**. Only *then* do they fine-tune or self-host — for **cost and accuracy**, not latency. Deepgram itself is "transfer learning from general models"; that's the endgame, not the start.
3. **Turn detection + VAD is where latency is won or lost**, not the models. Semantic end-of-turn (Deepgram Flux, Silero VAD tuning) under 150ms. You already shipped the Silero VAD gate.
4. **WebRTC/UDP over WebSockets** for the media transport in production.

**Sabi is textbook on this path already:** streamed cascade (Groq Whisper → local faster-whisper; Claude Haiku → Groq → Ollama; ElevenLabs flash → Chatterbox), full-duplex barge-in loop, VAD gate live, and the `whisper-finetune` telephony-augmentation stack you built Jul 13 is *exactly* step 2's endgame. **Latency is not actually your bottleneck — accuracy on warbled Nigerian speech is.** That reframes the whole Gemini question.

## Is Gemini better? — Yes, as your STT. No, as speech-to-speech.

**Use Gemini 2.5 Flash as the STT (the Oluremi path) — recommended.** Why it fits Sabi specifically:
- It **takes a context prompt** ("expect a Nigerian name / a number 1–100"). Whisper structurally cannot. That context is *exactly* why it got "Oluremi" when every Whisper variant produced garbage. General benchmarks rank Whisper slightly higher (9.6 vs 8.8/10), but those are clean-audio, no-context — your real gold clips are the opposite, and context-prompted Gemini won there.
- **It slots into the cascade with zero loss of control.** Your deterministic numeric grading, curriculum path, guardrails, provider fallback, and voice all stay. You're swapping one box.
- ~590ms transcribe latency, streamable at 500–800ms chunks. Fine for turn-based tutoring.
- Cheap at your volume (fractions of a cent/call) — but **verify exact per-minute audio pricing when you enable billing** before committing.

**Do NOT move to Gemini native speech-to-speech (3.1 Flash Live) for Sabi right now.** It's genuinely impressive — 16kHz native A2A, lowest latency, hears hesitation/tone. But for a *curriculum tutor that must prove learning*, it fails on the things that matter:
- **You lose precise answer-checking.** S2S "understands" the child but gives you no clean, verifiable "she said 45." Your entire RCT / grant proof depends on deterministic pre/post assessment — a black-box audio model can't defend that to Gates/Mastercard.
- **Your child-safety guardrails don't transfer.** The prompt-engineering toolkit (system prompts, guard_input/guard_output) "does not apply directly to speech-to-speech models." You'd be rebuilding safety from scratch on a serving-minors product.
- **Weaker instruction-following + synchronous-only function calling** — bad for structured curriculum control.
- **No SIP endpoint** — you'd re-architect the Asterisk/AudioSocket path onto a Pipecat/LiveKit bridge with 8kHz↔16kHz resampling.
- **Unproven** on noisy Nigerian child telephony, and per-minute frontier-model cost vs. your self-hosted trajectory.

S2S is the right tool for open-ended chat/customer-service where latency dominates and there's nothing to grade. Sabi is the opposite.

1. **Enable billing on the Google project** → run the clean full bake-off (the blocker from Jul 15).
2. If names hold up, **wire context-prompted Gemini 2.5 Flash as the STT for name/word turns** inside the existing cascade; keep keypad + "say-it-as-digits" fallback for numbers (Gemini's numbers were still unreliable).
3. **Keep building the data moat** (real labeled Sabi call audio) to fine-tune your own model later — the standard endgame, for cost + accuracy, not latency.
4. Leave the pipeline architecture alone. Don't chase speech-to-speech until you have an RCT-proof product and a reason latency actually hurts you.
