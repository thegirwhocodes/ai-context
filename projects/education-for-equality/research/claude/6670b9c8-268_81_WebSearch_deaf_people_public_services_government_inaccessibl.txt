## Voting Access Barriers

People with disabilities have faced years of barriers to voting, including inaccessible polling sites, inaccessible online voter registration, and inaccessible voting machines that polling staff can't operate. Voters with disabilities are often met with a range of disproportionately burdensome barriers — from polling locations without ramps to restrictions on accessible voting options like vote-by-mail.

## Legal System and Courts

Deaf individuals often face significant barriers when navigating the legal system, particularly in court proceedings where auditory communication is the norm. Without access to interpreters and visual aids, they risk missing critical information, misunderstanding legal jargon, or being unable to fully participate in their own defense.

## Criminal Justice System

When prisons fail to provide accommodations, incarcerated deaf people may never be able to learn the rules of the prison, defend themselves in disciplinary hearings, or even hear meal call. Without these services, incarcerated people with hearing disabilities also cannot communicate with guards, medical staff, counselors, and family. As a result, people who are deaf or hard of hearing tend to be imprisoned more often, incarcerated longer, and returned to prison more quickly than others.

## Legal Protections

While the ADA and other federal laws require communication access (such as sign language interpreters and captioning), the search results indicate that these protections are frequently not implemented in practice.
