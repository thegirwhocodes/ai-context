## Key Finding: Quality vs. Quantity in RAG

Retrieval quality degrades predictably and mathematically as corpus size grows. This is a critical consideration when designing RAG systems.

## Practical Guidance on Scaling

If you're building a RAG system that will eventually exceed 10,000 documents, you need to plan for this from the start, which means hybrid retrieval with tuned BM25 weights, a reranking stage using a cross-encoder, metadata filtering, and a real offline evaluation harness with precision and recall metrics measured at target corpus size, not demo size.

A common mistake is that most teams use a vanilla FAISS index or whatever their vector database defaults to, feed in full documents chunked at 512 tokens, and call it done, which leads to degradation at scale.

## Improving Retrieval at Scale

Reranking alone can recover a large portion of the precision you lose at scale, where a cross-encoder reranker takes the top 50 retrieved chunks and re-scores them with full attention.

## Recent Research on Corpus Size

Recent 2026 research examining RAG scaling uses a retrieval setup with a fixed FAISS index constructed from a held-out corpus with size equivalent to 20% of the max pretraining tokens, and tests across multiple corpus sizes to study the scaling relationship between model size, corpus size, and performance.

The takeaway: there's no single "optimal" size—rather, the quality of your retrieval degrades with larger corpora unless you implement proper engineering practices like reranking, hybrid retrieval, and careful evaluation at scale.
