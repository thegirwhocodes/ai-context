## 🔎 What the research says (I did this myself)
The consensus for premium AI-product onboarding in 2026 is sharp and consistent:
- **60-second first value.** AI products live or die on reaching a *personalized* output fast; ~2 of 3 signups never reach first value in the average product. ([ProductLed](https://productled.com/blog/ai-onboarding), [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/user-onboarding/))
- **Context before content — 1–2 questions max**, then adapt the whole flow. Personalization lifts retention **30–50%** vs. generic. ([ProductLed](https://productled.com/blog/ai-onboarding), [SaaSFactor](https://www.saasfactor.co/blogs/why-users-drop-off-during-onboarding-and-how-to-fix-it))
- **Execution, not a tour.** Auto-generate the first real artifact; don't teach the UI. Gamma/Notion/Motion all do this. ([ProductLed](https://productled.com/blog/ai-onboarding), [Motion](https://www.usemotion.com/))
- **The aha = showing the user *their own data*.** Grammarly only asks you to install the extension *after* you've felt the value. ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/user-onboarding/))
- **OAuth is the most expensive friction point** (external dependency ≈ 3× a form field); make it feel unmistakably worth it, and never make it a hard wall. ([SaaSFactor](https://www.saasfactor.co/blogs/what-steps-should-your-signup-and-onboarding-include-to-reduce-drop-off))
- **Remove more than you add.** Slack won by deleting every step between signup and first message. ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/user-onboarding/))
