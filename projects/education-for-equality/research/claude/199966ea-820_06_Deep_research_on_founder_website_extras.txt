## Part 3: The 20 questions, answered concretely

### 1. OG image / link previews — **This is the #1 ROI fix. Build it tonight.**

When `naomiivie.vercel.app` gets pasted into Twitter, LinkedIn DMs, Slack, or iMessage, the preview right now is almost certainly either a generic Vercel-hosted screenshot or a blank card with just the title. Next.js 16 ships **no** default OG image — you have to define one. Per Vercel's official docs and Lee Robinson's writing on the topic, `next/og` (the maintained successor to `@vercel/og`) generates dynamic OG images at the edge via Satori + flexbox layouts ([Vercel — Introducing OG Image Generation](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-og-image-generation-fast-dynamic-social-card-images), [Next.js metadata docs](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/getting-started/metadata-and-og-images)).

**Spec:**
- **Size: 1200 × 630 px** (the universal OG/Twitter dimension — Slack, LinkedIn, Twitter, iMessage all render this correctly with `twitter:card = summary_large_image`).
- **Content to include:** the tagline she already has (*"I build voice AI that teaches Nigerian children to read on any phone"*), her name in Instrument Serif, a Sabi product hint (a phone glyph), the URL footer, and a credibility stamp (`MIT Sloan 2nd place · UNICEF · Wesleyan`).
- **Format:** Either ship a static 1200×630 PNG in `/public/og.png` and reference it in `metadata.openGraph.images` + `metadata.twitter.images`, OR ship a dynamic `app/opengraph-image.tsx` route using `ImageResponse` (recommended — same fonts as the site, can render per-page).

The build is 30–60 minutes. The payoff is permanent: every Slack DM where a YC partner shares her with a colleague becomes a recruiting moment instead of a blank rectangle. Of the senior founder sites I fetched, virtually every one ships a custom OG card; Naomi's site is the outlier here.

### 14. Mobile experience — **Verify three things explicitly tonight.**

The 2026 page-speed data is concrete: *mobile accounts for 62% of all eCommerce traffic, and only 42% of mobile sites pass all three Core Web Vitals while desktop pass rates are 63%* ([source — page speed statistics 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/page-speed-statistics-2026-revenue-impact)). For Naomi's audience — grant officers reading on iPhones in commutes, journalists on the move, YC partners scrolling between meetings — mobile is the default.

**Three checks tonight:**
- **Tap targets:** Every link in the project cards should be at least 44×44px. iOS Safari will tell her if any are too small via Inspector → Issues.
- **The hero tagline should not wrap awkwardly on iPhone SE width (375px).** The line is long — verify it doesn't break mid-word in landscape.
- **The headshot (when added) should be lazy-loaded** with explicit width/height to prevent CLS.

Tailwind v4's default container queries should handle most of this, but verify in actual Safari, not Chrome devtools simulation.

### 15. Loading speed / Lighthouse — **Aim for >90, not 100. Don't lose hours chasing the last 5 points.**

Current data: *"For every 100 milliseconds a website takes to load, the conversion rate can drop by 7%"* — but Google's John Mueller has stated explicitly *"Google doesn't use the X/100 lighthouse score for search; we use the core web vitals separately (LCP, CLS, FID)"* ([source — Lighthouse vs SEO 2026](https://www.coredna.com/blogs/How-lighthouse-score-affect-seo)). A 95 is functionally identical to a 100 for both conversion and SEO. **The marginal hours to chase 100 are better spent on the OG image and custom domain.**

What *does* matter: LCP < 2.5s, no layout shift when the headshot loads, and no render-blocking fonts. Naomi's stack (Next.js 16, Tailwind v4, no images currently) is already on the right side of this — adding a 200×200 headshot will not break it if she uses `next/image` with `priority={false}` and explicit dimensions.

### 16. Accessibility — **Mandatory floor. Not for "showing off" — for not getting filtered.**

Brittany Chiang's tagline literally leads with *"I build accessible, pixel-perfect experiences for the web"* — she made it her positioning. For Naomi, accessibility is a *floor*, not a brand. But it must be hit.

**The non-negotiable checklist:**
- **Alt text on every image** (the headshot: "Naomi Ivie" or "Naomi Ivie, founder of Sabi"; the OG card if it's an image; any project visuals).
- **Sufficient contrast** — Instrument Serif at small sizes on a light background can fail WCAG AA. Run [WebAIM contrast checker](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/) on the body/header pairs.
- **Keyboard navigation** — every link should be reachable by Tab, in order, with visible focus rings. Don't remove `:focus` outlines.
- **Semantic HTML** — `<main>`, `<section>`, `<h1>` once, `<h2>` for project cards. The editorial single-column structure makes this easy.

The grant-funder relevance is concrete: many foundations have accessibility statements in their grant guidelines and *do* run automated checks on applicant materials.

### 17. The "I am the customer" tier — **One sentence. Place it under the tagline or in contact.**

The Tandem application is the highest-leverage move in the entire brief. Tandem is "AI-native office leasing broker" — their ideal user is a *founder shopping for SF office space.* The site can say *"I'm planning to be in SF this summer and looking at office space for Sabi's first US team"* in the contact section.

This is a documented pattern in operator-hiring: *"founders care about what you shipped and what you learned… the keyword game is for ATS-screened large-company hiring, not for early-stage startup hiring where the founder reads the resume directly"* ([source — getting a job at a YC startup 2026](https://standout.work/blog/best-way-to-get-a-job-at-a-yc-startup)). When the founder of Tandem (or whoever screens) reads Naomi's site and sees *"looking at SF office space for Sabi's team this summer,"* she has just demonstrated she **is** the customer Tandem is building for. That's a level of conversion no resume bullet can match.

**Wording suggestion** (one sentence, in the contact block): *"In SF this summer — open to coffee, advising, and (if you're Tandem) help finding Sabi's first office."* Light, declarative, specific. The parenthetical earns a smile in the screening review.

### 18. 404 page — **Yes, but a small one. Not a project.**

Per the design research, *"a creative 404 page can reduce bounce rate by up to 40%"* ([source — best 404 examples](https://www.wix.com/blog/best-404-page-design)). For a founder site, the right tone is *brief and on-brand*. The Brett Terpstra pattern (linked from the same source) is "clean and clear, offering direct links to useful content."

**Recommended:** A `not-found.tsx` with three lines — *"Hmm, that page wandered off."* + a link to home + a link to Sabi. Same Instrument Serif. 5 minutes to ship. Don't over-engineer with animations.

### 19. Robots.txt + LLM crawlers — **Allow everything. Especially Claude-SearchBot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot.**

The 2026 strategic answer is clear: *"AI-referred traffic converts 4.4× better than standard organic search"* ([source — AI crawler management 2026](https://alicelabs.ai/en/insights/ai-crawler-management)). Anthropic split its crawlers into ClaudeBot (training) and Claude-SearchBot (live retrieval for citations); OpenAI split GPTBot (training) and OAI-SearchBot (search). The 2026 founder-strategic posture:

- **Allow:** OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot, Bingbot — these drive citation traffic *back* to her site.
- **Optionally block:** GPTBot, CCBot, Google-Extended if she doesn't want her writing used for training. (For Naomi: she has almost no writing yet; blocking is mostly symbolic. Allow everything for now.)

**Concrete robots.txt:** 4 lines saying `User-agent: *` + `Allow: /`. Done. Cite the founder-strategic rationale: when a journalist or grant officer asks Claude or ChatGPT *"who is the founder of Sabi voice AI?"*, she wants the LLM to have crawled her site, learned her positioning, and cited her in the answer. The cost of blocking is invisibility in the conversational-search lane that is replacing Google search in 2026.

### 20. Trust signals — **Add three. Skip the logo wall.**

The senior-founder consensus is restraint. Levels.io shows traffic ("5.8B total views") as a footer flex; Sahil Bloom shows subscriber count; Andrew Chen mentions essay count (650+). None of them ship "as featured in" logo strips. The pattern is **inline credibility woven into copy**, not a billboard.

**Three high-impact additions for Naomi:**
- **Inline press mentions in the recognition list:** *"MIT Sloan Hackathon — 2nd place ($3K)"* → *"MIT Sloan Hackathon — 2nd place ($3K) · [MIT News writeup]"*. The link is the trust signal.
- **Numerical proof on Sabi:** if Sabi has reached X children, taught Y lessons, or partnered with Z schools, the number goes in the Sabi card. *"Reached 1,200 children across Lagos schools"* is worth ten testimonials.
- **GitHub link with profile.** Not a follower badge (junior-coded), just the link. The cleaned-up GitHub is part of the trophy case per the brief — link to it.

**Don't add:** A logo strip ("As featured in MIT News, UNICEF, ..."). Of all the founder sites I fetched, **zero** had one. It reads as SaaS marketing, not founder presence. The 2026 PR research backs this: *"being featured everywhere can actually dilute your brand if the platforms, audiences, or angles don't align"* ([source — PR tactics that work 2026](https://brandfeatured.com/blog/pr-tactics-that-actually-work-in-2026)).

## Part 4: Patterns specific to her three audiences

The site is being read by people with very different decision criteria. The 2026 research backs treating these audiences differently:

**Grant funders (UNICEF, Schmidt Futures, foundations)** look for: organizational credibility, evaluation capacity, founder narrative, traction. They want to see *"organizational credibility, budget and financials, evaluation capacity, and key program staff bios"* ([source — grant funder relationship building 2026](https://grants.com/ultimate-step-by-step-guide-to-building-long-term-relationships-with-grant-funders-in-2026/)). They will read the Sabi page deeply. They will Google her name. **For this audience, the headshot, the JSON-LD Person schema, the deep `/sabi` case study page, and inline press links matter most.**

**YC partners / startup recruiters (Tandem, etc.)** look for: shipping velocity, taste, founder profile. *"Founders care about what you shipped and what you learned… they read 5% of cold applications because they have better signals to triage on."* **For this audience, the editorial restraint already on the site is correct.** The OG image is the most important addition (because partners share candidates in Slack), plus the "I am the customer" line and the resume PDF link.

**Journalists** look for: a headshot they can use, a one-line bio they can paraphrase, a press contact, prior coverage. **For this audience, the headshot + a press contact email + the OG card matter most.** A "press kit" page is over-investment for tonight but consider for the next iteration.

**Startup internship recruiters in 2026** specifically: the dominant new pattern is *"platforms that build a one-time profile from you, match it against open roles at hiring companies, and intro you directly to the founder when both sides say yes"* ([source — YC jobs 2026 strategy](https://standout.work/blog/best-way-to-get-a-job-at-a-yc-startup)). Her site is the off-platform version of that profile. The more it looks like a founder profile and less like a student portfolio, the more it gets forwarded.
