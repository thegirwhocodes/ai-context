## Literacy and Reading Outcomes

Median literacy rates of deaf high school graduates have remained consistently around the fourth-grade level since the beginning of the twentieth century, with about one in five deaf students graduating with reading skills at or below the second-grade level and about one in three having reading skills between the second- and fourth-grade level. Approximately half of deaf students in the United States were reading below the fourth grade level at high school graduation, with only 7–10% reading at the seventh grade level or above.

## Achievement Gap with Hearing Peers

Achievement of deaf and hard-of-hearing students continues to lag behind that of their hearing peers, and many do not acquire the knowledge and skills to reach their full potential. Students who are deaf and hard of hearing often experience notable disparities in performance compared to their hearing peers in literacy.

## Key Contributing Factors

Most deaf and hard-of-hearing children arrive at school with significant delays in language development relative to hearing peers. Many deaf and hard-of-hearing students enter school lacking fluency in either a signed or a spoken language.

## Role of Language Proficiency

Students highly proficient in American Sign Language outperformed their less proficient peers in nationally standardized measures of reading comprehension, English language use, and mathematics, with ASL proficiency being the single variable significantly predicting results on all outcome measures.

## Educational Attainment

Deaf students graduate high school at a 6% lower rate than hearing students. In 2017, 5% of deaf people ages 18-64 were currently enrolled in postsecondary education and training programs, compared to 11% of hearing people.
