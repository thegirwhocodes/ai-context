## Key Organizations and Initiatives

**Mizizi Elimu Afrika (formerly Zizi Afrique Foundation)**
Education stakeholders gathered in Nairobi on March 10, 2026, for the unveiling of Vision 2040: Strong Foundations for Lasting Change and the rebranding of Zizi Afrique Foundation to Mizizi Elimu Afrika, marking a fresh phase for the African-led organisation, which focuses on improving learning outcomes and ensuring children and youth acquire essential skills needed to succeed in school and life. The name "Mizizi," meaning roots or foundations, underscores the organisation's focus on foundational learning, including early literacy, numeracy, life skills and values that enable learners to develop the competencies necessary for future academic and personal success.

**Human Capital Africa (HCA)**
Human Capital Africa (HCA) is an advocacy organisation dedicated to improving foundational literacy and numeracy outcomes in Africa. HCA has established the African Foundational Learning Ministerial Coalition in partnership with the Association for the Development of Education in Africa (ADEA), which facilitates peer learning among education ministers, fostering collaboration and shared commitment to addressing foundational learning challenges.

**Gates Foundation's Global Education Program**
The Gates Foundation collaborates with partners to spur innovation and adopt effective, evidence-based solutions that can close the numeracy evidence gap, scale effective programs that strengthen both numeracy and literacy, integrate effective artificial intelligence and education technology tools into existing education programs, and support learning transitions for bilingual students and those in upper primary.

**UNESCO and Research Initiatives**
In an ongoing project supported by Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, Education Sub Saharan Africa (ESSA) and the Research for Equitable Access and Learning (REAL) Centre are mapping educational research that evidences practices to improve FLN skills among children.

**UNICEF's Support Programs**
In South Africa, UNICEF is supporting and championing the 'Early Grade Reading Programme' to implement evidence-based programming that can improve the overall quality of teaching and in-turn learning across key foundational learning areas: early childhood development, reading and literacy in home languages, and numeracy.
