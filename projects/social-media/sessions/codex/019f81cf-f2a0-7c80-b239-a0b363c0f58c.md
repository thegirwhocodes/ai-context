## Codex · 2026-07-20T23:27:02.743Z · final_answer

Honestly, the design is already pretty. The way to 10× it is to make every swipe feel inevitable—not add more decoration.

### 1. Strengthen the theological nuance

Your current wording could accidentally sound like the person’s evil doesn’t matter. Add this clarification early:

> This doesn’t excuse what they did.
> It means their actions don’t get the final word.

That makes the message compassionate, mature, and much more powerful.

### 2. Turn it into a story, not separate quotes

My recommended carousel:

1. **God loves you too much not to teach you that lesson.**

2. **This doesn’t excuse the evil someone did to you.**

3. **But sometimes, what you’re going through is bigger than the person who caused it.**

4. **God may be preparing you for a destiny that requires something new from you.**

5. **Discernment. Boundaries. Courage. Endurance. Wisdom.**

6. **There are assignments ahead that will require the very strength this season is building.**

7. **Maybe God isn’t only bringing you through it. Maybe He’s building something in you through it.**

8. **“Let patience have its perfect work…” — James 1:4**

9. **What is God teaching you in this season?**
   *Save this for the day the lesson feels too heavy.*

### 3. Create visual movement

Instead of repeating the identical cream card:

- Cover: mostly full photograph with dramatic oversized text.
- Slide 2: cream card for the serious clarification.
- Slide 3: zoom into the open sky.
- Slide 4: use the distant trees.
- Slide 5: minimal slide with the five qualities arranged almost like poetry.
- Slide 6: use the grass/field crop.
- Slide 7: return to the full photograph for the emotional payoff.
- Final slide: warm cream background with a small piece of the photo.

It would feel like the reader is gradually moving through the landscape while moving through the lesson.

### 4. Make the text more scroll-friendly

Aim for roughly 8–16 words per slide. Use:

- Playfair Display Semibold: 80–100 px
- Cormorant Garamond Italic: one emotional phrase per slide
- Montserrat SemiBold: 20–24 px for numbering and your handle
- Terracotta only for the most important phrase
- Small `@yourhandle` on every slide so reposts still lead back to you

The single biggest improvement would be adding the “this doesn’t excuse the evil” slide. It protects the message, deepens it, and makes the eventual conclusion hit much harder.
