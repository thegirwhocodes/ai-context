## Mobile Phone Ownership

Nigeria had 150 million cellular mobile connections in early 2025, representing 64.0 percent of the total population. However, some of these connections may only include services such as voice and SMS, and some may not include access to the internet.

When it comes to smartphone penetration specifically, smartphone penetration in Nigeria reached 85 percent across its 200 million population. Market projections indicate that 140 million people will own smartphones by the end of 2025, representing one of the fastest adoption rates across the African continent.

## Internet Penetration and Usage Gap

There were 107 million individuals using the internet in Nigeria at the start of 2025, when online penetration stood at 45.4 percent. This reveals a significant gap—while 85% have smartphones, only 45.4% actively use the internet, indicating a usage and adoption gap between device ownership and actual internet utilization.

An estimated 128 million people remain offline, highlighting the country's significant digital divide.

## Feature Phone vs. Smartphone Usage

The shift toward smartphones is evident: The shift from basic feature phones to internet-enabled devices is transforming how millions access financial services, conduct business, and connect with the digital economy. According to GSMA, smartphone penetration in urban Nigeria was 59 percent and 26 percent in rural areas in 2023.

This urban-rural divide underscores the usage gap, with feature phones still prevalent in rural areas where internet access and digital services adoption remain lower.
