## Major Complaints and Problems

Finding people with DTS horror stories to tell was not difficult. Soldiers have reported numerous issues with the system:

**Financial Impact**: Even though soldiers are filling out required voucher forms, DTS sometimes doesn't reimburse the travel card company, and after delays like two months, soldiers' travel cards get declined while DTS representatives refuse to correct errors. When problems with DTS prevent young soldiers from paying their bills on time, it can create lasting damage to their credit scores.

**Voucher Processing Issues**: Administrative and bureaucratic mistakes often result in requests for reimbursements being rejected, so soldiers' credit card bills are not paid on time. Earlier data showed that about 18 percent of vouchers were rejected and soldiers were asked to resubmit them during fiscal year 2004—a churning process that added to delays and frustration.

**Usability Frustration**: Service members have slogged through the jungle of digital paperwork that is DTS, only to file their forms and then find out at the eleventh hour that there was a problem.

**Submission Requirements**: Soldiers are required to use a government credit card and then submit a travel voucher within five days of the end of their trip, but they don't always have the time to do so.

The Pentagon has been working on a replacement system, with plans for a new system called "MyTravel" that will allow Department of Defense users to book trips and manage travel-related transactions, though full operation won't be until 2025.
