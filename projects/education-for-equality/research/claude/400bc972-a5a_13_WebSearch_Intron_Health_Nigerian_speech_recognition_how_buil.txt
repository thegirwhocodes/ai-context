## How It Was Built

Intron Health was launched in 2020 by Tobi Olatunji and helps healthcare workers digitize medical records by converting speech into text. The startup has developed Africa's first clinical speech recognition platform, which boasts up to 92% accuracy rate on medical terminology with heavy accents.

## Data Collection & Scale

Sahara v2 supports more than 500 distinct African accents, and was built using more than 14 million audio clips totaling over 50,000 hours from more than 40,000 speakers across 30 African countries. Earlier versions were trained on 3.5 million audio clips (16,000 hours) from over 18,000 contributors, representing 29 countries and 288 accents.

## The Sahara Method

Intron expanded its speech recognition platform, Sahara, to support 57 languages, adding 24 new ones as it deepens its push into healthcare, legal, financial services, and telecom. The company curated datasets of African voices, combining publicly available datasets with their in-house collection.

## Clinical Applications

The platform helps doctors across Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa and Uganda complete documentation seven times faster, significantly accelerating the adoption of Electronic Health Records (EHR).
