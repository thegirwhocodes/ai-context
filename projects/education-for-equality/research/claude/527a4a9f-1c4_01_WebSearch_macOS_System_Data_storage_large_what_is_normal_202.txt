## What's Normal

In 2026, healthy system data Mac storage usually sits at 12-25 GB on macOS Tahoe, Sequoia, Sonoma, or Ventura. More specifically, typically, it is around 12 GB, but depending on usage, anything between 15 and 30 GB can also be normal.

However, system data over 30GB usually means there's something worth cleaning, with the culprit typically being old app caches, local Time Machine snapshots, or leftover temp files from updates. If it exceeds 50 GB, it's worth investigating.

## What System Data Includes

System Data includes all types of data that do not fall into other categories of storage: Applications, Documents, iCloud Drive, iOS files, Photos, etc., along with other system files, downloaded items, and cache.

## How to Reduce It

Sometimes your Mac builds up an unnecessary amount of System files due to a bug, but most of the time it's a normal part of how the operating system works. Some effective methods to reduce System Data include:

- Deleting old Time Machine snapshots, iOS backups, .dmg files, and clearing caches while avoiding core system files.
- Emptying the Trash on your Mac can also help reduce System Data.
- Simply restarting your Mac can often resolve some of the issues you may be experiencing.

It's recommended to back up your Mac before deleting anything categorized as System Data, as some files in this category are essential to macOS, and deleting the wrong files can cause instability, data loss, or even prevent your Mac from booting properly.
