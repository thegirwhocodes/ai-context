## Foreign Holdings of U.S. Treasuries

Foreign investors hold about $8 trillion of Treasury securities, which is about 30 percent of the market. Governmental sources hold 47.7% ($3.8 trillion) of foreign holdings in U.S. federal debt, while private investors hold the other 52.3% ($4.2 trillion). The top three estimated foreign holders of federal debt by country, ranked in descending order as of December 2023, are Japan ($1.1 trillion), China ($0.8 trillion), and the United Kingdom ($0.7 trillion).

## Safe Asset Demand and Treasury Appeal

Treasuries offer safety and are backed by the full faith and credit of the United States government, with recourse to the independent U.S. legal system. During recent periods of high market volatility, foreign investors as a group actually increased their allocations to U.S. Treasuries.

## SVB Crisis and Systemic Risk Response

On March 12, the U.S. Department of the Treasury, Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, and Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) invoked the "systemic risk exception," to guarantee 100 percent of demand deposits—even those above the $250,000 statutory cap—at SVB as well as New York-based Signature Bank.

## Deposit Insurance Backstop Measures

With the Bank Term Funding Program (BTFP), an eligible institution can pledge its holdings of U.S. Treasury bonds, agency debt, and mortgage-backed securities as collateral, with up to $25 billion from the Exchange Stabilization Fund as a backstop for the BTFP, plus full collateralization by U.S. Treasuries and other qualifying assets.
