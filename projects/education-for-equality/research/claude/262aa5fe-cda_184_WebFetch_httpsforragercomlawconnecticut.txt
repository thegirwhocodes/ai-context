# Connecticut Cottage Food Law: Allowed Foods Analysis

## What the Law Permits

Based on Connecticut's cottage food law (PA 18-141), **only non-perishable, non-potentially hazardous foods are allowed**. The statute specifically permits:

**Allowed categories:**
- Bread and baked goods
- Candy and confections
- Condiments (honey, syrups, vinegars)
- Dry goods (cereals, pasta, spices)
- Preserves (jams, jellies)
- Snacks (popcorn, granola, crackers)

## Prohibited Foods (Including Prepared Meals)

The law explicitly prohibits:
- "Perishable baked goods"
- "Low-acid canned foods"
- "Sauces"
- "Meat jerkies"

**The key restriction:** Only "non-potentially hazardous" foods are allowed—"Most foods that don't need to be refrigerated (foods without meat, cheese, etc.) are considered non-potentially hazardous."

## Your Specific Questions

**Nigerian dishes and prepared meals:** Jollof rice, stew, beans, pounded yam, soups, egusi, ofada, and prepared meat dishes would **all be prohibited** because they:
- Require refrigeration
- Contain meat or other potentially hazardous ingredients
- Are perishable

These are temperature-controlled safety (TCS) foods requiring commercial kitchen licensing.

**Bottom line:** Connecticut's law restricts home food operations to shelf-stable items only. No prepared hot meals or cooked dishes are permitted under this law.
