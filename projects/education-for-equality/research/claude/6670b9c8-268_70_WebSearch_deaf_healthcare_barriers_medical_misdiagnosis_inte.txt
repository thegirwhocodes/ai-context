## Healthcare Barriers for Deaf Patients

Healthcare is routinely inaccessible to deaf people due to communication and linguistic barriers, with individuals whose primary language is American Sign Language (ASL) often denied access because most providers do not provide adequate communication access through qualified interpreters.

## Interpreter Shortage

The Registry of Interpreters for the Deaf (RID) shows there are only just over 10,000 certified ASL interpreters in Canada and the United States combined. In some areas like Southwest Louisiana in 2017, there were only three registered ASL interpreters serving around 800 Deaf people.

## Medical Misdiagnosis and Safety Risks

When patients do not have access to qualified interpreters, they face serious risks including misdiagnosis, medication errors, and lack of informed consent. Emergency departments lack interpreters of sign language, which results in delays, misdiagnosis, and more significant stress for patients.

## Emergency Department Challenges

Deaf sign language users experienced significantly longer treatment times and lower satisfaction in emergency care, reflecting systemic communication barriers. Although interpreter services were technically available in many sites, they were inconsistently implemented and frequently bypassed altogether.
