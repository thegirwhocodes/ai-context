Context recovered (last two sessions — Azure/Gemini voice work, board-meeting emails, Lagos contract, co-founder search). Here's the straight answer.

## 1. Can you legally do both? Yes — if you get one document right

California is the friendliest state for this:

- **Non-competes are void** (Bus. & Prof. Code §16600; SB 699/AB 1076 make it unlawful to even *include* one). So no SF startup can stop you from having your own venture.
- **Cal. Labor Code §2870** says an invention-assignment agreement *cannot* reach work you do (a) entirely on your own time, (b) without company equipment/facilities/trade secrets, and (c) that isn't related to the employer's business or its actual/anticipated R&D.

That third condition is where Sabi gets dangerous. If you join an **AI, voice, edtech, or Africa-market** startup, they can argue Sabi is "related to the employer's business" and their PIIA swallows your IP. A fintech or devtools job — no argument.

**Three non-negotiables before you sign anything:**
1. List Sabi on the **"Prior Inventions" / Exhibit A** of the PIIA — name it, the repos, the trademark, the curriculum. Blank exhibit = you assigned everything.
2. Get a **written carve-out + moonlighting consent** in the offer letter ("Employee may continue to develop Education for Equality / Sabi on their own time"). Startups grant this routinely for a non-competing venture. Ask at offer stage, when you have leverage — not after.
3. **Never** touch Sabi on the company laptop, wifi, Slack, or Google account. That's what actually kills the §2870 defense.

## 2. Time

| Role | Realistic hours/wk | Left for Sabi |
|---|---|---|
| Employee, seed/Series A SF startup | 50–60 | ~10–15 (nights/weekends) |
| Employee, later-stage (B+) | 45–50 | ~15–20 |
| **Co-founder** of another startup | 65–80 | ~0 — incompatible |

10–15 h/week is enough to **maintain** Sabi — keep prod healthy, take board calls, ship small fixes. It is **not** enough to run a Lagos pilot, chase the LASUBEB contract, and rebuild the voice stack. Those need a daytime person in the loop. That's the real trade, not the legal one.

## 3. When money actually shows up

**As an employee (the fast path):**
- Salary starts day one; California requires semi-monthly pay, so **first paycheck lands ~2–3 weeks after your start date**. Ballpark SF new-grad/junior at a funded startup: ~$120–170k.
- **Equity is not pay.** Standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff means **nothing vested for 12 months**, and even vested options convert to cash only at an acquisition, IPO, or tender offer — typically 7–12 years out, and usually never. Budget as if the equity is worth $0.

**As a co-founder of a new startup:** you pay yourself nothing until a real round closes — typically **6–18 months in**. Pre-seed founders take $0–60k; after a ~$2–4M seed, SF founder salaries run roughly $100–150k. So the first dollar is a year away and conditional on fundraising.

**From Sabi itself:** you can't draw a salary until there's an entity with a bank account and a **grant with a personnel line item** — E4E's 501(c)(3)/CAC is still pending via Goodstack. Most edtech grants fund program costs and allow staff salaries; that's your first realistic paycheck from Sabi, not revenue.

## 4. The thing that could override all of this

If you're on an **F-1**, the SF job is the easy part (CPT during school, OPT after). Being *paid by your own venture* is the hard part — F-1 self-employment is heavily restricted, and a company you control generally can't sponsor you because there's no valid employer-employee relationship. If that's your status, the SF job isn't just compatible with Sabi — it's the mechanism that keeps you in the country while Sabi grows. Tell me your visa status and I'll map the specifics.

**My read:** take the job, at a company with no edtech/voice/Africa overlap, with the carve-out in writing — and use the salary to fund the co-founder search you started yesterday, so someone owns Sabi's daytime while you own its direction.
