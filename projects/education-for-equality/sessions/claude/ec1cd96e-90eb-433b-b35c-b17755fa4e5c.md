## Quick structural note first

As a **Business Analyst (BA)**, you don't formally pick a practice — McKinsey staffs you as a generalist for your first ~2 years. But you *do* express preferences ("affinity") through internal tools, and over time you build a portfolio that pulls you toward a practice. So the right question is: which practices should I signal interest in and target studies from?

## McKinsey's practice landscape

**Industry practices** (the "what sector"):
- Healthcare Systems & Services
- Life Sciences (pharma/biotech)
- Public Sector
- Social Sector
- Education
- Financial Services
- Consumer & Retail
- Technology, Media & Telecom (TMT)
- Advanced Industries (auto, aerospace, semi)
- Energy & Materials
- Travel, Logistics & Infrastructure
- Private Equity

**Functional practices** (the "what kind of work"):
- Strategy & Corporate Finance
- Operations
- Marketing & Sales
- Organization / People & Org Performance
- Risk & Resilience
- Sustainability
- Transformation

**Firms-within-the-firm** (where the most interesting people often live):
- **QuantumBlack** — McKinsey's AI/ML arm. Data scientists + engineers + consultants building production AI.
- **McKinsey Digital** — product, design, engineering for digital transformations.
- **McKinsey Health Institute (MHI)** — non-P&L research/advocacy on global health.
- **McKinsey Global Institute (MGI)** — economic research arm; very academic.
- **Leap by McKinsey** — venture/business-building (helps clients launch new businesses).
- **Generation** — McKinsey-founded nonprofit, youth employment in emerging markets.
- **McKinsey.org** — pro bono / social impact arm (Rockefeller-funded work, refugee response, etc.).

## What fits *you* specifically

Given E4E (voice AI for African education), the Sabi pipeline, your DataFest healthcare-divide work, and your mission orientation — the cluster that lights up is:

1. **Social Sector Practice** — direct fit. Works with foundations (Gates, Rockefeller), multilaterals (World Bank, UNICEF — you've already touched UNICEF), and NGOs. This is where mission-driven BAs gravitate.
2. **Education Practice** — small but real. EKOEXCEL, NALABE, the World Bank RCTs you've read about — those are this practice's bread and butter. They'd recognize your work.
3. **QuantumBlack** — your strongest *technical* fit. You've actually shipped a Whisper + Claude + Chatterbox voice pipeline on a Hetzner GPU. Most BAs cannot say that. QB has a separate "Associate Data Scientist" track, but generalist BAs can also affinity into QB studies.
4. **McKinsey Health Institute** — your DataFest work on the MyChart digital divide is exactly the genre of analysis MHI publishes. Worth mentioning in interviews even if you don't target it long-term.
5. **Public Sector** — adjacent to Social Sector; lots of African government work runs through here (especially out of the Lagos and Nairobi offices, which is worth knowing).

## Honest tradeoffs

- The **mission-aligned** path (Social Sector / Education / MHI / McKinsey.org) is intellectually right for you but the work moves slower, budgets are smaller, and exits are narrower (foundations, nonprofits, impact investors — which may be exactly what you want).
- The **technical** path (QuantumBlack / Digital) puts you with engineers and pays better attention to the AI builder side of you, but the studies are often for banks, telcos, and CPG companies — not education.
- The **highest-leverage combo** is probably to signal **Social Sector + QuantumBlack affinity** and angle for studies that sit at the intersection (AI for development, ed-tech strategy for foundations, digital health in LMICs). Those exist and they're rare — being the BA who can credibly do both is a strong position.

## What to actually say in the application / interviews

Lead with E4E. Mention the Education and Social Sector practices by name. Mention QuantumBlack as your technical home. Don't over-list — naming 2–3 practices signals focus; naming 8 signals you haven't thought about it.

## Claude · 2026-05-18T01:56:39.177Z

If "business exposure" means *learning how companies actually work — P&L, ops, commercial mechanics, executive decisions* — the ranking is roughly:

1. **Private Equity & Principal Investors (PEPI)** — the highest *volume* of company exposure. Due diligence engagements are short (2–4 weeks), so a BA might see 6–10 different companies in a year, dissecting each one's financials, competitive position, and ops. No other practice gives you that many distinct businesses that fast.

2. **Strategy & Corporate Finance** — the highest *altitude* of exposure. CEO/CFO-level conversations: market entry, growth strategy, M&A, portfolio reshaping. Fewer companies, but you see how the top of the house actually decides things.

3. **Transformation** — the highest *depth* per company. You sit inside one company for 12–18 months working across every function (revenue, cost, ops, org). You learn how a P&L is actually moved.

4. **Operations** — the most *tangible mechanics*. Supply chain, manufacturing, procurement, service ops. You see how a business physically runs, not just its slides.

5. **Growth, Marketing & Sales** — pure commercial side. Pricing, sales force, channel strategy, customer growth. Revenue-side P&L work.

**For a founder specifically:** PEPI + Strategy & Corporate Finance is the combo that teaches you the most about how businesses get built, valued, and scaled. Social Sector and MHI are mission-aligned but commercially thin — you'll learn program design and stakeholder management, not how to run a P&L.

**Practical note:** BAs don't *choose* PEPI from day one — it's typically an affinity you build by saying yes to DD studies. But you can signal it early and most offices will accommodate.
