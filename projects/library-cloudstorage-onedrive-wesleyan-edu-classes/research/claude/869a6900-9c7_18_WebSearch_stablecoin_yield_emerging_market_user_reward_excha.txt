## The GENIUS Act Loophole

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) released a regulatory proposal in February 2026 that would restrict third-party yield arrangements, targeting what many view as a loophole in the GENIUS Act framework.

## The Core Issue

GENIUS does not explicitly prevent exchanges from paying rewards on stablecoins held on their exchange (which can be structured to be effectively equivalent to yield)—and many do. Specifically, crypto exchanges like Coinbase and Kraken were offering approximately 3.5–5% rewards on USDC, a USD-pegged stablecoin issued by Circle, by the end of 2025.

## Two Competing Models

The industry has fractured into two camps: The Compliance Model (Gemini) which offers 0% yield on GUSD holdings while competing through "active" spending rewards via credit cards that operate outside the legal definition of "interest," and the Subscription Model (Coinbase/Kraken) which utilizes exchange-level "rewards" programs bundled behind paid memberships to offer 3.5%–5% returns.

## Emerging Market Impact

Standard Chartered estimated stablecoin adoption could pull $1 trillion from emerging-market bank deposits over roughly three years, with savings usage rising materially by 2028, assuming stablecoins continue to function as quasi-savings vehicles rather than pure transactional instruments, which is exactly what happens when platforms offer rewards.

## Legislative Standoff

Coinbase withdrew its support for the CLARITY Act in mid-January 2026, stating the bill would effectively "kill" stablecoin rewards, and the Senate postponed its scheduled vote as the standoff intensified.
