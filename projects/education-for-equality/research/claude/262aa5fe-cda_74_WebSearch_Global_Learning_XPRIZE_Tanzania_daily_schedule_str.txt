## Global Learning XPRIZE Tanzania Overview

Through a first-of-its-kind randomized controlled trial in rural Tanzania, 2,500 tablets were distributed to children across 141 villages, engaging a 700+ person ecosystem with partners including UNESCO, the World Food Programme, and the Tanzanian government. Children ages 7-10 in 170 villages in Tanzania participated in a 15-month-long independent controlled evaluation of the finalists' Swahili apps.

## Winning Technologies

Two co-winners emerged: onebillion, whose tablets teach through games and stories, and Enuma, whose Kitkit School software achieved the highest learning gains.

### onebillion's Approach

"Onecourse is made of thousands of learning units, some could be on reading activities, some could be on numeracy activities — it's a modular course, it's built around the child's day and adapts to their needs." onebillion's software solution merged numeracy content with literacy material to offer directed learning and creative activities alongside continuous monitoring that enables the software to respond to children's individual needs.

### KitKit School's Approach

KitKit School developed a program featuring a game-based core and flexible learning architecture designed to help children learn independently, irrespective of their knowledge, skill, or environment. Enuma's Kitkit software, with 20K+ activities, achieved the highest learning gains in the prize.

## Learning Outcomes

Prior to the field test, 90% of the children could not read; at the end of the testing phase this figure was halved, demonstrating the effectiveness of the technology in providing basic education to children living in these communities.
