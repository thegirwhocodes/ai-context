## Voice Memos and iCloud

Yes, you can sync Voice Memos:

1. System Settings → your name → iCloud.
2. Click **See All** beside “Saved to iCloud.”
3. Turn on **Voice Memos**.
4. Confirm your recordings appear on another Apple device.

Apple confirms that Voice Memos will then appear across devices signed into the same Apple Account. [Apple Voice Memos iCloud instructions](https://support.apple.com/guide/voice-memos/see-your-recordings-on-all-your-apple-devices-vma6cc4d0571/3.2/mac/26)

However, Apple does not provide a Voice Memos equivalent of OneDrive’s “Free Up Space.” Syncing preserves and synchronizes the collection, but may not remove the nearly 5 GB of recordings from your Mac.

To make them genuinely cloud-only:

1. Create a `Voice Memos Archive` folder in OneDrive.
2. Drag older recordings from Voice Memos into that folder. Apple officially supports dragging recordings into Finder. [Apple Voice Memos export instructions](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/guide/voice-memos/vm05f9fa82d4/mac)
3. Wait for OneDrive to finish uploading.
4. Verify several recordings through OneDrive’s website or another device.
5. Right-click the archive folder → **Free Up Space**.
6. Only then delete the archived recordings from Voice Memos and empty Voice Memos’ Recently Deleted folder.

Important: deleting a synced memo removes it from Voice Memos on every device. Exporting also may not preserve Voice Memos-specific edit layers or transcription metadata, although it preserves the playable audio.

## Messages and iCloud

Messages can get much closer to cloud-only than Voice Memos.

1. Open Messages.
2. Messages → Settings → iMessage.
3. Enable **Messages in iCloud**.
4. Click **Sync Now**.

Apple says iCloud stores the message history and keeps only recently accessed messages locally, although attachments may remain until macOS needs the space. [Apple Messages in iCloud instructions](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/set-up-messages-mm0de0d4528d/icloud)

Your current Messages storage is approximately:

- Attachments: 1.30 GB
- Generated previews: 804 MB
- Messages temporary files: 1.13 GB
- Databases and other data: about 320 MB

After syncing, use System Settings → General → Storage → Messages to review large attachments. Deleting something while Messages in iCloud is enabled deletes it across your Apple devices. [Apple’s Messages deletion guidance](https://support.apple.com/guide/messages/delete-messages-and-conversations-icht1035/mac)

## Mail and iCloud

You cannot move Gmail into iCloud Mail simply to make it cloud-only. Gmail is already stored on Google’s servers; Apple Mail downloads a local cache for offline access and search.

Your Mac currently has two separate email caches:

- Apple Mail: **4.69 GB**
- Spark Desktop: **1.57 GB**

That means you are storing roughly **6.26 GB** of local email data across two clients.

Your options:

1. Choose one mail client—Apple Mail or Spark—and remove the accounts from the other.
2. Use Gmail in the browser and remove the Gmail accounts from both desktop clients.
3. In Apple Mail: Settings → Accounts → Account Information → set **Download Attachments** to `None` or `Recent`.
4. System Settings → General → Storage → enable **Optimize Storage**, which lets macOS retain only recent email attachments when space is needed. [Apple storage optimization](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/sysp4ee93ca4/mac)

Do not use Mail’s **Message → Remove Attachments** casually. For IMAP accounts such as Gmail, Apple says that can also delete the attachment from the mail server. [Apple Mail attachment guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/guide/mail/mlhlp1123/mac)

Before removing an account, verify there are no important drafts or “On My Mac” mailboxes that exist only locally.
