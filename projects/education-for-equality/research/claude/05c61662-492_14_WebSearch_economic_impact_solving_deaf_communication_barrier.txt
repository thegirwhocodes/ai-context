## Key Findings:

**Interpreter Shortage Scale:**
There are approximately 10,000 certified American Sign Language (ASL) interpreters in the United States, while about 500,000 Deaf or hard-of-hearing Americans use ASL, creating a ratio of roughly 50 to 1 Deaf or hard-of-hearing ASL users to ASL interpreters.

**Current Status in 2026:**
The supply of certified interpreters is stagnant or shrinking, largely due to the closure of interpreter education programs following the withdrawal of federal funding, with this shortage compounded by quality concerns stemming from insufficient linguistic fluency, limited support, and inadequate feedback in training.

**Communication Barriers and Consequences:**
Many facilities still rely on unqualified interpreters, family members, or inadequate video interpreting services, resulting in communication that is often inaccurate or incomplete, affecting safety, understanding, and equal participation.

**Economic Impact:**
