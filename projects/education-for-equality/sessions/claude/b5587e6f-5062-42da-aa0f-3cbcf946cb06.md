## 1. The feedback system — no, it's not efficient, and I found exactly why

I pulled the **last two real calls** off the server:

| Call | Number | End | Duration | Feedback? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `be48a49b` | +234 803 337 4126 (Nigeria) | `audiosocket_closed_by_asterisk_or_network` (caller hung up) | 3:08 | **❌ never offered** |
| `894b6040` | +1 860 436 7048 (your tester line) | `sabi_wrap_up` (natural) | 8:47 | ✅ captured (42s note) |

**Root cause — the code only offers feedback if the line is *still open* AND Sabi reached a graceful wrap.** In [voice_realtime.py:515](sabi-server/voice_realtime.py:515):

```python
return bool(messages) and not hungup and end_reason in FEEDBACK_OFFER_END_REASONS
```

`FEEDBACK_OFFER_END_REASONS` = `{sabi_wrap_up, max_call_seconds, normal_loop_complete, feedback_requested}`. **The moment the caller hangs up, `hungup=True` and feedback is skipped entirely.** That's the Nigerian call — the natural way a child ends a call (thumb on the red button when they feel done) is the one path that guarantees *no feedback ask*.

And even the **success** path is slow: the feedback **prompt itself played for 23.5 seconds** before the beep (`prompt_played_seconds: 23.5`). On an 8:47 call that already hit the hard cap, you're asking a tired caller to sit through a 23-second monologue before they can speak. Anyone less patient than you hangs up during the prompt.

**What the research says** (I searched it myself — IVR/post-call survey best practice):
- **Pre-announce it in the greeting.** "At the end I'll ask you one quick thing about how today went." Callers who know it's coming don't bail. ([Verint](https://www.verint.com/blog/top-10-best-practices-for-optimizing-post-call-interaction-surveys-using-ivr/))
- **State the length, keep the prompt to one line.** "This takes 20 seconds" beats a paragraph. Completion collapses after ~2 min and most abandon *during* a long prompt. ([FluentStream](https://www.fluentstream.com/blog/best-practices-for-effective-post-call-surveys/), [Sigma Voice](https://sigmavoice.com/post-call-survey-scripts))
- **Ask while it's fresh, before goodbye** — not after Sabi has signaled the call is over (which cues the hangup). ([Vonage](https://www.vonage.com/resources/articles/ivr-survey/))
- **For our case specifically:** since kids *will* hang up, the realistic backstop is an **SMS follow-up** (we already have the AT shortcode SMS helper) or **coordinator debrief** — not relying on the voice note.

**My recommended fixes** (I can implement):
1. Fold the feedback ask into the wrap-up turn *before* Sabi says goodbye — capture it while the line is guaranteed live, not in the `finally` block after the loop breaks.
2. Cut the prompt from 23.5s to ~5s: "Before you go — after the beep, tell me one thing about today's call."
3. Pre-announce in the greeting.
4. Add the SMS fallback for calls that ended `hungup` (fire an AT text: "How was Sabi today? Reply with anything.").
5. **Bigger point for the child pilot:** the open 90s voice-note was built for *adult testers* (you). Real 8-year-olds won't leave a reflective memo — swap to a one-word in-lesson check ("too fast, too slow, or just right?") plus coordinator debrief.

## Claude · 2026-07-07T20:19:50.162Z

Yes — `*` is on **every** phone, including the most basic feature phones. The standard telephone keypad has 12 keys, and `*` and `#` are two of them — they've been there since touch-tone phones existed:

```
 1   2   3
 4   5   6
 7   8   9
 *   0   #
```

On the cheap Nokia/itel/Tecno feature phones common in Nigeria, `*` is the bottom-left key, right next to `0`. Every one of them can send it as a DTMF tone over the call — that's the same signaling our keypad-answer fallback already relies on.

I actually picked `*` **on purpose** for a basic phone:
- **Universal** — no smartphone or app needed, it's a physical key on every handset.
- **Collision-free** — during a numeracy lesson a child might press number keys (or we ask them to key in a numeric answer as a fallback). `*` is never a numeric answer, so a frustration press can't be mistaken for "5" or "9".

Sabi also names it plainly in the greeting — *"press the **star key** on your phone"* — so a child knows exactly which one.

If you'd still rather use a number (say `0`), I can switch it in one line (`SABI_FEEDBACK_HOTKEY_DIGIT`), but I'd recommend staying with `*` for the collision-safety reason. Want to keep `*` and deploy?
