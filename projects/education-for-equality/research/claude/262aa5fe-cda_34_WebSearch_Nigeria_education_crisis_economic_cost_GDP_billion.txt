## Out-of-School Children Crisis

Nigeria has about 10.2 million primary-school-age children and 8.1 million lower-secondary-age children out of school, the largest such population anywhere in the world. More recent estimates indicate that UNICEF estimates 18.5 million Nigerian children were out of school by late 2024, about 15 percent of the global total.

## Learning Poverty

A 2024 World Bank assessment reports that 72.6 percent of Nigerian children aged 7–14 cannot read and understand a simple text. Additionally, one in four children never enters primary school, and three in four who do attend cannot read or do basic numeracy.

## Budget Allocation Issues

Nigeria's 2024 federal allocation was roughly 8.2 percent of the education budget, about 3.3 percent of GDP. Nigeria unveiled its 2025 appropriation bill in December 2024 with an allocation of N3.52 trillion to education—representing 7.3 percent of the total budget. UNESCO recommends that countries invest 15–20 percent of public spending or 4–6 percent of GDP in education.

## World Bank Support

The World Bank approved three operations in Nigeria, totaling $1.08 billion in concessional financing, including $500 million for the HOPE-EDU program.

## Teacher Salary Shortfalls

In the 2025 budget, only ₦1.64 trillion is allocated for personnel, leaving a ₦290 billion shortfall.
