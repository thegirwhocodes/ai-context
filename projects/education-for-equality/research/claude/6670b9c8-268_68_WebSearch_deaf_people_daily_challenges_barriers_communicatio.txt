## Communication Barriers

Barriers to accessibility for deaf people can be classified into three main categories: communication barriers, architectural barriers, and technological barriers, with communication barriers often resulting from a lack of written or visual information and a gap in understanding between service providers and individuals. Limited opportunities to learn sign languages and a lack of awareness about deaf culture fuel communication barriers.

## Employment Challenges

Employment presents significant obstacles. Only 57.1% of adults with hearing loss are employed, compared to 73.3% of hearing people, and people with hearing loss earn about 25% less; their mean wage was $23,481, compared with $31,272 for typical-hearing peers. A survey among 53 British deaf people showed accessibility and inclusivity in the workplace is still a problem, with respondents reporting exclusion from conversations with colleagues (83%), loneliness at work (69%), exclusion from social events (59%), and bullying and unkindness at work (34%).

## Education and Access

Prejudices surrounding Deafness have historically hindered the full participation of individuals with hearing loss in many experiences, limiting their access to education, employment, culture, and social interaction.

## Daily Life Challenges

For most people, everyday tasks such as going to the supermarket, ordering a drink at a coffee shop or phoning the GP are so routine that they barely require thought, but for members of the Deaf community, these same activities can be filled with invisible barriers or moments of exclusion.
