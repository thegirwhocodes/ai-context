## What the market actually pays

**Per flyer, direct to client (entry-level freelancer, which is you):**
| Level | Rate |
|---|---|
| Entry-level freelance | **$150–350** |
| Mid-level (3–5 yrs) | $400–800 |
| Senior | $900–1,500+ |
| Boutique agency | $800–1,500 |

**By flat-fee complexity:** simple $75–200 · standard $200–500 · complex w/ custom illustration $500–1,200.

**By client type — this matters most:**
- **Event promotion — $250–600** ← the highest-paying flyer category, and it's exactly the work you have
- Real estate — $200–500
- Restaurant/menu — $200–450

**Hourly:** entry-level $25–45/hr, and a flyer is 2–3 hours of work.

**Other services:** social posts $5–50 each, or $100–1,000+/month as a package. Brand/identity work $300–5,000+.

**Add-ons people forget to charge for:** rush delivery **+25–50%**, extra revisions **$50–100 each**, unlimited commercial usage **2–3×** base.

## The finding that changes your plan

**Fiverr and direct clients are two different markets with a ~10× gap for identical work.**

On Fiverr, flyer gigs sit at **$13–35**, and a new seller with no reviews realistically can't launch above **$15–35** for a basic tier — buyers there filter by price and review count, and you have neither yet. Fiverr also takes **20%**, so a $35 gig nets you **$28**.

Direct to a business, that same flyer is **$150–250**.

So your $65–75 instinct is the one number that works badly in *both* channels — it's roughly **2× too high** to win on Fiverr as a new seller, and **half** of what a direct client would have paid you without blinking. That price doesn't undercut anyone; it just leaves money on the table with businesses while still losing the marketplace race.

## What I'd actually charge

- **Direct clients (IG DMs, Facebook groups, Nextdoor, Reddit): $150 per flyer.** You're squarely in the $150–350 entry band and your portfolio looks well above entry. If you need a same-day close, run **"$99 for my first 3 clients, then $150"** — that's still 3.5× what Fiverr would net you, and the discount has an honest reason attached.
- **Events specifically: quote $200.** It's the top-paying flyer category and promoters treat graphics as marketing spend, not a personal expense.
- **Fiverr: $35 / $75 / $150** across Basic/Standard/Premium — and treat the first 2–3 orders as *buying reviews*, not earning income. Fiverr is a review-farming channel, not your income channel.

**The bigger point:** flyers are the cheapest thing you sell. Your portfolio is deck-heavy — OmniCue, Sabi, the consulting deck — and pitch decks and brand systems go for **$200–500** and **$300–5,000+** respectively. You're leading with your lowest-value service. Lead the cold messages with decks and brand kits, and let flyers be the entry point people negotiate *down* to.
