I found extensive information about the Army Talent Alignment Process (ATAP). Here's a comprehensive overview of the system and the issues that have been documented:

## What is ATAP?

The Army Talent Alignment Process (ATAP), also known as "faces to spaces," determines how many individuals (faces) will participate in the market (spaces), and the specific job opportunities and locations that must be filled. ATAP is a decentralized, regulated, market-style hiring system that aligns officers with jobs based on preferences.

## Key Problems and Issues Documented

### Signal Problems
One significant issue that emerged early was related to how officers communicated interest to units. Officers adjusted their preferences multiple times to signal interest to different units at different times, which resulted in an unlimited number of signals for officers to use and generated substantial problems.

### System Transition Issues
The Army is migrating ATAP marketplaces from AIM2 into IPPS-A, and officers must ensure their Year Month Available (YMAV) information is accurate during this transition to IPPS-A.

### Resume and Information Quality
The system relies on officers providing accurate information in their resumes and profiles, which creates challenges in ensuring data quality for the matching process.

## Current Status and Improvements

In the first cycle, around 50% of more than 14,000 officers who went through the process for their summer 2020 moves got their first choice assignment—meaning they and the unit had each other as their first choice. The Army is currently piloting a one-sided marketplace pilot (ATAP 25-02) for pre-key developmental (KD) active-duty captains, where the unit is removed from the equation and the marketplace algorithm purely considers the officers' preferences for their next assignment.
