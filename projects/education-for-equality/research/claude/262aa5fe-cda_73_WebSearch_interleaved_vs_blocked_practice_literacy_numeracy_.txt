## Research on Interleaved vs. Blocked Practice for Children's Literacy and Numeracy

I found substantial research comparing these practice approaches for children's learning. Here are the key findings:

### Effectiveness and Long-Term Learning

While students made more errors during interleaved practice and perceived it as more difficult, they showed superior performance on both trained and untrained words at immediate posttest and 8-week follow-up in a study of German third-graders learning spelling. Interleaved learning, relative to blocked learning, facilitates natural category learning in both children and young adults.

The bulk of the evidence supports the superiority of interleaved over blocked training, although there are noteworthy boundary conditions.

### How Interleaving Works

Interleaving is essentially about discrimination. When learners encounter different items back-to-back, they must pay attention to what distinguishes one from the next. This strengthens their ability to categorize and apply the right rule or strategy. The key thing to understand about interleaving is that it leads to poorer performance in the short term BUT better learning in the long term.

### Application to Numeracy

Pupils who were exposed to interleaved practice displayed significant improvements in their performance levels after the intervention for basic arithmetic. There is strong evidence that interleaved practice benefits children's mathematics problem solving.

### Important Caveats

These benefits were primarily observed in students with average to high prior spelling knowledge. Additionally, blocking can be more effective than interleaving for rule-based learning. Research has found an interaction between learning strategy and sequence; that is, when memorizing, interleaving is more effective than blocking for classifying based on similarity, but when trying to find a rule, blocking is more effective than interleaving for classifying based on the rule.
