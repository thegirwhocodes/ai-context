## Key Systems and Initiatives

**Voice-Based Literacy Systems:**

Allô Alphabet is a voice-based application deployed in Côte d'Ivoire that uses an interactive voice response (IVR) system to deliver voice-based lessons and quizzes to foster early literacy skills such as phonological awareness, with later introduction of letters via SMS on low-cost mobile devices.

**SMS-Based Learning Platforms:**

Shupavu291 is a popular SMS-based study tool used in Kenya, with analysis showing it was used by more than 1.3 million students between 2016 and 2018. Eneza Education, founded in 2011, uses SMS-based learning to make affordable learning materials widely accessible to pupils in remote and low-income communities in four African countries.

M-Shule is a mobile learning management platform that uses the technology most readily available in Kenya and sub-Saharan Africa and syncs it with primary school curricula, using artificial intelligence to deliver personalized learning support over text message and chatbots.

## Effectiveness and Impact

Mobile learning is used as a supplement for formal and informal schooling during disruptions with equivalent or higher intensity, as parents feel responsible to ensure continuity in schooling. Research in Niger and Senegal demonstrated that the familiarity of mobile phones and SMS messaging could make them an effective modality for improving learning outcomes in adults participating in education programs.

## Advantages for Low-Resource Contexts

In most parts of Africa, available solutions are limited to SMS and Voice, as this is the only medium that works on most basic devices.
