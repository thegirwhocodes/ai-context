Based on the search results, here's what research shows about the economic returns to literacy and numeracy:

## Wage Premium Comparisons

For British 34-year-olds in 2004, an additional standard deviation in literacy skills attracted a wage premium of around 14% and better numeracy skills a premium of around 11%. However, among prime-age workers across countries, a one-standard-deviation increase in numeracy skills is associated with increased hourly wages averaging some 18 percent.

## Returns in Developing Countries

Research on developing countries shows significant economic returns to foundational skills. A one-standard deviation increase in literacy skills is associated with an earnings increase of 14.6% in developing countries, which is statistically indistinguishable from the average returns of 17.7% estimated for developed countries.

For a specific developing country context, in Indonesia, after adjusting for family background in childhood, a one standard deviation difference in foundational skills is associated with an 11 percent increase in adult earnings.

## Cross-Country Heterogeneity

The wage premium paid to those with better literacy or numeracy varies across countries, reflecting the relative supply of and demand for such skills. In developing countries, there is considerable heterogeneity in the returns, with the lowest estimates found in Armenia and Ukraine at 2% (not statistically significant) and 6%, respectively, and the highest reported in Kenya at above 30%.

## Joint Effects

When both literacy and numeracy are examined together, improved literacy and numeracy skills narrow the labour market outcomes gap between individuals with different levels of formally recognised education, but do not close it completely.
