## Overview of Nigerian Pidgin English

Nigerian Pidgin English (Naijá) is one of the main languages of Nigeria and is an English-based creole with a Niger-Congo language syntax, consisting of many loan words from different Nigerian, other West African, and European languages.

## Word Lists & Resources

One comprehensive dictionary contains over 2,200 pidgin words, phrases and slangs, 360 pidgin statements used in everyday activities, and 280 Nigerian proverbs all written in pidgin.

## Common Words & Phrases

Some examples of Nigerian Pidgin words include:
- "How far?" (Hello), "Wetin?" (What?), "No wahala" (No problem), "Abeg" (Please), and "Wahala" (Trouble)
- 'Chop' means 'food', so 'I wan Chop' means 'I want to eat'
- 'Walahi' is a Hausa word that means 'sincerely or truthfully', and 'Koro' is borrowed from Isoko

## Cultural & Linguistic Significance

Among Nigerians, using Pidgin shows that you can identify with everyone irrespective of status, and for visitors, learning Pidgin shows you're making an effort to integrate.
