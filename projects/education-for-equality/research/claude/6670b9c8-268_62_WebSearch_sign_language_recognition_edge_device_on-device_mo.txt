## Sign Language Recognition on Edge Devices

Sign Language Recognition (SLR) systems have gained significant popularity in recent years, but despite the existence of various Deep Learning models to classify sign languages, the deployment of these models on resource-constrained devices remains a challenge.

## Model Compression and Optimization Techniques

Recent research demonstrates effective approaches to deploying SLR on edge devices. One study focused on creating a word-level custom dataset of 33 classes and trained three deep learning models (LSTM, BiLSTM, and Encoder-only Transformer) for dynamic sign language recognition, with trained models optimized using quantization techniques and converted to .tflite format for efficient deployment on resource-constrained devices like smartphones.

### Quantization Results

For BiLSTM, the model size was compressed to 3,862.01 KB (around 72% reduction from standard TFLite), with improved testing accuracy (95.71%), faster inference time (21.85 ms), and increased IPS at 35.97, while resource usage remained low (18% CPU, 12 MB RAM).

LSTM preserved its 94.7% accuracy at a model size value of 1,254.49 KB and an IPS of 51.02, demonstrating that on-the-fly quantization can be effective for robust and stable models.

## TinyML and Edge Deployment

Tiny Machine Learning brings intelligence directly onto microcontrollers and low-power devices, allowing models to run locally in real time without relying on the cloud. Recent work shows S3D-Conv1D achieved 98.4% float32 accuracy, and after INT8 quantization, it retained accuracy within 0.18% while compressing nearly 4× into sub-megabyte binaries (895.9 KB).
