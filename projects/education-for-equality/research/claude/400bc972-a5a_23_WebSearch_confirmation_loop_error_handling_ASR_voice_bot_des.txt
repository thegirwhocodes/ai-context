## Error Handling and Confirmation Strategies

ASR misrecognition occurs when speech recognition converts spoken input into incorrect text, including simple transcription errors or dangerous plausible substitutions such as "cancel" being interpreted as "schedule." The three-attempts rule means that after three failed attempts to understand or resolve an issue, the system should offer an escape path such as human handoff instead of continuing the retry loop.

Recognition errors can be mitigated by detecting that an error has occurred, after which the virtual assistant might initiate a recovery strategy such as asking clarifying questions or asking the user to repeat a certain part of the request.

## Children-Specific Challenges

Commercial ASR engines continue to underperform with children's speech, with key challenges including higher acoustic variability, shifting formant frequencies due to vocal tract development, and irregular pronunciation. The core challenge for ASR systems in educational settings is not only to transcribe the speech but to make sure that the errors made by language learners are transcribed faithfully, which is needed to provide language learners with corrective feedback, a key component of foreign language development.
