## Recent Advances in Sign Language Recognition (2025)

**Few-Shot Learning Approaches:**
Recent research explores a Few-Shot Learning (FSL) paradigm, specifically utilizing Prototypical Networks, where instead of training a classifier to memorize specific class boundaries, the model is trained "episodically" to learn a generalizable metric space, and an embedding of a query video is classified by finding the nearest "prototype".

Experimental results on the WLASL dataset demonstrate that this metric learning paradigm achieves 43.75% Top-1 and 77.10% Top-5 accuracy on the test set, outperforming a standard classification baseline by over 13%.

**Zero-Shot and Data-Efficient Methods:**
SignVLM is a framework that integrates a frozen CLIP visual encoder with a Transformer-based temporal decoder, emphasizing data efficiency which enables effective learning under zero-shot and few-shot conditions.

The model achieved state-of-the-art performance on KArSL and LSA64, outperformed RGB-based approaches on WLASL-100, and remained competitive on AUTSL despite using a single RGB modality and a lightweight architecture, demonstrating that CLIP's spatial embeddings can generalize effectively without requiring extensive dataset-specific tuning.

**Cross-Lingual Few-Shot Learning:**
Cross-lingual few-shot transfer—pretraining on a data-rich source language and adapting with only a handful of target-language examples—offers a scalable alternative, though conventional coordinate-based keypoint representations are susceptible to domain shift that is particularly detrimental in the few-shot regime.

**Real-Time Systems:**
Transfer learning using pre-trained VGG16 models can recognize hand poses while utilizing low computational resources, with real-time testing confirming practical useability for those with speaking and hearing disabilities.
