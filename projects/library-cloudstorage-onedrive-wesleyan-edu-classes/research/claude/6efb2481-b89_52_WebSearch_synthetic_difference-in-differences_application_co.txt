## Overview of Synthetic Difference-in-Differences

Synthetic difference-in-differences is a new estimator for causal effects with panel data that builds on insights behind the widely used difference-in-differences and synthetic control methods, with desirable robustness properties and strong performance in settings where conventional estimators are commonly used.

## Key Contributors

The method was developed by Arkhangelsky, Dmitry, Susan Athey, David A. Hirshberg, Guido W. Imbens, and Stefan Wager, with their work published in 2021 in the American Economic Review.

## Methodology and Properties

The synthetic difference-in-differences method combines attractive features of both difference-in-differences and synthetic control approaches—like synthetic control, it reweights and matches pre-exposure trends to weaken reliance on parallel trend assumptions, and like difference-in-differences, it is invariant to additive unit-level shifts and allows for valid large-panel inference.

The SDID estimator has doubly robust properties: it is consistent under a wide variety of weighting schemes given a well-specified fixed effects model, and consistent with appropriately penalized synthetic control weights when the basic fixed effects model is misspecified.

## Application Example with Countries

In a country-level economic application, the dataset contains observations on annual real GDP for 111 countries for 48 consecutive years, starting from 1959.

The methodology has been applied across various fields including policy evaluation, and continues to be used in empirical economic research.
