## A. EXTERNAL CURRICULUM VALIDATION IN NIGERIA

### A.1 UBEC — Universal Basic Education Commission

**Leadership (current 2026):**
- **Executive Secretary: Hajiya Aisha Garba** (Dr. Aisha Mahmood Garba) — appointed by President Tinubu. Background: Senior Education Specialist at World Bank with 24+ years driving impactful programmes in education.
- HQ: Universal Basic Education Commission, Abuja.

**Statutory mandate (relevant to validation):**
- Formulates policy guidelines for the UBE programme
- Prescribes and monitors minimum standards for basic education nationwide
- Ensures the implementation of approved curricula, syllabi, and instructional materials
- Note: UBEC ratifies/recommends — NERDC is the actual curriculum developer.

**EdTech-relevant pathway:**
1. **Public-Private Engagement and Partnerships (PPEP) Framework** — UBEC is currently (as of late Nov 2025, in Lagos, with UNICEF) overhauling this framework. The new PPEP framework will provide "clear, actionable guidelines for engaging relevant government agencies and a diverse range of stakeholders, including corporate organizations, development partners, civil society groups, and philanthropic foundations." For Sabi, this is the formal track for partnership; the framework is still being drafted as of May 2026, which is an opportunity to be early.
2. **UBEC EdTech Festival** — UBEC has been running EdTech Festivals at the UBEC Digital Resource Centre, Abuja (in collaboration with CcHUB). This is where EdTech vendors get visibility with UBEC leadership.
3. **NITDA partnership** — Aisha Garba and NITDA DG Kashifu Inuwa announced (Dec 2025/Jan 2026) a roadmap "to embed digital skills into the core of the basic education system" — entry point for AI/voice tech.

**Process for a non-government curriculum to get UBEC endorsement:**
- There is **no published fee schedule** for curriculum endorsement (unlike NERDC textbook certification). The pathway is relational/political: it goes through partnership MOU rather than a vendor application form.
- Typical pattern: NGO/edtech vendor secures pilot with a State SUBEB (Lagos LASUBEB is the most receptive), generates learning outcome data, then UBEC recommends nationally.
- Contact: through MTN Plaza area or UBEC HQ Abuja; no public partnerships email — recommend writing to Executive Secretary's office directly with a proposal deck.

**Current state (2026):**

## C. LEGAL STRUCTURE DECISIONS FOR US-NIGERIAN NONPROFIT

### C.1 501(c)(3) application timeline (2026 realities)

**Form 1023 (Standard / Long Form):**
- IRS issues 80% of determinations **within 191 days** (~6.5 months)
- Realistic range: **6-9 months**, sometimes 3-12 months depending on backlog/complexity
- Filing fee: **$600** (Form 1023)
- Length: ~28 pages plus schedules

**Form 1023-EZ (Streamlined):**
- IRS issues 80% of determinations **within 22 days** (~3 weeks)
- Typical approval: **2-4 months**
- Filing fee: **$275**
- **CRITICAL eligibility:**
  - Annual gross receipts **cannot exceed $50,000** in past 3 years AND projected next 3 years
  - Total assets **cannot exceed $250,000**
  - Must be a nonprofit corporation incorporated in a US state (unincorporated associations cannot use it)
  - Tax-exempt status not previously revoked
  - **Excluded:** hospitals, schools (literal schools), churches, housing providers, foundations
- **Current backlog (May 2026):** IRS currently reviewing 1023-EZ applications submitted before **February 24, 2026** — backlog of ~3 months for the "streamlined" form

**Expedited Processing:**
- **Form 1023-EZ CANNOT be expedited.**
- Form 1023 (long form) can be expedited only with compelling reason (e.g., disaster relief, specific grant deadlines that require 501(c)(3) status). Naomi would need to document a specific grant or pledge contingent on 501(c)(3) determination.

**For Sabi/E4E:**
- If Naomi expects E4E to stay under $50k gross receipts for the next 3 years (probable if mostly grant-funded with grants <$50k), **Form 1023-EZ is the right path** — 2-4 month approval, $275 fee.
- If she expects a major grant ($100k+) that would push her over $50k in a year, file Form 1023 (long form).

### C.6 Connecticut LLC vs CT nonprofit corporation (current status review)

**If currently incorporated as CT LLC:** Most states don't allow direct conversion of LLC → nonprofit corporation. Two paths:

**Path A: Articles of Conversion (if CT allows)**
- File Articles of Conversion with CT Secretary of State
- Assets transfer automatically to the new nonprofit
- Must change tax status from "partnership" to "corporation" (file IRS Form 8832)
- Apply for new EIN
- Then file Form 1023/1023-EZ for 501(c)(3) recognition

**Path B: Dissolve LLC + form new CT nonprofit corporation**
- Dissolve the LLC properly (settle debts, distribute remaining assets)
- File new Certificate of Incorporation as **nonstock corporation** with CT Secretary of State
- Adopt nonprofit bylaws (3+ directors required)
- Get new EIN
- File Form 1023 / 1023-EZ
- This is cleaner from a 501(c)(3) review standpoint

**CT-specific requirements:**
- **3+ directors required** for nonprofit corporation
- CT Secretary of State Certificate of Incorporation **standard template does NOT meet IRS 501(c)(3) requirements** — Naomi needs to add custom purpose/dissolution clauses (Foundation Group, Harbor Compliance, Northwest Registered Agent all sell templates ~$50-200)
- CT charitable solicitation registration required separately if soliciting donations in CT

**Recommendation:** If E4E is currently a CT LLC and Naomi wants 501(c)(3) status, **Path B (dissolve + reform as nonstock corporation) is cleaner** unless there are significant assets/contracts in the LLC that would be costly to transfer. Talk to a CT nonprofit attorney — flat-fee setup typically $1,500-3,500.
