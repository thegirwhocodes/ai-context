Based on the search results, I found relevant information about Milan Burnett's 2014 methodology for calculating the economic cost of out-of-school children (OOSC).

Milan Thomas and Nicholas Burnett of the Results for Development Institute (R4D) prepared research on this topic. Two methods (developed in Burnett et al. 2013) were used to estimate the economic cost associated with OOSC in seven countries – Cambodia, Indonesia, Lao PDR, the Philippines, Thailand, Timor-Leste, and Viet Nam.

Regarding the wage premium methodology: The literature measures the returns to education in terms of wage premia – the expected percentage difference in earnings between those who complete a given level of education and those who do not. Wage premia estimates provide a measure for the direct private benefit of education completion.

For the primary completion formula approach: The additional increase in aggregate income that primary-enrolled OOSC would be expected to generate due to the access they gain to secondary education is calculated by multiplying the wage premium to secondary education by the rate of continuation from primary to secondary school and the rate of secondary school completion.

The methodology uses a micro approach: The first estimation approach uses labor market data to estimate the total earnings that will be forfeited in the near future due to undereducated workers if primary school enrollment patterns do not change.
