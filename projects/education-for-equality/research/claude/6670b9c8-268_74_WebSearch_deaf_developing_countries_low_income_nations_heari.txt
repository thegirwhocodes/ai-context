I've completed a web search on hearing loss and deaf services in developing countries. Here are the key findings:

## Global Statistics

Around 466 million people worldwide (over 5% of the world's population) have disabling hearing loss and 34 million of these are children. About 80% of people with hearing loss are currently living in low- and middle-income countries. More specifically, more than 70 million people in the world are deaf and out of them, 80% of deaf people are living in developing countries.

## Geographic Distribution

The greatest burden of disabling hearing loss is in the Asia-Pacific area, southern Asia, and sub-Saharan Africa. The prevalence in this age group is greatest in South Asia, Asia Pacific and sub-Saharan Africa.

## Service Gaps

The availability of hearing services in developing countries is severely limited. The current production of hearing aids meets less than 10% of the global need and less than 3% of developing countries' needs. Additionally, the lack of availability of services for fitting and maintaining these devices, and the lack of batteries are also barriers in many low-income settings.

Health-care services need to be prepared for the projected increases in burden, particularly in low-income and middle-income countries where ear specialists and audiologists are scarce.

## Educational and Employment Impact

The lack of services has serious consequences. In developing countries, children with hearing loss and deafness rarely receive any schooling. UNESCO estimates that 75% of Deaf children worldwide do not have access to formal education.
