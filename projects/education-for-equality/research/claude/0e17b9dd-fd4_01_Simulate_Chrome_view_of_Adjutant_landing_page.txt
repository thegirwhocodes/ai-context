## 2. Website-Product Fit — Friction Points

### 2.1 The 5-second test — does the visitor know what Adjutant is?

**Mostly yes, but with caveats.** The hero gives:
- Title: "Speak it. Sign it. Move out." — this is brand voice, NOT product positioning. A military judge will get the rhythm. A non-military judge (e.g., Mohindra, who's MIT Lincoln Lab civilian-side) will read it as flavor without understanding what the product does until they hit the lede.
- Lede: "voice-first, fully offline AI assistant for the Army's bureaucratic tail" — this is the actual proposition and it's good, but it's buried under a 3-line poetic stack and is `data-reveal` invisible at first paint.

**Failure mode**: at 1440×900, the `data-reveal` opacity-0 starts the title invisible. First paint = blank near-black with only the nav and the `CHARLIE MIKE / SCSP HACKATHON · BOSTON 2026 / GENAI.MIL TRACK` strip visible. The user sees a blank page for ~900ms while reveal animations stagger in. That's a third of the 5-second budget gone before they read a single word.

### 2.2 Hero owns three voices — they fight

Three competing semantic systems are stacked in the hero:
1. **Military spec-sheet voice** (mono caps amber/ash): `CHARLIE MIKE / SCSP HACKATHON · BOSTON 2026 / GENAI.MIL TRACK` — implies internal/insider context.
2. **Dramatic poetry voice** (sans + italic serif accent): "Speak it. / Sign it. / *Move out.*" — implies brand tagline.
3. **Engineering brief voice**: the spec-sheet (Pipeline / Models / Footprint / Network) — implies technical depth.

Three voices ≠ three pieces of evidence. They compete. A first-time visitor doesn't know whether Adjutant is a polished consumer brand, a hackathon project, or an enterprise infra tool. Linear.app picks ONE voice (premium product), Anduril picks ONE voice (defense gravitas), Sana picks ONE voice (productivity-AI confidence). Adjutant picks three.

### 2.3 The "View source" CTA goes to `https://github.com`

**Both** secondary CTAs in `index.html` (lines 78 and 616) link to `href="https://github.com"` — the literal homepage of GitHub.com. Click and you land on GitHub's marketing page, not on the Adjutant repo. This is a credibility-killing dead link that a judge WILL click (the GenAI.mil judges value open-source and self-hostability — that's the whole selling pitch).

### 2.4 The marquee competes with the hero CTA

The marquee starts immediately below the hero (`scroll ~900px`), with a 60s-loop horizontal scroll inside its `overflow: hidden` container. On a 1440×900 laptop, when the user is still finishing reading the hero, they see motion at the bottom of their viewport — that motion will visually pull attention DOWN, away from the "Run the live demo" CTA. The CTA is the most important element on the page; the marquee is a credibility flourish. They're fighting for the same eyeballs at the same moment.

Worse: because `marquee__track` uses `padding-left: 22rem` to clear the pinned label, on smaller widths the entire track stays right of viewport-center until the scroll catches up. The visual entry of the first regulation chip is delayed.

### 2.5 The demo console doesn't say "click here to demo"

Inside the console, the only affordance is a state row (a 14-px dot) and "Press start to begin" — the actual mic-grant CTA is the "Start voice ●" button. But it's labeled like a transport control, not a "DEMO" call-out. A judge with 90 seconds will scroll past unless they already understand they're meant to interact.

### 2.6 The pipeline section is informationally dense but visually flat

5 stages × 4 lines of mono-monotone copy each = 20 chunks of similar visual weight. There's no progression diagram, no arrows between stages, no latency timeline. For a defense-tech audience that's used to architecture diagrams, this reads as a wall of bullet points where it should read as a flow.

### 2.7 The wow moment is buried at scroll ~5200px

The "one voice request → three filled PDFs" is the single most differentiated demo moment. It comes after pipeline + forms + (interactive demo). By the time a 90-second judge gets there, they may have already decided. This should be a hero secondary panel or right after the demo console.

### 2.8 Comparison matrix — every cell that isn't "us" is em-dash

Look at the matrix: the GenAI.mil / CamoGPT / Ask Sage / Milnerva / SergeantAI columns have em-dashes for nearly every row. This reads as "we cherry-picked the comparison." A real eval table would have nuance — e.g., GenAI.mil DOES do retrieval-grounded generation, just not from local Army regs. CamoGPT DOES cite sources in its response, just not section-anchored. Em-dashes everywhere look like the author didn't actually use the competitor tools.

The `partial` cells DO try to give nuance ("generic", "one AR", "listens", "tactical") — those are good. But 20+ em-dashes look like a hit-piece, not an evaluation.

### 2.9 No live latency meter

The pipeline copy says "Total ESV→FAO ≤ 1.4 s on M2." But there's no live readout. A defense-tech AI demo NEEDS a wall-clock latency meter visible during the demo — Mohindra and Liu's first question will be "how fast." The tier-status pills show latency for HOT/WARM/COLD via `/health` polling, but there's no end-to-end ESV→FAO clock in the UI.

### 2.10 No proof of provenance / no eval table

For the GenAI.mil track + Lincoln Lab judges, the make-or-break is: do you actually KNOW your answers are right? The page says "architecturally cited" and "architecturally refuses" but shows no eval numbers. No "% of queries that retrieve correct AR section," no "false-refusal rate," no benchmark against GenAI.mil's hosted answer. The claims section asserts these properties but doesn't prove them.

### 2.11 Persona is fictional but presented as real

`PERSONNEL FILE · 2026-04-25` framing + "SGT Maya Chen · 82nd Airborne · Fort Bragg" + a placeholder photo (CSS gradient with "E-5" rank tag) reads as a record. But there's no disclaimer that she's a composite. For a defense audience, that's risky — they will immediately wonder if she's real.

The bigger problem: **a persona section here is a B2B-marketing-deck pattern.** Anduril doesn't have a persona section. Palantir doesn't. Linear doesn't. Sana has user logos. The persona feels like a UX-class artifact, not a defense-tech proof point.

### 2.12 The footer says "Built on a 2025 8 GB MacBook Air M1" but title says "2026"

`PERSONNEL FILE · 2026-04-25` (line 578) and `SCSP Hackathon Boston · GenAI.mil track · April 2026` (line 629) say 2026, but `Built on a 2025 8 GB MacBook Air M1` (line 632) says 2025. Today's date per the system reminder IS 2026-04-25, so the persona-file date is correct, but a M1 MacBook Air was a 2020 model. There is no "2025 8 GB MacBook Air M1." This will get pedantic-attention from any hardware-aware judge.

### 2.13 No favicon, no OG image asset

The `<head>` has no `<link rel="icon">`. Chrome will show a blank page-icon in the tab — this looks unfinished. The OG tags exist but reference no `og:image` (line 9-11 only set title/description/type). Sharing the URL on Slack/Teams/Twitter will produce a no-image card. Defense-tech audiences share links — this matters.

### 2.14 No JS = no demo console

The demo's mic button, tier status, and reply pane are all wired in `app.js`. With JS disabled (or while JS loads on a slow connection), the demo console renders but does nothing — the Start button is unbound, the reply panel is `hidden` and stays hidden. There's no "JS required for demo" notice. Not a deal-breaker for Mohindra's MacBook, but a static-render issue.

The reveal-on-scroll animation also fails open: every `data-reveal` element STARTS at `opacity: 0` via CSS, and only `marketing.js`'s IntersectionObserver flips them to `.in`. **If JS fails to load, the entire page is invisible.** That's catastrophic for a demo URL — the noscript fallback should set `[data-reveal] { opacity: 1; transform: none; }`.

### 2.15 Fonts don't match the brand

`--sans` falls back to `ui-sans-serif, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Inter"...` — this means on a Mac it renders SF Pro, on a non-Mac with no Inter it falls back to Helvetica Neue/Arial. Anduril and Linear ship custom display faces. Sana uses an explicit Söhne (or Söhne-like) display. Adjutant uses system fonts. This will read as "fast" but never as "premium." The italic serif `--serif: "Iowan Old Style", "Charter", "Georgia"...` is a beautiful fallback choice on macOS — Iowan Old Style is system-installed — but on Windows/Chrome it falls back to Georgia, which is far less elegant.

### 2.16 Color contrast — borderline on body copy

`--paper-2: #d2cdc1` on `--ink-2: #0f0f0e` — that's 14.7:1, well past WCAG AA.
`--ash: #8a847a` on `--ink: #0a0a0a` — that's 8.4:1, fine.
`--ash-2: #5a554c` on `--ink: #0a0a0a` — that's 4.5:1, **right at the WCAG AA threshold** for normal text. The footer uses `--ash` for `.foot__meta` (fine) but `--ash-2` shows up in `.matrix td.no` (em-dash cells, where contrast doesn't matter much). The bigger issue: `.sheet dd s { color: var(--ash-2); }` — that's the strikethrough "yes" inside `required: ~~yes~~ none`. Hard to read.
`.hero__scroll` uses `var(--ash)` 0.78rem caps — fine but small.

The amber on dark ink (`--amber: #d4a85a` on `--ink: #0a0a0a`) is 9:1 — fine.

### 2.17 Cursor-dot z-index 60, pointer-events: none

`.cursor-dot { z-index: 60; pointer-events: none; }` — good, it doesn't block clicks. But `.grain { z-index: 50; pointer-events: none; mix-blend-mode: overlay; }` — fine.

### 2.18 Magnetic button + reveal transform conflict

The magnetic effect in `marketing.js` lines 90-101 sets `el.style.transform = translate(...)` directly via inline style. The reveal animation sets `transform: translateY(28px)` → `translateY(0)` via the `.in` class. **Both compete for the same `transform` property.** When a `data-magnetic` button is also `data-reveal` (e.g., the hero CTAs are `data-reveal` on the parent `.hero__ctas`, but the buttons themselves are `data-magnetic` — that's safe — BUT the nav `.nav__cta` is `data-magnetic` and gets a hover-pull transform that competes with `:hover { transform: translateY(-2px) }` from `.btn:hover`).

Specifically: on the nav CTA, hover triggers BOTH `.btn:hover { transform: translateY(-2px); }` AND the magnetic mousemove handler that sets `style.transform = translate(...)`. The inline style wins (specificity), so the `translateY(-2px)` lift is killed during magnetic-pull and reappears on mouseleave. Visually janky.

### 2.19 No `:focus-visible` styles

Search the CSS for `:focus-visible` — there are zero. There are no `:focus` outlines either. Default browser focus rings will appear, but they'll look like a bug on this dark theme. WCAG 2.4.7 requires visible focus. Keyboard nav is broken-looking.

### 2.20 Backdrop-filter Safari support

`.nav { backdrop-filter: blur(14px) saturate(140%); -webkit-backdrop-filter: ... }` — good, the `-webkit-` prefix is included. Safari 9+ supports it. No issue.

### 2.21 Smooth scroll + magnetic + reveal = jank on mid-range hardware

Three concurrent animation systems run during scroll: (a) `data-reveal` IntersectionObserver fires opacity transitions; (b) the hero parallax in `marketing.js` line 142-160 does `transform: translate3d(0, ${y * 0.18}px, 0)` on the hero title every scroll frame; (c) the marquee runs a 60s linear infinite animation. On the demo M1 8GB MBA the page tests on, this is probably fine. On Mohindra's machine (unknown), if there are any other tabs open, this could hitch.

### 2.22 Section that should be merged/reordered

- **Wow moment + Forms supported** should be combined or wow should come first. Forms is the dry "here's what we support"; wow is "look what one query does." Lead with wow, then expand into the form catalog.
- **Pipeline + Architectural claims** should be merged. The 5-stage pipeline is *what runs*; the 3 claims are *why it matters*. Right now claims comes after the matrix — the visitor reads architecture, then forms, then a wow moment, then a competitor hit-list, then the why-it-matters. Bring claims forward to right after pipeline, and put the matrix later as the "kill shot."
- **Persona** should either be cut or moved up to right after the problem. As placed, it competes with the final CTA for closing energy.

## 3. Comparison to Reference Sites

### vs. anduril.com
Anduril leads with cinematic full-bleed video, a 1-line declarative tagline, and a single primary CTA. Adjutant has 3 lines, a spec strip, two CTAs, and a marquee — 5+ visual elements competing in the first viewport. Anduril feels like infrastructure; Adjutant feels like a portfolio piece.

### vs. sana.ai
Sana opens with a clean H1, a sub, ONE pill CTA, and a single product-screenshot below the fold. Adjutant opens with three voice systems fighting. Sana's copy is product-led ("AI for the way you work"); Adjutant's title is brand-led poetry that requires translation.

### vs. linear.app
Linear ships tight typography (custom Inter Display variant), a precise color system, and a hero that resolves in 600ms. Adjutant uses system Inter (fallback chain), and the hero is invisible at first paint due to `data-reveal`. Linear's CTA hierarchy is unmistakable — primary is filled, secondary is text-only outline. Adjutant's primary (paper) and secondary (ghost border) are visually distinct but the GitHub link being broken sabotages the secondary completely.

### vs. palantir.com
Palantir lays out long-form essay-style sections with disciplined eyebrows. The Adjutant eyebrows (`§ 01 · The problem`) borrow this register and execute it well. The closer comparison: Palantir spec-sheets technical claims with PROOF (case-study links, named programs). Adjutant claims without proof.

### vs. milieuskin.com
Milieu's edge is tactility — micro-interactions, magnetic buttons, cursor systems. Adjutant has all three, but they're "off the shelf" implementations. Milieu ties them to the brand (skin / touch / sensorial) so they feel meaningful. Adjutant's cursor dot, magnetic buttons, and grain overlay all read as "premium template" rather than "Adjutant's identity." The grain is good — it ties to printed-paperwork register. The magnetic buttons don't tie to anything tactical. Cut the magnets, double down on the grain.

## 5. Browser-Rendering Issues

- **`overflow-x: hidden` on body** — already set on line 86. The marquee `overflow: hidden` is also set. Magnetic-button transforms can't push elements off-canvas because they're contained in their grid cells. Safe.
- **Cursor dot blocking clicks** — `pointer-events: none` is set (line 113). Safe.
- **Magnetic transform vs `.btn:hover { transform: translateY(-2px) }`** — conflict on the nav CTA. The inline style from magnetic wins; the lift-on-hover is killed during pull. Visually noticeable.
- **Magnetic transform vs reveal `transform: translateY(28px) → 0`** — only conflicts when an element has BOTH `data-magnetic` AND `data-reveal`. Looking at the file: no element has both directly. The nav CTA has `data-magnetic` only. The hero CTAs have `data-magnetic` (lines 74, 78) and their *parent* `.hero__ctas` has `data-reveal`. No conflict because they're on different elements. **Safe.**
- **Safari backdrop-filter** — supported with `-webkit-` prefix.
- **`.grain` `mix-blend-mode: overlay`** — works in all modern browsers but combined with `position: fixed` and `z-index: 50` it sits over everything including the cursor dot UNLESS dot has higher z-index. Dot is z-60 — wins. OK.
- **Hero parallax + reveal** — hero title has `data-reveal` AND gets `style.transform = translate3d(0, ${y * 0.18}px, 0)` from parallax. If the user scrolls before reveal completes, the inline parallax transform overrides the reveal transform and the title may pop in mid-translation. Edge case — only visible if user starts scrolling within 900ms of load.
- **Reveal `will-change: opacity, transform`** on every `[data-reveal]` — there are ~30+ of these. `will-change` on too many elements creates GPU layers and burns memory. Should only apply briefly during the transition.
- **Pipeline `repeat(5, 1fr)` at 1440px** — 5 columns × ~250px each is tight but workable. Each stage card is ~200px with padding, copy fits. At 1100px breakpoint it switches to 2-col, which leaves stage 5 alone in row 3. Awkward orphan.
