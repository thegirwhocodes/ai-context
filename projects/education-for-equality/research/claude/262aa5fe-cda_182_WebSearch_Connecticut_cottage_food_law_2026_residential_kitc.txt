## License Requirements

Connecticut requires a state cottage food license before you can operate. Producers need to apply for a $50 license, get a home inspection, and take a $15 food safety training course.

## Food Safety Training

To operate a cottage food business in Connecticut, you must first complete an approved food safety training course. You must take a ServSafe Food Handler training course before getting your license. There are a number of ways to do this in-person, but you can take the course online for $15 in just a few hours.

## Home Kitchen Inspection

Connecticut requires a home kitchen inspection before granting a cottage food license.

## What You Can Sell

Allowed Products include non-TCS foods (baked goods, jams, candy, etc.) These permitted goods typically include baked goods, jams, jellies, and candies.

## Sales Limits

Under Connecticut's cottage food law, you cannot exceed $50,000 in annual revenue from the sale of your homemade goods.

## Labeling Requirements

Connecticut requires that you label each product with certain information, such as the name and contact information of your cottage food business, ingredients, and the presence of any major food allergen.

## Sales Methods

You may advertise and take orders for your product online, but you must directly deliver such products to consumers and you may only deliver products within the State of Connecticut. Sales and product delivery must be directly from the producer or designee to the consumer, in a person-to-person transaction. Products may not be delivered by mail or by other third party delivery services such as UPS or FedEx, or by mobile food ordering companies.

For the most current and official information, you should contact the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection Food and Standards Division.
