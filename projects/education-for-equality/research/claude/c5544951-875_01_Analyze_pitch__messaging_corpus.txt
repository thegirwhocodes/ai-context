## A. VOICE ATTRIBUTES

**1. Urgent without hysteria — the crisis is real and quantified, not merely alarming**

The writing never dials up emotional panic for its own sake. Urgency arrives via compacted statistics, then pivots immediately to what is being done. Every document contains a version of this move: state the scale, anchor it to a human moment, then redirect to agency.

- "18.3 million out-of-school children — the highest number in the world." — UNICEF_VENTURE_FUND_APPLICATION
- "The question is not whether this can be fixed. The question is how fast we can reach them." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 14), PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE (Slide 4)
- "Nigeria loses $40 billion annually — more than Ghana's entire GDP — because its schools don't teach." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 1 spoken)

**2. Evidence-driven to a rigorous, almost prosecutorial degree**

No claim in any document floats without a citation. The TECHNOVATION_APPLICATION even has a full source-verification table. The framing is always: here is the claim, here is who proved it, here is the study. This gives the voice weight and credibility that is distinguishable from advocacy language.

- "A World Bank RCT demonstrated that AI tutoring like this produces 1.5-2 years of learning gains in just six weeks." — UNICEF_VENTURE_FUND_APPLICATION
- "A Nature 2025 study found Nigerian children solve arithmetic 9x more often when framed as market transactions vs abstract math (48.5% vs 5.4%)." — TECHNOVATION_APPLICATION
- "Benefit-cost ratio of AI tutoring: 161-260 (World Bank) — the highest of any education intervention measured." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 9)

**3. Plainspoken, grounded — conspicuously avoids nonprofit puffery**

The partner knowledge file explicitly lists hype words to avoid ("revolutionary," "solves education," "guaranteed outcomes"). The actual documents honor this. Complex things are described simply. Nothing is dressed up.

- "Sabi is not a concept. It is live at sabi.eduforequality.org" — UNICEF_VENTURE_FUND_APPLICATION
- "We're not building another education app that sits in an app store. We're building something a mother hears about at church, dials after dinner, and hands to her child for five minutes." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 14)
- "Just a call." — PCE_NVA_APPLICATION (appears multiple times as a deliberate brevity flourish)

**4. Personal and grounded in lived experience — founder voice is the moral authority**

The founder's Lagos childhood appears in multiple documents as the bridge between the statistics and the reader's empathy. The personal story is never embellished; it is used sparingly but consistently to earn the right to speak about the crisis.

- "I grew up in Lagos, Nigeria. My mother is a teacher — I attended school on a scholarship tied to her salary. Some days we didn't have food. Every morning on the way to school, I saw children my age hawking groundnuts in traffic so their families could survive. I knew there was no difference between me and them except opportunity." — PCE_NVA_APPLICATION
- "Growing up in Lagos, Nigeria, I attended school on a scholarship tied to my mother's salary as a teacher. Sometimes we didn't have food. Every day on the way to school, I saw children my age hawking groundnuts in traffic or fixing cars so their families could survive. I knew there was no difference between me and them." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 1 spoken)
- "Education gave me a way out. I want to give every child who didn't get one." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 1), PCE_NVA_APPLICATION (nearly identical in both)

**5. Warm toward children, cold-eyed toward the system**

When describing learners, language is patient, specific, and humanizing. When describing the school system, language is exacting and unsparing — "structurally broken," "the system counts them as educated. They are not."

- "You are not unusual. You are the majority." — PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE (Slide 1)
- "The school building isn't failing them. The learning is." — PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE (Slide 3)
- "The system will count you as 'educated.' You will not be." — PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE (Slide 1)

**6. Self-aware about what has and hasn't been proven (epistemic honesty)**

This runs explicitly through the partner knowledge file and implicitly through every application. The voice distinguishes between "designed around evidence-backed principles" and "proven at scale." It is notably unusual for a startup or nonprofit pitch to make this distinction so cleanly.

- "The evidence should be presented carefully. It supports the plausibility and design logic of Sabi, but it does not mean Sabi itself has already been proven at scale." — E4E_PARTNER_KNOWLEDGE
- "Safe claim: Sabi is designed around evidence-backed principles such as Teaching at the Right Level, phonics-based foundational literacy, oral practice, and frequent short learning interactions. Unsafe claim: Sabi is proven to close learning gaps at scale." — E4E_PARTNER_KNOWLEDGE
- "The core product works. Now we need to make its impact provable." — UNICEF_VENTURE_FUND_APPLICATION

**7. Confident, not boastful — the product does the bragging**

The voice states accomplishments (MIT 2nd place, $3K, 472 lessons, working demo) without inflating them. The ask is clear and the credentials are named but not over-celebrated.

- "Education for Equality is woman-led, open source, and already recognized: we won 2nd place at the MIT Africa Business Challenge." — UNICEF_VENTURE_FUND_APPLICATION
- "The core product works. Now we need to make its impact provable." — UNICEF_VENTURE_FUND_APPLICATION
- "Live now." — TECHNOVATION_APPLICATION (one of the most minimalist confidence moves in the corpus)

## B. TONE PATTERNS

**Pattern 1: Storytelling / Anecdote — immersive, second-person, sensory, slow-burn**

The pitch intro and slide deck opener use a very specific structure: put the reader inside a child's body, build concrete specifics (the 5am wake time, the 40-minute walk, 80 students, no textbooks), hold the tension for a full paragraph, then shatter it with the revelation that "you are not unusual, you are the majority." The tone is novelistic and controlled.

- "Imagine you're 10 years old in Lagos, Nigeria. You wake up at 5 AM. You walk 40 minutes to school because your family can't afford the bus fare. When you arrive, your classroom has 80 students and one teacher — who teaches every subject, because there's no one else. Today, she isn't here. She wasn't here yesterday either." — PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE (Slide 1)
- "Next month, your mother pulls you out of school for the term. She needs you to sell groundnuts at the market so the family can eat. When you come back three months later, the class has moved on." — PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE (Slide 1)

**Pattern 2: Data / Evidence — compressed, tabular, source-cited, calibrated**

When the tone shifts to evidence, it uses tables, one-liners, and tight annotations. The data is never dumped raw — it's filtered through a controlling frame ("This is not a story. This is 33 million children.") and then closed with a one-line synthesis. The evidence sections read like a well-edited court brief.

- "This is not a story. This is 33 million children." — PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE (Slide 2)
- "Only 16% of planned learning actually happens." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 3, one-liner)
- "Learning is worth 4x more than attendance. A child who can't read after 10 years of school earns almost the same as one who never went." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 4, one-liner)

**Pattern 3: Ask / Call-to-Action — declarative, short, no qualifications**

The closes strip away all complexity. The ask sections go short, direct, and imperative. Full stops after single clauses. The product is presented as already existing and accessible.

- "The question is not whether this can be fixed. The question is how fast we can reach them." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 14), PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE (Slide 4)
- "Education gave me a way out. I want to give that to every child who didn't get one. That's what Education for Equality is." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 1)
- "Woman-led. Open source. Nigeria-built. Ready now." — UNICEF_VENTURE_FUND_APPLICATION (four-word sentence fragments as a staccato close)
- "Try Sabi live: https://sabi.eduforequality.org" — PCE_NVA_APPLICATION, TECHNOVATION_APPLICATION

**Pattern 4: Product description — procedural, feature-listed, friction-obsessed**

When describing what Sabi actually does, the tone shifts to almost journalistic specificity. Every barrier removed is named explicitly. The design shows the writer thinking about every objection a skeptic might raise.

- "No smartphone needed — works on any $5 phone. No internet needed — runs on basic mobile towers. No cost to the family — toll-free. Persistent memory — knows your name, your level, your mistakes." — SABI_SLIDE_DECK (Slide 7)
- "No smartphone, no internet, no app — just a call." — PCE_NVA_APPLICATION

## D. WORDS/FRAMINGS THEY AVOID

- "revolutionary"
- "solves education"
- "AI teacher replacement"
- "guaranteed outcomes"
- "fully accredited" (unless accreditation actually exists)
- "Cambridge-accredited" (only "Cambridge-aligned" is safe)
- "proven to close learning gaps at scale" (too strong for the current stage)

**Savior framing — absent by design**

There is no language anywhere of E4E or Sabi "rescuing" or "saving" children. The frame is access and delivery, not rescue. The children are depicted as capable and eager learners being failed by a broken system, not as victims requiring charity. Sabi is described as a tutor, not a teacher replacement. The partner knowledge file explicitly flags "AI teacher replacement" as an unsafe claim. In every document, the word used is "tutor" (voice AI tutor, AI voice tutor, patient tutor on the phone).

## E. SENTENCE & STRUCTURE STYLE

**Sentence length: deliberate alternation between compression and expansion**

Story sections use long, immersive sentences that accumulate detail ("Your classroom has 80 students and one teacher — who teaches every subject, because there's no one else. Today, she isn't here. She wasn't here yesterday either."). Then the punch lands short ("You are not unusual. You are the majority."). The rhythm is set up to shock.

Evidence and close sections run short by default. Fragments are used deliberately: "Live now." "Just a call." "Woman-led. Open source. Nigeria-built. Ready now."

**Statistics: always contextualized, never raw**

No statistic appears without either a source citation or a human anchor. The TECHNOVATION_APPLICATION even appends a full source-verification table. The characteristic move is stat + source + consequence: "70% of Nigerian 10-year-olds cannot read a simple sentence (World Bank 2022). Children sit in classrooms for a decade and come out unable to read." — PCE_NVA_APPLICATION

**Second person in storytelling**

The pitch intro and slide deck opener are fully written in second person ("you wake up," "your classroom," "your mother pulls you out"). This is a deliberate choice to close the empathy gap between the reader/judge and the child being described.

**Tabular presentation of data**

Every pitch deck slide that carries evidence uses tables with three columns: intervention/claim | data/figure | source. This is consistent across the MIT deck, the PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE, and the TECHNOVATION application source verification table.

**Rhetorical devices:**
- Anaphora: "No smartphone. No internet. No app." / "Woman-led. Open source. Nigeria-built. Ready now."
- Tricolon: the three-barrier removal phrases appear in multiple documents
- The rhetorical turn ("But we know what works." — PITCH_INTRO_ANECDOTE Slide 4): problem block, then pivot with a one-line opener before the solution block
- Parenthetical specificity: numbers in parentheses after claims serve as real-time citations without breaking sentence flow

**Formatting habits:**
- Bold used for key statistics within tables (always bolded in the table, not inline in prose)
- One-liners at the bottom of each slide as the "carry-out" line — the single sentence judges/funders will remember
- Headers are neutral nouns or brief questions ("The Learning Crisis," "The Infrastructure Collapse," "Kids Want to Learn. The Schools Just Aren't There.")
- Bullet lists in product description sections; tables in evidence sections; prose paragraphs in story and ask sections
