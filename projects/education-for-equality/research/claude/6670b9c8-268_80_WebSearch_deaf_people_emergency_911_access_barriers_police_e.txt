## 911 Emergency Access Barriers

The ability to communicate with authorities by text in an emergency is limited to less than 10 percent of the U.S. Despite laws and regulations to make things equal and accessible, emergency communication, in most cases, is not functionally equivalent to those who can hear.

Getting in touch with 911 can be a struggle for those who are deaf or hard of hearing and people with speech disabilities or who speak only limited English. Members of the Prelingual/Culturally Deaf community have expressed difficulties in accessing and communicating with EMS, police, and fire, with advocates expressing fear about what this has cost in terms of loss of life and physical harm.

## Police Shooting Deaths and Communication Failures

Several documented cases highlight the fatal consequences of communication breakdowns during police encounters:

Oklahoma City Police encountered Magdiel Sanchez, a 35-year-old deaf man holding a metal pipe. When Sanchez failed to comply with orders to drop it, officers fired at him and killed him, despite neighbors yelling that Sanchez was deaf and couldn't hear their commands.

Daniel Harris and Darnel Wicker were shot and killed because they couldn't hear the officers' orders. Seattle Police Officer Ian Birk shot and killed John Williams, a Deaf Native American carver, who was holding a carving knife and a block of wood.

In the past year alone, too many deaf and hard of hearing people have died at the hands of police who denied them effective communication, and the general approach by law enforcement of using lethal force when commands are not followed is dangerous for deaf and hard of hearing people, and even more so for those of color.

## Systemic Barriers in Police Interactions

When individuals who are deaf or hard of hearing encounter law enforcement, they may face challenges in communicating their needs, understanding instructions, or accessing important information. These stories highlight the woeful lack of training about and awareness of Deaf culture and communication within police departments across the nation, illustrating the urgent need for systemic change.

While some police departments receive training on dealing with deaf or disabled people, there doesn't appear to be any national training policy, even after high-profile police killings of deaf people.
