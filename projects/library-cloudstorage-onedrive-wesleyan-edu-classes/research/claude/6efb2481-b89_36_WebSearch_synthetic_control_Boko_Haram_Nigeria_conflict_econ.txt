## Synthetic Control Methodology and Boko Haram Economic Effects

Researchers leveraged the breakout of the Boko Haram insurgency in Northeastern Nigeria in 2009 to study its effects in neighboring areas in Cameroon, Chad, and Niger, finding strong negative effects on regional economic activities, particularly in areas within 200 km from the Boko Haram area.

## Key Economic Impacts

Strong negative effects on regional economic activities—proxied by reductions in nighttime lights—were found particularly among areas within 200 km from the Boko Haram area, with this negative impact concentrated in urban areas, as trade was impacted and economic uncertainty rose.

The spillover economic effects of foreign conflict are large, due to reduced trade and uncertainty among other factors.

## Variation in Resilience Effects

Smaller resilience effects were found in areas with worse geography, less agricultural development, more limited infrastructure, and weaker markets and institutions, suggesting that conflicts may have larger spillover effects in geographically challenging and/or economically poorer regions, as is the case in various regions of Africa.

The research appears to be from a recent 2025 publication analyzing the conflict's spillover effects using synthetic control methods to isolate the economic impact of Boko Haram on neighboring countries.
