The “Nationwide” column isn’t needed for this calculation.

### 2. Decide which company’s duplicate salespeople to eliminate

Compare the annual **cost per account covered**:

| Account type | PureFlow | ClearSpring | Eliminate |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Retailers | $50,000 ÷ 30 = $1,667 | $45,000 ÷ 30 = $1,500 | PureFlow |
| Distributors | $50,000 ÷ 25 = $2,000 | $50,000 ÷ 20 = $2,500 | ClearSpring |
| Wholesalers | $47,000 ÷ 25 = $1,880 | $50,000 ÷ 25 = $2,000 | ClearSpring |

You keep whichever team covers an account more cheaply.

### 3. Calculate the savings

**Retailers**

- 600 overlapping accounts ÷ 30 accounts per PureFlow salesperson
- = 20 redundant PureFlow salespeople
- 20 × $50,000 = **$1,000,000**

**Distributors**

- 40 overlapping accounts ÷ 20 accounts per ClearSpring salesperson
- = 2 redundant ClearSpring salespeople
- 2 × $50,000 = **$100,000**

**Wholesalers**

- 100 overlapping accounts ÷ 25 accounts per ClearSpring salesperson
- = 4 redundant ClearSpring salespeople
- 4 × $50,000 = **$200,000**

Therefore:

\[
\$1{,}000{,}000+\$100{,}000+\$200{,}000
=\boxed{\$1.3\text{ million per year}}
\]

The key insight is: **only eliminate duplicate coverage, and eliminate the more expensive coverage for each customer type.**

## Naomi · 2026-07-20T23:53:01.244Z

whats the mindset behind synergies questions

## Codex · 2026-07-20T23:53:18.395Z · final_answer

The core mindset is:

> **What becomes unnecessary—or newly possible—only because the two companies combine?**

Mathematically:

\[
\text{Synergy} = \text{Before-merger result} - \text{After-merger result}
\]

For costs, that means **money spent before but no longer needed afterward**.

### For cost-synergy questions

Use this sequence:

1. **Find the overlap**
   What are both companies currently doing separately—sales coverage, offices, factories, software, management, etc.?

2. **Identify what can be removed**
   If both companies cover the same customer, perhaps only one team is needed after the merger.

3. **Choose what to keep**
   Compare productivity and cost. Keep the cheaper or more efficient resource.

4. **Convert the overlap into resources**
   In your problem:

   \[
   \text{Redundant salespeople}
   =
   \frac{\text{Overlapping accounts}}{\text{Accounts covered per salesperson}}
   \]

5. **Convert resources into dollars**

   \[
   \text{Savings}
   =
   \text{Redundant salespeople}
   \times
   \text{Cost per salesperson}
   \]

6. **Add all categories together**

### The before-and-after picture

Before the merger:

- PureFlow pays someone to cover an account.
- ClearSpring pays someone else to cover that same account.

After the merger:

- The combined company needs only one person covering it.
- The cost of the unnecessary coverage becomes the synergy.

### Two major synergy types

- **Cost synergies:** spending less—eliminating duplicate employees, offices, systems, advertising, or factories.
- **Revenue synergies:** earning more—cross-selling products, reaching new customers, or charging more. Usually you ultimately calculate the **additional profit**, not merely additional revenue.

### Best mental shortcut

Whenever you see “synergies,” ask:

> **What can the combined company do more cheaply or profitably than the two separate companies—and how much is that difference worth?**

The most common traps are double-counting overlap, cutting resources that are still needed, and using the wrong team’s productivity or salary when calculating the savings.

Yes—**the merged company has more accounts than PureFlow alone**, but it also inherits **ClearSpring’s salespeople**.

The comparison is not:

> PureFlow before → bigger company after

It is:

> PureFlow + ClearSpring before → combined company after

For example:

- PureFlow covers accounts A, B, and C.
- ClearSpring covers accounts C and D.
- Before the merger, there are **5 instances of account coverage**: A, B, C, C, D.
- After the merger, there are only **4 unique accounts**: A, B, C, D.

Account D is added to PureFlow’s original customer base, but the ClearSpring team already handled D. The only unnecessary coverage is the second person/team covering C.

So:

\[
\text{Combined accounts needed}
=
\text{PureFlow accounts}
+
\text{ClearSpring accounts}
-
\text{overlapping accounts}
\]

The merger doesn’t automatically add brand-new nationwide customers. Winning currently uncovered accounts would be a **revenue synergy**, but this question asks only about **cost savings from eliminating duplicate sales coverage**.
