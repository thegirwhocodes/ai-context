I've completed a web search on Nigeria's primary school physical infrastructure conditions. Here are the key findings from recent UBEC reports and assessments:

## Physical Condition of Classrooms

Eighteen percent of primary classrooms are in "bad" condition, though another source indicates a more severe situation, with 40% of classrooms in bad condition in the basic education sector.

## Infrastructure Deficits

Over 50% of public schools in Nigeria lack adequate classrooms, desks, and basic amenities such as water and sanitation facilities. The infrastructure gaps are extensive: at primary school level, the nation needs 907,769 new classrooms and also requires 200,085 classrooms at the Junior Secondary School level, making a total of 1,107,854 classrooms needed at the basic education level.

## Overcrowding and Specific Facility Issues

In urban centres, overcrowded classrooms are the norm, with student-to-classroom ratios sometimes exceeding 100:1. Additionally, 62% of schools lack access to potable water; 78% of schools do not have functional toilets.

## Safety Standards

Just 14 percent of schools meet basic safety requirements such as perimeter fencing and lockable gates.

## General Infrastructure Challenges

Nigeria's basic education sector continues to face serious challenges, including dilapidated infrastructure, overcrowded classrooms, a shortage of qualified teachers, and high dropout rates.
