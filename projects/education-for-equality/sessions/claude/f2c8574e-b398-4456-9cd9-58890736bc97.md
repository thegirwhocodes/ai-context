## Category A — Real Nigerian Pidgin (words/phrases, not coinages)

**Askam** — *Feel:* "ask it." *Why:* names the core AI-tutor behavior (a child asks, it answers). *Sample:* "Anything you no sabi, askam." *Risk:* coinage = more ownable; check no existing "Askam/Ask'em" app.

## Honest recommendation

**Keep Sabi as the master name of the voice channel — it is still the strongest single word you have, and nothing here clearly beats it.** Here's the reasoning, not just the verdict:

1. **Sabi is the only candidate that is simultaneously pan-Nigerian (Pidgin, not one ethnic language), one clean syllable-pair, self-describing ("know"), and frictionless in a phone greeting.** Every Yoruba option (Ore, Egbon, Imole, Imo, Eko, Gbo) is beautiful but **narrower** — it privileges Yoruba speakers and adds tone/consonant hazards over a noisy 2G line for non-Yoruba kids. For a product whose whole thesis is *universal reach over feature phones*, the pan-ethnic Pidgin root is a strategic asset, not just a vibe.

2. **The real weakness of Sabi is trademark, not branding** — it's a common word, so owning it broadly is the hard part. That's an argument for a **distinctive sub-form or wordmark** (stylized "Sabi™", or a coinage like **Sabio/Askam** held defensively), **not** for abandoning a name that already tests well with kids.

3. **Where I'd genuinely push you to test alternatives:** the only two that add something Sabi lacks are **Ore/Padi** (relationship warmth — "friend" vs "know") and **Egbon** (the literal older-sibling promise). If after live phone tests you find children respond more to *warmth* than to *function*, the cleanest answer is a **hybrid**: keep **Sabi** as the name, and let the AI introduce itself relationally — *"Hello, my name is Sabi. I'm your padi / your learning friend."* That captures both axes without a rename or a second trademark fight.

So: **keep Sabi; don't rename. Use "friend/older-sibling" (padi / ore / egbon) as the AI's self-description in the greeting, not as a competing brand. Reserve a coinage (Askam or Sabio) only as a defensive/secondary mark.** Before committing spend on any of these, run a one-question field test — *call three kids, have the AI say each name, ask "wetin you go call am?"* — and put **Padi, Oya, Imole, and Eko through a formal Nigerian IPO + deity/state-name check** (Oya = òrìṣà, Imo = state, Eko ≈ EKOEXCEL) before they go anywhere near a poster.

*Not legal advice — every "Risk" line above is a flag for a real trademark/cultural check, not a clearance.*
