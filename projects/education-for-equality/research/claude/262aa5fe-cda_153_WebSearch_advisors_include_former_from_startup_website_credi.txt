## Website Credibility and Name-Dropping Concerns

A name-brand advisor who never delivers is one of the most common — and expensive — mistakes founders make. Many founders make the mistake of treating advisors as ornaments: impressive names that add credibility to a pitch deck but contribute little in practice.

The practice of using advisor names for credibility without substance is widespread. Celebrity or big name advisors might look cool on a pitch deck, but you're probably not getting enough value from them. Additionally, simply name dropping isn't going to cut it.

## Red Flags and Ethical Concerns

Avoid the allure of high-profile names who may not be accessible or committed enough to contribute meaningfully. There is a whole market of people who collect advisory chairs and don't do much work but retain stock.

There's also a concerning example of fraud in the search results: Asenqua resurfaced in 2015 with a new website where several of the executives listed turned out to be fictional, with one managing director's LinkedIn profile copied almost word-for-word from a deceased media financier.

## Best Practices for Legitimate Advisory Boards

Before formalizing any advisor relationship, check references and talk to founders the prospective advisor has previously worked with. When engaging with someone you don't know well, build up the relationship first, meet with them a number of times, ask them for help before they become official advisors.
