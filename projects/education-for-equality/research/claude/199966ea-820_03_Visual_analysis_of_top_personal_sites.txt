## CROSS-SITE SYNTHESIS

### Universal patterns the best sites share (2024–2026)

1. **One-line tagline immediately after the name** — never a paragraph. Patrick Collison has no tagline at all. Sahil has three nouns (Founder/Writer/Painter). Brittany has *"I build accessible, pixel-perfect experiences for the web."* Geoffrey has *"malleable software"* in twelve words. Maggie's is a single sentence. The tagline must fit on one breath.

2. **Dated, chronological work archive as the centerpiece** — not a "case studies" gallery. Steve Krouse, Sahil, Levels, Geoffrey, Maggie, Rauch — all of them present writing and projects as dated lists, newest first. The date itself is a credibility signal (consistency over years).

3. **Restrained color** — Brittany's navy + mint is the most "colorful" Tier 2 example. Most are monochrome with one accent. Tobias uses red minimally. Maggie's warm cream/sage is the warmest, and even that is muted. No vibrant brand palettes.

4. **System fonts or one tasteful custom** — Inter (Lovin, Lee, Brittany), Geist (Rauch), Georgia/serif (Geoffrey, Maggie, Gwern), or just system stacks (Collison, Krouse, Levels). Never multiple custom fonts. The serif vs sans choice is the only real fork.

5. **Dark/light toggle** is now table stakes for tech founders.

6. **Single column or fixed sidebar + content column** — those are the only two layouts. Lovin and Brittany do sidebars; everyone else does single column. No carousels, no full-bleed hero videos (Friedman's placeholder excepted).

7. **Self-deprecation as credibility signal** — "mediocre developer" (Maggie), "Failure to Build a Billion-Dollar Company" (Sahil), "over-engineered playground" (Lovin), "I proposed to my girlfriend in Quordle" (Krouse). The pattern: lead with humility, let the body show competence.

8. **Inline links over logo walls** — none of these sites have a "logos of companies I worked at" row. Affiliations appear as hyperlinked words inside prose ("work at Cursor," "founder of Stripe").

### What's dated vs current in 2026

**Dated:**
- Logo-wall hero sections
- "Hero CTA buttons" (Get In Touch / View My Work)
- Carousel testimonials
- Bento-grid feature blocks (overdone in SaaS but stale for personal sites)
- Big animated headlines that say "Hi I'm X, a Y based in Z"
- Three-column "Services" sections
- Skill-bar percentages
- Generic stock photography
- Long forms / "Contact me" sections

**Current in 2026:**
- One-line bio + dated archive
- Editorial serif typography ("New Editorial" trend per Typewolf and Figma's 2026 reports)
- Expressive minimalism — minimalism + one warm human element
- Type-driven hierarchy, not color
- View transitions and subtle scroll-driven effects (not parallax)
- Dark/light + ambient theming
- Real-time integrations (last-listened track, current location, status dot) used sparingly
- Open-source the site (link the GitHub repo)
- Honest "Now" pages, "Antilibrary" pages, "Failure" essays

### Founder voice vs engineer voice

| | Founder voice (Tier 1 + Tobias) | Engineer voice (Tier 2) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tagline** | Identity nouns ("Founder, Writer, Painter") or absent | Action verbs ("I build…") |
| **Sections** | Essays, recognition, ventures | Projects, experience, writing |
| **Tone** | Philosophical, decisive, sometimes wry | Friendly, accessible, technical |
| **Photo** | Often absent (Collison, Krouse, Levels) or stylized (Tobias) | Frequently present (Geoffrey, Lovin, Comeau) |
| **Self-description** | Achievements stated, then critiqued (Sahil's failure essay) | "Currently exploring X at Y" |
| **Failure narrative** | Center stage (Sahil) | Mostly absent |
| **Length** | Bare ("Launching Soon") or sprawling (Gwern) | Medium-length, balanced |

For Naomi specifically: she sits at the intersection. She's a *founder*, but the audience writing recommendations and writing grants needs the *engineer voice* warmth — credibility plus a feeling of "I can talk to this person." The right blend is closest to **Geoffrey Litt + Brittany Chiang + Maggie Appleton**: serif-driven editorial polish, dated archive, mission statement front and center, warm but not casual, one good headshot.

### Five design moves to steal for Naomi's site

1. **The Geoffrey Litt opener.** Circular headshot left, name as H1, then one sentence that names the *thing she works on* — not her job. Geoffrey: *"works on malleable software."* For Naomi: something like *"builds voice AI tutors for Nigerian children who don't have schools."* No "I'm a senior at Wesleyan who…" preamble — the audience can infer.

2. **Brittany Chiang's structural rhythm without the dark theme.** Two-column sticky-sidebar layout *would* feel too engineering-heavy for grant funders. But the sectioning — About → Recognition (her version of Experience) → Sabi & other ventures (her Projects) → Writing → Footer — is exactly the right order. Keep the sidebar nav anchor highlighting on scroll, but use the warm cream/sage editorial palette of Maggie Appleton.

3. **Editorial serif + Inter combo.** Use a refined serif for the name and section H2s (something like Fraunces, Tiempos, GT Sectra, or Editorial New) and Inter for body/nav. This signals "thoughtful publication" rather than "developer portfolio" — which is what grant funders, journalists, and professors respond to. Avoid Geist (too Vercel/dev-coded).

4. **The "named recognitions" treatment.** Don't do a logo wall. Do what Geoffrey Litt does with his archive: a clean dated list — *"2026 · UNICEF AI Ventures (inaugural cohort)"* / *"2025 · MIT Africa Business Challenge, 2nd place ($3K)"* / *"2025 · Wesleyan New Venture Competition, Grand Prize"* / *"2024 · Morgan Hacks, 1st place"*. Each as a single line. Italic for the award, regular for the year, no descriptions unless something needs context. The restraint is the credibility.

5. **The Sabi pull-quote.** One section near the top with a real quote from a child user (or a teacher, or a partner), styled as a literary blockquote with attribution — *Maggie Appleton's* essay-card style applied to one human testimonial. This is the move that converts "credible founder" into "credible founder of a mission-driven company." Grant funders need exactly this evidence.

### What to avoid

- **No three-column "Services / About / Contact" landing.** Naomi isn't selling consulting.
- **No "Built with" tech-stack badges** in the footer (Brittany does it lightly — for an engineering audience. For Naomi's audience, that signals "developer" when the goal is "founder").
- **No dark navy.** Brittany's palette is iconic but it's *the* dev portfolio template; her site has been cloned thousands of times. Cream/warm-white + a single editorial accent (a deep terracotta, a deep ink-blue, or a muted forest green) reads more "mission-driven founder" than navy.
- **No video hero.** Friedman's placeholder uses one but it's a coming-soon page. For a real portfolio it always feels gimmicky.
- **No cursor spotlight effect.** Brittany's. Beautiful, but flag-it-as-dev-portfolio.
- **No giant "Hi, I'm Naomi 👋"** opener. That's the Steve Krouse move for engineers. For a founder targeting funders, it under-positions her.
- **No long bio paragraph.** Patrick Collison has zero bio on home. Krouse has three lines. The right move is two sentences total in the hero — one for what she does, one for the institutional anchor (Wesleyan + UNICEF cohort).
- **No "Currently exploring…"** trope. That's the engineer-voice tic. Founders state what they're building, not what they're "exploring."
- **No carousel of project images.** Sabi gets one section with: tagline, one screenshot or audio waveform, one impact metric, one pull quote, one link.
