## Overview

A missed call is a deliberate mobile telephony practice in which the caller dials a number but terminates the connection after one or two rings, before the recipient can answer, thereby generating a notification of an incoming but unanswered call on the recipient's device without charging the caller for airtime.

## Zero-Cost Model

The missed call (also known as "flashcall" in Pakistan, "beep" in Africa, and other regional names) costs nothing. As the call is not actually completed and connected, it does not carry a cost to the caller, hence they can conserve their remaining prepaid credit.

## Prevalence in Africa

Studies estimate between 20 to more than 30 percent of the calls made in Africa are just split-second flashes. In Sub-Saharan Africa, estimates indicate 20-30% of all calls were intentional misses by the mid-2000s, enabling recipients to callback without mutual expense.

## Education and Social Impact

Missed call campaigns can be applied to generate awareness campaigns against epidemics and natural disasters, where users can leave a missed call to indicate interest if they want further information. For instance, Kirusa Konnect facilitated the Ebola Education Campaign to educate Ghanaians on the Ebola epidemic.

## Callback Service Model

It needs a smaller infrastructure set-up to receive missed calls and offers huge capacity to receive user requests, with a return call being made when capacity for calling back is available.

The model represents an innovative adaptation to high airtime costs in developing regions, enabling cost-free user engagement for educational, health, and communication purposes.
