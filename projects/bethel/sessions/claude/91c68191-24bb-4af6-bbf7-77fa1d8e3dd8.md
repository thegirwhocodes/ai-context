## The big picture

The AI-email world splits into **two archetypes**, and Sage sits deliberately between them:

1. **Growth-machine SaaS** (Superhuman, Fyxer) — won on *distribution + a hard-to-copy data/onboarding moat*. Both are closed, VC-funded, server-side. Superhuman's endgame was getting **acqui-hired by Grammarly**.
2. **Privacy-respecting / draft-only tools** (Cora, SaneBox, Inbox Zero) — constrain the AI, keep a human in the send-loop, treat your data carefully. This is Sage's family.

**The single hardest problem for all of them is distribution.** Not the AI. Not the product. Getting in front of users.

## What actually repeats (success factors)

1. **A moat that isn't the LLM** — proprietary data (Fyxer), an onboarding ritual (Superhuman), per-user personalization (SaneBox), or community/open-source (Inbox Zero). "Wrap GPT/Claude" is not defensible; prompts are portable and anyone can copy them.
2. **A compounding distribution channel** — open source is the one a solo builder can actually run.
3. **A vertical beachhead** — Fyxer didn't sell "email AI to everyone," it sold to recruiters and realtors first.
4. **Personalization as the anti-incumbent weapon** — the thing Google can't ship globally.

## What kills them (failure modes)

1. **Platform dependence on Gmail** — Google can ship your feature for free, overnight (SaneBox's near-death).
2. **Absorption** — the default exit is acqui-hire, even for the category leader. Independence is the exception.
3. **Can't monetize / VC incentives** — funded email tools get pushed toward an exit rather than durable independence.

## What this means for Sage (the actionable part)

**1. Your distribution play is open source.** It's the only channel that compounds for a solo/local-first builder, and Inbox Zero proved it in *this exact category*. Caveat the research insisted on: **GitHub stars ≠ users ≠ revenue** (Nginx had 12k stars at a $670M acquisition; there's a whole "6M fake stars" critique). Open source gets you *discovered* — it doesn't get you *adopted*. ([source](https://www.getinboxzero.com/blog/post/why-build-an-open-source-saas))

**2. Lead with ownership-as-*longevity*, not privacy.** This is the sharpest finding for you. The Ink & Switch "local-first software" paper (the one that coined the term) nails Sage's strongest argument: *"If the service shuts down… without the servers there is normally no way for you to continue running your own copy of that software."* That's **exactly** what killed the graveyard apps — and exactly what "you own your agent" defeats. Your pitch should be **"your agent keeps working even if we disappear,"** which is concrete and provable. ([Ink & Switch](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/))

**3. Do *not* sell on privacy as the primary hook.** The hard data: a peer-reviewed study (n=6,083) found **86% of Google users won't pay anything for added privacy** — even though 76% say they'd prefer their data not be collected. The classic privacy paradox. Privacy/local-first should be the *trust reinforcement* under benefits people actually pay for (control, your-voice drafts, no lock-in), not the price tag itself. ([Public Choice study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11127-019-00642-2)) *(This is the weakest single data point — 2019, Google-specific, hypothetical WTP — but it's directionally consistent across studies.)*

**4. Draft-only is validated — stop treating it as a constraint.** Cora ships it commercially at $15–20/mo. Frame Sage's code-level send-gate as a *feature* ("autonomous on judgment, gated on consequence"), which you already say in the README — the market backs you up.

**5. Build a personalization/data moat, or you're a wrapper.** Fyxer's lesson applied to you: Sage's defensibility is the **accumulated model of your voice + your guardrails + your triage decisions** over time. That's the thing a Gmail feature or a fresh competitor can't replicate. Make that compounding and explicit.

**6. The one real strategic fork:** *pure local-first vs. an optional hosted tier.* Inbox Zero monetizes a hosted convenience tier on top of the open repo — easiest revenue + fuels the open-source flywheel. But a server **partially undercuts** the "no SaaS server, you own it" purity that is Sage's sharpest edge. The research couldn't resolve this for you — but it flagged it as **a decision to make on purpose, not by drift.** My read: keep the *core* agent pure local-first (that's the wedge), and if you ever want revenue, a hosted *sync/backup* convenience for multi-device is the least-compromising place to charge.

**Pricing reference points** (what's actually working in-market): Cora $15–20/mo, Inbox Zero $18–42/user/mo. *(Superhuman's exact pricing got refuted in verification — don't trust the "$30/$40" numbers floating around.)*
