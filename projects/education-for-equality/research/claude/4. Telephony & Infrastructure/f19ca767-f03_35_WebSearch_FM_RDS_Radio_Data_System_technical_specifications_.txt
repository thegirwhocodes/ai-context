## Technical Specifications

RDS is a communications protocol standard for embedding small amounts of digital information in conventional FM radio broadcasts. The standard began as a project of the European Broadcasting Union (EBU), but has since become an international standard of the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC).

### Subcarrier and Data Rate

Both versions carry data at 1,187.5 bits per second (about 1.2 kbit/s) on a 57 kHz subcarrier, so there are exactly 48 cycles of subcarrier during every data bit. The RDS system is Dual Side Band modulated onto the 57 kHz subcarrier of a commercial FM transmitter. The RDS mode transmitted is actually one PSK signal identically duplicated and mirrored across the 57 kHz carrier for robustness and redundancy. This individual PSK signal is about 2.2 kHz wide, and the combined DSB modulation of RDS is about 4 kHz.

## Text Transmission Capacity

RadioText allows text transmissions of up to 64 characters per message. RDS can carry 1187.5 bits/s. An error correction code is used in the transmission of the data. This brings the netto data rate down to 730 bit/s, allowing for approximately 100 characters per second of usable data.

## RDS2 Enhancement

The update uses three additional subcarriers, to allow for a higher bitrate (maximum 4750 bits per second), and thus transmission of additional data such as images.
