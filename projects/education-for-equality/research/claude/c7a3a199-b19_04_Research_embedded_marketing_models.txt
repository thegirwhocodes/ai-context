## Summary of Key Findings

### 1. Embedded Marketing / Branded Content Integration

Product placement is a $23 billion industry. The most relevant models for Sabi are: **sponsored content** (brand pays for content that aligns with its values), **branded educational content** (brand creates genuinely useful education featuring its products -- e.g., Safaricom's M-PESA financial literacy kit teaches money management using M-PESA, Lifebuoy's EUR 30M handwashing program embeds their soap brand into school curricula in 5+ countries), and **edutainment integration** (Sesame Street model where corporate sponsors like PNC Bank, Sanlam, LEGO Foundation fund specific educational segments).

### 2. Telco-Sponsored Education Programs

The biggest deal found: **Airtel-UNICEF $57M partnership** across 13 African countries (zero-rated education platforms, school connectivity, teacher training). **Viamo's 3-2-1 service** is the closest model to Sabi -- operating in 16 countries, completely free IVR education via toll-free short codes, with MNOs absorbing airtime costs worth $12 billion in cumulative free minutes. Airtel alone contributed the equivalent of 25 years of free airtime ($3.2M retail value). **Safaricom + Eneza (Shupavu291)** uses carrier billing at KSh 10/week with a tiered revenue share. **Vodacom Siyakha** reached 7.5M users with zero-rated education content in South Africa.

### 3. Zero-Rating Commercial Structure

Three models: (a) Telco absorbs cost as CSR/retention (Wikipedia Zero -- no money changed hands, operators volunteered; Vodacom e-School), (b) Content provider pays telco for sponsored data (Zando paying Vodacom, Facebook Free Basics), (c) Advertising-supported (MTN Ads zero-rated campaigns -- 100M+ impressions/month, 4.4% CTR). Nigerian telcos are currently reluctant due to post-devaluation financial pressure, but NCC is pushing them to zero-rate education -- it is a recommendation, not yet a binding regulation.

### 4. Revenue Share Percentages

**Nigeria**: Standard VAS split is 60% MNO / 40% content provider (NCC-negotiated). MTN Nigeria generated NGN 70B+ in VAS revenue in 2021. **Kenya (Safaricom)**: Published PRS rate card shows IVR revenue shares from 75% content provider / 25% Safaricom at low price points (KSh 5-25/min) down to 50/50 at KSh 70/min. At the price points relevant to education, content providers keep MORE of the revenue.

### 5. Why Telcos Care

Viamo has proven that when education services mature in a market, 5-8% of the operator's subscriber base uses them regularly. Benefits: reduced churn (increasing retention by 5% = 25-95% profit increase per Bain), higher ARPU from engagement, regulatory goodwill (NCC pushing for education zero-rating), competitive differentiation, PR value. Acquiring a new telecom subscriber costs 5-7x retaining an existing one. Average global telco churn is 26%; in African prepaid markets it ranges 20-70%.

### 6. Implications for Sabi

The optimal revenue model combines: (a) donor/grant funding, (b) telco CSR partnership where MNO absorbs call costs in exchange for branding (Viamo model), and (c) embedded marketing where brands pay for natural product mentions in lesson scripts ("If Amina has 3 packets of Indomie noodles..."). Pre-lesson sponsor tags (5-10 seconds), branded math problems, and branded modules ("The MTN Counting Module") are all viable based on industry precedent. Voice is low bandwidth (cheaper to zero-rate than data), feature phone reach is unique, and NCC regulatory tailwind is real.
