# AI-Driven Sign Language Generation with Non-Manual Markers: Technical Overview

## System Architecture

The prototype employs a modular three-stage pipeline converting English text to photorealistic ASL video:

**Module 1: Text-to-ASL Representation**
- Uses GPT-4o with few-shot prompting (1,494 in-context examples from ASLLRP dataset)
- Dual tasks: (1) English-to-gloss translation, (2) linguistic feature detection
- Detects yes/no questions, wh-questions, conditionals, and negation
- Vocabulary constrained to 3,915 word-gloss pairs
- Multi-prompting approach to handle token limits

**Module 2: Gloss-to-Skeletal Pose**
- Motion Matching technique selecting from dictionary of 12,681 pose sequences
- Optimization minimizes "economy of motion" using weighted Euclidean distance across body parts
- Expression blending augments facial features post-stitching
- Eyebrow adjustments for question types and negation markers
- Linear blending with 20-frame transitions between signs

**Module 3: Pose-to-Video Frames**
- U-Net architecture with Imagen-style encoder/decoder
- Conditioned on signer identity for visual consistency
- Enhanced rasterization: convex polygons per body part, color-coded fingers, orientation-based hand shading
- Three-loss training: frame-level L1, hand-specific L1, and LPIPS perceptual loss
- Automated quality filtering using optical flow and pose tracking validation

## Non-Manual Marker Generation

The system generates non-manual cues through:
- **Eyebrow movements**: Raised for yes/no questions and conditionals; furrowed for negation
- **Zero-shot classification** of linguistic features from English text
- Post-hoc expression blending rather than end-to-end integration
- Holistic facial adjustment without directional gaze variation

## Evaluation Metrics and Performance

**Text-to-Gloss Translation:**
- BLEU-4: 0.276 (80% of 1,843 sentence pairs with preprocessing)
- ROUGE-L: 0.664
- METEOR: 0.560
- Ablation studies examined preprocessing, example quantity, vocabulary constraints, and grammar rules

**Linguistic Feature Detection:**
- Yes/no questions: precision 0.98, recall 0.93
- Wh-questions: precision 0.93, recall 0.98
- Negation: precision 0.79, recall 1.00 (false positives on negative sentiment)
- Conditionals: precision 0.95, recall 0.05 (severe underfitting)

**Video Generation (Module 3):**
- L1: 2.83E-05 (proposed vs. 31.71E-05 baseline)
- PSNR: 23.346 dB
- SSIM: 0.864
- FVD (Fréchet Video Distance): 173.78

## Datasets

**ASLLRP (Module 1):**
- 2,119 English-gloss pairs (1,843 after cleaning)
- Continuous and isolated videos
- Detailed manual/non-manual annotations
- Trimmed to isolated utterances

**How2Sign (Module 3):**
- 35K high-resolution clips
- 16K+ word token vocabulary
- 60K training frames, 15K test frames
- 13 distinct signer identities

## User Study with DHH Participants

**Demographics:** 30 Deaf/Hard-of-Hearing signers, primarily ASL-fluent

**Section 1 (Visual/Motion Quality):**
- Compared AI (Annotations), Video Retargeting, and raw human videos
- Evaluated understandability, visual quality, naturalness on 5-point scales
- 3 videos per type per participant

**Section 2 (Translation Quality):**
- Compared AI (Annotations), AI w/o Expr, AI (Full), Raw Video
- 21 videos across 6 sentence types (wh-questions, yes/no questions, statements, conditionals, negation, fingerspelling)
- Participants provided English translations then rated semantic similarity, grammar quality, facial expression accuracy
- Repeated evaluation to assess expression blending impact

**Key Findings:**
- Participants understood content frequently but rated signing quality lower than human signers
- Expression blending improved comprehension for questions
- Interest in potential use cases: doctor's offices, video conversations, in-person interactions

## Limitations and Challenges

**Acknowledged Constraints:**
- Temporal consistency issues in video generation
- Limited non-manual marker coverage (primarily eyebrows)
- "Economy of motion" selection ignores semantic sign variation
- Conditional statement detection failure (5% recall)
- Negative sentiment confusion with negation markers
- Small dataset size (1,843 sentence pairs)
- Context-free sentence processing only
- No handling of sign classifiers or complex linguistic structures
- Anthropomorphic uncanny valley effects despite photorealism

**Technical Gaps:**
- Lacks directional gaze and head tilt variation
- Single neutral pose for fingerspelling non-dominant hand
- Manual data cleaning and ground truth correction required
- Vocabulary constraints limit spontaneous expression

## Key Insights

The research demonstrates that LLM-based few-shot prompting substantially outperforms prior neural machine translation approaches (0.276 vs. 0.002-0.124 BLEU-4), though "significant room for improvement remains." The modular architecture enables component replacement as technology advances. Critical findings emphasize that "larger, high-quality, comprehensive ASL datasets" are essential, along with authentic DHH community collaboration throughout development rather than post-hoc evaluation.

Human-centered findings revealed tension between technical advancement and user acceptance: participants appreciated improved visual fidelity but maintained skepticism about signing naturalness compared to human signers, suggesting "quality and overcoming technical limitations could increase acceptance among DHH individuals."
