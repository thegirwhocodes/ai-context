Nigeria loses about $40 billion in potential revenue due to the less educated population, according to a report by SB Morgan (SBM) Intelligence. This $40 billion figure represents lost revenue productivity due to less educated population, which is larger than the combined GDP of Mali, Chad and Burkina Faso.

SBM, a research organisation, said the rate of regular school attendance and early childhood education remains poor in the country. The report cited data from the United Nations Children's Fund revealing that about 20 million Nigerian children between the ages of 5 and 14 are classified as out-of-school.

On the economic impact of out-of-school children, SBM Intelligence referenced data showing that adults who didn't receive foundational education could not contribute to the country's productivity in the long term, with Nigeria losing roughly 7.83% of its GDP in lost revenue productivity due to less educated population.
