## Research Evidence on Mother Tongue-Based Literacy Instruction

Field experiments in South Africa demonstrate that improving teaching of mother tongue literacy boosted both mother tongue and English reading skills, while improving teaching of English literacy negatively impacted mother tongue literacy for struggling students.

The theoretical and empirical case for mother tongue instruction is compelling—children learn better when they can associate new concepts with familiar words from their home language, and numerous studies have shown that children who start learning in their mother tongue tend to perform better academically with deeper understanding of subject matter and superior comprehension skills.

## African Context Challenges

The situation in African countries presents unique challenges due to their high degree of linguistic diversity and larger linguistic differences between the mother tongue and second language, making the transition from mother tongue to a linguistically very different second language daunting for students.

Globally, approximately half of the children attending school are taught in languages they do not know, and when learners cannot access education in a language they already understand, it can make it harder for them to learn.

## Implementation Approaches

South Africa is adopting an approach where subjects are initially taught in the mother tongue for the first three years with a subsequent shift to English in Grade 4.

The main activities necessary for a successful language of instruction program include the presence of high-quality teaching and learning materials in the mother tongue, improved curriculum with bilingual materials in classrooms, and outcomes that include perceived improvements in first and second language reading skills.
