## What Spotlight is

| RAM | Process |
|---|---|
| 500 MB | A WebKit/Safari web content process |
| 190 MB | Spotlight (`mds_stores`) |

| 73 MB | The IDE's Electron core |

None of these is a runaway. They're all **normal sizes**. The problem is the machine, which brings us to the real diagnosis:

## The actual problem: you have an **8GB MacBook Air (M1)** — and you're swapping **8GB to disk**
```
Total RAM:  8 GB
Swap used:  7,999 MB  ← ~8 GB spilled onto your SSD because RAM ran out
```
Your workload (dev + video editing + music production + browser + email) wants **way** more than 8GB, so macOS is compressing memory and dumping ~8GB onto the SSD as "swap." That swapping **is** the slowness — and it also wears your SSD over time. You're not doing anything wrong; **8GB is just too small for what you do.**

## "Should I just increase the RAM?" — ⚠️ You can't
This is the hard truth about Apple Silicon: **M1 RAM is unified memory soldered onto the chip. It is physically not upgradeable.** There's no RAM slot. No shop can add memory to an M1 Air. The only way to get more RAM is a **different/new Mac.**

## "Should I get an SSD?"
An SSD **does not add RAM** and won't fix the slowdowns/swapping. What an external SSD *is* good for: **offloading your video and music project files / media libraries** so your internal drive isn't crammed (you've got only ~6GB free internally — that's also tight). So: an SSD helps your **storage** problem, not your **RAM** problem.

## What actually fixes it

## Claude · 2026-06-03T18:01:28.184Z

Great questions — let me clear up each one, including a misconception in the last one.

## What is swapping?
Your **RAM** is the fast workspace where apps keep what they're actively using — like your **desk**. When the desk fills up and a new thing needs space, macOS takes some stuff that's currently on the desk and moves it to a **slower overflow shelf** — a hidden file on your **SSD** called the *swapfile*. That moving of data from RAM out to disk (and back when needed) is **swapping**.

So swapping = **using a chunk of your SSD to pretend you have more RAM than you physically do.** The catch: the SSD is **far slower** than RAM, so every time the system needs something that got swapped out, it has to fetch it from disk — and *that* is the lag you feel.

## Is it necessary?
**Yes — leave it on.** It's a safety valve. Without it, the moment RAM filled up, apps would just crash or refuse to open. Swapping lets you keep working instead. **A little swapping is normal and totally fine** on every Mac.

Your problem isn't *that* it swaps — it's *how much*: you're swapping **~8GB**, basically a whole second copy of your RAM, **constantly**. That's not a healthy safety valve doing its job; that's a flashing sign that **8GB of real RAM is too little for your workload.**

## How do you stop it?
You don't disable swapping directly — on macOS that requires turning off security protections and risky terminal commands that can make the system unstable. **Don't do that.**

The right way to "stop" heavy swapping is to **remove the pressure that causes it**:
- **Run fewer heavy apps at once** (your "separate my work" habit — keep doing that).
- **Quit background hogs**: Adobe Creative Cloud helpers, Spark Desktop, spare browser windows.
- **The permanent fix: more RAM** (the 32GB Mac mini). With enough RAM, the desk never overflows, so swapping naturally drops to near zero — *without* disabling anything.

## "Did it swap to create more storage space?" — No, the opposite ❗
This is the key misconception to fix. Swapping does **not** create or free up storage. It does the reverse: it **takes ~8GB of your SSD and uses it as fake RAM.** So swapping actually **eats** disk space, it doesn't make it.

And this matters for you: your internal drive only has **~6GB free.** When you swap heavily *and* the disk is nearly full, you're squeezing the same small space from both sides — and if the SSD ever fills completely, swapping can't happen and the **whole system can freeze or crash.** So freeing up disk space (move media to an external SSD, empty Trash, clear caches) is worth doing too.

**One-line summary:** Swapping borrows slow disk space to cover for too-little RAM. It's necessary in small doses, but yours is working overtime because the Air only has 8GB — and it *costs* you storage, it doesn't create any. The clean fix is more RAM, not disabling swap.
