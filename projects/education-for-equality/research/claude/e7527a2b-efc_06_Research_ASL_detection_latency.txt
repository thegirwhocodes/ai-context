## A. MediaPipe Optimization

### A1. MediaPipe Holistic vs Hands-Only

- **Holistic**: 543 keypoints (468 face + 33 pose + 21+21 hands), runs pose detection first then crops for face/hands. ~30-50ms on CPU.
- **Hands-only**: 42 keypoints (21 per hand), skips pose and face entirely. ~15ms on CPU (Lite) or ~30ms (Full).
- **Expected savings**: 15-25ms per frame if you switch to Hands-only with Lite complexity.
- **Accuracy tradeoff**: You lose facial expressions (important for ASL grammar -- non-manual markers like raised eyebrows carry grammatical meaning) and body pose (shoulder shifts, body leans). For isolated sign recognition of 300 vocabulary words, hands-only may be sufficient for most signs, but you will lose accuracy on signs that require facial/body context.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Easy. Change `mp.solutions.holistic` to `mp.solutions.hands`, update keypoint indices.
- **Recommendation**: If your TGCN model uses the 55-keypoint format (13 body + 21 left + 21 right), you cannot simply drop to hands-only without retraining. However, if your angle-based 64 features are computed purely from hand landmarks, this is a clean win.

### A2. model_complexity=0 vs 1

- **complexity=0 (Lite)**: ~15ms CPU, ~5ms GPU. Reduced depth neural network, higher jitter, lower accuracy on occluded joints.
- **complexity=1 (Full/default)**: ~30ms CPU, ~8ms GPU. Balanced.
- **complexity=2 (Heavy)**: ~80ms+ CPU, ~15-20ms GPU. Highest accuracy.
- **Expected savings**: Switching from 1 to 0 saves ~15ms per frame (from ~30ms to ~15ms).
- **Accuracy tradeoff**: Higher jitter in landmarks, especially when hands are partially occluded. For sign language where hand shape is critical, this could reduce recognition accuracy by 2-5%.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Trivial -- single parameter change.
- **Works on M1**: Yes.

### A3. MediaPipe on Apple M1 GPU/Neural Engine

- **Current state**: MediaPipe Python on macOS runs on CPU only. The Metal GPU backend for MediaPipe has had longstanding compilation issues on macOS (GitHub issue #5656). MediaPipe was primarily designed for mobile GPU (OpenGL ES/Vulkan on Android, Metal on iOS).
- **Workaround via CoreML**: You could theoretically extract MediaPipe's TFLite models, convert them to CoreML, and run them on the Neural Engine. The M1 Neural Engine is ~3.5x faster than M1 GPU and ~5.5x faster than M1 CPU for supported models. But this requires significant engineering to replicate MediaPipe's full pipeline (palm detection -> hand tracking -> landmark extraction).
- **Implementation difficulty**: Hard. Essentially rebuilding MediaPipe's pipeline with CoreML.
- **Expected savings if achieved**: Could bring per-frame processing down to ~5-8ms.
- **Recommendation**: Not worth it unless you're building a production product. The engineering effort is weeks of work.

### A4. MediaPipe Tasks API vs Legacy Solutions API

- **Tasks API**: Newer, unified API with "end-to-end optimized pipeline performance." Better optimized graph scheduling.
- **Legacy Solutions API**: What most tutorials use (`mp.solutions.holistic`).
- **Expected savings**: Modest, likely 2-5ms due to better graph optimization. No hard benchmarks comparing the two directly.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Medium. Requires rewriting MediaPipe integration code to use the new API.
- **Works on M1**: Yes.

### A5. Frame Skipping

- **Process every 2nd frame**: Effectively doubles your available time per frame. Instead of needing to finish in 33ms (30fps), you have 66ms.
- **Process every 3rd frame**: 100ms per frame budget, very comfortable.
- **Accuracy tradeoff**: For sign language, temporal resolution matters. Most signs take 0.5-2 seconds. At 30fps, a 1-second sign = 30 frames. At 15fps (every 2nd), you get 15 frames -- still adequate for most recognition models (TGCN was trained on 50-frame sequences). At 10fps (every 3rd), you get 10 frames -- this starts to lose fast transitions.
- **Expected savings**: Does not reduce per-frame latency, but reduces CPU load, preventing thermal throttling and leaving CPU headroom for other pipeline stages.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Trivial.
- **Recommendation**: Process every 2nd frame. Use the "skipped" frames to interpolate keypoints linearly for smoother input to the recognition model.

### A6. Resolution Reduction

- **Key insight**: MediaPipe internally resizes input to 128x128 (detection) and 256x256 (tracking). Feeding it 640x480 vs 320x240 mainly affects the resize operation and any pre-processing you do.
- **Expected savings**: Minimal for MediaPipe itself (2-5ms savings from smaller resize). Bigger savings in OpenCV capture and any pre-processing.
- **Accuracy tradeoff**: Minimal -- MediaPipe handles internal resizing well. At very low resolutions (below 240p), hand detection may fail more often.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Trivial. `cv2.resize(frame, (320, 240))` before passing to MediaPipe.
- **Recommendation**: Use 320x240 or even 256x256. The savings are small but free.

## B. Model Inference Optimization

### B1. ONNX Runtime for TGCN

- **Benchmark data**: ONNX Runtime provides ~20% speedup over PyTorch for CPU inference (some benchmarks show up to 2.97x with the x86 quantization backend). For your tiny TGCN model (3.5MB), the absolute savings are small.
- **Expected savings**: From 5-10ms down to 3-7ms. Saves ~3-5ms.
- **For GCN specifically**: INT8 quantized GCN on ONNX can achieve 1.3-4.3x speedup depending on graph topology.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Easy-Medium. `torch.onnx.export(model, ...)` then `ort.InferenceSession("model.onnx")`.
- **Works on M1**: Yes, ONNX Runtime supports macOS ARM64.

### B2. TensorFlow Lite

- **Your Kaggle 1st-place option**: The `sign/kaggle-asl-signs-1st-place` model is already TFLite format (10.7MB, 250 signs), taking MediaPipe landmarks directly.
- **Expected savings**: TFLite is highly optimized for CPU inference. Could replace your TGCN + DTW pipeline entirely if the 250-sign vocabulary is sufficient.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Medium. Install `tflite-runtime` (~5MB), write inference wrapper, adapt input format.
- **Works on M1**: Yes. `tflite-runtime` runs on macOS ARM64.

### B3. CoreML Conversion

- **The Nuclear Option for M1**: Convert your TGCN to CoreML format and run on the Neural Engine.
- **Benchmarks**: M1 Neural Engine is ~3.5x faster than M1 GPU for supported operations. MobileNetV2: 15s on Neural Engine vs 82s on CPU (5.5x).
- **Expected savings for TGCN**: From 5-10ms down to 1-2ms. The model is so small that the overhead of CoreML loading might negate the benefit.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Medium. `import coremltools; coreml_model = ct.convert(traced_model)`. But GCN layers may not be fully supported -- you may need custom layer implementations.
- **Recommendation**: Not worth it for your tiny TGCN. The absolute savings (3-8ms) do not justify the effort. CoreML is better suited for larger models.

### B4. INT8 Quantization of TGCN

- **Benchmark data**: PyTorch x86 INT8 quantization achieves 2.97x geomean speedup on CPU. For GCN layers specifically, speedups of 1.3-4.3x depending on topology.
- **Expected savings**: From 5-10ms down to 2-4ms. Saves ~5ms.
- **Accuracy tradeoff**: Typically <1% accuracy loss with post-training quantization. For GCN, the DEGREE-QUANT paper shows quantization-aware training can maintain accuracy.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Easy-Medium. PyTorch dynamic quantization: `torch.quantization.quantize_dynamic(model, {torch.nn.Linear}, dtype=torch.qint8)`.
- **Works on M1**: Partially. The x86 quantization backend is Intel-optimized. On ARM64/M1, quantization still works but the speedup may be less dramatic (~1.5-2x instead of 3x).

### B5. Batch Inference

- **Concept**: If you have multiple sign candidates, run them through the model in a single batch.
- **Expected savings**: Minimal for your use case. You typically have one sign candidate at a time. Batch inference helps when you have parallel work, which your sequential pipeline does not produce.
- **Recommendation**: Skip unless you implement a multi-hypothesis approach.

## C. Pipeline Architecture Optimization

### C1. Sliding Window Detection (Instead of Pause-Based)

- **Current approach**: Wait for 0.6s pause, then process the entire buffered sequence.
- **Sliding window approach**: Run recognition on a rolling window of the last N frames continuously. Use CTC (Connectionist Temporal Classification) loss for continuous recognition without explicit segmentation.
- **Expected savings**: Eliminates the 600ms pause threshold entirely. Recognition happens in real-time as frames arrive. This is potentially your single biggest win -- saving 600ms.
- **Accuracy tradeoff**: More false positives, requires careful thresholding. CTC-based models handle this well but require retraining.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Hard. Requires either retraining with CTC loss or implementing a sliding window with confidence thresholding on your existing models.
- **Intermediate approach**: Reduce the overlap -- start running recognition on partial sequences while still accumulating frames. If confidence exceeds a threshold, emit early.
- **Works on M1**: Yes.

### C2. Overlap Detection and Recognition

- **Concept**: Start running your TGCN/LSTM/DTW models on the partial sign sequence while the person is still signing. Update the prediction as more frames arrive.
- **Expected savings**: 100-300ms. Instead of waiting for the full sequence, you can have a "draft" prediction ready by the time the person stops signing.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Medium. Run inference every N frames on the current buffer. Keep a running "best guess."
- **Accuracy tradeoff**: Early predictions may flip. Use a stability check -- only emit when the top prediction has been stable for 3+ inference cycles.

### C3. Predictive Buffering / Pipeline Parallelism

- **Concept**: While waiting for sign completion, pre-warm the LLM connection, pre-load TTS, start generating the LLM prompt. As soon as the sign is recognized, fire the LLM call instantly.
- **Expected savings**: 50-100ms from connection pre-warming, potentially more from overlapping stages.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Easy-Medium. Use asyncio or threading to prepare downstream stages while upstream is still processing.

### C4. Reduce Pause Threshold from 0.6s

- **Current**: 0.6s pause threshold.
- **Research**: In natural ASL conversation, pauses between signs average 0.1-0.3s, with phrase boundaries at 0.5-1.0s. A 0.6s threshold catches phrase boundaries but misses individual sign boundaries.
- **Safe minimum**: 0.3-0.4s for individual sign detection. Below 0.3s, you risk splitting multi-part signs.
- **Expected savings**: 200-300ms by reducing from 0.6s to 0.3s.
- **Accuracy tradeoff**: May split compound signs or classifiers that have brief internal pauses.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Trivial. Change one number.
- **Recommendation**: Reduce to 0.35s and test. Combine with a "motion energy" threshold -- only trigger sign completion when hand velocity drops below a threshold AND the pause exceeds 0.3s.

- **Huge win**: If only one sign is recognized, skip the LLM entirely. Map the sign label directly to its English word. No sentence assembly needed.
- **Expected savings**: 100-500ms (entire LLM call eliminated).
- **Implementation difficulty**: Trivial. `if len(recognized_signs) == 1: output = sign_to_word[sign]; else: call_llm()`.

### D3. Pre-Cached TTS Audio

- **Expected savings**: 500-1000ms (entire TTS call eliminated for cached words).

### D4. Streaming TTS

- **Concept**: Start playing audio as soon as the first chunk is generated, before the full sentence is synthesized.

## E. System-Level Optimization

### E1. Python GIL

- **Key insight**: OpenCV releases the GIL during C/C++ operations (image processing, camera capture). NumPy also releases the GIL during array operations. MediaPipe's underlying C++ code also runs outside the GIL.
- **You ARE GIL-bottlenecked if**: Your Python glue code (feature extraction, data copying, model input preparation) takes significant time between native calls.
- **Threading approach**: Use a separate thread for camera capture (I/O-bound, GIL-released). This overlaps capture with processing.
- **Multiprocessing approach**: Run MediaPipe in a separate process, pass keypoints via shared memory or a queue. Eliminates GIL contention entirely but adds IPC overhead (~1-2ms per frame).
- **Expected savings**: 5-15ms from threaded camera capture. More from multiprocessing if your Python glue code is heavy.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Easy for threading, Medium for multiprocessing.
- **Recommendation**: Thread the camera capture immediately. Consider multiprocessing only if profiling shows Python code is a bottleneck.

### E2. NumPy with Apple Accelerate

- **Current state**: Default pip NumPy uses OpenBLAS. You can build NumPy with Apple's Accelerate/vecLib for optimized BLAS/LAPACK on M1.
- **How**: `conda install -c conda-forge numpy "libblas=*=*accelerate"` or build from source with `NPY_LAPACK_ORDER=accelerate`.
- **Expected savings**: Minimal for your workload. Accelerate shines on large matrix operations. Your 64-feature extraction and small model inference won't benefit much. Maybe 1-2ms across the pipeline.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Easy (conda) to Medium (source build).
- **Recommendation**: Low priority. Only worth it if you're already using conda.

### E3. Process Priority

- **macOS allows setting process priority**: `os.nice(-10)` or `renice` from terminal.
- **Expected savings**: Minimal on a modern M1 unless other processes are competing. Maybe 1-5ms reduction in worst-case latency spikes.
- **Implementation difficulty**: Trivial.

## F. Alternative Approaches

### F1. Google SignGemma

- **Architecture**: Vision transformer for hand shapes/facial expressions + compact language model (based on Gemini Nano/MatFormer architecture). Per-Layer Embeddings (PLE) for efficiency.
- **Claimed latency**: <200ms end-to-end on some devices. This includes recognition AND translation.
- **How they achieve it**: Fully on-device, no network calls, vision transformer optimized for edge, no separate MediaPipe + recognition + LLM pipeline -- it's a single end-to-end model.
- **Availability**: Limited preview as of mid-2025, broader release expected Q4 2025. May or may not be publicly available now (April 2026).
- **Trained on**: 10,000+ hours of annotated ASL video.
- **Recommendation**: Check if the preview is accessible. If available, it could replace your entire pipeline. But it may be locked to Google's ecosystem (Android/Pixel devices).

### F2. Skeleton-Based vs Video-Based

- **Skeleton-based** (your approach): MediaPipe extracts keypoints first, then a lightweight model (TGCN, LSTM, DTW) processes keypoints. Pros: Fast inference (2-20ms), small models (3-25MB), robust to background. Cons: Depends on MediaPipe accuracy, loses texture/appearance cues.
- **Video-based**: Feed raw frames to a large model (VideoMAE, I3D, SlowFast). Pros: Can capture subtle hand shapes and textures. Cons: Much slower (2-5 seconds per inference on CPU), huge models (300MB+).
- **Speed winner**: Skeleton-based is dramatically faster. DSLNet (skeleton-based) achieves 17.98ms inference vs VideoMAE at 2-5 seconds on CPU.
- **Accuracy comparison on WLASL-100**: DSLNet skeleton-based achieves 93.70%, VideoMAE video-based achieves 46.5%.
- **Your approach is correct**: Skeleton-based is the right choice for real-time applications.

### F3. Production System Architectures

Real production systems (SignAll, Sign-Speak) use:
- Multiple cameras (front + side views for depth)
- Skeleton-based pipelines for speed
- Hybrid cloud/edge: simple signs on-device, complex sentences to cloud
- Pre-cached common phrases
- Streaming results (show partial translation, refine as more signs come in)
