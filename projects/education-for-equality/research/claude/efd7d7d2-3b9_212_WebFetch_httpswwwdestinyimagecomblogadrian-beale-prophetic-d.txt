# Prophetic Dreams Decoded: 8 Steps to Hear God's Voice in Your Sleep

## Overview
Adrian Beale's article, excerpted from *The Divinity Code to Understanding Your Dreams and Visions*, presents a framework for interpreting prophetic dreams. The author emphasizes that approximately 60% of biblical dreams are metaphorical rather than literal.

## The 8 Steps (as presented in the article):

### 1. **Recognize Dreams as Primarily Metaphorical**
The vast majority of dreams function as parables, riddles, and puzzles requiring decoding. Rather than dismissing dreams connected to recent experiences, recognize that God uses "images fresh in our memories" to convey spiritual truth, similar to how Jesus taught through fishing and farming analogies.

### 2. **Consider Full Context**
Dream interpretation requires understanding the surrounding circumstances. The meaning of dream elements—like biblical names (Jacob vs. Israel)—depends entirely on context. A single element's interpretation from one dream cannot automatically transfer to another dream without considering the dreamer's circumstances.

### 3. **Meditate on the Dream as a Whole Before Interpreting**
Take time before offering interpretation, particularly with longer dreams. The overview matters: "We may have individual elements pegged for certain parts of the dream, but until we can see the overview, we will not know how they fit." This prevents premature false interpretations.

### 4. **Look Beyond Surface Scenes to Spiritual Meaning**
Examine the underlying message rather than literal images. Like Jeremiah observing the potter's wheel, dreamers should "look beyond the mere words of the dream" to understand God's intended message through all scenes presented.

### 5. **Maintain Accurate Dream Sequence**
Record events in proper order before finalizing interpretation. Sequence reveals meaning; disordered information disrupts intended significance. List main points systematically to preserve the dream's integrity.

### 6. **Distinguish Between Past, Present, and Future Contexts**
Not all dreams concern the future. Some reveal church conditions or hidden truths requiring prayer or action. Some use past scenes for context before addressing current or future direction. Timing and wisdom determine whether interpretation should be shared with others.

### 7. **Pay Attention to Repeated Dreams**
Repetition signals importance, imminence, or urgency. As Joseph explained to Pharaoh: "The dream was repeated to Pharaoh twice because the thing is established by God." Repeated dreams demand action and attention.

### 8. **Note Emotional Context and Symbolism Details**
Feelings within dreams carry interpretive weight equal to visual elements. Flying with exhilaration suggests moving in spiritual gifts; flying with fear indicates spiritual danger. Additionally, examine names (both places and people) carefully, gender representation, and overall emotional tone.

## Key Principles

**Spiritual Priority Over Physical:** "The spiritual well-being of a person, city, or nation is more important than their physical welfare" because it concerns eternity versus temporary physical life.

**Receptive Hearts Required:** Effective interpretation requires spiritual sensitivity, freedom from stress/overwork, and peace with God. Joseph's success stemmed from dealing with past hurt and being "filled with the Spirit of God."

**Not All Dreams Require Sharing:** Some dreams are informational only or calls to prayer. Timing and wisdom determine disclosure, particularly with dreams revealing church or community issues.

**Symbol Consistency Varies:** Context determines meaning. A shark might represent predatory danger in childhood contexts but could signify sexual, financial, or contractual threats in other situations.
