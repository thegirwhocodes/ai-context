## The Two Main Categories of Overhead

Management and general expenses, and fundraising expenses make up a nonprofit's "overhead" or "operating expenses."

## What's Included in Management and General Costs

Management and general costs are all the other costs that are needed to operate the organization and are shared across programs. More specifically, examples include administrative salaries, office rent, utilities, accounting services, and IT support.

Additionally, administrative expenses apply to the nonprofit's overall operations and management. For example, the costs of board of directors' meetings, committee meetings, general legal services, accounting, insurance, and bookkeeping.

## Fundraising Expenses

Fundraising expenses include costs for promoting and conducting fundraising campaigns, maintaining donor lists, preparing acknowledgment letters, conducting special events, and any other activities that involve soliciting contributions.

## Important Context on Overhead

A key point you may be missing: some expenses have both direct and indirect components. For example, a computer used for both program delivery and administrative tasks would need its cost allocated appropriately.

Additionally, a reasonable amount of overhead expenses is required to keep a nonprofit running responsibly and effectively. The search results emphasize that appropriate investment in overhead—including staff development, technology, and infrastructure—is necessary for organizational sustainability and mission effectiveness.
