## The Five Pillars of Reading

The National Reading Panel, using decades of research has identified five critical areas of reading instruction: Phonemic Awareness, Phonics, Fluency, Vocabulary and Comprehension.

### Individual Components:

**Phonemic Awareness:** Phonemic awareness is the ability to focus on and manipulate individual phonemes in spoken words.

**Phonics:** Phonics is the study of the systematic relationship between the sounds we hear in words and the letters that spell them, or to say it more technically, the systematic relationships between phonemes and graphemes. Phonics teaches the alphabetic principle, which is the relationships between letters, and spoken language sounds.

**Fluency:** Fluency refers to the ability to read text accurately, quickly, and with proper expression.

**Vocabulary:** Reading Vocabulary is all the words that a student needs to recognize and understand when reading, and it is a part of a complex cognitive process of acquisition.

**Comprehension:** Reading comprehension is the ability to understand and make meaning from what has been read.

### How They Work Together:

These skills are interdependent — working together like pieces of a puzzle — and when taught in a strategic sequence, they help students become confident, skilled, and lifelong readers.

The research emphasizes that comprehension is the whole point of reading, but without the solid foundations of instruction in phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, and vocabulary it is incredibly difficult to achieve strong comprehension.
