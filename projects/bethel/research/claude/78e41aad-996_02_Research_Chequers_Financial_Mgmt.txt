### What Makes Them Attractive as an Acquisition Target

1. **Billion-dollar AUM milestone** crossed, which is a key threshold acquirers look for -- it signals operational maturity and institutional-level infrastructure.
2. **Fee-only, clean compliance profile** -- no commission-based conflicts, zero disciplinary history. This simplifies due diligence.
3. **UHNW niche with sticky clients** -- average balances of $4.3-4.7M with multi-generational relationships mean low attrition risk and high lifetime value per client.
4. **Small team, high revenue per advisor** -- 5 advisors managing $1B+ means each advisor manages ~$200M, well above industry median. Lean operations = attractive margins.
5. **Strong founder brand but potential key-person risk** -- Gorman's media presence (Forbes, Barron's, WSJ) is a growth engine but also a concentration risk, which an acquirer could mitigate by deepening the bench. Tanenbaum as CIO provides some leadership depth.
6. **Women-owned differentiation** -- in an industry focused on diversity, this is a genuine differentiator for acquirers building a diverse portfolio of firms.
7. **Tax-planning DNA** -- the firm's integration of tax and financial planning (driven by Gorman's legal background) is a high-value capability that's difficult to replicate and commands premium fees.
8. **Geographic expansion potential** -- currently licensed in only 6 states with a small Idaho office. An acquirer could scale the model nationally.
9. **Passive/index investment approach** -- low-cost, scalable portfolios mean the investment management side doesn't require expensive active management infrastructure.
