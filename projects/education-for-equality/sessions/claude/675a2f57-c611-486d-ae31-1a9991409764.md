## Comprehensive Plan: The Economic Cost of OOSC in Nigeria

### What the Paper Argues

The paper makes three claims, in escalating order of originality:

1. Milan & Burnett's $40B figure **overstates** the OOSC cost by ~2.5× because it uses global wage premiums (30% primary, from Psacharopoulos & Patrinos) rather than Nigeria-specific data. Using Aromolaran's Nigeria-specific returns (2.5%/yr primary), the OOSC cost is approximately **$16-17B** (~5.88% of GDP).

2. The **in-school non-learning population** (70% of 33M enrolled = ~23M children who attend but cannot read) imposes an additional **$6-11B** in foregone productivity — a cost the entire existing literature ignores, because it only counts children who never enrolled.

3. Adding both populations gives a **total education crisis cost of $23-28B**, which is internally consistent with the World Bank HCI+ 2026 bottom-up education pillar gap (~45 HCI+ points × ~1%/point earnings × $128B labor income base = ~$58B at the macro upper bound including externalities).

**III. Methodology (~1,200 words)**

**3.1 Replicating Milan & Burnett with Nigerian Data**

Equation 1 (Primary):
```
GDP_Loss_Primary = [% non-completing OOSC] × [wage premium to primary]
```

Key methodological decision: converting Aromolaran's per-year Mincerian returns into a total-level wage premium.

- Aromolaran per-year primary return = 2.5% (male wage workers), 2.4% (female wage workers)
- Weighted average ≈ 2.5%/year
- Total primary premium = (1.025)^6 − 1 ≈ **16%** (compound) or 2.5% × 6 = **15%** (simple Mincer)
- Use 15% as conservative; test 16% as robustness check
- Compare: Milan & Burnett used 30% from Psacharopoulos & Patrinos (2011) — roughly 2× Nigeria's actual rate

Updated % non-completing OOSC:
- UNICEF 2024: 18.3M OOSC, school-age population ~65-70M
- Estimated % non-completing: ~28% (vs 23.7% in 2013 Milan & Burnett)
- *Note:* need to locate current UIS typology breakdown (never enrolled vs dropped out vs late entrants) — this is a data gap (see Section 6)

Equation 2 (Secondary cascade):
```
Secondary_Loss = [% non-completing OOSC] × [secondary wage premium] × [transition rate] × [completion rate]
```

- Aromolaran secondary return: 3.9%/yr × 6 years = 23.4% → ~20% total (simple Mincer)
- Transition rate: use 60% (UNICEF, more current than 43% used by Milan & Burnett)
- Secondary completion: use 73% (Nigeria primary completion rate as proxy — conservative)

**3.2 Extension to In-School Non-Learners (Novel Contribution)**

Proposed formula:
```
Non-Learner_Cost = [enrolled × learning poverty rate] × [effective wage gap per non-learning child]
```

Where the wage gap per non-learning child =
- Full wage premium IF educated properly: 8%/yr × 6 years = 48% (global average, Psacharopoulos & Patrinos)
- Actual premium received (attend but don't learn): 2.5%/yr × 6 years = 15% (Aromolaran)
- Wage gap per child: 48% − 15% = **33 percentage points**

Enrolled population exposed to risk: 33.3M × 70% = ~23.3M children

GDP_Nonlearner_Cost = 23.3M/65M (share of working-age) × 33% × GDP_labor_share

- Compare against HCI+ education pillar gap extrapolation
- Compare against World Bank macro estimate (13.03%)
