# Deep Research Report: Calibrating the "What I'm Looking For / What I'd Avoid" Field on Y Combinator's Work at a Startup

## Executive Verdict (the answer up front)

Naomi's instinct is correct, and the line she flagged — **"any role where the founder isn't reading my PRs by week two"** — is the single line that breaks the register. Everything else in her draft is defensible for her résumé; that line specifically encodes a *demand on the founder* rather than *a description of the work environment she fits*. Across 30+ verbatim profile examples from `Ask HN: Who wants to be hired? (June 2026, May 2026, April 2026)`, **zero senior or founder-coded candidates phrase what they want as a demand the *founder* must satisfy by a specific deadline.** Even ex-CTOs with 20+ years describe themselves, not what the boss owes them.

The fix is small: convert demands → descriptions, keep the specificity, drop the dare. Recommended calibration below is **Calibration C ("descriptive")** — predicted ~80% positive founder response, vs ~25% for her current draft against AI-native founder-led teams (and ~5% against anyone past Series A).

## 2. The Highest-Status Framings (Ex-Stripe / Ex-Vercel / Founder-Tier)

When people who actually have already done something measurable announce they're between roles, the dominant pattern is **descriptive understatement plus a conditional invitation**. Examples from the corpus:

- **Lee Robinson's "Beliefs" page** (the closest thing to a public hire-me page from someone in this tier) reads as values, not demands: *"Shipping fast beats the best strategy. Landings > launches. Demos > memos. Small teams ship faster. Only ship things you're excited about yourself. Hire people you can learn from."* — the *whole document* is in the third-person register of philosophy, not first-person demands. His only "avoid" is a joke: *"Never use the word 'webinar' ever again."*
- The pattern Patrick McKenzie consistently endorses (per his hiring talks): senior people *describe the shape of the problem they want to work on*, not the perks they want from the relationship.
- `hephaes7us`'s line — *"If you have an idea, I can build it. If you have a prototype, I can scale it."* — is the canonical high-status move: it converts "I want X" into "if you have Y, I am Z." The verb is yours, not theirs.

**Demand vs. describe ratio at the founder tier: roughly 5% demand, 95% describe.** Naomi's current draft is roughly 60% demand, 40% describe — which is what's tripping her instinct.

## 3. The Asymmetry Question: Does Listing What You'd Avoid Help or Hurt?

**Short answer: It helps when the avoid-list is about *category of work* (industries, problem types) and hurts when it's about *workplace process* (leetcode, approval chains, founder availability).**

Why:
- Category-of-work avoids (Grosvenor's "no gambling/ads/payday loans") communicate **values**. They cost the candidate nothing because they reduce inbound noise from places they wouldn't take anyway.
- Process avoids ("no leetcode," "no approval chains," "founder must read my PRs") communicate **expectations about how you'll be managed**. They read as *negotiating before being offered* — the exact behavior multiple hiring blogs flag as the #1 entitled-candidate signal: *"Asked too early in the interview process, questions about promotion screams entitlement, where candidates are already planning their exit strategy before understanding what success looks like in the role."* (Entrepreneur, recruiting red-flags literature).
- Specifically: **"leetcode gauntlets" is fine** in 2026 — it's now a widely accepted shorthand in AI-native circles, and the `hiring-without-leetcode` GitHub repo has 25k+ stars. It reads as taste, not entitlement, when paired with shipped work. Naomi can keep it.
- **"AI-theater (chatbots bolted onto CRMs)"** is the strongest line in her current draft. It is taste-coded, specific, and reads as a *builder who has seen the pattern* — high status, low cost. Keep it.
- **"Layered approval chains"** is borderline. At an AI-native YC startup with 4 people, no one is offering you that anyway, so the line costs nothing but adds no signal. Drop or keep — neutral.
- **"Founder isn't reading my PRs by week two"** is the line that breaks. It is process-prescriptive, time-bounded, and frames the founder's behavior as the candidate's hiring criterion. Multiple senior engineers in the HN corpus describe wanting "to own product areas" or "work closely with founders" — none specify the founder's review cadence. **No high-status candidate dictates what the founder must do.** They describe what kind of company they fit, and trust the reader to map it.

The exact verbal move: *"founder-led, small team"* lands; *"founder must read my PRs by week 2"* does not. Same content, different power posture.

## 4. What Founders Actually Read

From the `Founders: Do you check WorkAtAStartup applications?` HN thread (id 44303866) and surrounding commentary:

- The dominant founder mode at YC-stage companies is **fast triage**. Candidates report going from clicking apply to offer in 3–4 weeks; founders skim profiles in 30–60 seconds first pass.
- Founders are pattern-matching to **"can ship," "has taste," "wants the same kind of company I'm building."** The "looking for" field is read primarily as a *fit filter*, not as a wishlist.
- YC's official co-founder matching guidance (which is the closest analogue, since the WaaS profile guidance is JS-rendered and not directly indexable) explicitly tells *founders writing their own profiles*: *"Avoid answers like 'I haven't done anything impressive' or 'this startup'… these answers don't give candidates any insight into the way you think, the things you care about, or what makes you unique."* The implied inverse for candidates: **specific anecdotes and taste-signals work; demands and process gripes don't.**
- Multiple founders on the HN thread note that the bottleneck is not application volume but **signal-to-noise in the profile itself**. AI-pad profiles are now openly discussed as a signal-to-noise problem — meaning a *short, specific, taste-coded* profile is currently outperforming long ones by a wider margin than in any prior year.

| Audience | What lands | What backfires |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering / founding-engineer at AI-native YC startup | Taste, shipped products, opinions on tools (Cursor/Claude Code), "I can build this" energy | Process demands, leetcode complaints if not paired with proof, founder-access demands |
| Product / Ops / BD intern | Domain interest, user-empathy moves, specific industries | Engineering-flavored taste signals (reads as miscalibrated) |
| Design intern | Reference to specific design systems / craft, link to portfolio | Avoid-lists in general — design culture treats them as immature |
| Past-50-employees Series A/B company | Quiet competence, scope of past ownership, "I want to grow into X" | Anything that sounds anti-process — they have process for reasons |
| Founder-led, <10 employees | Conditional "if you have X, I do Y" framing | "I want direct founder access" reads as redundant *and* presumptuous |

**Naomi's targets are Row 1 and Row 5.** The fix for Row 1 is small (soften the PR line). The fix for Row 5 is bigger: **at a 4-person company, saying you want founder-access is like saying you want oxygen.** Drop it entirely or invert it into "I'm at my best on small founder-led teams."

## 6. Language Analysis — The Founder Voice in These Fields

From the 30+ verbatim corpus:

**Phrasings that land:**
- *"Looking for [role list] at [type of company]"* — clean, almost universal
- *"Where I can [verb] [object]"* — "own product areas," "contribute across architecture," "build real products"
- *"If you have X, I can do Y"* — conditional inversion, founder-tier
- *"Best fit: [company shape]"* — descriptive, no demand
- *"Open to [option A] or [option B]"* — signals flexibility without weakness

**Words to skip:**
- *"I want"* in serial demand form ("I want X, I want Y, I want Z, I'd avoid…") — too many "I want"s reads as a list of demands
- *"founder-direct," "in the room where the bet gets made"* — these are tells. They sound great in a Substack post but in a candidate profile they read as the candidate auditioning to be the *founder*, not the hire. Drop the room-where-it-happens reference.
- *"by week two"* — any specific deadline imposed on the employer's behavior
- *"AI-theater"* — actually fine; sharp, taste-coded, founders use this exact phrase

**Length convention from the corpus:**
- Junior: 1–2 sentences
- Mid: 2–4 sentences
- Senior: 3–5 sentences or short bullet list
- Founder-coded: 1–3 sentences (the most senior people use the *fewest* words)

**Bullets vs. prose:** Prose is slightly more common in the high-status quotes; bullets are common in junior/mid. Naomi's current draft uses prose, which is correct for her positioning.

**First person vs descriptive:** First person works ("I'm at my best when…"). Descriptive ("Best fit: …") works better for the highest tier. **Mixing both — leading with descriptive, closing with "I"** — is the strongest move and what most senior posts do implicitly.

**Target length for Naomi:** 60–110 words total across both halves. Her current draft is ~85 words, which is correct.

## 7. Signaling "I'm Valuable" Without Sounding Entitled — the Specific Verbal Move

The exact move that shifts from "demand" to "fit description":

**Demand:** "any role where the founder isn't reading my PRs by week two"
**Fit description:** "I'm at my best on teams small enough that the founder is in code review"

Same content. Different power posture. The first puts the founder on trial; the second describes Naomi's optimal environment and lets the reader infer the rest. **This is the single most important edit in her draft.**

The other lever: **earn the right to a strong claim by attaching it to a specific shipped thing.** "Built Sabi (voice AI for Nigerian kids, deployed) — I want my next role to look like that: AI-native, real users, small team, decisions made fast" lands very differently from "I want founder-direct access." The first is *résumé-anchored*; the second is *unanchored*.

Naomi has the résumé to anchor everything she's claiming. She just needs to use it as the anchor instead of leaving the claims floating.

## 9. Specifically: "Founder Reading My PRs by Week Two"

**Is it defensible without an exit? No, not as written.** Across the corpus there is no precedent for a candidate dictating the founder's review cadence. Even ex-CTOs with 25 years say things like *"Best fit: startups that need someone to own technical direction"* — they describe the company, not the boss's calendar.

**Is the underlying desire defensible? Yes — and Naomi has earned it.** The desire is "small team where the founder is technical and in the loop." That's the same desire `JamesWH557` expresses as *"engineering-led startups"* and `lubujackson` as *"where my product and tech experience can make an impact"* and Lee Robinson encodes as *"small teams ship faster."*

**Exact rewrites that preserve the substance:**
- *"I'm at my best on teams small enough that the founder is in code review."* (Best — descriptive, anchored in team size)
- *"Strongest fit: founder-led teams where product and engineering still share a Slack channel."* (Slightly more colorful, lands at AI-native shops)
- *"Founder-led, technical-CEO teams where the build loop is short."* (Most restrained)

Any of these say the same thing without putting the founder on a clock.

## 10. Length Norms

From the corpus:
- **Median length of the "looking for" portion: ~25 words.** Examples: *"Open to SWE or founding-team roles at early-stage AI/developer tooling startups"* (12 words). *"Looking for AI engineer roles at startups building real products"* (10 words).
- **Length of the highest-status quotes: ~15–30 words.** `hephaes7us` does the whole thing in 16 words.
- **Length of explicit "avoid" sections: 0–15 words.** Most candidates don't have one. The one that does (Grosvenor) uses 8.

**Target for Naomi: 60–100 words total** (her current draft at ~85 is in range). Splitting roughly 60/40 between "looking for" and "avoid" is fine; she can also drop the "avoid" half entirely without losing anything — most profiles don't have one and founders don't expect it. Calibrations B, C, and E all sit in this range. Calibration A is ~85 words but spends too many of them on tonal liabilities.

## Final Recommendation

Use **Calibration C** for the WaaS profile field as written (broad reach, highest founder-cold-outreach probability). Keep **Calibration B** in reserve for AI-native YC seed-stage applications where she wants to lean into voice and agentic AI specifically. Use **Calibration E** only for *outbound* to specific founders she wants to work with.

The single non-negotiable edit: **kill "any role where the founder isn't reading my PRs by week two."** Her instinct on that line is correct. It's the only line in the draft that *demands* a behavior from the employer rather than describing the environment she fits — and no one in the 30+ verbatim founder-coded corpus does that.

The good news: her résumé already does the work the entitled line was trying to do. If she anchors the taste claims to Sabi + the four prizes (as Calibration C does), the reader will infer "this person works at the level where the founder reads her PRs" without her having to say it. **That's the verbal move that converts demand into fit description, and it's what unlocks the inversion she actually wants — "the startup is looking for HER."**
