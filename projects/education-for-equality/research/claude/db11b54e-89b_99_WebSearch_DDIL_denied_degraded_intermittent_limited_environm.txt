## What is DDIL?

DDIL stands for Denied, Disrupted, Intermittent, and Limited, a term used to describe situations where internet access is unreliable, unpredictable, or completely unavailable. More specifically, Denied refers to complete loss of signal/connectivity often due to physical separation, jamming, or damage to infrastructure; Degraded means signal/communication quality is reduced such as slower data transfer rates; Intermittent is when signal/communication is available only sporadically with frequent disruptions; and Limited refers to restricted signal/communication where bandwidth, range, or data transfer rates are significantly constrained.

## Military and Defense Context

For the DOD in the modern era of great power competition, DDIL is one of its toughest operational challenges. In austere and afloat conditions, DDIL networks can be a real problem for military operations, restricting access to real-time communications, limiting data transfer, and making it difficult to coordinate across military units and systems, while also affecting the accuracy and timeliness of intelligence.

## AI in DDIL Environments

From SIGINT analysts on reconnaissance planes to special forces commanders in the field and engineers on submarines, LLMs are not reliably available, and as a consequence, very few AI solutions are being built for them today. AI solutions for the DDIL environment must be designed for robustness, meaning an engineered system must be able to handle degradation of resources without catastrophic failure.

## Solutions and Approaches

Organizations can address DDIL challenges through various strategies including edge computing to process data closer to the source, reducing the need to send large amounts of data over constrained networks, and satellite communications using Low Earth orbit (LEO) or geostationary satellites to provide alternative communication channels in denied or degraded environments.
