## Key Distinctions

Phonological awareness is the ability to hear and manipulate parts of spoken language, with skills at a listening/auditory and spoken/verbal level – NO print – spoken words requiring ears only! Phonemic awareness is all about the sounds in oral language, and phonics is all about the sounds in print.

## Oral-Only Phonemic Awareness Activities

Several effective activities can be done without any print:

**Sound Segmentation:** Using LEGO bricks, beads, or pennies, say a word and have your child show you how many sounds the word makes. For example, top = t-o-p = three sounds, so your child would place three objects in a row.

**Rhyming and Blending:** Tell your child you're going to communicate in "snail talk" and they need to figure out what you're saying. Take a simple word and stretch it out very slowly (e.g., /fffffllllaaaag/), then ask your child to tell you the word.

**Sound Blending Games:** Here's an easy phoneme blending game you can play while talking a walk. For blending, you can say, "I see a sign that says s-t-o-p" Then your child has to blend the sounds to guess your word — stop.

## Importance for Pre-Reading

Strong phonemic awareness is one of the strongest predictors of later reading success. These audio-based activities build essential foundational skills before children encounter written text.
