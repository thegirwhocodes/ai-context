One view on your last video does not mean the channel is doomed. YouTube says each upload is evaluated individually; one underperforming video does **not** penalize the whole channel. New channels also need a small library before viewers—and YouTube—can understand what the channel consistently offers. [YouTube’s recommendation guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651?hl=en)

For your music channel, I’d do this for the next month:

- **2–3 Shorts weekly**, cut from your strongest singing moments.
- **One complete cover every 1–2 weeks.**
- Keep the subject consistent: intimate Christian/worship covers, your face, your voice, guitar.
- Begin each Short directly on the strongest lyric or vocal moment—no introduction, title screen, or setup.
- Make most Shorts around **15–35 seconds**, unless a longer section genuinely holds attention.
- Put **“Full cover on my channel”** in the caption or pinned comment.
- Reuse one performance for several genuinely different Shorts: strongest note, most meaningful lyric, harmony moment, intimate opening, etc.

After posting, evaluate:

- **Viewed vs. swiped away**
- **Audience retention/completion**
- Rewatches
- Comments, shares, and subscribers earned

Then make more of the moments people actually finish. YouTube explicitly emphasizes the opening seconds, retention, viewer satisfaction, and sustainable consistency—not raw upload volume. [Performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650?hl=en) [Shorts scheduling guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13616979?hl=en)

So the real answer is: **yes, persist—but run a 12-Short experiment, not an endless posting lottery.** Don’t judge yourself from one video or quit before YouTube has enough material to test. Your new cover already gives you several Shorts; post the strongest emotional section first.
