## Why Google/Sign-Speak Are Instant and You're Slow

The fundamental difference is **architecture**. Here's what they do vs what you do:

### Your Pipeline (Serial, Wait-Then-Process)
```
Camera → buffer 30 frames (1 SECOND OF WAITING) → feed all 30 to LSTM → get word → Claude API (200-500ms) → TTS
Total: ~1.5-2.5 seconds per sign
```

**You wait for the signer to FINISH, then process.** That 30-frame buffer is a full second of dead time before anything happens.

### How SignGemma/Sign-Speak Do It (Streaming, Continuous)
```
Camera → vision transformer processes CONTINUOUSLY → CTC decoder emits words AS signing happens → text appears in real-time
Total: <200ms from sign to text
```

**They never wait.** Three key architectural differences:

### 1. Streaming Sliding Window (not batch buffer)
Instead of collecting 30 frames and then processing, they use a **sliding window** — the model looks at the last N frames continuously and outputs predictions every single frame. Words appear as you sign, not after you stop.

### 2. CTC (Connectionist Temporal Classification) Loss
This is the secret sauce. CTC lets the model:
- Handle **variable-length input** without knowing where signs start/end
- Output words **mid-stream** without waiting for a pause
- Handle signs that blend together (coarticulation)

Your LSTM needs exactly 30 frames with a clear start/stop. CTC models don't.

### 3. End-to-End Model (no separate LLM call)
SignGemma: vision transformer → compact language model → English text. **One model, one pass.**
You: MediaPipe → feature extraction → LSTM → Claude API call → text. **Four steps, two of which hit the network.**

## What You'd Need to Change

| Change | Impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Replace 30-frame buffer with sliding window** | Words appear while signing, not after | Medium — modify `asl_detector_v3.py` |
| **Run LSTM on every frame, emit when confidence spikes** | Eliminates 1s buffer wait | Medium |
| **Drop Claude verification for medium-tier** | Saves 200-500ms per uncertain sign | Easy — already done |
| **Pre-cache TTS audio** | Speech in <10ms not 50-800ms | Easy |
| **Use Groq instead of Claude for sentences** | 50ms vs 200-500ms | Already set up |
| **Train a CTC-based model** (long-term) | True streaming recognition | Hard — needs GPU + data |

**The single biggest win right now:** Kill the 30-frame buffer requirement. Instead, run the LSTM on a sliding window and emit predictions the moment confidence crosses the threshold — don't wait for hands to stop.

## Impact of Dactyl — The Full Picture

### The Problem We're Solving

**466 million people** with disabling hearing loss. **$980 billion/year** global cost. Here's what happens without a solution:

#### Lives Lost
- Deaf people have **5-9 years lower life expectancy** than hearing people (Canada data) — simply because they can't communicate with doctors
- **60% of deaf patients** experience communication barriers in healthcare — discharged without understanding their diagnosis
- Police have **killed multiple deaf people** for not responding to verbal commands (Magdiel Sanchez, Daniel Harris, John Williams)
- **40% of deaf people in the UK** have experienced communication breakdowns in emergencies

#### Education Failure (Unchanged for 100+ Years)
- Median reading level of deaf high school graduates: **4th grade** — this has not changed since the early 1900s
- 1 in 5 graduate reading at **2nd grade level or below**
- After 4th grade, deaf students' literacy **increasingly falls behind** — the gap widens every year
- But: students proficient in ASL **outperform** their peers in reading, English, and math — the problem isn't deafness, it's access

#### Employment & Economics
- **50:1** ratio of deaf people to interpreters in the US
- Interpreter supply is **shrinking** — education programs closing due to lost federal funding
- Deaf employment rate: **54%** vs 70% hearing (16-point gap)
- Deaf wages: **25% less** than hearing peers
- Every **$1 invested** in hearing care returns **$16** over 10 years (WHO)

### What Dactyl Changes

| Barrier | Current Reality | With Dactyl |
|---|---|---|
| **Doctor visit** | No interpreter available, patient guesses at diagnosis | Real-time captions + sign translation on glasses |
| **Emergency** | Can't call 911, can't hear police commands | Bidirectional communication through glasses |
| **Job interview** | Must book interpreter days in advance, often unavailable | Instant translation, no third party needed |
| **Family dinner** | Dinner Table Syndrome — deaf child understands nothing | Parents wear glasses, child's signs become speech |
| **Classroom** | 4th grade reading level after 12 years of school | Real-time lecture captions + sign recognition |
| **Daily errands** | Can't order coffee, can't use drive-through, can't call doctor | Communication anywhere, anytime |

### The ROI

- WHO says **$1.40 per person per year** scales hearing care globally
- A **90% reduction** in unaddressed hearing loss = **$2.4 trillion** in productivity gains
- Interpretation services cost only **$4 per medical encounter** (0.5% premium) but most facilities don't provide them
- Dactyl costs **$0 per encounter** after the hardware — no interpreter booking, no scheduling, no shortage

### Why This Matters Now

The interpreter shortage is **getting worse, not better**. Programs are closing. The supply is shrinking while demand grows. Technology isn't replacing interpreters — it's filling the **90% of moments where no interpreter exists**:

- The hallway conversation
- The impromptu meeting  
- The emergency room at 2am
- The dinner table every night
- The police stop on a dark road

**Dactyl doesn't replace interpreters. It fills the gap where interpreters will never be.**
