## Core Storytelling Frameworks

A popular business storytelling framework is Situation-Complication-Resolution (SCR), popularized through "The Pyramid Principle" by Barbara Minto at McKinsey Consulting. This framework consists of: Situation (facts about the current state), Complication (action is required based on the situation), and Resolution (the action is taken or recommended to solve the complication).

## Timing for 10-Minute Presentations

For presentations, consider that each major content slide will take approximately one minute to present, so for a 10-minute presentation, aim for about 10 slides with content.

## Data Storytelling Structure

Data storytelling combines data, visual storytelling, and a clear narrative so the audience understands both the evidence and the meaning behind it. A recommended structure includes: starting with a question (not a spreadsheet), showing one core insight per slide, using the simplest chart that answers the question, and ending with a takeaway, implication, or recommendation.

## Narrative Arc and Story Elements

Build a narrative arc by introducing the problem, revealing the insight, and leading your audience toward a solution, which keeps your audience engaged. Data storytelling uses narrative elements including characters, setting, conflict, and resolution.

## Presentation Design Principles

Use horizontal alignment where each slide functions as a coherent unit showing one single aspect of the narrative, with the core message in the title rather than just a label. Vertical alignment means that if you read all the slide titles in sequence, they should tell the full story of the presentation, creating a coherent and comprehensive narrative.
