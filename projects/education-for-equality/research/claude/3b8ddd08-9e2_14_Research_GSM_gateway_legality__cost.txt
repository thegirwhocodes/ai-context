## PART 1 — LEGALITY IN NIGERIA

### 1.1 What the NCC actually prohibits — exact definitions

The NCC's own press release ("NCC Discusses Call Masking/Refiling and SIM Boxing at TCP, Lagos") gives the authoritative definitions:

- **Call Masking:** *"a phenomenon whereby an international call is masked to appear as a local call on any GSM network in Nigeria."*
- **SIM Boxing:** *"electronic boxes or devices with multiple SIMs that have the capacity to terminate calls at local interconnect rates."*
- **Interconnect Bypass Fraud (IBF):** *"one of the most prevalent frauds in the telecom industry today."*

**The fraud mechanism — CONFIRMED.** The crime is exploiting the rate differential by **terminating international calls at the local rate**. The NCC's own figures: a masked international call is terminated at the **local rate of ₦3.90/min** instead of the lawful **international termination rate of ₦24.40/min**, and the fraudster pockets the difference. This is theft of interconnect/settlement revenue from operators and government. **The illegality is defined by the international-to-local misrepresentation, not by the device itself.** The interconnect-bypass industry source (abhandshake.com) confirms: the fraud is *"reroute international calls… making them appear as local calls"* — *"the fraud is the rate evasion strategy, not the technology alone."* That same source explicitly acknowledges **legitimate** GSM-gateway uses: network-failover/backup, testing, and analytics.

NCC estimates industry losses to call masking + SIM boxing at **~$3 billion**.

### 1.2 The legal test that matters most: TYPE APPROVAL

This is the single most important legal fact for Sabi, and it cuts against a GSM gateway:

> **"These SIM boxes are never type-approved by the Commission, a clear indication that they are being used illegally in the country."** — NCC

Under Nigerian law, **all** telecom terminal equipment imported, sold, or deployed must be **NCC type-approved** (Type Approval is mandatory for "any and all telecommunication based devices"). A multi-SIM GSM/VoIP gateway (GoIP, Ejoin, etc.) is, as a class, **not type-approved** — and the NCC treats non-type-approval as prima facie evidence of illegal use. So even a *domestically-only* gateway sits in a category the NCC has publicly branded illegal. The hardware itself is the legal exposure, independent of whether you ever route an international call.

Aggravating factor: the cheap gateways openly advertise **"IMEI Change" / IMEI-switching and SIM-rotation** features (seen on the DBL G1 and SysMaster/VMGate product pages). Those features exist specifically to defeat fraud detection and are hallmarks of SIM-boxing — possessing a device marketed on IMEI-spoofing is bad optics and bad law.

### 1.3 Is a small, type-approved gateway with NIN-SIMs placing DOMESTIC callbacks to consenting enrolled users legally distinct from illegal SIM-boxing?

**Conceptually yes — the activity is distinct** (no international traffic = no interconnect-bypass fraud, the thing the law actually targets). **But in Nigerian practice the distinction is fragile**, for three reasons:

1. **The device class isn't type-approved.** There is no "small type-approved GSM gateway" category you can buy off the shelf and point to. The NCC's stance ("never type-approved → illegal") means the burden is on you to prove legitimacy after the fact, not on them to prove fraud.
2. **The NCC and operators detect by *pattern*, not by intent.** Fraud-detection systems flag: high call volume from a single SIM, many SIMs in one location, short call durations, and velocity anomalies (per Subex/Infosys/Vonage detection literature). A bank of SIMs in one building auto-dialing hundreds of children **looks exactly like a SIM box** to an automated fraud-management system — and the operator will block the SIMs first and ask questions never. Modern detection explicitly targets operations that "use genuine local SIM cards and mirror legitimate traffic patterns."
3. **Enforcement is aggressive and uses blunt instruments.** In Feb 2018 the NCC **barred ~750,000–1,000,000 numbers** suspected of masking and **suspended six interconnect-exchange licensees** (citing **Section 45(1) and (3) of the Nigerian Communications Act 2003**). They suspended Medallion Communications' Interconnect Clearinghouse Licence for 90 days and disconnected ICSL and Solid Interconnectivity from all networks.

**Conditions that keep a domestic auto-dialer on the right side of the *fraud* line** (necessary but, given type-approval, not fully sufficient):
- **Zero international traffic** — every call leg domestic, terminating on-net to enrolled Nigerian numbers. This is the bright line for the IBF offence.
- **NIN-registered SIMs in the org's name** (mandatory anyway under the Registration of Communications Subscribers Regulations 2022 — unregistered/pre-registered SIM use is itself an offence under **Section 65 NCA 2003**, carrying the small SIM-offence penalties: 2 months/₦15,000 or 6 months/₦20,000 in reported convictions).
- **No IMEI spoofing / SIM-rotation-to-evade** — use real, stable IMEIs.
- **Documented user consent** (enrolment records) — relevant to the separate anti-spam/DND regime (§1.4) and to rebutting a fraud allegation.
- **Modest scale** — a handful of SIMs, human-plausible call patterns. The more SIMs and the higher the velocity, the more it reads as a SIM box regardless of purpose.

### 1.4 Rules against an org owning multiple SIMs and auto-dialing its own users (DND / anti-spam / telemarketing)

- **No prohibition on an organization owning multiple SIMs per se.** The constraint is registration (NIN, in the registered entity's name) and the SIM-box/type-approval issue above.
- **Do-Not-Disturb (DND) / 2442:** The NCC's `2442` DND short code lets subscribers send **STOP to 2442** for full DND. **Critical for Sabi: the NCC's 2442 directive covers *text messages*, NOT voice calls** — *"the directive… only covers text messages, and not telemarketing calls. There is no time restriction… so messages and calls can be made anytime of the day."* So an outbound *voice* callback is not blocked by DND today.
- **Anti-spam / telemarketing enforcement** is grounded in data-protection, not call-blocking: the NCC has vowed to *"arrest and prosecute"* telemarketers who **harvest subscriber numbers through dishonest means** (legal basis: Section 35(1) Consumer Code of Practice Regulations 2007; Section 9(1) Registration of Communications Subscribers Regulations 2022; Section 4.2 Internet Code of Practice; plus NDPA 2023 data-protection law). **The protection is against unlawfully *obtaining* the numbers** — calling children **whose guardians enrolled them and consented** does not run afoul of this. Consent + enrolment records are your shield here.

### 1.5 At what point does this need a license? Penalties.

**VAS license threshold.** Running a value-added voice service (IVR, call-centre, content services) for the public is a **Value Added Services (VAS)** activity. The line: a pilot calling your own enrolled, consenting users from your own SIMs is arguably internal/non-commercial; the moment it becomes a **public-facing service** (especially with a short code, premium/reverse-billing, or aggregator integration) it needs a **VAS licence**. Costs (NCC, 2024–25):
- Application form **₦1,000** + **₦500,000** non-refundable admin charge (5% of the ₦10,000,000 licence fee).
- **Annual operating levy = 1% of gross revenue** for non-network operators.
- A short-code/content service additionally requires an **MOU with a licensed VAS Aggregator** (and Sabi's prior research priced the aggregator path: AT shared USSD ₦53,750/mo; dedicated USSD ₦200k integration + ₦70k/mo + MNO connection fees up to ₦500k–₦770k).

**Penalties for getting it wrong:**
- **Operating a communications service without a licence:** offence under **Section 31 NCA 2003**.
- **Selling/deploying equipment without approval:** **Section 133 NCA 2003**; **interfering with a network:** **Section 131**.
- **Type-approval / general contravention (Enforcement Regulations 2019):** NCC can **seal premises, seize transmitters/equipment, and fine ₦5,000,000 per contravention + ₦500,000 per day** of continuing contravention.
- **SIM-registration offences (§65 NCA):** the small fines/imprisonment noted above.
- **Cybercrimes Act 2015** (for IMEI-counterfeiting / fraud-tool devices): up to **7 years imprisonment and/or ₦5,000,000 fine**.
- **Interconnect/masking:** licence suspension/withdrawal under **§45 NCA**; mass number-barring (the 750k–1M precedent). Note also the recent climate of aggressive NCC fining (₦12.4bn across operators in Jan 2026; MTN's historic ₦275bn SIM fine) — the regulator is not shy.

**Net legal read for Sabi:** A **domestic-only, NIN-SIM, consenting-user, small-scale callback rig** does **not** commit the interconnect-bypass *fraud* the NCC is hunting. **But** (a) the gateway hardware class is not type-approved and is publicly branded "illegal" by the NCC, (b) the call pattern is indistinguishable from a SIM box to automated fraud detection and will likely get the SIMs blocked, and (c) scaling to a public service triggers VAS licensing. The legally clean paths Sabi's earlier research already identified — **0800 toll-free (callee-pays, works at ₦0, type-approved, fully licit)** and **AT/SIP outbound callbacks (~₦3/min, licensed carrier)** — avoid all of this. A GSM gateway should be treated as **high regulatory risk**, suitable at most for a tiny throwaway technical experiment, never as production infrastructure.

### 2.2 USB GSM modem + Asterisk/FreeSWITCH alternative

- **Huawei USB dongles** (E155X, E173, E169, K3520, K3765, etc.) compatible with **`chan_dongle`** for Asterisk: **~$10 each + shipping**. Each dongle = 1 voice channel + SMS + USSD.
- **Host:** a **Raspberry Pi (~$35–75)** or any cheap Linux box running Asterisk/RaspBX/MikoPBX. Needs a **≥1.2A power supply** per modem (under-powering causes "dongle disconnected" call failures).
- **Caveats:** not all Huawei modems do voice out-of-the-box — some need a firmware flash / voice-enable; reliability per dongle is mediocre; a powered USB hub is needed for several modems.
- **All-in for ~4 channels:** ~4×$10 dongles + $60 Pi + hub/PSU ≈ **$120–$150** — the cheapest DIY path, but the most fiddly and least reliable.

### 2.3 Android phone + Tasker/ADB alternative

- **Hardware:** any cheap Android phone (**$30–$60** used; could reuse an existing handset).
- **Software:** **Tasker** (~$3 one-time) with **AutoInput**; auto-dial works via Tasker's Call action (Auto Dial), can be granted **ADB permissions** to bypass some limits; Kolmisoft markets an "Android-powered VoIP termination — no SIM boxes" approach for exactly this.
- **Limits (significant):** call automation often only fires when **Tasker is in the foreground**; some actions need **root**; **one call at a time per phone** (no concurrency); DTMF/IVR navigation is brittle; Android battery/OS call-management interferes. Fine for a **proof-of-concept dialing a handful of kids sequentially**, useless at pilot scale.

### 2.4 Running costs

- **SIM acquisition:** SIM packs are effectively free/near-zero (₦100–₦500); **NIN registration** is free but mandatory and now biometric — each SIM must be tied to a registered NIN in the org's name. Acquiring *many* SIMs to one entity itself draws scrutiny.
- **Per-minute on-net call costs (cheapest plans, July 2025 — from techcabal):**
  - **Glo Super Talk: 15.5 kobo/sec = ₦9.30/min** (cheapest) **+ ₦15/day** first-call access fee. (Glo Talk On: 22 k/s = ₦13.2/min, no daily fee.)
  - **9mobile MoreLife Complete: 16.38 k/s = ₦9.83/min + ₦7.68/day.**
  - **MTN Pulse: 23 k/s = ₦13.8/min.** (BetaGist/XtraValue 30 k/s = ₦18/min.)
  - **Airtel SmartTalk: 25 k/s = ₦15/min.** (SmartGist 33.33 k/s = ₦20/min.)
  - Daily-access-fee plans aren't true bundles; bonus-airtime plans (Airtel OverJara, 9Konfam) bill bonus minutes at penalty rates (₦50/min, 83 k/s) — avoid for automation.
- **FUP / throttling / blocking risk (the real cost):** there is no published "FUP" for voice, but **fraud-management systems will throttle or block SIMs** showing SIM-box-like patterns — high volume from one SIM, short durations, single-location velocity. **Expect SIMs to be barred**, forcing constant re-acquisition and re-registration. This is the hidden, recurring, and potentially fatal operating cost.
- **Maintenance/reliability:** GSM modules overheat and drop; SIMs get blocked; firmware quirks; power sensitivity; one site = single point of failure. Carrier-grade reliability it is not.

### 2.5 Realistic all-in: GSM gateway vs AT SIP outbound (~₦3/min)

**Assume a pilot lesson = ~10 min/child, ~3 sessions/week.**

- **300 children × 10 min × 3/wk × 4 wks = 36,000 min/month.**

**Option A — GSM gateway (Glo Super Talk @ ₦9.30/min):**
- Airtime: 36,000 × ₦9.30 = **₦334,800/mo**, **plus** ₦15/day × (SIMs) access fees, **plus** ~$300 one-time hardware, **plus** SIM-replacement churn from blocks, **plus** an on-the-ground operator to babysit it.
- **Effective ≈ ₦335k+/mo and legally exposed.** Cheaper per-minute than you'd think, but the daily fees, blocking churn, and illegality swamp the saving.

**Option B — Africa's Talking SIP/outbound (~₦3/min):**
- AT outbound is billed at **~1 unit/min ≈ ₦3/min** (AT help center: outbound dialing charged "1/- per minute" from your stash; Nigeria unit ≈ ₦3). 36,000 × ₦3 = **₦108,000/mo**, **no hardware, no SIMs, no NIN registration, fully licensed, no blocking risk.**
- **AT is both cheaper (₦108k vs ₦335k) AND legal.** The GSM gateway's supposed cost advantage **evaporates** — local airtime (₦9.30/min on Glo) is *more* expensive per minute than AT's ₦3/min SIP rate, before you even count hardware, labour, and legal risk.

**For a tiny 10-child test:** GSM gateway ≈ free-ish minutes but ~$200 hardware + setup time; AT ≈ 10×10×3×4×₦3 = **₦3,600/mo (~$2.30)** with zero capex. AT wins at every scale.

> **Important correction to the premise:** the assumption that a GSM gateway is cheaper "vs paying AT ₦3/min" is **backwards**. Nigerian retail on-net airtime is **₦9–15/min**; AT's wholesale SIP outbound is **~₦3/min**. The gateway only beats SIP if you're terminating *international* traffic (the illegal case). For *domestic* callbacks, **SIP is cheaper and legal.**

### 2.6 African orgs using GSM gateways legitimately

- **Generic legitimate GSM-gateway use is well-documented** (call-centre least-cost routing cutting telephony bills "up to 60%," network failover/backup) — voip-info.org, Hypermedia, abhandshake.com. "Call Africa" (Rwanda, callafrica.co.rw) markets GSM-gateway services.
- **BUT no reputable African IVR/edtech org is documented running its service on a SIM-bank GSM gateway.** The leaders Sabi already researched all use **licensed/legal rails:** **Viamo 3-2-1** (operator-zero-rated shortcode over SIP/E1, MNO donates airtime), **Eneza/Shupavu291** (Safaricom billing integration), **GeoPoll/Arifu** (zero-rated reverse-billed shortcodes). The GSM-gateway/"GSM termination" ecosystem in Africa (goantifraud.com's "make money on GSM termination in Africa," SysMaster, VMGate) is overwhelmingly the **grey/illegal international-termination market**, not legitimate service delivery. **Adopting that toolkit would associate Sabi with exactly the fraud the NCC is prosecuting.**

## BOTTOM LINE

- **Legality:** The NCC's prohibited act is **terminating international calls as local** to steal the interconnect margin (₦3.90 vs ₦24.40/min) — confirmed. A purely **domestic** callback rig to **consenting, NIN-registered enrolled users** does **not** commit that fraud and is **not** blocked by DND (which is SMS-only) or by any rule against owning multiple SIMs. **However**, the gateway hardware class is **not NCC type-approved** and is publicly branded "illegal"; the call pattern is **indistinguishable from a SIM box** and will likely get SIMs **blocked**; IMEI-change features common on these devices invite Cybercrimes-Act exposure (7 yrs / ₦5M); and a public service needs a **VAS licence** (₦500k + 1% gross revenue). Enforcement is harsh (₦5M/contravention + ₦500k/day; mass number-barring; licence suspension under §45 NCA).
- **Cost:** Hardware is cheap (4-port GoIP ~$207; 8-port ~$300; DIY Huawei-dongle+Pi ~$120–150; Android+Tasker ~$30–60 but unreliable/sequential-only). **But the economics don't favour it:** cheapest Nigerian on-net airtime is **₦9.30/min (Glo) + daily fees**, *more expensive* than **AT SIP outbound at ~₦3/min**. For a 300-child pilot, AT ≈ **₦108k/mo, legal, zero capex** vs a gateway ≈ **₦335k/mo + hardware + SIM-block churn + legal risk.**
- **Recommendation:** **Do not build the GSM gateway for production.** It is more expensive per minute than AT for domestic calls, legally precarious, and reputationally toxic (it's the fraudsters' toolkit). Stay on the rails Sabi's prior research already validated: **0800 toll-free for ₦0 inbound + AT/SIP for outbound callbacks**, and pursue the **NCC March-2026 educational zero-rating list**. A GSM gateway is justified only as a one-off, single-SIM, throwaway lab experiment — never as the channel children actually depend on.
