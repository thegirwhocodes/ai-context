# Sabi Pricing Evaluation — 7 NGOs × 10,000 students, $10/child target

## TL;DR

**$10/child is a good, defensible price — but only if you cap minutes.** At realistic engagement (~3 calls/week), $10/child gives you 60–75% gross margin. At the full program design dose (2 calls/day, literacy + numeracy), $10/child **loses money on every child** unless telephony gets zero-rated — the implied rate ($0.0028/min) is below even your cheapest possible delivery cost. The fix: price it as **$10/child with ~900 included minutes** (≈150 calls), overage at ~$0.01/min. And no, you're not leaving meaningful money on the table — $10 sits above your own published mid-scale tier ($5–8) and right in the comparator band (Rori $5, EIDU $11, ConnectEd $12, EKOEXCEL $24).

## 1. What a Sabi minute actually costs us

From [Cost Breakdown.md](5. Cost & Feasibility/Cost Breakdown.md), [Sabi Revenue.md](5. Cost & Feasibility/Sabi Revenue.md), and the [700k production stack model](5. Cost & Feasibility/SABI_700K_PRODUCTION_STACK_COST_MODEL_2026-06-29.md):

**All-in delivery cost: ~$0.002/min (best case) to ~$0.012/min (current callback + Google TTS).** Call the honest planning number **~$0.004–0.006/min** at 70k-student scale.

## 2. The 7-NGO math

Revenue: 7 NGOs × 10,000 students × $10 = **$700,000** ($100k per NGO) for a 10-month program (~43 weeks), literacy + numeracy.

The whole answer hinges on dose — minutes per child:

| Engagement scenario | Min/child (10 mo) | Total minutes (70k kids) | **Implied price/min at $10/child** | Cost @ $0.0033/min | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full design dose — 2 calls/day, 7 days (1 literacy + 1 numeracy, 6 min) | 3,612 | 253M | **$0.0028** | $834k | **−19% (loss)** |
| School-days dose — 2 calls/day, 5 days | 2,580 | 181M | **$0.0039** | $596k | +15% (thin) |
| Realistic engagement — 3 calls/week (the base case in your own unit-economics doc) | 774 | 54M | **$0.0129** | $179k | **+74%** |

With the **current callback telephony (₦15/min)**, the picture is worse: full dose costs ~$2.8M against $700k revenue, and even realistic engagement only nets ~15%. **The current production telephony path cannot support $10/child at any serious dose.**

**So: what should we charge per minute?** Working backwards from $10/child at realistic engagement, and keeping ~60%+ margin over the $0.004–0.006 cost floor:

> **Charge NGOs ~$0.012–0.015/min (₦19–24/min), or equivalently $10/child with a ~900-minute fair-use cap** (≈150 calls ≈ 3.5 calls/week), overage billed at $0.010/min. If an NGO genuinely wants the full 2-calls-a-day dose delivered, that's a **$18–25/child** product — or the telephony gets sponsored (the [toll-free sponsor research](5. Cost & Feasibility/TOLL_FREE_PILOT_SPONSOR_RESEARCH_2026-06-29.md) Airtel/MTN Foundation asks exist precisely for this).

The cap isn't a gimmick — it's the mechanism that makes a flat per-child price survivable when engagement varies 5× between kids.

## 3. How this feeds the API pricing

Your API tiers are $0.05 (Basic) / $0.08 (Pro) / $0.12–0.15 (Enterprise). Here's the collision with the $10/child target:

- At **$0.05/min, $10 buys only 200 minutes/child/year** — about 33 calls, under one call a week. Nobody can run real tutoring on your API at Basic pricing while hitting African edtech per-child economics.
- Your own delivery cost is $0.004–0.006/min. The managed NGO price ($0.012–0.015/min) and the API Basic price ($0.05/min) are **different products at different altitudes** — that's fine, but it means the API as currently priced serves *low-minutes, high-value-per-minute* buyers (fintech IVR, health hotlines, government services, assessments), **not** high-dose edtech delivery.
- **Recommendation: add a committed-volume tier at ~$0.015–0.02/min for 5M+ min/month.** That's still 3–4× your cost (healthy margin), still the cheapest Africa-optimized option on earth, and it's the only tier at which another edtech could build a <$15/child program on your rails. Without it, every serious edtech buyer will look at your prices, do the same math I just did, and try to self-host instead.

## 4. Is the pricing good in general? Leaving money on the table?

Against the market (from [Sabi Revenue.md](5. Cost & Feasibility/Sabi Revenue.md) — the background workflow is re-verifying these as current):

| Provider | $/min | vs Sabi API Basic |
|---|---|---|
| Vapi | ~$0.14 real-world | Sabi 64% cheaper |
| ElevenLabs Conversational | $0.08–0.10 | 38–50% cheaper |
| Bland AI | ~$0.09 | 44% cheaper |
| Retell AI | $0.07+ | 29% cheaper |

**On the API: mildly, yes.** You're the *only* Nigerian-accent/Pidgin/2G-optimized pipeline — that's a premium feature priced at a discount. Basic could be **$0.06–0.07** and still undercut everyone. I'd raise Basic slightly and add the volume floor tier; you'd capture more from small buyers and lose none of the big ones.

**On the NGO price: no.** $10/child is *above* your own published mid-scale managed tier ($5–8 for 1K–50K students) and above the government-pricing doc's state-contract recommendation ($5–7). NGOs are less price-sensitive than SUBEBs, and $10 still reads as "less than half of EKOEXCEL, comparable to ConnectEd" — squarely in the fundable band. If anything, don't go lower.

## 5. Are we running at a loss?

- **Per-child variable margin: profitable** at realistic dose with inbound/cheap telephony; **loss-making** at full dose or on the current callback path. The minute cap + telephony fix resolves this.
- **Fully loaded: the 7-NGO book alone doesn't carry the company.** $700k revenue against ~$830k/yr fixed staff cost (FY27 plan) plus a required production infra build-out (the current server holds ~500–700 students as-deployed; 70k students needs the queued/pooled architecture). That's consistent with the existing plan — grants and government contracts carry fixed costs; NGO deals should be judged on gross margin, and at the capped price they're strongly gross-margin positive.

## 6. Before you quote anyone — three blockers from your own docs

1. **Verify the Africa's Talking rate** (₦3 per *second* vs per *minute*) — flagged urgent in March, still unresolved. It's a 60× swing that decides whether inbound is $0.002/min or $0.11/min.
2. **Literacy routing**: the Next.js AT path is numeracy-only; $10/child sells *both* subjects, so the Python/Asterisk path must carry literacy before any NGO contract starts.
3. **ElevenLabs is first in the TTS chain.** At paid rates ($0.028/min) it single-handedly blows the NGO budget. Get Impact Program approval or flip the chain to YarnGPT-first before 70k-student volume.

## First: what a "moat" even is

A **moat** is just *the thing that stops a competitor from copying you and stealing your customers.* Like the water around a castle. If anyone could build Sabi in a weekend, you have no moat. The five below are the only things that actually protect a company long-term.

**1. Data flywheel.**
A "flywheel" is a heavy wheel that's hard to push at first, but once it's spinning, each push makes it spin faster and it becomes hard to stop. In business it means: *a loop where each step makes the next step stronger, so success builds on itself.*

Sabi's version: kids call Sabi → those calls give you recordings of real Nigerian children speaking → you use those recordings to make Sabi understand Nigerian accents better → because it understands them better, more kids succeed and more organizations sign up → more kids call → even more recordings. Round and round, getting stronger each loop. **A competitor starting today has none of those recordings, and can't get them without years of calls.** That's the moat.

**2. Workflow integration.**
This means *you're so woven into how someone does their daily work that ripping you out would break everything.* If an NGO runs its whole literacy program through Sabi — enrollment, lessons, progress reports — switching to a competitor means rebuilding their entire operation. They won't. That stickiness protects you.

**3. Distribution.**
*You can reach customers that others can't.* If you have the telco partnerships, the government relationships, and a community of developers who already know Sabi, a competitor with better tech still can't get to the customers. Getting in front of people is often harder than building the product.

**4. Brand / trust.**
*People pick you because they trust you, even if a cheaper option exists.* When LASUBEB or a parent trusts "Sabi teaches my child safely and well," that trust took years to earn and a competitor can't buy it overnight.

**5. Network effects.**
*The product gets more valuable as more people use it.* Once lots of African developers build on Sabi's voice tools, others join because that's where everyone already is — like a language: it's useful *because* everyone speaks it.

**"Become the system of record"** = *be the official place where the important information lives.* If Sabi is where every child's learning history is stored, you become the source of truth. Nobody wants to leave the place that holds all their records.

**Put together:** pick a hard, data-heavy problem (African-accent voice), become the place all that data lives, and make sure every single call quietly makes your product smarter. Do that and the moat gets deeper every day on its own.

## 🎯 How to actually do the 3 trickier habits

**How to do "80/20" (do the few things that matter):**
1. Write down everything on your plate.
2. Ask of each: *"If I only did this one thing this month, would the company clearly move forward?"*
3. Star the 2–3 that get a loud "yes." Right now those are: **pilot proof, one telco partnership, the server fix.**
4. Do the starred ones first, every day, before anything else. The rest waits — and most of it turns out not to matter.

**How to do delegation (get things off your plate):**
1. Look at your task list. For each task ask: *"Am I the only person who can do this?"*
2. If **no** → give it to someone (Sonia for pilot ops, a future hire for docs, etc.).
3. Only three things stay yours: (a) what the product should be, (b) the big relationships, (c) not running out of money.
4. When you hand something off, write down *what "done" looks like* so they don't have to guess.

**How to do time-blocking (protect real work):**
1. Each morning, look at your "Deep work" blocks.
2. Put ONE important task in each block — not five.
3. During the block: phone away, notifications off, one task only.
4. Everything that pops up ("quick thing!") gets written on a list and handled in your Admin window — not now.
5. Golden rule: **if it's not on the calendar, assume it won't happen.** So block it.
