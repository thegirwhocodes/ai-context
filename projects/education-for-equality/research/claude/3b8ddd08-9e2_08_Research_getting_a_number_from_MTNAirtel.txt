## 1. Toll-free 0800 numbers in Nigeria

### What an 0800 number is
An 0800 number is a cross-network, internationally recognized toll-free number that works across **all operators (MTN, Airtel, Glo, 9mobile)**. It's a **reverse-charge** service: the *organization* (Sabi) pays for all incoming calls so the *caller* (the child) pays nothing. This is exactly the model Sabi wants. The 11-digit toll-free line covers all networks; account holders get a monthly Call Detail Record (CDR) emailed.

### Two routes to obtain one
- **Directly from MTN EnterpriseBusiness / Airtel Business** — possible in principle, but in practice they push you to authorized VAS providers, and the law (below) bars them from hosting your service anyway.
- **Through an NCC-licensed VAS aggregator/provider** — this is the standard and faster route. You pick a provider, choose/customize a number, sign a Service Level Agreement, complete KYC, fund a deposit, and the number is assigned to your line.

## 2. NCC Value Added Service (VAS) licensing — the framework and the chain

### Official NCC license fees (5-year Individual Licences) — from ncc.gov.ng/licence-fees-pricing
| License type | Fee (₦) | Validity |
|---|---|---|
| **Special Numbering Services (SNS)** | **3,000,000** | 5 years |
| **Content Services using Short Code** | **500,000** | 5 years |
| **VAS Aggregator** | **10,000,000** | 5 years |
| Call Centre Services | 500,000 | 5 years |
| Call Directory Services | 500,000 | 5 years |
| Prepaid Calling Card | 1,000,000 | 5 years |

**Application mechanics (per NCC + Adeola Oyinlade legal guide):** Apply on **eservices.ncc.gov.ng** (Licensing Application Management System). Pay a **₦1,000 form fee** + a **non-refundable 5% administrative charge** of the license fee on submission (e.g. ₦500,000 for the Aggregator license, since 5% of ₦10M = ₦500k), then the **remaining 95% on approval.** Documents required: Certificate of Incorporation, Tax Clearance Certificate, certified Memorandum & Articles of Association, feasibility report, passport photos of directors/representative, technical staff certificates, Form CO7 (directors list), registered address + utility bill, project funding evidence, and budget/deployment cost estimates.

**Critical for a short-code service:** A **Content Service (Short Code) license requires** you to present either (a) an **Agreement/MOU with a licensed VAS Aggregator**, or (b) a CBN Mobile Money license or National Lottery license. You cannot get a short code without an aggregator relationship.

### The mandatory MNO → Aggregator → Content Provider → Developer chain
From the full **NCC VAS Aggregator Framework** (the governing document):

- **Network operators (MTN/Airtel)** provide only the final link to the subscriber. They are **explicitly NOT allowed to host or distribute VAS directly**, and are **not entitled to common short-code assignment.** They provide termination to aggregators and may optionally provide billing/revenue-collection on behalf of content providers.
- **Aggregators** are the *only* players allowed a **direct physical connection** to operator networks. They multiplex signals from many content providers to all operators. They may **not host content** or inspect data beyond routing. Aggregators must connect to operators via **at least two independent links.** **Operators and content providers are barred from owning aggregators.**
- **Content & Application Service Providers** (the current "VAS licensee" tier — what Sabi's service most resembles) host applications/content and have *logical* links to operators **but must physically connect through aggregators (at least two aggregators each).** They handle billing, user authentication, QoS, complaints.
- **Developers** are unlicensed creators — they only need "simple registration," not an NCC license.

**Revenue/billing rules that matter for "free to the child":**
- "**Transport cost**" (the airtime/data of carrying the call) belongs entirely to the operator and must normally be paid by the subscriber — **UNLESS** there is prior mutual agreement: *"The content and application provider can offer a VAS free of transport cost if there is a prior mutual agreement between it and the network operator,"* and *"The operator can also offer free SMS or data service to a subscriber for the purpose of accessing any VAS product."* **This clause is the legal basis for zero-rating** — it requires an explicit agreement with MTN/Airtel.
- Operators must keep separate prepaid accounts (Voice/SMS vs Data/VAS).
- Records of every VAS transaction kept ≥3 months; QoS requires content providers to offer a **24-hour toll-free customer helpline.**

**Useful escape hatch (Annex 2b):** *"Large VAS providers… that generate a preset level of traffic may be granted content service license that will enable them to connect directly to network service providers for the transfer of their own in-house traffic alone."* If Sabi scales, it could potentially connect directly rather than perpetually through an aggregator.

### Does Sabi *need* a VAS license?
- To merely **rent a toll-free 0800 line through an aggregator** (Section 1 route): **No own license needed** — you ride on the aggregator's/provider's license. This is the practical near-term path.
- To **own a dedicated short code or special number** and operate at scale as the named licensee: you'd need a **Content Services (Short Code) license (₦500k)** *plus* an aggregator MOU, or a **Special Numbering Service license (₦3M)**. The **₦10M Aggregator license is not what Sabi wants** — MNOs and content/short-code licensees are *ineligible* to hold it anyway.

- **Dedicated short code:** ~**₦250,000–₦400,000**; one provider quotes ₦250,000 (~$220) for 6 months, another ₦400,000; setup **2–4 months.** You own the number, unlimited keywords, can sublease.

### MTN EnterpriseBusiness / Airtel Business
Both run enterprise arms (MTN Business at mtn.ng/business with "Business Call Assist"; Airtel Business toll-free at airtel.com.ng/business/toll-free) but **neither publishes self-serve toll-free pricing** — they route enterprise toll-free/short-code requests through their VAS/aggregator partners and account managers. Expect to engage an account manager and sign a corporate SLA; the actual provisioning still happens via the aggregator layer.

## 5. Eligibility — does Sabi need a Nigerian (CAC) entity?

- **For renting a number through an aggregator:** every Nigerian provider's KYC requires a **CAC Certificate** (plus proof of address and signatory ID). A US nonprofit alone won't clear KYC — you'll need a Nigerian-registered entity, or to have the aggregator/local partner hold the line.
- **For an NCC VAS/short-code/SNS license:** requires **Certificate of Incorporation, Nigerian Tax Clearance, MemArt, registered Nigerian address + utility bill** — i.e. a Nigerian-registered company. The legal guide notes both foreign and local companies "can apply" but doesn't exempt foreigners from the Nigerian-incorporation documents; in practice you register locally.
- **NGO route:** A US nonprofit can register a Nigerian arm as **Incorporated Trustees under Part F of CAMA 2020** via CAC — minimum **2 trustees** (18+, sound mind), a detailed constitution, governance structure. **Foreign nationals can be trustees** but must show valid ID (passport) and evidence of Nigerian residency status. CAC is the regulator (cac.gov.ng); 2025/2026 guides confirm the process is open to foreign-founded NGOs.

**Net:** Sabi will need either a **Nigerian-registered entity (company or Incorporated Trustees)** to hold the number/contract, or it can operate **under the aggregator's license** in the very near term while the local entity is set up. For the Foundation zero-rating path, having a Nigerian CAC entity (and ideally government/UNICEF endorsement) materially strengthens the case.
