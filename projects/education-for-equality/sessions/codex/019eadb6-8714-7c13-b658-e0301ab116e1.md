## Codex · 2026-06-09T18:50:51.585Z · final_answer

Workspace plans are basically for **shared people + admin + privacy**, not magic shared memory.

The point is:

- **One shared billing/admin space**: you invite your partner, manage seats, permissions, billing, and access from one place.
- **Workspace-only GPTs**: you can publish custom GPTs so only people in your workspace can use them.
- **Shared GPT creation**: teammates can discover/use internal GPTs without making them public.
- **Better data controls**: Team/Business/Enterprise workspaces generally give stronger controls over whether chats are used for training, admin settings, SSO/compliance options depending on plan.
- **Centralized collaboration**: everyone is under the same org identity instead of random personal accounts and public links.

But a workspace plan does **not automatically mean**:
- your partner can see every chat you have,
- your custom GPT remembers your private conversations,
- Codex local files/secrets are shared,
- your project context magically follows every person.

Think of it like Google Workspace: it gives the org, permissions, shared docs/folders, and admin controls. But you still choose which docs/GPTs/repos to share.

## Codex · 2026-06-09T18:52:51.979Z · final_answer

Yes. To make a **workspace-only custom GPT**:

1. Go to [chatgpt.com/gpts](https://chatgpt.com/gpts).
2. Click **My GPTs**.
3. Open the GPT you want.
4. Click **Edit GPT**.
5. If it’s still a draft, click **Create** first.
6. Click **Share** in the editor.
7. Under sharing level, choose either:
   - **Your workspace**: everyone in your workspace can find/use it.
   - **Anyone at your workspace with the link**: only workspace members who have the link can access it.
   - **Invite-only**: share with specific users/groups if your workspace supports it.
8. Choose permission:
   - **Can chat**: they can use it only.
   - **Can view settings**: they can use, duplicate, and inspect config.
   - **Can edit**: they can help maintain it.
9. Click **Save**.
10. Copy the GPT link and send it to your partner.

If you don’t see **Your workspace**, it usually means one of these:
- you’re in your personal workspace, not the shared workspace;
- your workspace admin disabled GPT sharing;
- your role doesn’t have permission to create/share GPTs;
- the GPT uses an app/action that blocks that sharing option.

OpenAI’s help page confirms the sharing levels include **“Anyone at your workspace with the link”** and **“Your workspace”**, and that availability depends on plan, workspace settings, and permissions: [Sharing and publishing GPTs](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8798878-building-and-publishing-a-gpt).
