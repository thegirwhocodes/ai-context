## Shared Access Patterns

Much of mobile phone use in Africa is based on shared access, rather than ownership. Research shows this pattern is particularly pronounced among children. For example, in Malawi only 8% of children owned their own phone, yet 35% of children said they had used a phone in the week before the survey. Similarly, data from across regions shows varying ownership patterns: in Malawi, only 0.4% of females and 0.9% of males owned their own mobile, while 10.8% of females and 8.1% of males had access to a household member's mobile.

## Key Learning Access Barriers

**Cost and Affordability**: Costs of both devices and voice and data services may be a greater constraint on Internet use than network coverage. The African average for mobile data and voice costs is around 12% of gross national income per capita, ranging as high as 65% in Malawi.

**Limited Personal Device Ownership**: Just 17% of adults report owning a computer themselves, while another 11% say someone else in the household owns one.

**Parental Digital Skills Gap**: A major barrier to the development of shared practices is parents' potential lack of basic digital skills.

**General Accessibility Issues**: People often cite other challenges to owning or using a mobile phone, including the cost of mobile data, difficulty reading and typing, or relying on someone else's phone.
