## Educational Applications

UNICEF has used Interactive Voice Response as a complement to girls' empowerment programs, with over 6,000 adolescent girls from eight regions in Ethiopia participating in life skill training and information sessions delivered through pre-recorded voice messages on mobile phones. This demonstrated the significant contribution of IVR training in enhancing knowledge, shifting attitudes, and changing practices related to child marriage and FGM.

Mobile for Malis (MfM) Audio MOOCs were developed and delivered to open up access to learning materials and processes to the unreached, usually hindered by limitations such as data connectivity, distance from experts and limited literacy.

## Health and Literacy Programs

IVR systems have emerged as a widely used form of mHealth technology particularly effective among people with lower levels of education, and previous research has demonstrated the effectiveness of IVR systems in educating and engaging users with limited literacy skills, particularly during public health crises such as the Ebola epidemic and the COVID-19 pandemic.

In one malaria literacy program, results showed a 30.33% increase in knowledge, 21.77% in attitude, and 35.38% in practice among the local language group.

## Why IVR Works for Low-Literacy Populations

Speech user interfaces have the potential to accommodate different users irrespective of the level of education, and can provide interactions that meet the capabilities of low literacy users in underserved rural communities. By allowing low-literacy populations to interact with technology, IVR systems form an avenue to build technological skills in developing countries, and developing countries have a prevalence of mobile phones even in rural areas, which allows room for IVR technology to support social good projects.
