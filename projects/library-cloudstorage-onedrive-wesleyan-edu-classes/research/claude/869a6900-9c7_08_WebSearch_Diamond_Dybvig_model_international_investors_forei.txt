## Diamond-Dybvig Model Overview

The Diamond–Dybvig model views bank runs as a type of self-fulfilling prophecy: each depositor's incentive to withdraw funds depends on what they expect other depositors to do. At the heart of this phenomenon is the Diamond-Dybvig Model, a foundational framework that explains how banks' role in transforming illiquid assets into liquid liabilities makes them inherently vulnerable.

## International and Open Economy Extensions

The search results indicate that researchers have extended the Diamond-Dybvig framework to international settings. A model was developed in which financial crises in emerging markets may occur when domestic banks are internationally illiquid, with runs on domestic deposits that may interact with foreign creditor panics, depending on the maturity of the foreign debt and the possibility of international default.

Financial liberalization and increased inflows of foreign capital, especially if short term, can aggravate the illiquidity of banks and increase their vulnerability.

## Policy Solutions

Diamond and Dybvig argue that a better way of preventing bank runs is deposit insurance backed by the government or central bank, and sufficient deposit insurance can eliminate the possibility of bank runs.
