## Key Findings

Nigeria loses about $40 billion in potential revenue due to a less educated population, according to a report by SBM Intelligence. This $40 billion figure represents roughly 7.83% of Nigeria's GDP and is larger than the combined GDP of Mali, Chad, and Burkina Faso.

## Educational Challenges

SBM noted that the rate of regular school attendance and early childhood education remains poor in the country. About 20 million Nigerian children between the ages of 5 and 14 are classified as out-of-school, according to data from the United Nations Children's Fund.

The report identified that the country's highest out-of-school children rates are in the north owing to the late acceptance of formal education in the region. Only 61 percent of six to eleven-year-olds attend primary schools regularly in Nigeria, according to a 2021 National Bureau of Statistics survey.

## Economic Impact

The report noted that adults who did not receive foundational education cannot contribute to the country's productivity in the long term, resulting in Nigeria losing $40 billion in lost revenue productivity.
