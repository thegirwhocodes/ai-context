Based on the search results, here are the key findings about dual coding theory and the effectiveness of simultaneous vs. sequential audio-text presentation:

## Simultaneous Presentation is Optimal

For optimal learning results, the chances of overloading a learner's cognitive system should be minimized by displaying both verbal and visual materials simultaneously rather than successively. This principle applies broadly to multimedia learning using dual coding principles.

## Theory Remains Robust and Effective

Dual coding theory still stands today as one of the most robust ways of understanding the invisible processes that happen in our heads as we attempt to make sense of incoming stimuli. Despite the cases where associations between words and images cannot be formed—hence making the recall of items significantly more difficult—DCT is still valid over a wide range of cases and empirical studies indicate it can be used to improve memory.

## Research Evidence

Recent research supports dual coding effectiveness. A meta-analysis by Butcher (2006) found that combining text with relevant diagrams improved comprehension by 0.48 standard deviations compared to text alone, and Mayer (2009) demonstrated that multimedia instruction following dual coding principles improved transfer test performance by 89% over text-only conditions.

There is a substantial amount of research evidence that indicates that we encode visual and auditory stimuli differently and that when visual information is paired with and layered upon linguistic information retention is superior to when linguistic information is the sole source of input.

## Audio and Text Relationship

Verbal information is sequential in construction, while visual information was defined by Paivio as being synchronous, or simultaneous in structure—with diagrams allowing several elements to be viewed in one go, whereas words have to be processed one at a time.
