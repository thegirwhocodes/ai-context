The study "Exclusion from Education: The Economic Cost of Out-of-School Children in 20 Countries" extends a 2013 background study by Results for Development Institute (R4D), updating economic cost estimates with the latest UNESCO data and expanding the analysis to 20 low- and middle-income countries.

**Key Findings:**

A typical country in the sample is expected to forfeit earnings valued between 1% and 2% of GDP due to its out-of-school population, and accounting for indirect, non-earnings losses, that average cost rises to between 5% and 6% of GDP.

For all countries in the sample, the estimated economic gain from achieving universal primary education exceeds the estimated increase in public spending required to enroll those out-of-school children in primary school.

The research was published through Results for Development Institute and Educate A Child, with the updated version released in 2014.
