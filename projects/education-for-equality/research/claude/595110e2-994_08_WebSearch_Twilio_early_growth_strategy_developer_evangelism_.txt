## Early Growth Strategy & Developer Evangelism

Twilio is known for using platform evangelism to acquire customers. The first developer evangelism hires in SaaS were at Twilio.

Twilio's Developer Evangelists work to raise awareness of Twilio and its products within the greater developer community, leading top-of-funnel activities such as writing technical blog posts with code, being visible at tech events including conferences and hackathons, and supporting local and online developer communities.

## Revenue Scaling & Market Expansion

The product-led growth model eliminated the need for expensive sales infrastructure by letting developers self-serve and experience immediate value, allowing Twilio to grow capital-efficiently during its formative years.

Early adoption by high-growth startups like Uber created a virtuous cycle where Twilio's success became directly tied to its customers' scaling, generating organic word-of-mouth validation within developer communities.

Twilio went from 900k developers in 2016 to 2 million developers in 2017 to 10 million in 2020. In 2010, Twilio employed 16 people; in 2020, it employs nearly 3,000.

## Go-to-Market Strategy

Twilio focused on developers through evangelism and using them as entry points within customer accounts since they influence buying decisions. Twilio exemplifies the land-and-expand model, whereby companies sell to developers and then upsell to the enterprise.
