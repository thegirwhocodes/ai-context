## The Crisis Trigger

In March 2023, Circle, the issuer of USDC, announced that it was unable to access a portion of its dollar reserves held as deposits at Silicon Valley Bank (SVB). Specifically, Circle was unable to withdraw $3.3 billion of USDC reserves from SVB (around 8% of total reserves).

## The Run and Flight to Safety

This announcement precipitated a surge in redemption requests by holders of USDC, causing the stablecoin to lose its peg against the dollar. The run on USDC started on March 9, when investors doubted Circle's ability to access its deposits at SVB, causing the price of USDC to trade as low as $0.87 per coin.

## The Safe Haven Shift

During the depeg, investors demonstrated a preference for transferring their holdings from USDC to Tether (USDT) or fiat currency. This represents the flight to safety behavior you mentioned, with investors moving away from the compromised USDC to USDT (perceived as more stable) or exiting to fiat entirely.

## Resolution

USDC's price on the secondary market recovered sharply after the Federal Reserve's backstop announcement on Sunday, and fully recovered once Circle began processing redemptions on Monday, March 13.
