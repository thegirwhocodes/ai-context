## Synthetic Control Method Applications

The synthetic control approach has been used to examine oil discovery and production impacts, finding that oil discovery and production have a positive impact on economic growth but a negative impact on the reduction of unemployment through a crowding out effect.

One study comparing Ghana (which discovered oil in 2007) to 40 other sub-Saharan African countries without such discoveries over the period 1998–2018 employed this methodology. The synthetic control method compares actual trends of development indicators with a counterfactual created by taking a weighted average of trends of other regions—a 'synthetic' comparison unit.

## Resource Curse Evidence in Africa

Discoveries of oil in countries like Angola and Nigeria have produced little to no effect and even sometimes negative effects on economic growth. In actual real per capita growth terms, non-renewable resource producers did no better during the commodity boom than economies lacking non-renewable resources, despite vastly more favorable external terms of trade.

The synthetic control method has been noted as among the first quasi-experimental approaches to study the resource curse in a cross-country setting, allowing for causal analysis for individual countries.
