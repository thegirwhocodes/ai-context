## Key Failure Patterns

The primary killers are commoditization by foundation model providers, GPU burn rates exceeding $1M per month before reaching sustainable revenue, and the absence of a data moat.

### Commoditization and Margin Compression

Inference cost per million tokens dropped 80% from 2023 to 2025 — fatal for startups whose only moat was a margin between API cost and customer price. As foundation models become more similar in capability and pricing continues to fall, margins compress to nothing.

Lower prices mean lower margins, which means less capital for product development, which means faster commoditization.

### The "Wrapper Problem"

Any product that is fundamentally an API wrapper around a foundation model has zero defensibility. A wrapper around an LLM is a thin interface over someone else's intelligence. When the underlying model improves, your product advantage shrinks. When a competitor copies your UX, your edge disappears. When the model provider ships your core feature natively, your differentiation collapses overnight.

### Platform Risk and Lock-in

Most wrapper companies don't own proprietary data, embedded workflows or deep integrations. A customer can switch to a competitor, or directly to ChatGPT, in minutes. There's no moat, no lock-in, no defensibility.

## Historical Parallels: The API Era

The lessons from the earlier API era are striking. AWS API Gateway, launched in 2015, directly competed with companies like Apigee and Mashery by offering lower pricing, tighter integration, and better distribution. Kong, Postman, Swagger UI, Insomnia, and ReDoc provided free or community-driven alternatives that made it harder for proprietary API platforms to charge for basic functionalities.

Vertical APIs won by owning hard problems; horizontal APIs mostly got commoditized. Vertical API outcomes were orders of magnitude larger, with Stripe alone reaching a $95B valuation. These companies captured value by owning messy, domain-specific workflows. Horizontal platforms, by contrast, built interchangeable plumbing and saw much smaller exits.

## What Actually Creates a Moat

Five real moats exist in the AI era: data flywheel, workflow integration, distribution, brand and trust, and network effects. The survivor playbook is consistent: pick a workflow in a regulated or data-intensive vertical, become the system of record for it, and let every user interaction feed back into improving the model.
