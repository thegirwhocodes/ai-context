Here's the actual math, using Sabi's own documented numbers plus the Gemini pricing I looked up.

## Per-call cost, old vs. new

**Self-hosted (GPU) lane** — telephony + Claude Haiku + local Whisper/Chatterbox (marginal cost ≈ $0):
- Africa's Talking: $0.00188/min × ~6.5 min call ≈ **$0.012**
- Claude Haiku: ~**$0.0045**/call
- STT/TTS (self-hosted): **$0** marginal
- **Total: ~$0.02/call**, dominated entirely by telephony

**Gemini Live lane** — API-metered audio in+out:
- Gemini audio: ~$0.075/min in + $0.30/min out ≈ $0.375/min × ~6.5 min ≈ **$2.44**
- Telephony: AT ≈ $0.012, but if still on Twilio it's $0.2263/min × 6.5 ≈ **$1.47** (this is why the memory shows Sabi switched back to AT — Twilio was flagged as "driven by cost")
- **Total: ~$2.45–3.90/call**

That's roughly a **120–200x jump in marginal cost per call**, going from self-hosted to Gemini Live.

## Where the fixed cost flips the winner

GPU box = **$200/mo flat**, regardless of volume, up to its concurrency ceiling (VRAM is currently only ~8.4GB/20GB used, so there's real headroom before you'd need a second box).

Break-even: $200 ÷ ~$2.45 marginal Gemini cost ≈ **~80 calls/month**.

- **Below ~80 calls/month** (early pilot, canary phase): Gemini Live is actually cheaper — you're not paying for idle GPU capacity.
- **Above ~80 calls/month**: the GPU has already paid for itself, and every call past that point is ~$2.45 cheaper self-hosted. The gap only widens with volume, since GPU marginal cost stays ~$0.

## At real scale, this isn't close

Sabi's own cost model (in [project_e4e_tech.md](../.claude/projects/-Users-naomiivie-Education-for-Equality/memory/project_e4e_tech.md)) targets **$2,000–4,000/month in compute at 1M children**. That target is *only reachable self-hosted*. Run 1M children through Gemini Live at ~$2.45/call even once a month each, and you're at **~$2.45M/month** — roughly 1,000x the self-hosted target. Gemini Live at scale isn't a cost optimization, it's a different cost category entirely.

## What this means practically

- **Gemini Live only pencils out as a pilot/canary tool** — which matches how it's actually being used right now (one allowlisted number), not as "the production lane for everyone." The earlier framing ("Gemini is meant for everyone") is a product/quality goal, but the cost math says that's not viable without either (a) staying self-hosted at scale, or (b) subsidy.
- **This is exactly what the Google/Microsoft startup credits are for** — $200–350K in Google AI credits or Azure credits could cover a lot of Gemini Live minutes during pilot, but credits run out; they don't change the underlying unit economics for 1M children.
- **The realistic long-term shape**: self-hosted GPU (Whisper/Chatterbox/Ollama) stays the cost-controlled backbone for scale, Gemini Live stays a premium/quality layer for a subset of calls (or gets revisited if Google's pricing drops materially) — not a full GPU replacement.

**Does 15 testers exceed free tier?** The constraint that actually matters isn't total calls/day, it's how many are **mid-call at the same instant** — Live API sessions are persistent connections, not quick request/response calls. One third-party source states the free tier cap is **3 concurrent sessions**; I could not independently confirm that exact number from Google's own docs pages (the rate-limits page is JS-rendered and didn't return the table content to my fetch), so treat it as directionally right, not certain. Practically: 15 testers calling at their own convenience through the day probably won't often collide at 4+ simultaneous, but it's not safe to assume — an evening rush (several testers all trying it after dinner) could absolutely trip it, and a dropped/rejected call is a bad experience to hand testers. **You should verify your actual limit at `aistudio.google.com/rate-limit`** — that's tied to your real key/project, not a generic number.

**What would it cost on paid tier?** This is the number that actually matters more, and it's not small:

- ~6.5 min/call × (~$0.075/min audio-in + ~$0.30/min audio-out) ≈ **~$2.44/call**
- 15 testers × "a few times a day" (say 2–3/day) ≈ **35–45 calls/day**
- **≈ $85–110/day → roughly $2,600–3,300/month**, just for Gemini Live audio — before telephony

**Bottom line:** for a 15-tester pilot specifically, Gemini Live on paid tier is expensive relative to self-hosting, and staying on free tier risks both dropped calls at peak overlap *and* has your testers' (children's, potentially) voice data used to improve Google's products — which is a real reason to not just "hope it stays under quota" for a real pilot.
