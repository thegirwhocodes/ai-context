## Essential Components for Independent Contractor Structure

It is critically important that each consultant sign an agreement confirming that it will protect any confidential information received as part of its engagement, both during and after the engagement, and that any work and inventions produced while working for the company related to the company's business belong to the company and not to the contractor.

According to Cooley's guidance, at minimum, a standard contractor agreement should include terms covering confidentiality obligations in contractor agreements to prevent contractors from using or disclosing company confidential information, and IP assignment provisions so that companies own the intellectual property rights in work product created by contractors.

## Independent Contractor Classification

To minimize misclassification risk, the contractor agreement should include provisions demonstrating the contractor has the right to control how work is performed, including the right to set working hours, decide where work will be performed, control the manner of work, and provide for payment based on milestones, commissions, or by the job rather than hourly or weekly.

## Additional Provisions

The simplified form does not include other types of provisions that may need to be added, including an acceptance procedure, a method for handling change proposals, a prohibition against reverse engineering, a provision requiring the consultant to maintain insurance in specified amounts, and/or provisions related to government contracts.
