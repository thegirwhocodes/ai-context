## Technical Architecture Pipeline

VSL Labs has built a modular pipeline that processes translation requests through several stages: an API layer that accepts text or audio input, a text preprocessing module that handles long texts and performs cultural adaptation, a translation module that uses T5 or GPT-4 to translate English into glosses plus stage directions, a gloss-to-database mapping module that maps generated glosses to their sign database containing actual motion data, post-processing and synthesis that adds behaviors for emotions and special cases, and a 3D avatar rendering stage that produces a 3D animated avatar performing signs with appropriate facial expressions, body movements, and timing.

## NLP and Language Processing

The first step in VSL's process is to translate written or spoken text into structured sign language through natural language processing (NLP), where the system interprets the meaning of the text and glosses it—converts it into a format that reflects the grammar of sign language. This includes linguistic simplification when appropriate for the target audience—an acknowledgment that different users (children vs. adults, different cognitive abilities) may require different translation approaches.

## AI Models

The system uses either T5 (described as their in-house model) or GPT-4 to translate from the source language (English) into a sequence of glosses (written representations of signs) plus what the presenter calls "stage directions"—instructions for how the avatar should perform each sign.

## 3D Avatar Rendering

The final step is to render the signs using 3D animation. VSL uses Unity, a game engine, to create real-time animated avatars. These avatars are designed to be lifelike, moving fluidly and mimicking human body language rather than looking stiff or robotic.

## Overall Technology Stack

VSL Labs utilizes Generative AI, Natural Language Processing, and Computer Vision to offer real-time, contextually accurate, and visually engaging sign language translation.
