## Phone Sharing and Access in Nigerian Households

In low-income Nigerian households, 8.5% of individuals had access to phones that belonged mostly to relatives who lived in the same house with them for an average of 80 minutes per day. More than three-quarters of respondents reported their phones as personally owned and not shared with others.

## Who Answers Calls - Child Domestic Workers

An important aspect of phone access in low-income Nigerian families involves child domestic workers. While employers sometimes say "If anything happens, they have my number. They will call me," most of the girls do not own phones, and communication is often only possible through their employers or the random goodwill of others, which makes it harder for them to reach out in desperate situations.

## Regional Context on Phone Sharing

While specific data on who answers calls in low-income Nigerian family households is limited in these results, broader African research shows that people without their own phones may share one with someone else; when Kenyans were asked, 58% of non-cell phone owners said they shared one with someone else, and 21% of Kenyan mobile phone owners said they shared their phones with others.
