# Selection Guidance for MIT Solve Applicants

## What It Takes to Be Selected

Solutions must demonstrate strong alignment with Solve's mission. The organization evaluates based on: "Alignment," "Potential for Impact," "Feasibility," "Innovative Approach," "People-First Design," and later-stage criteria including "Scalability," "Partnership Potential," and "Technical Feasibility."

Teams should prioritize community-centered development. As the guidance states, solutions must show "the experience, relationships, data, and knowledge to develop and deliver a successful solution."

## Solution Stage Requirements

Applicants can submit at multiple development phases:

- **Concept:** Ideas with prototypes under development (Indigenous Communities Fellowship only)
- **Prototype:** Initial working versions with minimal user testing
- **Pilot:** Launched in at least one community, still iterating
- **Growth:** Established and available across communities
- **Scale:** Operating in multiple countries, prepared for significant expansion

## Selection Process & Timeline

The evaluation occurs in stages: initial staff screening, expert reviewer scoring (top 50 advance), Challenge Leadership Group judging (15 finalists selected), then virtual pitches followed by final selection. Winners are notified in July.

## Application Timeline

Most applicants report "several hours" completing applications. Submissions open September through November annually.

## Typical Winners

Solve's portfolio shows 50% women-led teams, representation across 68 countries, with 41% at growth/scale stages and 33% at pilot stage.
