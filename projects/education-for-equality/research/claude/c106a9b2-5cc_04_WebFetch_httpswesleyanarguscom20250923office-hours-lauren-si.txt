# Lauren Silber on Writing Pedagogy and Affect Theory

## Writing Pedagogy & Student Voice

Silber emphasizes that students often arrive believing they need to improve their writing, but leave realizing "they're already a writer, and they have their own voice." She challenges students to examine intentionality: "why is paragraph three not paragraph seven?" This practical question prompts reflection on structural choices rather than default execution.

## Peer Tutoring Philosophy

The Writers Room operates as transformative space because it remains non-evaluative. Silber describes it as "this third space on campus" where students can "be their honest selves" with peers who "meet you there and help you with the next step."

## Core Writing Values

Silber prioritizes narrative awareness over product. She regularly asks students reflective questions to determine whether they're making conscious writing choices or simply reproducing what they believe earns grades. She describes this as understanding "why their writing is coming out the way it is."

## Affect Theory Connection

Teaching affect theory reveals that "emotions are not just inside of us" but structurally shape our world. She moves classroom discussion from individual feelings toward "power and structure and discourse and rhetoric."

## On "Failure"

The interview title references failure, though Silber frames writing challenges as opportunities for questioning and discovering voice—reframing struggle as part of meaningful writing development rather than deficiency.
