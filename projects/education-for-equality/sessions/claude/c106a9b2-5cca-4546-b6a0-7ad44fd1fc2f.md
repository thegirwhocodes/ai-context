## Hybrid "Toll-Free Hook → AT Callback" — Possible, but Not Seamless

What you're describing is a **cost-arbitrage hybrid**: child dials 0800 (free), Sabi greets them on Alpha (₦14/min), then ~30–60s in says *"Stay on, I'll call you back"*, hangs up the toll-free leg, and dials them back via AT SIP outbound at ₦3/min. Costs drop ~78% per call.

Technically possible. Operationally messy. Here's the honest breakdown.

### What "calling back" actually means at the protocol level

A phone call isn't a single object — it's a session between two endpoints negotiated through your server. To switch carriers mid-conversation:

1. Sabi (on Alpha SIP) plays the "hold on" message
2. Server hangs up the Alpha leg → child's phone disconnects, screen returns to home
3. Server initiates a new outbound INVITE through AT SIP to the child's MSISDN
4. AT routes through MTN/Airtel/Glo to the child's handset → handset rings
5. Child sees "Unknown number" or AT's caller ID, presses answer
6. New SIP session established → Sabi greets and resumes from saved state

That's **steps 2–6 = 8 to 25 seconds of dead air**, plus a manual answer step.

### The user-experience problems

| Issue | Severity | Why it matters for kids |
|---|---|---|
| **Dead-air gap (8–25s)** | High | Kids assume the call ended and walk away. Especially under-7s. |
| **Manual re-answer required** | High | Most feature phones don't auto-answer. The child has to physically press the green button again on a number they don't recognize. |
| **Caller-ID changes mid-session** | Medium | Child sees Sabi's 0800, then an unfamiliar AT local number. Some parents may have taught kids "don't pick unknown numbers." Trust friction. |
| **Phone in shared-use households** | High | If the child shared the phone (common — only 14% of children own one personally per UNICEF), the parent or sibling may pick up the callback instead. |
| **Network-busy during callback** | Medium | If MTN/Airtel routing has a hiccup, the callback fails entirely. Toll-free SIP doesn't have this problem because it's routed inside one carrier's network. |
| **Confusion ("did Sabi hang up on me?")** | Medium | Even with a clear "I'll call you back" message, comprehension at age 8 is uneven, especially for a child who just started literacy lessons. |
| **Engagement drop on re-pickup** | Medium | The mental momentum of a lesson is broken. Kids returning after the gap have lower task focus per cognitive-load research. |

### Carrier/STT/LLM lag — not the bottleneck, but adds up

The voice pipeline itself adds latency on top of the carrier handoff:

- New SIP session warm-up: ~200–500ms
- AT call routing through MNO: 2–8s (this is the slow part — Nigerian last-mile carrier setup)
- Whisper STT first-token: ~400–800ms
- Claude Haiku: ~600–1,200ms
- Chatterbox TTS: ~1.5–3s

Once the callback connects, normal turn latency is ~2–4s — fine. The problem is the **carrier-to-carrier transition**, not the AI stack.

### When this hybrid actually works

A few designs make it tolerable:

**Option A: Front-load the 0800 leg as a "registration call."**
First call ever: child dials 0800, Sabi captures their phone number via DTMF or callerID, says "Sabi will call you every day at 4pm" — and hangs up. From day 2 onward, **all calls are AT outbound** at ₦3/min. Toll-free is only the on-ramp. This is the cleanest model.

**Option B: USSD-triggered callback (no toll-free at all).**
Child dials a USSD code (free to send) → server queues a callback → AT calls the child within seconds. Same UX as toll-free for the child (free, single tap), but no Alpha bill, no carrier handoff. Issue: USSD push during voice calls is unreliable in Nigeria, and this isn't relevant if you've already committed to 0800.

**Option C: Use the 0800 leg for the full call.**
Don't switch carriers mid-call. Pay the ₦14/min and live with the cost. UX is seamless. This is what the budget assumes.

### What I'd recommend

**Don't try to do mid-call carrier swap.** The 8–25s gap + manual re-answer + caller-ID change makes it brittle, especially for the literal use case (a child whose parents may not own a phone, who just started reading lessons). You'll lose 20–40% of calls at the handoff and never know if it was telephony, comprehension, or distraction.

**Better hybrid:** Use Option A — toll-free 0800 is the **first-touch / registration / re-engagement** channel (kid hears about Sabi at school, dials 0800 free), and AT SIP outbound at ₦3/min is the **lesson channel** (Sabi calls them at agreed-upon times). Best of both worlds:

- Acquisition cost (toll-free 0800 inbound, ~30s registration call): ~₦7 per new user
- Daily lesson cost (AT outbound at ₦3/min × 7 min): ~₦21 per call
- 300 kids × 240 lessons × ₦21 = ~₦1,512,000 ($945) **vs. $4,741 if all calls go through toll-free**

That's **80% cheaper than full toll-free** and still ₦0 to the child — and avoids the mid-call handoff entirely.

## Within-Subject Design (10 kids × multiple approaches) — Better Than the Split

You're right to push back. The split (5 kids × 2 arms × 10 days) has problems:

1. **n=5 per arm is too small** — confidence intervals are huge, you'd need >30% absolute differences to detect anything.
2. **Inter-child variability swamps the signal** — kid #3 is more attentive than kid #7; when only 5 kids are in each arm, the more-attentive-kid effect overwhelms the actual carrier-switch effect.
3. **You learn less** — each kid only experiences one model, so you can't ask "did the *child* find the callback annoying?" because they don't have the contrast.

A **within-subject design** (every kid tries every model) fixes all three.

## Proposed schedule — 10 kids × 14 days × 4 conditions

| Day | Kids 1-10 do | What you're measuring |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | All on Alpha 0800 (full call) — **baseline** | First-touch acquisition, no friction |
| 2 | All on Alpha 0800 (full call) | Day-2 return rate after baseline experience |
| 3 | **A1: Drop & AT callback** (no number saved) | Cold callback friction |
| 4 | **A1: Drop & AT callback** (no number saved) | Replication |
| 5 | Sabi prompts all kids: *"Save this number as Sabi"* | Onboarding to AT number |
| 6 | **A2: Drop & AT callback** (with number saved) | Hot callback friction (post-save) |
| 7 | **A2: Drop & AT callback** (with number saved) | Replication |
| 8 | **B: Scheduled AT outbound only** (no 0800 today) | Scheduled-callback model |
| 9 | **B: Scheduled AT outbound only** | Replication |
| 10 | **C: 0800 acquisition only Day 1, AT outbound rest** | The hybrid I recommended last round |
| 11 | **C: continued AT outbound** | Replication |
| 12 | Free choice — child picks how they want to connect | **Revealed preference** — what do they actually do? |
| 13 | Free choice | Replication |
| 14 | Exit interview + post-test | Qualitative + literacy gain |

This gives you **3–4 calls per condition per kid** = 30–40 data points per condition instead of 5. Statistical power goes up ~6x for the same total budget.

## What you can actually measure with this design

| Metric | How |
|---|---|
| **Reconnect rate** | % of "I'll call you back" events where child picks up within 30s |
| **Time-to-reconnect** | Median seconds from hangup to RTP-flowing again |
| **Day-2 return rate** | Of kids who completed day 1 in condition X, % who answered day 2 |
| **Adult-intercept rate** | % of callbacks answered by parent/sibling |
| **Caller-ID save effect** | Reconnect rate before vs after Sabi prompts "save this number" (within-kid) |
| **Lesson completion** | % of started lessons that hit the natural endpoint |
| **Child preference** | Day 12-13 free-choice behavior — which model do they pick? |
| **Engagement carryover** | Does the carrier-switch friction reduce *next-day* engagement? |

The within-subject contrast on caller-ID save (day 4 vs day 6, same kid, same approach minus the save step) is gold — that single comparison validates or kills the "save the AT number" mitigation.

## What I'd watch out for

- **Order effects.** Kids who do Alpha-only first may be biased to expect a single number. Counterbalance: run two cohorts of 5, one starts on Alpha, one starts on AT outbound. Then compare.
- **Day-of-week effects.** Don't put all the "novel" conditions on weekdays and "familiar" ones on weekends — kids are home/distracted differently. Spread conditions across both.
- **Spillover between conditions.** Once a child has saved Sabi's AT number on day 5, they can't unsave it for day 8's test. The save effect is permanent, so the order matters: cold conditions FIRST, save event in the middle, warm conditions AFTER.
- **Practice effects on lesson content.** If you reuse the same lesson, kids get better at it independent of the callback model. Use different lesson modules for each condition, or measure on a fixed diagnostic instead of progress.
- **Don't ask the child "which one did you prefer?"** at the end — kids often say "the most recent" or "the one with the friendliest tone." Use **revealed preference** (free choice on days 12-13) over **stated preference**.

## One operational caveat

You **can't run the carrier-switch conditions until Alpha toll-free is activated.** Per Alpha's email, that's 24-hour activation after deposit + SIP fee paid. So either:
- (a) Pay the $568 setup before the focus group starts (turns it into commitment, not a test) — but at least you've already decided to use toll-free for the 300-kid pilot, so the setup cost is amortized either way.
- (b) Limit the focus group to AT-only conditions (drop Alpha 0800 baseline) — cheaper, but you lose the ability to compare against the "all toll-free" reference. Not recommended.

(a) is the right move if your plan is "300-kid pilot uses toll-free regardless." The focus group informs *how* you use toll-free, not whether to commit to it.

**10 kids × 14 days × 4 conditions in a within-subject design** is far stronger than 5 kids per arm. You get:
- 6x more statistical power per dollar
- The critical "before save" vs "after save" within-kid contrast
- Revealed preference (free-choice days)
- Same telephony cost (~$5)
