## MIT OpenCourseWare (OCW)

MIT OpenCourseWare (OCW) is a free, publicly accessible, openly-licensed digital collection of high-quality teaching and learning materials, presented in an easily accessible format. Users can browse through, download, and use materials from more than 2,500 MIT on-campus courses and supplemental resources, all available under a Creative Commons license for open sharing.

The project was announced on April 4, 2001, and uses the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike license. The program was originally funded by the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation, the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation, and MIT.

The vast majority of MIT OCW materials (unless marked to the contrary) are published under a Creative Commons license (CC BY-NC-SA). Community-led language translations of MIT OpenCourseWare materials serve as a high-impact example of the power of OpenCourseWare's Creative Commons licensing, which grants everyone the right to revise materials to suit their particular needs and redistribute those revisions to the world.

MIT OpenCourseWare is a free and open collection of material from thousands of MIT courses, covering the entire MIT curriculum. Knowledge is your reward. Use OCW to guide your own life-long learning, or to teach others. MIT does not offer credit or certification to users of OCW – and asks for nothing in return.

## OpenStax

OpenStax provides free, peer-reviewed, openly licensed textbooks that are available in digital formats. These textbooks cover a wide range of subjects, from mathematics to social sciences, and are licensed under the Creative Commons BY license.

OpenStax primarily uses the CC BY license, with some exceptions using CC BY-NC-SA. For educators seeking comprehensive, peer-reviewed textbooks for introductory college courses, OpenStax stands out as a leading provider with a clear licensing framework and supplementary materials.

OpenStax CNX was one of the first Open Educational Resources (OER) initiatives along with projects such as MIT OpenCourseWare and the Public Library of Science.
