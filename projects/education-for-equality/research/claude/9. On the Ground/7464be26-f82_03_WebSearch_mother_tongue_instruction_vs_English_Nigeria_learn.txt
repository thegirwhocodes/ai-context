## Research Evidence on Mother Tongue Instruction vs. English in Nigeria

The research on language of instruction in Nigeria presents **conflicting findings**, with significant variations depending on context and methodology.

### Key Research Findings

**Evidence Favoring Mother Tongue Instruction:**

Students taught in their mother tongue demonstrated better comprehension, engagement, and critical thinking skills compared to their English-only counterparts. Additionally, students were more motivated and learned more effectively when taught in their mother tongue.

Students who received initial literacy in their mother tongue performed significantly better in the English language. Learning first in mother tongue (in grades 1−4) improves mathematics test scores later (in grade 5) by 0.159 standard deviations.

**Evidence Favoring English Instruction:**

A recent (August 2024) study presents contrary findings: Students taught in English outperformed their peers taught in indigenous languages. This departure from the norm underscores the need to reassess generalized conclusions derived from less linguistically diverse contexts, as they might not apply in settings like Nigeria.

### Implementation Challenges

Implementing mother-tongue instruction requires textbooks, teaching materials, and teachers trained to deliver instruction in indigenous languages – resources that remain scarce. The policy provides little guidance on how to define the "language of the immediate environment" in multilingual contexts, and in urban areas shaped by migration, children's mother tongues often differ from the dominant community language.

### Context Matters

The conflicting evidence suggests that outcomes depend heavily on context, implementation quality, and whether there is proper infrastructure and teacher training to support mother tongue instruction.
