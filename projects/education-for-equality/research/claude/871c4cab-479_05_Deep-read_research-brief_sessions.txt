### The Full Economic Case for OOSC in Nigeria

**The paper's three escalating claims:**
1. Milan & Burnett's **$40B figure overstates** the OOSC cost by ~2.5× because it uses global wage premiums (30% primary, from Psacharopoulos & Patrinos) rather than Nigeria-specific data. Using Aromolaran's Nigeria-specific returns (2.5%/yr primary), the OOSC cost is approximately **$16–17B (~5.88% of GDP)**.
2. The **in-school non-learning population** (70% of 33M enrolled = ~23M children who attend but cannot read) imposes an **additional $6–11B** in foregone productivity — a cost the existing literature ignores because it only counts never-enrolled children.
3. Adding both = **total education crisis cost of $23–28B**, internally consistent with the World Bank HCI+ 2026 bottom-up education pillar gap (~45 HCI+ points × ~1%/point earnings × $128B labor income base = ~$58B macro upper bound including externalities).

**Literature/methodology details (verbatim figures):**
- **Milan & Burnett (2014)** micro approach: GDP loss = [% non-completing OOSC] × [wage premium to primary] + probability-weighted secondary cascade. Macro approach: Barro & Lee (2010) cross-country growth model. For Nigeria: micro = **7.83% GDP**, macro = **13.03% GDP**. Applied to Nigeria by **CSEA/IDRC** using MICS 2013 data, **10.5M OOSC, GDP $510B → $40B**. Authors' own limitation (page 24–25): "countries with significant OOSC are the ones for which wage premia are least likely to be available."
- **Aromolaran (2004/2006)** returns to education — GHS 1996–99, N=93,999 total, 9,065 male wage earners:
  - Primary: 2.5% men / 2.4% women (wage workers); 3.2%/1.9% (self-employed)
  - Secondary: 3.9%/4.4% (wage); 3.7%/3.8% (self-employed)
  - Post-secondary: 10.4%/12.2% (wage); 13.7%/15.4% (self-employed)
- Historical: **1966 Psacharopoulos found 30% primary returns in Western Nigeria** — a ~90% collapse by the 1990s. Cause: UPE 1976–81 massively expanded primary graduate supply; school quality collapsed.
- **Oyelere (2007, IZA DP 3097)**: IV estimate **2.8%/yr** using UPE timing as instrument — statistically indistinguishable from Aromolaran's OLS.
- **In-school non-learning gap:** World Bank HCI (2020) 10.2 expected years vs ~5 learning-adjusted years → 5.2 years produce zero learning. HCI+ (2026): education pillar 64/188, harmonized test score 318/625 (barely above 300 floor). Learning Poverty (2022): **70%** of Nigerian 10-year-olds cannot read a simple sentence (72.6% cited elsewhere). **NALABE 2017: 41.5% English reading at Primary 5; everyday math (35.2%) weakest at every grade**; knowledge > comprehension > higher-order (memorization without understanding). **EKOEXCEL 2019–20 baseline: 28% of P2–5 couldn't read a single word; 40% couldn't solve 28−17; average reading comprehension 10%.**
- **HCI+ cross-validation:** each pillar point ≈ 1% future earnings; Nigeria education pillar gap vs LMI top performer = 45 points → ~45% higher earnings possible. Labor income base (~45% of $285B GDP) = ~$128B → 45% × $128B ≈ **$58B upper-bound education cost**.

**Methodology conversions:** Aromolaran 2.5%/yr → total primary premium = (1.025)^6 − 1 ≈ **16% compound** or 2.5%×6 = **15% simple Mincer** (use 15% conservative). Milan & Burnett used 30% (~2× Nigeria's rate). Non-learner wage gap: full premium if educated 8%/yr×6=48% minus actual 2.5%/yr×6=15% = **33 percentage point gap** per non-learning child. Exposed population: 33.3M × 70% = **23.3M children**.

**Replication table (Milan & Burnett vs This Paper):** % non-completing OOSC 23.7% → ~28%; primary premium 30% → 15%; GDP $510B(2013) → $285B(2025); OOSC micro cost 7.83%=$40B → ~4.2%=~$12B; secondary premium 14%→20%; transition 43%→60%; secondary completion 50%→73%; secondary cascade 0.71%→~2.45%; total micro 7.83% → ~6.65%=~$19B. **Calc B:** non-learner cost ~$13–15B. **Calc C cross-validation:** bottom-up $19B+$13–15B = **$32–34B**; HCI+ top-down $58B; ratio 58%.

**Data gaps identified:** updated OOSC typology (MICS 2021; was 74% never-enter/5% dropped/21% late-entrants in 2013); Milan & Burnett Tables 1–4 exact Nigeria rows (request via r4d.org/interlibrary loan); **NLSS 2018–19 microdata at microdata.worldbank.org Catalog #3827** (biggest potential upgrade — first Nigeria wage returns in 20 years); GDP nominal vs PPP; Psacharopoulos & Patrinos 11.7% SSA average vs 30% completion-level premium clarification.

Key data: **UNICEF 2024 = 18.3M OOSC** (note: other sessions use 20M); school-age ~65–70M; GDP 2025 ~$285B; UBEC enrolled 33.3M.

### Feature-phone AI delivery research (extensive)

**Sabi's biggest holes (voice-only):** #1 **no visual component (fatal for literacy** — can't teach letter recognition by voice); no permanent record; no writing/tactile; assumes English; expensive at scale; Whisper 27% WER on Nigerian English (worse for kids); no interactivity beyond conversation; phone dependency. **Voice works much better for math than literacy** (math is naturally oral/mental; literacy requires seeing symbols). Voice math excellent for: counting, number recognition, addition, subtraction, multiplication tables (chanting), word problems, skip counting. Struggles: place value, multi-digit borrowing, geometry, fractions, graphs.

**Voice + SMS dual-channel innovation:** Voice teaches the sound, SMS shows the letter shape, keypress for quizzes, AI adapts, SMS inbox = growing textbook. **Nobody has built this exact combination.** Closest: **Allô Alphabet** (Côte d'Ivoire — IVR phonics + SMS letters, 60-child pilot, RCT underway); **M-Shule** (Kenya, AI SMS, 7% exam-score gain, 11,000+ learners, 80 schools); **Eneza** (6M learners); **Shupavu291** (1.3M students); **Rising On Air** (10M+); **IVR India** (5,000 kids/400 villages).

**Voice+SMS holes (14 found):** Critical — (1) feature-phone SMS storage 20–150 messages fills up; (2) voice+SMS can't run simultaneously on 2G (SMS arrives after call; sequential still works, ~0.48 SD simultaneous optimal); (3) airtime cost on families (₦30/lesson, ₦900/mo = 2–3% of ₦30–50K income) → **must be toll-free/callback**; (4) parent controls phone (~30–60 min/day child access). Significant — no handwriting, short audio attention (3–5 min), no pronunciation feedback, SMS 160-char limit (70 with Yoruba diacritics), b/d/p/q confusion on 128×160 screens, dropped calls. Moderate — multiple children/one phone, button-mashing, no peer learning, stale content.

**Nigerian voice-AI startups:** **YarnGPT** (TTS — Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa/English, 16 voices, open-source HuggingFace, ₦500 min deposit, ~₦0/request beta, 80 calls/day, 2,000 char limit). **Intron Health (Sahara)** — best STT, **~92% on accented Nigerian English**, outperforms Whisper/GPT-4o/Google/AWS/Azure; 18,000+ speakers, 300+ accents; also 80+ TTS voices/40+ accents. **NaijaVoices dataset** (1,800 hrs, 5,000+ speakers, 75% WER reduction fine-tuning Whisper). **Zero-cost delivery research:** FM radio = free outbound (Interactive Radio Instruction/IRI, Rising On Air taught 10M+ one-way — ~80% of learning works with no return channel); toll-free 0800 (sponsor-funded, ~$33,000/year for 1,000 children daily via MTN/Dangote/UBA Foundation CSR); telco zero-rating (like COVID helplines); flash/callback (Nigerian "flashing"); WhatsApp voice notes (data, not airtime).
