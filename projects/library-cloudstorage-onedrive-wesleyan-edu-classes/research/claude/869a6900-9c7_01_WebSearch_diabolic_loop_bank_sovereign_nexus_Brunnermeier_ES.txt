### The Sovereign-Bank Diabolic Loop and ESBies

Markus K. Brunnermeier and colleagues propose a simple model of the sovereign-bank diabolic loop and establish four key results.

**Key Findings:**

First, the diabolic loop can be avoided by restricting banks' domestic sovereign exposures relative to their equity. Second, equity requirements can be lowered if banks only hold senior domestic sovereign debt. Third, such requirements shrink even further if banks only hold the senior tranche of an internationally diversified sovereign portfolio – known as ESBies in the euro-area context.

**What is the Diabolic Loop:**

The "diabolic loop" between the credit risk of sovereigns and that of banks was a hallmark of the euro-area crisis. A decline in sovereign creditworthiness reduced the market value of domestic sovereign debt held by banks, diminishing their balance sheet value and perceived solvency, and limiting their lending activity. This bank distress raised the likelihood of government bailouts, intensifying sovereign distress and creating a "bailout loop".

**ESBies Solution:**

Financial intermediaries would use a well-diversified portfolio of euro-area sovereign bonds to back the issuance of a senior tranche (ESBies) and a junior tranche (EJBies). ESBies would have very little exposure to sovereign risk, owing to the "double protection" of diversification and seniority.
