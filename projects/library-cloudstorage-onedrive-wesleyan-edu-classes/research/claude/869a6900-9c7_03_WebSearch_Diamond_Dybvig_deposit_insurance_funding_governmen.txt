## Summary of Search Results

Ryuichiro Izumi's 2020 work studies a negative feedback loop between the fiscal position of the government and the health of the banking sector, constructing a model of financial intermediation in which the government issues and may default on debt, banks hold some of this debt (tying their health to that of the government), and the government's tax revenue depends on the quantity of investment that banks are able to finance.

Izumi compares the effectiveness of government guarantees, liquidity regulation, and a combination of these policies in preventing self-fulfilling bank runs, finding that in some cases a combination of the two policies is needed to prevent a run, while in other cases liquidity regulation alone is effective and adding guarantees would make the financial system fragile.

The research extends the classic Diamond-Dybvig model to address how deposit insurance and financial regulation function differently when there is a feedback loop between sovereign debt and banking system stability. This relates directly to the "diabolic loop" concept found in the broader literature on sovereign-bank interconnectedness, where concerns about the solvency of sovereigns foment concerns about the solvency of banks given their high exposure to government bonds, and financial stress reinforces concerns about sovereigns given the increased likelihood of intervention to save the banking system.
