## Best Approaches in 2025

### Avatar vs. Video Approaches

Avatar-based systems like Handtalk translate text into avatar signed videos of either Brazilian Sign Language (Libras) or ASL using a gloss-based system. However, there are tradeoffs. Some companies like Signapse focus on generating sign language videos for train stations and public announcements using life-like signers over avatars, which improves authenticity and cultural acceptance.

Research shows user perception varies: Avatars perceived as robotic or as failing to capture the nuances of human signing can hinder effective communication and result in negative user perception.

### Gloss-Based Translation

Speech or text is converted to an ASL gloss, which is then used to produce an ASL video. However, glossing has limitations. Gloss doesn't capture the full grammatical complexity of ASL—it's more of a literal, word-for-sign translation. Additionally, glosses are so parsimonious that they are missing data essential for effective language sign generation.

### Multi-Modal Avatar Generation

Recent advances address these limitations. New prototypes generate ASL videos with manual markers (hand shape, location, movements, and palm orientation) as well as non-manual markers including facial expressions and eyebrow movements, using few-shot approaches with GPT-4o to translate English text into intermediate ASL representations.

### Current Challenges

Current systems often fail to meet user needs due to poor translation of grammatical structures, the absence of facial cues and body language, and insufficient visual and motion fidelity. As of 2025, no system has achieved human-like quality in full conversational sign language generation.
