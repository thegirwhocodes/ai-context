## Key Findings

In 2022, the World Bank, UNESCO, UNICEF, and other international partners reported that learning poverty had increased by a third in low- and middle-income countries, with an estimated 70% of 10-year-olds unable to understand a simple written text.

This generation of students now risks losing $21 trillion in potential lifetime earnings in present value, or the equivalent of 17% of today's global GDP, up from the $17 trillion estimated in 2021.

The learning poverty rate was 57% before the pandemic, but the learning crisis has since deepened.

### Regional Impact

The economic cost is disproportionately borne by low- and middle-income countries, in which this generation of students could lose $11 trillion of lifetime earnings.

### Context for the Estimate

The $21 trillion in lifetime earnings losses is estimated under an intermediate scenario, which expressed another way means on average a student could lose $29,162 in lifetime earnings.

These figures come from "The State of Global Learning Poverty: 2022 Update" report, a collaborative effort by major international organizations addressing the education crisis exacerbated by COVID-19.
