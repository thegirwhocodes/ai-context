## Bible Study: God Made Me Different—Speaking Boldly Without Fearing People

Biblical assertiveness is not insulting people or taking revenge. Scripture teaches you to speak truthfully and courageously, defend appropriate boundaries, and answer with wisdom—without returning evil for evil.

### 1. God intentionally made you who you are

Read:

- **Psalm 139:13–16** — God formed you and saw you before your birth.
- **Exodus 4:10–12** — Moses felt different and inadequate, but God said, “Who hath made man’s mouth?”
- **Isaiah 43:1–4** — God calls you by name and declares that you are precious.
- **Jeremiah 1:4–8** — Jeremiah felt unqualified, but God told him not to fear people.
- **John 9:1–3** — Jesus rejected the belief that a person’s difference automatically meant punishment or inferiority.
- **1 Corinthians 12:14–22** — Different members of the body are necessary; the members that appear weaker are indispensable.
- **Ephesians 2:10** — You are God’s workmanship, created for work He prepared for you.
- **1 Peter 2:9** — God calls His people “a peculiar people”—His own special possession.

Truth to remember: You do not need to apologize for being someone God intentionally created.

### 2. Fear God more than people’s opinions

Read:

- **Proverbs 29:25** — Fear of people becomes a trap, but trusting God brings safety.
- **Ecclesiastes 5:7** — “Fear thou God.”
- **Ecclesiastes 7:21–22** — Do not take every negative thing people say to heart.
- **Ecclesiastes 12:13–14** — The final duty is to fear God and obey Him because God—not the crowd—will judge every work.
- **Isaiah 51:7–8** — Do not fear people’s insults or become discouraged by their criticism.
- **Matthew 10:26–31** — Do not fear people; you are valuable to God.
- **John 5:44** — Jesus warns against seeking human praise instead of God’s approval.
- **John 12:42–43** — Some people remained silent because they loved human approval.
- **Galatians 1:10** — Living primarily to please people conflicts with serving Christ.
- **1 Thessalonians 2:4** — Speak to please God, who examines the heart.

### 3. What Ecclesiastes says about people’s words

Study **Ecclesiastes 7:1–22** closely.

Especially read:

- **Ecclesiastes 7:5** — A wise person’s correction is more valuable than empty praise.
- **Ecclesiastes 7:9** — Do not become quickly angry.
- **Ecclesiastes 7:20** — Nobody on earth always does right and never sins.
- **Ecclesiastes 7:21–22** — Do not give your heart to everything people say; remember that you have also spoken wrongly about others.
- **Ecclesiastes 8:1** — Wisdom changes a person’s face and softens harshness.
- **Ecclesiastes 8:2–6** — Wisdom recognizes the proper manner and time for speaking.
- **Ecclesiastes 10:4** — When someone in authority becomes angry, remain composed.
- **Ecclesiastes 10:12–14** — A wise person’s words are gracious, while foolish speech becomes destructive.
- **Ecclesiastes 3:1, 7** — There is “a time to keep silence, and a time to speak.”

Ecclesiastes does not say to accept every insult as truth. It says not to surrender your heart to every comment, not to react impulsively, and to use wisdom about when and how you answer.

### 4. Answering people assertively

Read:

- **Proverbs 10:19** — Restraint in speech shows wisdom.
- **Proverbs 12:18** — Reckless words wound; wise words heal.
- **Proverbs 15:1–2** — A gentle answer can turn away anger.
- **Proverbs 15:28** — A righteous person thinks before answering.
- **Proverbs 16:32** — Self-control is greater than physical power.
- **Proverbs 22:3** — A prudent person recognizes danger and takes refuge.
- **Proverbs 25:11–12** — The right word, spoken appropriately, is precious.
- **Proverbs 26:4–5** — Sometimes foolishness should not be answered; sometimes it must be answered so it is not mistaken for wisdom.
- **Proverbs 31:8–9** — Speak up and defend those who are vulnerable.
- **Matthew 5:37** — Let your “Yes” mean yes and your “No” mean no.
- **Ephesians 4:15, 25–27** — Speak truth in love; address anger without allowing it to control you.
- **Colossians 4:6** — Speak graciously while knowing how to answer each person.
- **2 Timothy 1:7** — God gives power, love, and a sound mind—not fear.
- **James 1:19–20** — Be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger.
- **1 Peter 3:15–16** — Be ready to answer, but do so with gentleness and respect.

### 5. Examples of firm answers in Scripture

Jesus and Paul sometimes remained silent, sometimes asked a question, and sometimes confronted falsehood directly.

Read:

- **Matthew 21:23–27** — Jesus answered a hostile question with a discerning question.
- **Matthew 22:15–22** — Jesus recognized manipulation and answered wisely.
- **Matthew 26:62–64** — Jesus knew when to remain silent and when to speak plainly.
- **John 8:48–49** — When insulted, Jesus denied the false accusation without returning an insult.
- **John 18:22–23** — When struck, Jesus calmly challenged the injustice: if He had spoken wrongly, they should provide evidence.
- **Acts 16:35–39** — Paul asserted his legal rights after being mistreated.
- **Acts 22:24–29** — Paul questioned an unlawful punishment.
- **Acts 23:1–5** — Paul spoke strongly but corrected himself when confronted with Scripture.
- **Acts 25:8–12** — Paul firmly defended himself and appealed to Caesar.

### 6. What you must not “shoot back” with

Scripture does not permit revenge, cruelty, humiliation, threats, or insult for insult.

Read:

- **Leviticus 19:18** — Do not seek revenge or hold a grudge.
- **Proverbs 24:28–29** — Do not say, “I will do to him as he hath done to me.”
- **Matthew 5:44** — Pray for those who mistreat you.
- **Luke 6:27–31** — Do good rather than copying your enemy’s behavior.
- **Romans 12:17–21** — Do not repay evil with evil; overcome evil with good.
- **Ephesians 4:29–32** — Reject corrupt and malicious speech.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:15** — Do not return evil for evil.
- **1 Peter 2:21–23** — Jesus did not retaliate when insulted.
- **1 Peter 3:9** — Do not return insult for insult.
- **Jude 9** — Even Michael did not pronounce an abusive judgment; he left judgment to the Lord.

### 7. Short scriptural responses you can use

These follow the biblical patterns of truth, clarity, questions, and boundaries:

- “That is not true, and I will not accept it as truth.”
- “What do you mean by that?”
- “Please explain why you believe that.”
- “You may disagree with me, but you may not speak to me disrespectfully.”
- “I am willing to talk when we can speak calmly.”
- “No.”
- “I heard what you said, but I do not agree.”
- “God knows me, and I will not measure myself by that comment.”
- “I am leaving this conversation now.”
