## What is a Normal Upward-Sloping Yield Curve?

An upward sloping yield curve—often referred to as a normal yield curve—reflects the market's baseline expectations, signaling that long-term bonds are offering higher yields than short-term bonds. Lenders require a "term premium"—greater compensation for tying up capital over longer periods given the greater uncertainties and risks associated with the distant future (i.e., inflation, changes in economic growth, or borrower creditworthiness).

## Economic Significance

When the curve is steep or upward sloping, investors generally anticipate continued economic growth and, potentially, moderate inflation. An upward-sloping yield curve, characterized by higher long-term yields than short-term yields, is typical and indicates an absence of concerns over a recession and rate cuts by the Federal Reserve.
