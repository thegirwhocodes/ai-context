Based on the search results, here are key findings about FAISS IndexFlatIP scaling with 500K vectors on MacBook M1:

## Performance on M1 MacBook

IndexFlatIP has been demonstrated to be slightly faster than IndexFlatL2 when using faiss-cpu on an M1 chip with vector dimensionality of 100. However, when running FAISS on a Mac with an M1 processor, users have achieved an average latency of around 7 milliseconds for 1000 searches.

## Performance Limitations

There are documented performance issues with IndexFlatIP on CPU-based systems. IndexFlatIP doesn't seem to use parallelization correctly, with custom flat implementations being 2x to 3x faster than IndexFlatIP. Additionally, on CPU, faiss.IndexFlatIP is ~18x slower than using PyTorch operations (torch.mm and torch.topk) when running on an index of 2M documents of dimension 768.

## Scaling Considerations

IndexFlatIP is a brute-force index that performs exhaustive searches using inner product and serves as a baseline, while it guarantees accuracy, it may not be the most efficient for large datasets due to its high computational cost. Flat indexes are 100% accurate but have completely unoptimized search-speeds, which fits smaller index use-cases or scenarios where search-time is irrelevant.

For 500K vectors specifically, you may experience latency challenges with IndexFlatIP on M1, particularly if coming from GPU environments like Colab.
