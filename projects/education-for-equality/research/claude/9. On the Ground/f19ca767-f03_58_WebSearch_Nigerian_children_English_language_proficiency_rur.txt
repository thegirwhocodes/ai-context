## Language Context in Nigeria

Nigerians speak over 520 indigenous languages alongside English, which is the official language. The three major indigenous languages are Hausa (63 million speakers), Yoruba (47 million speakers), and Igbo (46 million speakers).

## English Proficiency and Rural Areas

Not all Nigerians speak English fluently. English is used by only a small minority of the country's urban elite as a first language, and is not spoken at all in some rural areas. Proficiency varies significantly between urban and rural areas, with education level being the primary determinant of English fluency.

Although English is the 'official' language in Nigeria, and the most common medium for reading and writing, it is only spoken by 20% of the population. Of primary school pupils interviewed in one tutoring program in 2012, only 4% reportedly speak English at home.

## Rural Learning and Mother Tongue Education

Results from programs show that during early learning, children in rural areas learn faster in their local language. In rural areas most teachers adopt the use of the Mother tongue or language of the immediate environment for those that can speak it; teachers that cannot speak language of the immediate environment adopt the use of English language which most pupils do not understand.

## Speech Recognition and Accents

Research has examined machine learning algorithms applied to recognizing three Nigerian ethnic groups (Yoruba, Igbo, and Hausa) and distinguishing them based on their accents by constructing Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) features from audio samples. A person's accent can be a pointer to his/her first language or mother tongue.
