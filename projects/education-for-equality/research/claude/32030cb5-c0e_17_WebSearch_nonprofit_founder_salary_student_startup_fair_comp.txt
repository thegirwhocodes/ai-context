## Key Compensation Rules for 501(c)(3) Nonprofits

A 501(c)(3) can pay employees, including founders, as long as you follow IRS rules on reasonable compensation and avoid private benefit. However, any compensation must be a payment for real work performed, not a share of the organization's net earnings.

## What Constitutes "Reasonable" Compensation

The IRS defines reasonable compensation as the amount that would ordinarily be paid for similar services by similar organizations (taxable or tax-exempt) under similar circumstances. The IRS requires that nonprofit salaries should be "reasonable" and "not excessive."

## Determining Fair Compensation

The best way to approach this is to consider the overall job description, your qualifications and earnings history, plus what other similar sized nonprofits pay someone in a similar position, and balance that against the overall income picture of your nonprofit. Be sure to factor in your geographic location, organization size, job description, and the qualifications and experience required for the position in order to find comparable salary ranges.

## Special Considerations for Startup Nonprofits

If you are a startup nonprofit founder, there are unique challenges to taking a salary, especially during the first two years of operations, and founders should first work to get their organization to a place where it is mostly self-sustaining. For small organizations, $50,000 to $65,000 is a more typical full-time salary.

## Governance and Documentation

The board of directors should review and vote on compensation on a recurring annual basis and whenever a new position is created, as this is the safest way to avoid allegations of excessive compensation.
