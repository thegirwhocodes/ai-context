## GENIUS Act Overview

The Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins (GENIUS) Act became law on July 17, 2025, marking the first comprehensive federal framework governing cryptocurrency in the United States.

## Yield Prohibition

The GENIUS Act prohibits stablecoin issuers from offering any form of interest or yield to stablecoin holders, but does not explicitly prohibit affiliate or third-party arrangements that might offer interest-bearing products. Section 4(a)(11) of the GENIUS Act prohibits PPSIs and FPSIs from paying the holder of any payment stablecoin any form of interest or yield (whether in cash, tokens, or other consideration) solely in connection with the holding, use, or retention of such payment stablecoin.

## Emerging Markets Connection

Stablecoins have grown rapidly in use, facilitating crypto trading, cross-border payments and dollar access in emerging markets.

## Key Regulatory Requirements

Permitted payment stablecoin issuers must maintain reserves backing outstanding payment stablecoins on at least a one-to-one basis, consisting only of certain specified assets, including US dollars and short-term Treasuries.

## Notable Controversy

While the prohibition appears to create a blanket ban on interest, the restriction may not apply to the current market practice involving exchanges where the issuer passes interest on reserves to the exchange, which then pays the investor yield. GENIUS does not explicitly prevent exchanges from paying rewards on stablecoins held on their exchange.
