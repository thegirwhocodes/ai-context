## How the top startups actually do it — and where ours stood

The thing that separates a real startup contract from a generic freelance template is **IP chain-of-title**. The YC / Stripe Atlas / Cooley playbook treats one clause as sacred: a **present-tense assignment** — "Contractor *hereby irrevocably assigns*," not "agrees to assign." That exact word choice decided a Supreme Court case (*Stanford v. Roche*, 2011) — a researcher's later "hereby assign" beat his employer's earlier "agree to assign," and the institution lost its patent. Investors kill more deals over a missing or future-tense assignment than almost anything else, because a gap means the company may not own its own core work. Our v1 had a plain "belongs to the Organization" line — fine for a generic contractor, **not investor-grade**. Top startups also never rely on "work made for hire" alone, because US copyright law only allows it for 9 narrow categories (software/curriculum/data aren't on the list) — so they layer WFH *plus* an assignment backstop, *plus* further-assurances, *plus* an irrevocable power of attorney, *plus* a moral-rights waiver, *plus* a carve-out for the contractor's pre-existing IP. Ours had none of that scaffolding.

Three more places ours was behind best practice:

- **Children's data was under-built.** The serious orgs (and every major funder — Mastercard, Gates, UNICEF, World Bank, USAID) require a controller/processor split, parental-consent gating, data minimization, a 72-hour breach line, and an explicit *no secondary use / no model-training* bar. Ours gestured at NDPA but didn't have the processor mechanics.
- **Safeguarding was a paragraph, not a system.** Because Sonia is physically in front of children, funders screen for a specific package: a **Code of Conduct built on the IASC Six Core Principles**, a **Nigerian Police Character Certificate** (POSSAP, ~3-month validity) as a condition before fieldwork, mandatory training, a **no-adult-alone-with-a-child** rule (this is literally how Pratham/ASER run assessments), and a named reporting focal point. This is the single biggest gap for an org with E4E's funding ambitions.
- **The related-party piece needs governance, not just a disclosure line.** Under IRS §4958 your mother is a "disqualified person" (an ancestor of a founder). The way top founder-led nonprofits make a family hire bulletproof is the **rebuttable-presumption process**: ≥3 fair-market comparables (you already have the ₦250–400k Lagos range), approval by people who are *not* her and *not* you (both recused), and contemporaneous minutes — to be ratified once a real board exists. Nigeria's CAMA Part F (§838) reaches the same place: bona fide, reasonable pay for actual services is allowed. Ours had the acknowledgement but not the playbook.
