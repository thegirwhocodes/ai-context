## Hours of Instruction in Adult Literacy Programs

**Research Study Parameters:**

One research study examined instructional approaches of equal length (100 hours), while instructional sessions may last up to 60 instructional hours, with learners evaluated at various intervals throughout the instructional period. Additionally, a study comparing intervention programs randomly assigned 300 adults with basic reading skills below the seventh grade level.

**Current Instruction Intensity:**

Routine adult classroom instruction typically provides on average about 3–6 classroom hours per week, which researchers found was often insufficient for significant gains.

**Hours Needed for Mastery:**

Research suggests considerable instruction is necessary: For many learners, mastery of automatic, efficient decoding skills would require many more hours of instruction and practice, and steeper growth patterns may emerge when a critical number of instructional hours are surpassed.

**Barriers to Adequate Instruction:**

Attrition rates of 40% or higher are typical between the time of assignment to an educational program to post-intervention testing some 50 to 100 hours later, highlighting challenges in implementing programs with sufficient instructional hours.

The research indicates that while studies have examined instruction at 60, 100, and up to 300 hours total, mastery requires many more hours of instruction and practice than currently available in most adult literacy programs.
