Nina Panickssery is a researcher at Anthropic (since 2023) with expertise in deep learning and machine learning interpretability. Her research focuses on language models, AI alignment, AI interpretability, and ML safety.

Before transitioning to ML research, she worked as a software engineer at Stripe and studied Biomedical Engineering at Imperial College London.

**Research Focus:**

She has published notable work including "Steering Llama 2 via Contrastive Activation Addition," which won an Outstanding Paper Award at ACL 2024, and has co-authored papers on many-shot jailbreaking and refusal in language models. One of her projects aims to improve understanding of sycophancy in language models by finding a "sycophancy vector" in an LLM's latent space.

**Collaboration:**

She collaborates with AI safety researchers through MATS, SPAR, and the Anthropic Fellows Program. She is also listed as a mentor for the Anthropic Fellows Program.
