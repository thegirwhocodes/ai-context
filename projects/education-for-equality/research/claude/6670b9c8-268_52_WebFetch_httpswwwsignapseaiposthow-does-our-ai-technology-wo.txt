# Signapse AI Technology Pipeline: Complete Technical Overview

## Two-Stage Translation Architecture

Signapse implements a dual-step process converting English to British Sign Language video:

**Stage 1: Text to Gloss**
- Converts written English to "Gloss," a written representation of BSL grammar and syntax
- Example: "What is your name?" becomes "NAME YOU WHAT"
- Uses Large Language Models (LLMs) trained on thousands of BSL gloss sentences
- The system learns "complex BSL structure and grammar" from training data

**Stage 2: Gloss to Video**
- Converts gloss sequences into photorealistic video using computer vision technology
- Blends between approximately 12,000 recorded glosses from Deaf native signers
- Creates seamless transitions between signs

## BSL-Specific Linguistic Elements

**Sign Variants**: The AI selects contextually appropriate signs (e.g., "MATCH" differs for sports vs. lottery contexts)

**Directional Verbs & Placement**: The system "places concepts in space and amends verb directions to show which concept performs the action and who receives it." Example: "HELP" moves from helper to recipient.

**Non-Manual Features (NMFs)**: Incorporates facial expressions, head movements, and mouth patterns that modify meaning, indicate questions, or express emotion. Raised eyebrows signal yes/no questions; furrowed brows indicate "wh" questions.

## Video Generation Technology

- Uses "~30 cameras" in capture studio for high-quality recordings
- Records "~40 versions of each directional verb"
- Generates "photo-realistic" digital signer appearances
- Produces "seamless and realistic videos, converting diverse recordings into a consistent high-quality appearance"

## Quality Assurance

All external translations reviewed by BSL Data Collection team at gloss stage before video generation, with multiple manual QC checks and Deaf native BSL user verification before release.
