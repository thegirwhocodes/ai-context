## PART 2: Cross-country alternatives

### A) Identifying a Nigeria-specific β in cross-country panels

A two-way FE panel `growth_it = α_i + γ_t + β·OOSC_it + X_it'·θ + ε_it` gives a **pooled global β**, not Nigeria-specific. To get a Nigeria coefficient you have three reasonable routes:

1. **Interaction term:** `β1·OOSC_it + β2·(OOSC_it × Nigeria_i)`. The Nigeria-specific coefficient is `β1+β2`. With country FE, the linear `Nigeria_i` dummy drops out but the interaction is identified. This is the **simplest, most defensible** approach for an undergraduate paper.
2. **Mundlak / Correlated Random Effects:** allows individual heterogeneity; equivalent to FE in the time-varying coefficients but lets you also keep time-invariant variables. Not standard for getting *country-specific β* without extension. (https://blog.stata.com/2015/10/29/fixed-effects-or-random-effects-the-mundlak-approach/)
3. **Synthetic control (Abadie):** Treats Nigeria as the treated unit, builds a counterfactual from a donor pool of African non-oil-rich countries. Powerful but unconventional for a growth-regression paper and probably overkill for May 14.

**For an undergraduate empirical paper on a one-week timeline, the interaction term is the right tool.**

### B) Canonical cross-country growth-education panel papers

| Paper | N (countries) | T | Estimator | Identifies country-specific? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mankiw-Romer-Weil 1992** | 98, 75, 22 | cross-section | OLS | No |
| **Bils & Klenow (2000) AER** ([http://klenow.com/BKHK.pdf](http://klenow.com/BKHK.pdf)) | 52 | cross-section/calibrated | Calibration + OLS | No |
| **Pritchett (2001) WBER** | ~91 | 1960-1985 differences | OLS, IV | No |
| **Krueger-Lindahl (2001) JEL** ([NBER 7591](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7591)) | ~110 | 5 obs/country | FE differenced | No (pooled) |
| **Cohen-Soto (2007) JEG** | 95 | 1960-2000, 10-yr panels | OLS, FE, IV | No |
| **Hanushek-Woessmann (2012) JEG** | 50 (24 OECD core) | cross-section | OLS, IV | No (pooled) |
| **Cohen-Leker (2014)** | ~95 | extended Cohen-Soto | OLS, FE | No |
| **Caselli-Esquivel-Lefort (1996) JEG** | ~97 | 5-yr panels | System GMM | No |

**Sample size norm:** N≈40-100 countries, T=5-10 (when using 5- or 10-year averages), so typical N×T = 200-1000 obs. None of these papers identify a *country-specific* education coefficient; that is a deliberate methodological extension.

### C) Smallest credible cross-country panel

- For canonical growth regressions, **N ≥ ~30 with T ≥ 4** is the practical floor (Bond-Hoeffler-Temple 2001 GMM estimation paper discusses this).
- **Sub-Saharan Africa** panels with ~40-44 countries × 10-15 years are well-precedented. A 2024 study using SSA 2010-2023 panel of 44 countries with FE is at https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7099/12/11/300.
- **ECOWAS** (15 countries) is plausible but small-N — see https://www.researchgate.net/publication/367575408_Education_and_Economic_Growth_in_the_Economic_Community_of_West_African_States_ECOWAS
- "Anglophone Africa" by itself (~5 countries) is too small for credible inference.

### D) Within-country sub-national education-growth panels in other federal LDCs

- **India state-level**: closest analog. Drèze & Kingdon (1999) on student-teacher ratios; Khanna (2023) JPE on Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan with regression discontinuity (https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/721619). India works because Indian state GDP (NSS, MOSPI) has been published annually for decades — Nigeria simply lacks this.
- **Brazil municipality-level**: Bezerra Barros, Pessoa Nunes Neto & Vergolino (1970-1996 convergence); multiple panel papers on Brazilian municipalities. Brazil municipality GDP is published by IBGE, again unlike Nigeria.
- **The Boko Haram precedent for Nigeria:** Bertoni, Di Maio, Molini, Nisticò (2019, *J. Dev. Econ.*) "Education is forbidden" used **3 rounds of GHS-Panel + ACLED conflict data**, individual fixed effects + DiD. This is the closest existing template. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304387818302505 . They studied *household/individual-level* enrollment, not state-level GDP — confirming that state-level GDP is too data-poor in Nigeria for a growth-regression style paper.

### ★★★ Recommended (BEST / SECOND-BEST tied — pick based on advisor's panel-method preference)

**Option 1: Cross-country panel with Nigeria interaction (BEST given timeline)**

- **Data:** UNESCO UIS / WDI for OOSC rate, WDI growth + capital, PWT 11.0 for capital stock
- **Equation:**
  `Δlog(GDP_pc)_it = α_i + γ_t + β1·OOSC_it + β2·(OOSC_it × Nigeria_i) + θ·X_it + ε_it`
  Nigeria-specific coefficient = β1 + β2; test H0: β2=0
- **Estimator:** Two-way FE, cluster-robust SE at country
- **Effort:** ~2 days. WDI data already in the student's pipeline.
- **Why this works:** Directly responds to the professor's "use a panel" feedback, identifies a Nigeria-specific effect, and is methodologically conventional.

**Option 2: Nigeria state×year panel of OOSC and a GDP proxy** (more ambitious)
- **OOSC panel:** MICS 2007 + MICS 2016-17 + MICS 2021 + NDHS 2008/2013/2018/2023 + NEDS 2010/2015 → up to ~7 state-year observations per state, 36+1 states ≈ 250 obs.
- **GDP proxy:** Use VIIRS nightlights 2012-2024 (annual) at state polygon level, **with gas-flare mask** for Niger Delta (Henderson et al. flag this). - **Equation:** `Δlog(lights)_it = α_state + γ_t + β·OOSC_it + θ·X_it + ε_it`
- **Effort:** ~5-7 days. Heavy data-merging. Nightlight extraction is the time sink — use the AidData pre-extracted polygon zonal stats if available.
