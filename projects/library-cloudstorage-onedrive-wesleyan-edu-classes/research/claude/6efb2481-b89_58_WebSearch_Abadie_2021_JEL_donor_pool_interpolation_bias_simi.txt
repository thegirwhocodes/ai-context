## Donor Pool Selection and Interpolation Bias

It is important to restrict the donor pool to units with characteristics that are similar to the affected unit, because while the restrictions placed on the weights do not allow extrapolation, interpolation biases may still be important if the synthetic control matches the characteristics of the affected unit by averaging away large differences.

## Guidance on Similar Predictors

The approach in Abadie and L'Hour (2021) favors synthetic controls composed by untreated units with values of the predictors close to the values of the predictors for the treated unit, which ameliorates potential interpolation biases that could emerge from averaging outcomes between untreated units that are far from the treated unit in the space of the predictors.

## Practical Recommendation

Researchers trying to minimize biases caused by interpolating across regions with very different characteristics may restrict the donor pool to regions with similar characteristics to the region exposed to the event or intervention of interest, by progressively limiting the pool of potential donors to those with predictors less than 3 standard deviations from the treated unit, 2.5 standard deviations from the treated unit, and so on.

The core message is that researchers should actively trim the donor pool to include only comparable units in order to reduce interpolation bias, which arises when synthetic controls average outcomes from donors with substantially different predictor values.
