Deep research done (myself, web). Here's what the companies that already solve "assign tasks to non-technical board members" actually do — and exactly what it means for your backend.

## What the board-software leaders do
Board tools (Boardable, OnBoard, BoardPro, Convene, Decisions) that specifically sell to **non-technical, volunteer, nonprofit boards** converge on the same playbook:

1. **A per-person "My Tasks" view.** Each board member logs in and sees *only their own* assigned items — with due date and a status toggle. They never wade through everyone's work. This is the #1 friction-reducer.
2. **Tasks are born where the work is** — created from a meeting, agenda, or dashboard and linked to that context, so the task carries its "why" with it (no hunting).
3. **Automated reminders do the chasing** — deadline alerts fire automatically so "nothing slips through" and the admin (you) never has to nag.
4. **Ease-of-use is the explicit product** — every vendor leads with "intuitive for non-technical members, minimal training, works on phone or desktop." For this audience, simplicity beats features.

## The single most important pattern for someone who's *never* done this
**Act-from-email with no login** — the "one-click action" pattern (Gmail One-Click Actions, Outlook Actionable Messages, Oracle APEX in-email approvals, Azure Logic Apps):

- The task email itself contains the buttons — **"I'm on it" / "Mark done"** — right in the body.
- Clicking **requires no login**: a signed magic-link token identifies the person and updates status, then shows an instant "✓ Got it" confirmation.
- Your mom never opens Notion, never remembers a password, never learns a dashboard. She gets an email, reads one task, taps one button. That *is* the interaction.

This is the exact bridge for Sonia/Richard/Joshua — people who "have never done that before."

## What this means for your backend (the gap vs. what's built)
You're already 80% there — Codex wired Notion tasks + assignment emails, live on prod. But the current email's only button is **"Open advisor board,"** which forces a login. That's the friction the research says to remove. So **#4 + #5, done right =**:

1. **Add one-click-from-email task actions** — "I'm on it" and "Mark done" buttons that work via signed magic-link, no login, land on a branded one-line confirmation page, and update the Notion task status. *(This is the big unlock.)*

3. **Auto-reminders** — a daily cron that emails anyone with an overdue/ due-soon task, so you never chase.
4. **Keep the clean-tab architecture (#5)** — all of this lives inside the existing tabs, nothing baked into config.

I'd **leave the Google Drive piece for later** — it's a separate OAuth lift and not what makes it usable for a first-timer. The email-action flow is what actually delivers "extremely user-friendly for board members."

It should read **"2/2 · 100%"** = 2 of 2 questions correct on that call, 100%. Same bug hits a few other spots (call timings, learning-evidence lines).

## What the warm gradients mean
Two separate visual signals — and this is worth clarifying because the color is doing *less* than it looks:

- **The gradient bar itself (green→gold) means nothing by color** — it's just Sabi's brand fill. The *only* data is **how full the bar is**: the width = the indicator's progress (e.g. "4/4 positive gains" = full bar, "2/4 mastered" = half). So green-to-gold is decorative; fill-level is the metric.
- **The status pill color IS meaningful** — it's set by `indicatorKind()`:
  - **Green** ("signal visible", "strong", "mastery visible") = good — evidence is showing up.
  - **Gold/amber** ("emerging", "collecting") = in progress — data accumulating, not conclusive yet.
  - **Tan/warn** ("needs support", "needs scoring", "due") = needs attention/action.
  - **Red** ("blocked", "withdrawn") = problem.

So the "warm" (gold/tan) cards aren't bad — mostly they mean *"emerging / still collecting / not scored yet,"* which is expected in preview mode before real children are enrolled.
