## Attack Mechanism

Prompt injections involve malicious instructions hidden in contexts like websites, translation tasks, or emails, designed to manipulate the model into unintended behavior. The raw email body (controlled by anyone who can send you an email) can be passed directly to the LLM as untrusted content exposure. The model cannot distinguish policy from content unless you enforce boundaries, and prompt injection succeeds when systems treat external content as if it were a trusted control input.

## Limitations of Prompting-Based Defenses

There is no known method that provides 100% protection against prompt injection, and OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google have all acknowledged this. The "Attacker Moves Second" paper showed that prompting-based defenses collapsed to 95-99% attack success rates under adaptive conditions, and even training-based methods failed at 96-100%.

## Recommended Mitigation Approaches

AI Gateway guardrails such as safety filtering and PII detection can be applied to either the input or output of an agent (or both). Specialized LLMs like Llama Protection models have been fine-tuned to detect prompt injections, toxic content, or code interpreter abuse.

Architectural defenses for indirect prompt injection include trust labeling of context, instruction hierarchy enforcement (treating retrieved content as evidence, never orders), and context isolation (presenting retrieved text in quoted/boxed form).

The only approaches showing real promise are architectural (like CaMeL) — treating the LLM as untrusted and building deterministic guardrails around it.
