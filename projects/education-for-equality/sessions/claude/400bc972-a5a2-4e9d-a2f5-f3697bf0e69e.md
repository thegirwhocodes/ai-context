## What people actually do for loud + accented + phone (the real answer)

1. **Denoiser front-end** → **DeepFilterNet v3** is the pick. Free (MIT/Apache), modern, ~40 ms. *Not* RNNoise (free but unmaintained since 2024, aging on modern noise). 3. **Noise-robust STT model** (below).
4. **Keyterm biasing** → weight numbers ("two", "ten") and phonics words. Real feature in Deepgram/Intron; for Whisper it's `initial_prompt`. Genuinely helps when noise makes a word borderline.

## Fundamentals for every artifact

**Honor what's already there** Look for an existing design system first — CLAUDE.md, a tokens or theme file, existing component styles. **Pair typefaces** Typography carries the page even when the page isn't about typography. The Artifact CSP blocks font CDNs, so don't link a webfont URL and risk a silent fallback. Keep running text near 65 characters wide; set a type scale and stay on it; give headings `text-wrap: balance`, body text room to breathe, and uppercase labels a touch of letter-spacing.

**Choose neutrals, don't default to them.** A pure mid-grey reads as unconsidered; a grey with a slight hue bias toward the page's accent reads as chosen. Pure white and near-black are fine grounds when they suit the subject — the point is that the neutral was picked, not inherited.

A design that deliberately commits to one visual world (a neon arcade screen, a letterpress invitation) may stay single-theme — make it a choice, not an omission.

## Why training needs *far* more memory than running the model
When Sabi *uses* Whisper, only the model weights sit in VRAM (~3 GB for large-v3). When you *train* it, four things stack up in memory at once:

| What's in VRAM during training | Size (per parameter) |
|---|---|
| The weights themselves | 2 bytes |
| A high-precision master copy | 4 bytes |
| Gradients (which way to nudge each weight) | 2 bytes |
| Optimizer state (Adam keeps 2 running averages) | 8 bytes |
| **Total** | **~16 bytes/parameter** |

Plus "activations" (scratch space that grows with batch size). So the rule of thumb: **full fine-tuning needs roughly 16× the memory of the model's raw parameter count.**

## Run the math on Whisper sizes

| Model | Parameters | Full fine-tune VRAM (~16 B/param) | Fits in your 14 GB? |
|---|---|---|---|
| large-v3 | 1.55 B | **~25 GB** + activations | ❌ no (blows even the full 20 GB) |
| **medium** | 769 M | **~12 GB** + activations | ✅ yes (with a small batch) |
| small | 244 M | ~4 GB | ✅ easily |

That's why I said full large-v3 won't fit — the optimizer alone is ~12 GB before anything else.

## The way around it: LoRA
LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) **freezes** the big model and trains only tiny "adapter" matrices bolted onto it — a few million trainable params instead of 1.55 billion. So:
- The frozen large-v3 just sits there in inference mode: **~3 GB** (or ~1.6 GB if you 8-bit quantize it — that's "QLoRA").
- The adapters' gradients + optimizer state are **negligible** (<0.5 GB).
- **Total ≈ 5–8 GB** → **large-v3 LoRA fits comfortably in your 14 GB free.**

The output isn't a new 3 GB model — it's a small adapter file (tens of MB) you load *on top of* Whisper at inference time.

## Now — your real question: what does fine-tuning Whisper take? Do you transcribe every call?

**No. You never transcribe a single call from scratch.** Here's what it actually takes, cheapest → most effort:

**1. Use corpora that are *already labeled* (zero work from you).** [NaijaVoices](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20564) — 1,800 hrs of Nigerian speech, already transcribed, and it's **adult** speech (which, since your callers are adults right now, is a *perfect* match, not a mismatch). Fine-tuning Whisper on it gave a **75% WER improvement** in the paper. Plus AfriSpeech-200 (Intron's) and Common Voice. You can start fine-tuning *today* without labeling anything.

**2. Pseudo-label, then *correct* (not transcribe).** Run Whisper on your calls → get a rough draft → a person just **fixes the wrong words.** Correcting a draft is ~5–10× faster than typing from silence. You need a few hundred to ~2,000 utterances — not every call.

**3. Harvest free labels from the lesson itself.** This is the clever one: Sabi *knows* the expected answer. When a child answers a number question correctly (the lesson logic confirms it), that audio clip is *automatically* labeled with that number — free, perfect labels for exactly the number vocabulary that's failing. Every correct answer is a training example you didn't have to label.

**4. Targeted micro-recording.** Have a few Nigerian speakers (your team, Sonia's contacts) say the specific failing words — numbers 1–100, phonics words — over a phone. A few hours covering the exact things that break.

**How much data?** LoRA needs surprisingly little — **tens of hours**, sometimes less, for accent/domain adaptation. Not thousands. The Yoruba example was 120 hrs → big gain; numbers/accent can move with less.

**So the realistic recipe:** NaijaVoices (free, done) + a few hundred *corrected* Sabi utterances + auto-harvested confirmed answers → LoRA-fine-tune large-v3 on your GPU (it fits) → evaluate against the ground-truth clips you're already producing. That's the whole Intron/NaijaVoices playbook: **crowdsource + correct, never transcribe blind.**

The three-layer picture for numbers: **biasing = shipped today; extending the answer-matcher ("tati"→30) = a cheap next win that catches what biasing missed (t5/t9); fine-tuning = the durable fix.** Want the answer-matcher extension (quick), or shall we scope the fine-tune runbook?
