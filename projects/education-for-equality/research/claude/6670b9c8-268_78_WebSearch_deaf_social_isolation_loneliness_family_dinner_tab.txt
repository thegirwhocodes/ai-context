## Key Findings:

**What is Dinner Table Syndrome:**
Dinner Table Syndrome refers to the experience of Deaf individuals, especially children, during family meals or social events where conversation is dominated by spoken language. Deaf people have coined the term 'Dinner Table Syndrome' to express the profound isolation they feel in situations where communication becomes a barrier.

**The Core Issue:**
For deaf and hard-of-hearing people, however, the dinner table often represents loneliness and inaccessibility. An isolated deaf person can feel disconnected even when surrounded by family and friends, turning what should be a moment of togetherness into one of loneliness.

**Why This Happens:**
In the US, 90% of deaf children are born to hearing parents, and 88% of the parents do not learn a signed language to communicate with their child. They watch their family or friends enjoy their conversations with each other while struggling to keep up, or worse, finding themselves unable to understand anything.

**Psychological Impact:**
For many deaf and hard-of-hearing people, repeated exclusion at mealtimes can lead to feelings of isolation, frustration, and low self-esteem. Deaf and hard-of-hearing people feel excluded and become stressed out, lonely, angry, and feeling unimportant. The painful accumulation of being left out at the family table over a lifetime has caused many deaf and hard-of-hearing people to dread going home for the holidays.
