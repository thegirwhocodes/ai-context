## Key Research Findings

**Financial Dollarization and Banking Vulnerability**

Financial dollarization heightens the susceptibility of banking systems to external shocks, particularly when banks depend on U.S. dollar funding through domestic dollar deposits or international borrowing, as a rise in U.S. rates increases their funding expenses. If banks lend in local currency while borrowing in dollars, a tightening of U.S. monetary policy can create balance sheet mismatches, particularly if accompanied by exchange rate depreciation, leading banks to adopt a more conservative stance on extending new credit.

**Argentina's Banking Crisis and Deposit Outflows**

In Argentina's banking crisis, the loss of confidence triggered by government default and devaluation expectations resulted in a significant withdrawal of deposits, especially large in the two major public banks. Argentina experienced a drastic fall in financial dollarization in 2002 due to the compulsory conversion of dollar deposits to pesos (pesification) in the context of the currency board crisis.

**Types of Dollarization**

Liability dollarization refers to domestic financial contracts denominated in US dollars, while credit dollarization is the ratio of dollar-denominated loans over all loans in the domestic banking system.

**Policy Responses and Lessons**

Strategies to mitigate dollarization risks include strengthening local currency funding markets, enhancing foreign exchange risk management, and reinforcing macroprudential frameworks.
