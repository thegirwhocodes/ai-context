## Mental Health Crisis and Youth Mental Health Trends

Respondents from health systems described increased mental health disorders among adolescent patients, with increased levels of depressive and anxiety symptoms and suicidal ideation, highlighting the urgent need for accessible mental healthcare and prevention within primary care in the wake of COVID-19 and a youth mental health crisis.

## Primary Care Integration in 2026

More and more healthcare entities are embedding mental health professionals in primary care, emergency medicine, and specialty clinics to ensure early identification and intervention, and screening for conditions such as depression and anxiety alongside vital signs and chronic disease symptoms.

## Screening Benefits

Routine screening for depression, anxiety, and substance use in primary care settings enhances detection rates and reduces stigma, with evidence showing that integrated models improve patient satisfaction, treatment adherence, and clinical outcomes while lowering overall healthcare costs.

## Effectiveness of Integrated Care

The use of mental health screening forms, such as PHQ-9 and GAD-7, is higher in integrated care settings compared to primary care clinics, broadening the possibility of early identification and timely intervention of mental health disorders in integrated care.

Depression and anxiety symptoms improve significantly among patients treated in integrated care settings compared to those from the same demographics with similar baseline mental health scores.

## Recent Implementation Success

Screening for both anxiety and depression symptoms increased significantly, with anxiety screenings rising from 199,465 in 2018 to 1,119,006 in 2023 and depression screenings from 187,653 to 1,107,551.
