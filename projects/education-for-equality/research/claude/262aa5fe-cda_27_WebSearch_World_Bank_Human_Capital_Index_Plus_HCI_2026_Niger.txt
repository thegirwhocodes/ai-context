Based on the search results, here's what the World Bank Human Capital Index Plus (HCI+) 2026 measures:

## What the HCI+ Measures

The Human Capital Index Plus (HCI+) provides new country- and regional-level data tracking human capital accumulation from birth to age 65 and a metric for how gaps translate into forgone future labor earnings. For the first time, it captures how human capital gains – or losses – in the labor market affects lifetime productivity.

## Three Key Areas

HCI+ looks at country outcomes in 3 key areas—Health, Education, and Employment. More specifically:

- Health outcomes include adult survival and stunting, which are related to people's ability to learn and work productively.
- Education outcomes are related to schooling, including learning-adjusted years of schooling and tertiary education.
- On-the-job learning outcomes are in key employment indicators, including labor force participation, unemployment, and wage employment, which together influence skill accumulation and expected lifetime productivity.

## Methodology: Future Earnings Translation

Each point in the index corresponds to a one percent difference in expected lifetime earnings potential, making it possible to translate gains in education, health, or job quality into income terms.

## Nigeria's "111%" Finding

In Nigeria, existing deficits are costing 111% of future earnings. This means that current shortfalls in health, education, and skill development are reducing Nigeria's future labor earnings by more than the total potential earnings themselves.
