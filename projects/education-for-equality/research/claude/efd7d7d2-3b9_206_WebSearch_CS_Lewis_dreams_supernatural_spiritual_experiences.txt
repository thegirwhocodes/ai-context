## Overview

The Great Divorce is a novel by C.S. Lewis, published in 1945, based on a theological dream vision of his in which he reflects on the Christian conceptions of Heaven and Hell. The book was written by Lewis around 1944 in his effort to discredit the idea that Heaven and Hell are compatible with one another.

## Structure and Dream Framework

The writer, in a dream, boards a bus on a drizzly afternoon and embarks on an incredible voyage through Heaven and Hell, meeting a host of supernatural beings and coming to significant realizations about the ultimate consequences of everyday behavior. Lewis presents the Narrator's travels as a dream—an experience that is, by definition, subjective—but by qualifying the literal truth of his novel, Lewis focuses readers' attention on the spiritual, metaphorical truth of the Narrator's experiences.
