The size difference is deliberate quantization: the CoreML conversion uses **fp16 + int8-palettized weights** for most stages (compressed, ANE-optimized), versus the old MLX build's full bf16 precision throughout. Int8 palettization maps weight values to a small lookup table, which compresses much further than just halving precision — that's most of the ~3.7x size drop (327MB → ~87MB of actual model weights).

So it's not naive across-the-board compression; it's a targeted tradeoff. Quantized models like this are usually very close to indistinguishable from full precision by ear, but "usually" isn't "definitely" — that's genuinely something only your own listening can settle, since I can't hear the output myself.
