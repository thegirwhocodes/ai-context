## The Founding Engineer First Pattern

100% of top B2B startups hired at least one engineer among their first three employees, with over two-thirds hiring an engineer as their very first employee. Founding engineers are versatile, highly skilled folks who are the first technical hires that shape a startup's entire technological foundation.

## The Operator/Non-Technical Role

However, research shows a nuanced pattern beyond just engineering. Indian startup founders make predictable mistakes by giving too much equity to engineers and too little to operators, suggesting operators play a critical but undervalued role. Interestingly, when Kunal Shah was building CRED, he didn't start by hiring engineers but instead his first hires were product leaders and designers, showing exceptions to the engineer-first pattern.

## Who Makes the Company Take Off

For B2B SaaS, a first Sales/Customer Success hire is critical before you think you need them, as you need someone who can demo, manage POCs, and onboard paying customers. However, one big mistake founders make is hiring a sales person within their first 10 to 15 hires, with sales remaining founder-led until the company has at least four to five customers.

The research suggests that while founding engineers build the product, a generalist who can manage operations, customer support, and growth becomes essential for execution and scaling. Your first ten hires are the DNA of the company, indicating success depends on balanced hiring across product, operations, and customer-facing roles rather than any single role type.
