# Smart Glasses Comparison for Deaf and Hard of Hearing Users

## Products Reviewed

### **Hearview**
**Features:** Real-time captions with 95% accuracy across accents, supports up to 10 speakers with labels, transcription and translation in 30+ languages, noise-cancellation technology

**Considerations:** Requires Bluetooth pairing to phone and network connectivity for saving subtitles. Captions display only on right lens; left-eye version in development.

**Positioning:** "focused on doing one thing incredibly well—clean, precise, and easy-to-read live captions"

---

### **Captify**
**Features:** Designed specifically for hearing loss, noise cancellation, multiple language support, phone connectivity

**Cons:** Battery life concerns for all-day wear at events or conferences

---

### **XRAI Glass**
**Features:** Captioning and translation software for compatible AR glasses like XREAL

**Cons:** Experience depends on third-party hardware; larger frame sizes; requires smartphone for processing and power

---

### **Xander Glasses**
**Features:** Standalone functionality—all capabilities built into the glasses themselves, no phone dependency required

**Cons:** Significantly higher cost than alternatives, "several hundred dollars more"

---

### **SubLinq**
**Features:** Real-time transcription, translation, teleprompting, "Deaf Mode" specifically for deaf/hard of hearing users, multi-language support, custom display options

**Cons:** Comprehensive feature set may dilute focus; described as specialized add-on rather than purpose-built solution

---
