## Key Findings

### 1. What governments pay per student for edtech programs

The range is enormous depending on context:

- **$0.03/student** -- Rising On Air radio lessons (broadcast, not personalized)
- **$1.55/student/subject/year** -- Mindspark in India at government scale (Rs 130)
- **$5/student** -- Rori AI WhatsApp math tutor (marginal cost)
- **$5/year** -- Eneza/Shupavu via carrier billing (consumer-paid, not government)
- **$11/student/year** -- EIDU in Kenya (smartphone-based, government implementation)
- **$15/student/year** -- Khanmigo in US school districts (was $60, dropped to $35, now $15)
- **$19/student (implied)** -- HOPE-EDU Nigeria ($552M / 29M children, multi-year, includes infrastructure)
- **$48/student for 6 weeks** -- World Bank AI tutoring pilot in Edo State (GPT-4 via Copilot)
- **$60-100/student/year** -- NewGlobe government programs in Liberia (full managed service)
- **$124/student/year projected** -- World Bank AI tutoring scaled to full academic year
- **$1,000-3,000/student/year** -- US high-dosage tutoring programs

### 2. Nigeria-specific baseline

Nigeria's government spends only **~$25/pupil/year** total on primary education. EKOEXCEL and EdoBEST operate at significantly higher per-pupil budgets through state and World Bank funding, but exact NewGlobe contract values are not publicly disclosed.

### 3. The World Bank Edo AI Tutoring Pilot is Sabi's best comparable

At **$48/student for 6 weeks** ($124/year projected), it used GPT-4 in computer labs and achieved a 161-260x benefit-cost ratio. Sabi does the same thing via phone calls without requiring computer labs, internet, or smartphones -- making it radically cheaper.

### 4. Sabi's positioning

At **$0.24-2.44/student/year** at scale, Sabi would be the cheapest AI tutoring program globally. Recommended B2G pricing: **$5-15/student/year** in Phase 2 (with pilot data), dropping to **$2-5/student/year** at scale.

### 5. Contract structures

Governments use: per-student/per-year (most common for edtech), results-based financing (HOPE-EDU), large block grants via World Bank/GPE (multi-year), and telco CSR partnerships (zero cost to government). Outcomes-based contracting is a growing trend in the US.

### 6. No published Nigerian edtech software RFPs found

Government edtech procurement in Nigeria happens primarily through direct partnerships (EKOEXCEL model) or World Bank project frameworks, not open competitive tenders for software services.
