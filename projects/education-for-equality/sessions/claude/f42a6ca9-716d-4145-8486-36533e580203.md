# Should Sabi be its own CAC company, or stay inside E4E?

## Bottom line up front

**Don't pick. And don't abandon E4E.** The framing "Sabi company *vs.* E4E branch" is a false binary that, if you resolve it the wrong way right now, costs you money and optionality. The research lands hard on one conclusion: **the biggest funders split down the middle on nonprofit vs. for-profit, so any single-entity posture leaves money on the table.** The winning structure is the *parallel/hybrid* one your own [LEGAL_ROADMAP.md](7. The correction to your mental model: **"Sabi" is a brand and a product line, not yet a legal entity.** A brand can live inside a nonprofit today and be carried by a for-profit later. You don't have to decide Sabi's corporate body now — you have to decide its corporate body *at the moment it has revenue or an equity investor*, which on your own timeline is **year 2–3, not pre-pilot.**

So: **keep focusing on E4E. Register Sabi as its own for-profit CAC company only when a concrete trigger fires.** Below is why, with both sides argued honestly.

## The decisive finding: funders are split, so a hybrid beats either pure play

This is the load-bearing fact. The deepest-pocketed development funders do **not** agree on what legal form they'll back:

## Path A — Register Sabi as its own CAC for-profit company *now*

**Pros**
- **Only a share-capital Ltd can take equity.** uLesson raised a $3.1M seed (TLcom); Eneza Education (your closest structural twin — feature-phone USSD/SMS literacy/numeracy at *291#) took impact equity from FINCA Ventures and later exited. None of that is reachable by a nonprofit.
- **Only a Ltd can become a "labelled startup"** under the Nigeria Startup Act 2022 → up to a **5-year corporate-income-tax holiday**, R&D deductibility, ITF exemption. (Requires CAMA Ltd registration, <10 yrs old, owns digital-tech IP, ≥⅓ Nigerian founder shareholding — an Incorporated Trustee or Company-Ltd-by-Guarantee structurally *cannot* qualify.)
- **Clean cap table for the commercial vision** (Sabi for Organizations / Sabi for Developers API) from day one.
- Founder can hold equity directly.

**Cons (why it's premature today)**
- **You have nothing for the tax holiday to shelter and nothing to value for an equity round.** You're pre-revenue, pre-pilot (~10 kids). The CIT holiday only bites once there's profit; equity only prices once there's traction. These benefits are, by your own [SABI_PATH_TO_SCALE.md](SABI_PATH_TO_SCALE.md), **year 2–3 concerns.**
- **It locks you out of your actual near-term money.** Grants and government delivery — the Imagine Worldwide / Mastercard / Gates lane — are where pre-pilot ventures get funded. A for-profit can still win *some* philanthropy (Rori did), but you'd be swimming upstream for the bread-and-butter grants while you have no commercial revenue to justify the form.
- **Commercial revenue earned *inside* a Nigerian nonprofit is not tax-exempt anyway** (CITA s.23 only exempts profits *not* derived from trade/business; 2021 amendments tightened this for education). So there's no tax reason to rush the for-profit — you simply route commercial revenue through it *when it exists.*
- A for-profit edtech with no product and a solo founder is a weak fundraising story *and* a weak tax position. Worst of both worlds.

## Path B — Keep Sabi as a product/channel of the E4E nonprofit

**Pros**
- **Matches where your money is right now.** Pilot funding, MIT/Wesleyan prizes, CcHub/Mastercard, Gates, and especially **government-scale delivery** (the Imagine Worldwide → 3.5M kids playbook) all favor or require charitable status.
- **The Nigerian Incorporated Trustee (Part F) is the right delivery vehicle** and is *less* restrictive than people assume: CAMA 2020 s.838 bars distributing income to members, **but explicitly permits the entity to "engage in economic activities."** It's the standard Nigerian NGO form and your [CAC packet](7. - E4E is the **IP and trademark owner** — the durable home for the curriculum, the voice pipeline, and the "Sabi" mark, no matter what entity sells it later.

**Cons**
- **Can't take equity, can't qualify for the Startup Act holiday, ineligible for UNICEF VF.** A pure nonprofit forecloses the for-profit lane.
- **Scaled commercial activity doesn't belong here** — running per-student managed-delivery fees or per-minute API revenue through the Trustee is taxable *and* risks the entity's exempt character. The nonprofit can do cost-recovery and pilots; it shouldn't become the commercial P&L.
- If you *only* ever have the nonprofit, you cap your own upside and can't reward future hires/co-founders with equity.

## Landmines to respect when you *do* spin out (these are non-negotiable)

1. **FMV or bust.** When the 501(c)(3) licenses the Sabi IP/trademark to the for-profit, the nonprofit must receive **at least fair market value** — independent valuation, disinterested board members approving. Skipping this is *private inurement* and can blow up the exemption.
2. **You are a "disqualified person."** As founder/director, you **cannot take a cut of the spinout's deal proceeds.** You *can* receive bona-fide FMV **salary and employee equity** for services (governed by the IRC 4958 reasonableness standard). This is the single most common way founders accidentally torch a nonprofit — structure your compensation, don't extract value.
3. **Trademark license must keep quality control.** The 501(c)(3) licensing "Sabi" to the for-profit must retain control over how the mark is used, or you risk *naked licensing* and loss of the mark.
4. **Clean model exists:** a taxable for-profit subsidiary that remits after-tax profit to the nonprofit parent as tax-free dividends. (Note: the IRS does **not** require the nonprofit to hold majority control of a spun-out venture — that lets founder/investor equity breathe. Below 50% nonprofit ownership, the nonprofit's compensation restrictions on the for-profit fall away.)
5. **Cross-border tax is unanalyzed and needs Nigerian counsel** — US-501(c)(3)-parent licensing IP to a Nigerian Ltd raises transfer-pricing, withholding-tax-on-royalties, and related-party issues the research did *not* resolve.
