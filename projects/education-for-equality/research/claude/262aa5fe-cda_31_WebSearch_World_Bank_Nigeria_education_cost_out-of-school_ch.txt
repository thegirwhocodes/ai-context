## Out-of-School Children in Nigeria

UNICEF estimates 18.5 million Nigerian children were out of school by late 2024, about 15 percent of the global total.

## Education Budget and Allocation

In 2024, Nigeria's education budget was N1.59 trillion. Looking forward, for 2025, President Bola Tinubu proposed an allocation of N3.52 trillion out of N54.99 trillion, which equates to roughly 6.4 percent of total spending.

For context, Nigeria spends between six and seven percent of its budget on education, while Egypt devotes roughly 12 percent, South Africa nine percent, and Kenya eight percent.

## Cost of Education Expansion

According to World Bank research, secondary education will likely cost an additional US$ 6.7 billion (NGN 1,720 billion) on average per year. The World Bank also noted earlier projections that basic education spending would increase from N867 billion in 2020 to N1,897 billion by 2025.

## Economic Implications

Stakeholders express concern that while budget increases reflect a commitment to enhancing educational outcomes, they remain inadequate to address pressing challenges such as high dropout rates and a significant number of out-of-school children. Additionally, in 2023, a US$700 million loan by the World Bank was approved for Nigeria towards the empowerment of adolescent females and tackling the education crisis.
