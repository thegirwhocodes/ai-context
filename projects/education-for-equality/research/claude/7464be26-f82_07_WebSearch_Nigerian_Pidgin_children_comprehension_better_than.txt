## Studies on Mother-Tongue vs. English Instruction

Research found that students taught in their mother tongue demonstrated better comprehension, engagement, and critical thinking skills compared to their English-only counterparts. Additionally, these students exhibited a deeper understanding of concepts and were more active participants in classroom discussions, and mother-tongue instruction appeared to foster a greater sense of cultural identity and emotional connection to learning.

In another study, students educated in their indigenous language before transitioning to English had better comprehension skills than those educated solely in English, with the study emphasizing the importance of gradual transition, as abrupt introduction to English often leads to learning difficulties.

## Nigerian Pidgin Specifically

However, the research on Nigerian Pidgin specifically shows a different perspective. One study found that 94% of respondents posited that Nigerian Pidgin affects academic performance, with results indicating a significant relationship between Nigerian Pidgin and students' performance in English studies.

## Key Distinction

It's important to note that most of the positive findings relate to **mother-tongue instruction** in indigenous languages (like Yoruba, Igbo, and Hausa), rather than Nigerian Pidgin specifically. While Pidgin can facilitate communication across ethnic groups, the research suggests that for academic purposes, structured instruction in either standard English or established native languages appears more effective than pidgin-based instruction.
