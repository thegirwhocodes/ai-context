## Dollar Smile Theory Overview

The "Dollar Smile" theory was introduced by economist Stephen Jen in 2001, and it describes three scenarios for the USD: it strengthens during global crises (safe haven demand), weakens during stable global growth, and strengthens again when the US economy outperforms other countries.

## Safe Haven and Flight to Quality Mechanisms

The US dollar benefited from the global flight to safety into US Treasury bills in late 2008. More broadly, this phenomenon was evident during the 2008 financial crisis and the early stages of the Covid-19 pandemic, where global uncertainty triggered a surge in demand for the dollar through global deleveraging, a flight to quality and the unwinding of carry trades.

## Empirical Evidence by Crisis Period

**2008 Financial Crisis:** There was sharp appreciation of the currency during the 2008 financial crisis.

**2020 COVID-19 Pandemic:** Global uncertainty triggered a surge in demand for the dollar during the early stages of the Covid-19 pandemic.

## Recent Structural Changes

However, recent research suggests the dollar smile pattern is evolving. The dollar strengthens persistently and broadly only during episodes of severe global funding stress; in contrast to the yen and the Swiss franc, safe-haven shocks strengthen the dollar only temporarily. Additionally, structural shifts since the Global Financial Crisis have altered this dynamic, with capital flows and hedging behavior reducing the dollar's traditional safe haven role.
