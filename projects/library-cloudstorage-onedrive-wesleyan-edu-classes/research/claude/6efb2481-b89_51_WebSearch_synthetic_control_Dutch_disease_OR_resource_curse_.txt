## Synthetic Control Methodology Applied to Resource Curse Studies

Researchers have used synthetic control methods (SCM) to estimate the structural impacts of resource discoveries in major African resource economies, demonstrating how synthetic controls can be leveraged to evaluate national policy-relevant events in developing economies.

### Ghana Case Study

A notable application involves Ghana's oil discovery. By constructing a "Synthetic Ghana" from a donor pool of non-oil-producing sub-Saharan African countries, researchers estimated what Ghana's economic trajectory would have been without oil development. The sectoral analysis provided strong evidence of Dutch Disease, with findings suggesting that the oil boom induced a "Resource Movement Effect," crowding out non-resource tradable sectors and potentially increasing the economy's long-term vulnerability to commodity price shocks.

### Angola Context

Studies examining Angola have used statistical techniques to identify the presence of Dutch disease, including nonlinear autoregressive distributed lag (NARDL) models to identify cointegrated relationships between international oil prices and the real effective exchange rate. Despite being the second largest oil exporter in Africa, Angola continues to lag behind in most macroeconomic indicators, at least partially as a consequence of Dutch disease, which establishes a link between high resource endowments and lack of economic diversity through loss of competitiveness in non-resource sectors.

### Mexico and Mexico's Experience

During the mini-oil boom of 1979-81, countries like Mexico, Venezuela and Nigeria saw rapid appreciation in the exchange rate due to oil discovery, resulting in virtually no other industries remaining internationally competitive.
