# The one mental model everything hangs on

There are **two different questions**, and pros keep them separate:

| | Question | Name | "Did we…" |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Verification** | Does the product *work the way we built it to*? | Engineering QA | …build the thing **right**? |
| **Validation** | Does the product *actually solve the user's problem*? | Product validation | …build the **right thing**? |

A product can be perfectly verified (zero bugs) and still fail validation (nobody needed it). Or it can validate beautifully (kids love it) but be unverified (it crashes at 200k calls). **You have to pass both gates before public launch.** Most first-time founders obsess over one and skip the other. I'll teach both.

# Part 1 — Engineering verification (does it work as built?)

## 1. The testing pyramid
Engineers layer automated tests, cheapest-and-most-numerous at the bottom:

- **Unit tests** — test one function in isolation ("does `numeric_grading` mark 45 correct?"). Fast, hundreds of them.
- **Integration tests** — test that pieces work *together* ("does STT → grading → learning-state advance the lesson?").
- **End-to-end (E2E) tests** — drive the whole system like a real user ("place a call, answer a question, verify the lesson advances"). Slow, few, but highest confidence.
- **Regression tests** — a permanent test for every bug you've *already* fixed, so it can never silently come back. *This is the single highest-ROI habit in software.*

> **Sabi already does this** — your `*_regression.py` suites (feedback 31/31, keypad, flash-callback, guardrails 47/47, launch-gates) are exactly this. The fake-child harness is your E2E layer. You're ahead of most startups here.

## 2. Code review
No code reaches production without a second person (or a strong tool) reading it. Catches bugs, spreads knowledge, kills the "single point of failure" risk. Mechanism: **pull requests** — you propose a change, someone approves, then it merges. When you hire your Tech Lead, this becomes non-negotiable.

## 3. CI/CD (Continuous Integration / Continuous Deployment)
A robot that runs your entire test suite automatically on every change, and blocks the deploy if anything fails. "**Green build**" = all tests pass. This is what stops a 2am tired edit from breaking production. (Your Vercel deploys are the "CD" half; the "CI" half is running tests automatically before deploy.)

## 4. Environments — never test on production
The professional ladder of "places code lives":
- **Local / dev** — your laptop.
- **Staging** — a full copy of production, with fake data, where you rehearse. *Looks* like prod, hurts no real users.
- **Production** — the real thing, real users.

> Sabi's version of this is your **isolated test lanes** (AudioSocket 9020 for Chatterbox/Intron while 9019 stays untouched). That IS staging discipline applied to a phone system. Smart.

## 5. Monitoring & observability
After launch you're blind without instruments. Three things pros wire up *before* going public:
- **Error tracking** — Sentry (you have this on sabi-server now) catches crashes with a stack trace and alerts you.
- **Logging** — a searchable record of what happened, so you can reconstruct a failure.
- **Metrics/dashboards** — uptime, latency, cost-per-call, error rate, graphed over time.
- **Alerting** — a text/page when something breaks at 3am, so you find out before your users do.

The mantra: **"you can't fix what you can't see."** Launching without monitoring is flying blind.

## 6. Load / stress / performance testing
Before public traffic, you *deliberately* overwhelm the system to find its breaking point. "Works for 1 caller" ≠ "works for 200,000." You simulate the real peak load and watch what melts (server CPU, STT queue, database connections). **This is a live gap for Sabi** — your plan literally has "upgrade server for 200k kids" as a step; that upgrade must be *load-tested*, not hoped.

## 7. Security testing
- **Dependency scanning** — are the libraries you use full of known holes?
- **Penetration testing** — someone tries to break in on purpose.
- **Secrets hygiene** — no API keys in code (your `.env` discipline).
- For you specifically: **child-data protection** (NDPA/DPIA) is a security-and-compliance gate, not optional.

## 8. Rollback & disaster recovery plan
Pros assume they *will* break production, so they prepare the undo *before* launching: "if this deploy is bad, here's the one command to revert." Your Chatterbox/Intron lanes already have this ("flip the flag back, one container cycle"). Backups + a tested restore = you can survive a mistake.

# Part 2 — Product validation (is it the right thing?)

This is the entrepreneur half, and it's where founders who only think like coders get blindsided.

## 9. Dogfooding
"Eat your own dog food" — *you and your team use the product yourselves, daily, as a real user would.* You calling Sabi's line and going through a full lesson as if you were a child is dogfooding. It surfaces the awkward stuff no test catches (a prompt that plays 23 seconds before the beep — you found that by *listening*).

## 10. Usability testing
Sit a **real target user** (an actual Lagos child, not an adult tester) in front of the product and *watch silently*. Don't explain, don't help. Where they get confused is your bug list. The rule: **you are not your user.** An adult tester saying "thirty" clearly hides the fact that a distracted 7-year-old in a noisy room says "tati." Your whole STT fight exists because early testing was adults, not kids.

## 11. The beta ladder (this is the heart of "how you launch safely")
Nobody good goes from 0 → public. They climb a ladder, widening the audience only when each rung holds:

1. **Alpha** — internal only (you + team). Rough, expected to break.
2. **Closed / private beta** — a *small, hand-picked* group of real users under a nondisclosure/consent, who know it's early and give feedback. **Your 10-child pre-pilot is exactly this.**
3. **Canary release** — release to ~1% of traffic first; if the metrics stay healthy, widen. (Named after canaries in coal mines.) **Your single known-answer canary calls on the isolated lane are literally this.**
4. **Staged / phased rollout** — 1% → 10% → 50% → 100%, watching dashboards at each step, ready to roll back.
5. **General Availability (GA)** — full public launch. This is the *last* step, not the first.

> Sabi's climb: fake-child harness (alpha) → adult canary calls → **10-child pre-pilot (closed beta)** → 200k Lagos RCT (staged rollout) → public. You're already thinking in this ladder; you just may not have had the names.

## 12. Success metrics defined *before* launch
Pros decide **what "working" means in numbers** ahead of time, so they're not fooling themselves after. For a normal app: activation, retention, conversion. **For Sabi it's different and better: did the child actually learn** — pre/post assessment gains, the RCT. Define the number that means success *before* you launch, or you'll rationalize whatever you get.

## 13. Feedback capture built into the product
A way for users to tell you what's wrong *without* leaving. Your mid-call feedback hotkey (press `*`, Sabi stops and listens) is a beautiful example — most products bury feedback in a form nobody fills out; you put it one keypress away.
