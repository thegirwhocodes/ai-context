## Study Details

The paper "Adaptation to the Spillover Economic Effects of Foreign Conflict Shocks: Evidence from Boko Haram" is authored by Carlos Rodríguez-Castelán, B. Blankespoor, R. Jedwab and T. Masaki, published in World Development in 2025.

## Key Findings

The study examines how the Boko Haram insurgency in Northeast Nigeria impacted border regions in Cameroon, Chad and Niger using a difference-in-difference framework leveraging the breakout of the Boko Haram insurgency in Northeastern Nigeria in 2009.

The average effect within 50 km is −0.15, implying that Boko Haram reduces nighttime lights by 15 percent, with effects for 50–100 km, 100–150 km and 150–200 km showing -15, −11 and −7 percent reductions, respectively.

The study found strong negative effects on regional economic activities – proxied by reductions in nighttime lights – particularly amongst areas within 200 km from the Boko Haram area, with this negative impact concentrated in urban areas as trade was impacted and economic uncertainty rose.

The research found smaller resilience effects in those areas with worse geography, less agricultural development, more limited infrastructure, and weaker markets and institutions, suggesting that conflicts may have larger spillover effects in geographically challenging and/or economically poorer regions.
