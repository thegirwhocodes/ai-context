# Technical Details: Few-Shot Prototypical Networks for ASL Recognition

## Architecture Overview

The system combines three main components:

**Backbone Encoder**: A Spatiotemporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) processes skeletal keypoints partitioned into body (core joints), hands (21 per hand), and face regions. The network produces frame-level embeddings of dimension D=768.

**Multi-Scale Temporal Aggregation (MSTA)**: "Parallel 1D Convolutional branches with varying kernel sizes (k∈{3,5,7})" capture movements at different speeds. An attention pooling mechanism compresses variable-length sequences into fixed-size video representations using weighted temporal aggregation.

**Prototypical Classification**: Rather than fixed classifiers, the system computes class prototypes as mean embeddings from support examples, then classifies queries by Euclidean distance to these prototypes.

## Prototypical Networks for Sign Language

The approach leverages the principle that signs exhibit "standard" canonical motions. Each class prototype represents the averaged motion pattern: "the prototype 𝐩c is the mean vector of the embedded support samples." Classification uses softmax over negative distances to nearby prototypes.

The system trains episodically with N-way and K-shot configurations, where each episode contains N sign classes with K support examples each.

## Performance on WLASL

**Main Results**:
- 43.75% Top-1 accuracy (test set)
- 77.10% Top-5 accuracy
- 84.50% Top-10 accuracy

This represents "over 13%" improvement versus a standard classification baseline achieving 30.37% Top-1.

**N-Way Impact**: Testing showed consistent gains with episode difficulty:
- 50-Way: 40.62% Top-1
- 100-Way: 43.43% Top-1
- 200-Way: 43.75% Top-1

## Training Requirements

**Data**: The framework uses K=3 shots per class and Q=2 query samples per episode, trained for 50,000 episodes total with the Adam optimizer.

**Examples Needed**: The approach targets scenarios where "the vast majority of signs contain fewer than 13 instances," making it suitable for long-tail vocabulary distributions where most signs have 5+ examples available.

**Optimization**: Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) and custom episodic batch sampling enable scaling to higher N-way configurations.

## Cross-Dataset Generalization

Zero-shot evaluation on SignASL (19,000 videos, different signers and conditions):
- Prototypical model: 29.87% Top-1, 44.00% Top-5
- Baseline classifier: 24.12% Top-1

The 5% improvement demonstrates metric space robustness versus fixed classifiers.

## Comparison to Other Methods

The paper compares against "a standard classification baseline sharing the identical ST-GCN + MSTA backbone." The baseline uses 50 training epochs with a fully connected linear classifier, achieving substantially lower accuracy across all metrics.

The authors note their approach avoids dependence on external pre-training, unlike methods like Uni-Sign that "rely heavily on the availability of large-scale pre-training data."
