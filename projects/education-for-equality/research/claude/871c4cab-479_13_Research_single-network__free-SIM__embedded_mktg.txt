# Single-Network + Free-SIM + MNO Sponsorship for the Sabi Pilot — Research Findings

**Bottom line up front:** A single-network, free-SIM pilot is a genuinely strong operational and measurement move, and there are real precedents. But a *funded sponsorship deal from an MNO at pilot stage* is the part that's premature — sponsorship money follows proof, not the other way around. The right play is to go single-network and self-fund the SIMs/airtime for a small pilot, while *structuring the pilot so the MNO's incentives are visible* and using the pilot results to land the sponsorship at scale. On network choice, the conventional answer is MTN (coverage), but there's a contrarian case for **Glo** that's unusually strong right now — detailed in Point 5.

One honesty note up front: I could **not** verify the "Viamo 16% churn" figure as a documented public Viamo statistic. Multiple targeted searches did not surface it. What *is* documented is that Viamo's telco value proposition is customer segmentation/demographic data and content-driven engagement, and the *industry-wide* churn-reduction numbers from value-added services are real (10–14% range; up to 30% ARPU lift). Treat "16%" as needing a primary source before you put it in a pitch deck.

## 1. Strategic advantages of single-network + free-SIM (and the trade-offs)

**The on-net free triggers are real and substantial on Glo specifically:**

- **Glo CUG (Closed User Group, "POLCOM"):** FREE 24/7 calls and SMS between all members of the group, anywhere in the country. One account can register **up to 50 members**. Monthly fee ₦500 (first month free). This is the single most important finding for your model — if every pilot family's SIM is in *one Sabi CUG*, on-net calls among them are free, and you control the cost structure centrally. Calls outside CUG drop to ~20 kobo/sec. ([Campus Times](https://campustimesng.com/glo-closed-user-group-cug-subscription-services/), [Median Nigeria](https://www.medianigeria.com/what-to-know-about-glo-cug/))
- **Beep/Flash/Missed-call ("beeping"):** Costs the caller nothing because the call never connects — 20–30% of all calls in sub-Saharan Africa are intentional misses. A child can "beep" Sabi's number with zero balance, and Sabi calls back. This is the canonical zero-cost trigger for the population you're serving. ([LIRNEasia](https://lirneasia.net/2007/09/missed-calls-beeping-flashing-a-universal-strategy/), [Wikipedia: Missed call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missed_call))
- **Recharge bonuses (Glo):** 15% airtime bonus on every e-top-up ≥₦200; Yakata up to 2,200% bonus; Overload Reloaded lets bonus airtime be used for Glo-to-Glo calls 10pm–8am. These let a small top-up stretch much further *on-net*. ([Glo](https://www.gloworld.com/ng/glo-independence-day-double-celebration-offer), [SubBase](https://subbase.com.ng/blog/glo-get-15-extra-airtime-on-every-e-top-up/))

**Other single-network advantages:**
- **Simpler ops:** one set of APNs, one short-code/number regime, one billing relationship, one technical integration (E1/SIP) — Viamo integrates per-operator via E1 or SIP, so each added MNO is real engineering work.
- **One negotiation:** you can actually get a meeting and a deal with one MNO; you cannot meaningfully negotiate with four at pilot scale.
- **Clean measurement:** all CDRs (call detail records), call durations, and engagement data sit with one operator — far easier to prove impact and to give the MNO a clean attribution story.
- **Zero-rating leverage:** concentrating volume on one network makes a zero-rating ask (see Point 4) concrete and easy for the MNO to scope.

**Trade-offs you must weigh:**
- **Coverage gaps:** Glo is the weakest of the big three on network quality — 45 major outages Jan–May 2025, download speeds ~2× slower than MTN, only 13,800 km fibre vs MTN's 31,972 km, and rural insecurity hampering its towers. For out-of-school kids who are disproportionately *rural*, this is the central risk of choosing Glo. ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/07/16/glo-subscriber-loss-opens-door-for-9mobile-comeback/), [Punch](https://punchng.com/glo-nigeria-leading-infrastructure-expansion-in-telecom-sector/))
- **Family already has a SIM / dual-SIM behavior:** **66% of Nigerian phones are dual-SIM** (highest in the world). This cuts both ways: a free Sabi SIM does *not* require the family to abandon their existing line — it just goes in slot 2. That's good for adoption but means your "new subscriber" claim to the MNO is softer (it's an additional line, often lightly used, not a switched primary line). ([GeoPoll](https://www.geopoll.com/blog/mobile-penetration-nigeria/), [Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/business-services/why-multi-sim-ownership-is-increasing-in-nigeria/))
- **NIN registration friction:** Every SIM must be NIN-linked. Many out-of-school children and poor rural families may lack NINs — this is *the* operational blocker (it's literally what cost Glo 43M subscribers when enforcement hit). You may need to register SIMs under a parent/guardian NIN, which has its own implications.

## 2. Why an MNO actually benefits (the incentive alignment)

**Subscriber acquisition.** Each free SIM = a new NIN-registered, activated line. With 66% dual-SIM, you're realistically adding *secondary* lines, but operators still count and value gross adds — and for Glo specifically, which is *hemorrhaging* subscribers (lost ~21,000 internet users/day Dec 2024–May 2025, market share at a record-low 11.9–12.3%), net adds are existentially valuable right now. ([TechCabal: market share](https://techcabal.com/2025/05/27/glo-market-share-falls-to-a-record-low-of-11-9/))

**ARPU growth as families top up.** Nigerian ARPU is low but rising fast post-2025 tariff hike: **MTN Nigeria ARPU ~$3.60** (up ~66% in 2025), **Airtel Nigeria ~$2.10** (up ~24% YoY). The thesis: a family that gets a free SIM for their child's education forms a habit, tops up for other uses, and becomes a real revenue line. Even at $2/month ARPU, a cohort of a few thousand retained lines is a measurable revenue contribution against near-zero incremental cost (the airtime is on-net, which costs the operator close to nothing in marginal terms). ([TechCabal: ARPU](https://techcabal.com/2025/08/20/nigeria-telcos-arpu-rises-31-percent/))

**Retention / churn reduction.** This is the strongest *strategic* argument and the one with the best industry evidence (even if the specific Viamo "16%" isn't sourceable):
- GSMA Intelligence: operators offering value-added services saw **up to 30% ARPU increase** from higher retention/engagement.
- Free streaming/bundled free services cut churn ~**10%**; loyalty + usage alerts ~**14%**. ([Enable3](https://enable3.io/blog/telecom-loyalty-programs), [PortaOne/GSMA](https://blog.portaone.com/vas-opportunities-in-telecom/))
- A literacy service is *stickier* than streaming: a parent will not switch the SIM their child is learning on. That's a defensible churn-reduction story to pitch.

**Data / segmentation.** Viamo's documented telco value is exactly this: *"Viamo's relationship with network operators grants it the benefit of having access to customers' demographic information,"* and operators disclose geographic location so usage can be mapped. ([Borgen Project](https://borgenproject.org/viamos-services/)) Flip it: you can offer the MNO a clean, consented dataset on an underserved youth segment they otherwise can't reach.

**CSR/ESG.** This is the real near-term currency. Airtel Africa Foundation deployed **$6.2M in its first year** and committed **$57M** total to education; it supported **64 zero-rated learning platforms reaching 11M+ learners** and connected 3,296 schools across 13 countries. MTN and Glo run comparable foundations. A children's-literacy program is *exactly* the kind of headline a telco foundation wants. ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/airtel-africa-and-unicef-announce-multi-million-dollar-partnership-scale-digital), [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/airtel-africa-foundation-commits-6-2m-to-education-digital-and-financial-inclusion-across-africa/))

**What the precedents teach:**
- **Free-SIM-to-families** is real but is almost always **government- or foundation-funded** (Bayanihan, Eastern Cape), or done by an entity that *became its own MVNO* (STADIO). No one found it commercially sensible for an MNO to give a tiny pilot free SIMs on pure commercial terms.
- **Single-MNO exclusivity** is the *starting* structure (Viamo's original 3-2-1, Eneza/Safaricom) that later broadens. Starting single-network is well-trodden.
- The **VAS + revenue-share + discounted-rates + telco-collects-payment** model (Eneza/Safaricom) is the most replicable commercial structure if you ever charge.

## 4. Embedded marketing / MNO sponsorship from the start

**What the MNO can plausibly fund:** SIMs + on-net airtime + zero-rating + branding + CSR reporting. Precedent for each piece exists (STADIO zero-rates its platforms; Vodafone/Airtel zero-rate learning content; Viamo gets free distribution + operators *advertise its content*).

**Structuring options, by realism at pilot stage:**

- **(a) Branding ("Glo teaches Nigeria to read"):** Most realistic ask. Telco foundations *want* this headline. Low cost to them, pure CSR/PR value. Achievable even at pilot stage **if** you frame it as a foundation/CSR grant, not a marketing line-item.
- **(b) Embedded ads inside lessons (branded Naira word-problems, airtime-recharge mentions):** You already have `EMBEDDED_MARKETING_RESEARCH.md` on this. On the *MNO-partnership* side: this is the natural quid-pro-quo for sponsorship — the MNO funds free airtime, and lesson content uses Glo-branded scenarios ("Ada has ₦200 Glo airtime…"). But be cautious: NCC's Internet Code of Practice and consumer-protection regs, plus child-protection norms, make *advertising to children* sensitive. Keep it soft (brand presence, financial-literacy scenarios) rather than hard calls-to-purchase. Realistic only once you have a relationship — not a cold pilot ask.
- **(c) Subscriber acquisition as the commercial justification:** This is what you *lead with* to the commercial (not foundation) side of the MNO — especially Glo, given its subscriber crisis. But at *pilot* scale (hundreds of families) the gross-add numbers are too small to move a commercial team. It only becomes a real commercial pitch at scale (tens of thousands of lines).
- **(d) CSR/ESG metrics:** The most fundable channel today. Airtel Foundation's $6.2M and Vodafone/Airtel zero-rating CSR show the budget exists and the reporting appetite is high. Package the pilot as "X children, Y hours of literacy instruction, Z% reading-gain — attributable to [MNO]."

**Zero-rating is permitted but gated in Nigeria:** The NCC Internet Code of Practice allows zero-rating **only with prior NCC approval and only to further universal access** — preferential prioritization is forbidden. A children's-literacy zero-rating is a *textbook* "universal access" justification, so it's approvable, but it's a regulatory step, not a handshake. ([Templars/NCC](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/01/TEL24_Chapter-15_Nigeria.pdf), [ICLG Nigeria 2026](https://iclg.com/practice-areas/telecoms-media-and-internet-laws-and-regulations/nigeria))

**Realistic at pilot stage?** Branding + a modest CSR/foundation grant covering SIMs and a block of on-net airtime: **yes, plausibly.** Embedded paid ads, zero-rating approval, and a commercial subscriber-acquisition deal: **scale-stage**, because they need either regulatory process or numbers you won't have yet. The honest sequencing: *foundation/CSR grant now → commercial + zero-rating deal after pilot data.*

## 5. Recommendation

**Go single-network. Self-fund (or CSR-grant-fund) the free SIMs for the pilot. Do NOT bank on a commercial MNO sponsorship before you have data — but structure the pilot so the MNO can see its incentives, and pitch the CSR/foundation arm now for SIMs + airtime + branding.**

Rationale: every documented free-SIM-to-families program was government/foundation-funded or self-funded via MVNO; commercial MNO sponsorship reliably *follows* proof of engagement (Eneza got equity investment *after* traction; Viamo gets free distribution *because* it brings content + segmentation value). A pilot's gross-adds are too small for a commercial deal, but perfectly sized for a CSR grant and a "powered by" headline.

**Which network — the contrarian recommendation is Glo, with one big caveat:**

- **The case FOR Glo:** (1) **CUG gives you free on-net calls for up to 50 members per group** — directly exploitable for a closed pilot cohort, and the single best cost lever found. (2) Glo is **desperate for subscribers** (record-low 11.9–12.3% share, lost 43M subs, bleeding ~21k/day) — which means your free-SIM gross-adds and a retention/CSR story land *much* harder there than at MTN, and Glo is the most likely to say yes to a sponsorship/branding deal to generate good news. (3) Glo is **Nigerian-owned (Adenuga)** with a domestic-pride brand — "Glo teaches Nigeria to read" is on-brand for them.
- **The caveat AGAINST Glo:** network quality is its weakness — 2× slower than MTN, frequent outages, weak rural fibre. For a **voice** product serving **rural** out-of-school kids, call reliability is mission-critical. This is the one thing that could sink a Glo pilot.

**So the decision rule:**
- If your pilot is **urban/peri-urban** (Lagos, Abuja, where Glo has densified) → **Glo** is the smart, leverageable choice: best free-call mechanics + most motivated partner.
- If your pilot is **rural** → default to **MTN** for coverage reliability (52% share, best fibre/coverage, STADIO chose MTN for its MVNO), and accept a harder sponsorship conversation. Airtel is the strong middle option with the most *demonstrated* education-CSR spend ($57M commitment, UNICEF partnership) if you want a partner with an existing edu-program machine to plug into.

**Concrete next steps implied by the research:**
1. **Run a Glo coverage check** at your actual pilot sites before committing (Glo's quality is location-dependent; it's fine in Lagos/Abuja, risky rurally).
2. **Approach the foundation/CSR arm first**, not the commercial team — ask for SIMs + a block of on-net airtime + branding, framed as CSR with a reading-outcomes report.
3. **Set up a single Sabi CUG** for the cohort to zero out on-net call costs.
4. **Solve the NIN problem** explicitly (guardian-NIN registration) — it's the real operational blocker, and it's what already gutted Glo's base.
5. **Find a primary source for the "16% churn" claim** before using it; lean on the defensible GSMA VAS numbers (10–30%) and the *stickiness* argument instead.
