## 🌶️ Habanero hot sauce vs fresh habaneros — honest answer

**Is it a healthy substitute?** Safe and edible, but **not equivalent for Nigerian cooking.** The catalog's hot sauces are vinegar-based, which means:
- ✅ Same heat (Scotch bonnet & habanero are both ~100K–350K SHU — same species, *Capsicum chinense*)
- ❌ Vinegar tang + salt added — doesn't replicate the *fresh blended pepper paste* that's the base of Nigerian stew/jollof
- ❌ Less smoky/fruity than fresh

**Shelf life:**
- **Unopened: 2–3 years** at room temp
- **Opened: 6 months at room temp, 2+ years refrigerated** (the vinegar preserves it)

**Better alternatives for Nigerian stew:**
1. **Fresh habanero / scotch bonnet** from Stop & Shop / Asian market / Caribbean grocery (always best)
2. **Frozen pepper puree** — pre-blend a batch with onions/tomatoes when fresh peppers are cheap, freeze in ice cube trays
3. **Dried ground habanero powder** — Frontier sells cayenne/chili powder; you could add 1 tsp per stew

**Bottom line:** For authentic stew, fresh > hot sauce. Hot sauce works as a backup or condiment, but 12 bottles is way more than needed if it's not the primary cooking base.
