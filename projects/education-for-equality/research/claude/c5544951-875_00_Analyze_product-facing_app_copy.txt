## F. STRUCTURE AND FORMATTING HABITS

**Sentence length:**
- Hero subheads and body: medium-length sentences with em-dashes for rhythm. "We deliver free, AI-powered literacy and numeracy courses to anyone who needs them — on any phone."
- Feature callouts: deliberately short, punchy fragments. "No smartphone. No internet. No cost." / "Just a phone call."
- Stat card descriptors: one-sentence plain English under the number.

**Use of statistics:**
- Stats are always displayed as oversized bold numerals (e.g., "70%", "161-260x", "$40B", "47M") with a short explanatory sentence below and a small-print citation line beneath that. Pattern is consistent across landing and sabi pages.
- Citations are always present — never floating statistics without attribution.
- Pattern: large number → 1 bold sentence label → 1-2 sentence explanation → 1 tiny-print citation.

**Second person:**
- Not used in hero or body copy describing the situation. The voice addresses the problem as "children" / "learners" (third person) not "you."
- "You" appears only in CTA labels ("Start Reading," "Browse Grade 4 Lessons") and in the sample Sabi conversation ("You got it!").

**Em-dash usage:**
- Frequent and characteristic of the brand voice: "anyone who needs them — on any phone," "No smartphone. No internet. No cost," "their name, their level, their mistakes."

**Lists:**
- About page uses bullet lists with bold lead terms ("Donate - Help us expand..."). This is the only page using this pattern.
- Sabi page "How It Works" section uses numbered steps (1-5) with bold titles and a sentence description — more structured than bullets.

**Capitalization of section headings:**
- Title Case throughout: "The Learning Crisis," "Free Foundational Courses," "Why Learn With Us?", "The Economic Case for Foundational Skills," "The Science Behind Sabi," "Built for the Last Mile."

**Emoji and punctuation:**
- Zero emoji anywhere across all five pages.
- Exclamation points appear only inside the sample Sabi conversation dialogue ("You got it!") — never in UI headings or body copy addressed to the web visitor.
- Question marks used selectively in section headings: "Why Learn With Us?" / "What if we used the infrastructure that already exists?"

**Paragraph length:**
- Body paragraphs are short: typically 1-3 sentences. The founder story is the longest continuous prose (three short paragraphs of 2-3 sentences each).

## G. INCONSISTENCIES AND OFF-BRAND COPY

**1. About page is significantly behind the rest of the site in positioning.**
The about page subhead reads "Empowering underprivileged children in Nigeria through accessible, quality education." Meanwhile the landing and sabi pages have evolved to address adults as well as children, and explicitly position around foundational skills and AI delivery. The about page makes no mention of Sabi, AI, voice, or phone delivery. It describes the solution as "Video Lessons" and "Mobile Accessible" (i.e., a web app) — which is the old product framing, not the current voice-AI-over-phone framing. The about meta description still says "underprivileged children in Nigeria" (not "learners across Africa").

**2. "underprivileged" appears on the about page but nowhere else.**
The rest of the site uses "children who never had the chance to learn," "out-of-school children," or "every learner." "Underprivileged" is more dated nonprofit language and reads differently from the tone of the landing and sabi pages.

**3. "Video Lessons" feature card on about page is inaccurate.**
The about/Our Solution section lists four feature cards: "Free Curriculum," "Video Lessons," "Mobile Accessible," and "Progress Tracking." Three of these are web-app features and do not describe Sabi. "Video Lessons" in particular describes the old curriculum-app product, not the phone-based AI tutor that is now the primary product.

**4. "students" on about page vs. "learners" / "children" everywhere else.**
The about page uses "Students can track their learning progress." The rest of the site consistently uses "learners" or "children," not "students." This is a minor terminology inconsistency.

**5. "Cambridge-Aligned" capitalized badge vs. "Cambridge-aligned" in prose on subjects page.**
Subjects page line 134 uses the badge label "Cambridge-Aligned" (capital A), while line 103 in running prose says "Cambridge-aligned" (lowercase a). Minor but inconsistent.

**6. Stat format inconsistency between landing and sabi pages.**
Landing page: "70% Of 10-year-olds in Nigeria cannot read..." (percentage, stat card). Sabi page for the same fact: "7 in 10 — Nigerian 10-year-olds who cannot read a single sentence." These are the same statistic expressed differently, which is actually a deliberate stylistic choice (stat cards vs. prose), but they appear in structurally identical card formats on the two pages. A visitor who views both pages may notice the framing change.

**7. The advisor-board page is an internal admin tool with no gating.**
The page at `/advisor-board` is publicly routable and exposes internal ops language ("Advisor Board Command Center," "open asks," "Agent notes," "Naomi wants this set to naomi.ivie04@gmail.com") and a personal Gmail address. This is not a brand voice issue per se, but it is an exposure risk. It is the one page whose copy is entirely inward-facing and should not be visible to the public.

**8. Footer on sabi page links to "Curriculum App" and "Website" both pointing to the same URL (`https://eduforequality.org`).**
This is a placeholder / copy error, not a voice inconsistency, but it would look odd to a visitor.
