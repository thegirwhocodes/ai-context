## Key Findings:

**Spurgeon's Views on God Speaking Through Dreams and Visions:**
God has spoken to men in dreams, in visions of the night, when deep sleep falleth upon them, according to Spurgeon's sermon "The Majestic Voice." This indicates Spurgeon acknowledged dreams and visions as legitimate means through which God communicates.

**Spurgeon's Stance on Remarkable Supernatural Experiences:**
Interestingly, Spurgeon cautioned against believers expecting "remarkable dreams, or visions, or striking emotions of excited joy" as necessary indicators of salvation. He argued that some people unnecessarily delay their faith by waiting for such dramatic experiences.

**Spurgeon on Sensible Experiences:**
Spurgeon stated "the fewer you have of them, I think, the better—especially of things which are visionary. I rather tremble for those who talk much about sensible evidences", suggesting he was cautious about overemphasis on supernatural experiences.

**Spurgeon's Word of Knowledge Experience:**
The search also revealed an account of Spurgeon pointing to a man in his congregation and providing specific details about his personal life (that he was a shoemaker keeping his shop open on Sundays with specific profit information), which the man confirmed was accurate. Spurgeon described this as "a proof that God sometimes guides His servants to say what they would themselves never have thought of uttering."

Overall, while Spurgeon acknowledged God's ability to communicate through supernatural means, he emphasized caution against overreliance on such experiences for assurance of salvation.
