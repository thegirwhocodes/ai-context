## Evidence-Based Phonics Instruction in Developing Countries

**Core Instructional Components:**
Evidence-based reading instruction should deliver explicit, systematic and comprehensive instruction in six core skills: oral language, phonological awareness, systematic phonics, reading fluency, reading comprehension, and writing.

**Phonological Awareness Sequence:**
The developmental sequence of phonological awareness is typically described as progressing from metalinguistic awareness of rhyme, to awareness of syllables, and lastly phonemes.

**Phonics-Based Teaching:**
Evidence gathered over the past two decades from the science of reading suggests that all children can learn to read, provided they receive high-quality, systematic, and explicit phonics instruction. Systematic phonics instruction refers to teaching children the specific relationships between letters and sounds, and how to combine these to form words, so children learn to 'sound out' unfamiliar words by identifying each letter's sound and blending them together.

**Context for Africa and Developing Countries:**
Research synthesizing evidence from low- and middle-income countries reviews around 120 studies on effective reading instruction conducted across Africa, Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East covering more than 170 different languages. In Tanzania, teachers were found to lack sufficient training on the 3Rs (Reading, Writing, and Arithmetic), which affected their pedagogical knowledge of phonics instructional approaches.

**Implementation Recommendations:**
While the core principles of evidence-aligned reading instruction are universal, successful programs tailor instruction to address contextual needs and provide teachers with structured support, user-friendly materials, and ongoing professional development.
