Nigeria loses about $40 billion in potential revenue due to the less educated population, according to a report by SB Morgan (SBM) Intelligence. SBM, a research organisation, said the rate of regular school attendance and early childhood education remains poor in the country.

The report highlights significant challenges in Nigeria's education sector. Data from the United Nations Children's Fund revealed that about 20 million Nigerian children between the ages of 5 and 14 are classified as out-of-school. The 2021 National Bureau of Statistics multiple indicator cluster survey indicated that only 61 percent of six to eleven-year-olds attend primary schools regularly in Nigeria.

Regarding the economic impact, Nigeria loses a whopping $40 billion, roughly 7.83% of GDP, in lost revenue productivity due to a less educated population, which is larger than the combined GDP of Mali, Chad and Burkina Faso.

The report also emphasizes that children who are deprived of quality education are at risk of damaged foundation which exposes them to social incompetence that prevents them from being properly integrated into family, workplace or society.
