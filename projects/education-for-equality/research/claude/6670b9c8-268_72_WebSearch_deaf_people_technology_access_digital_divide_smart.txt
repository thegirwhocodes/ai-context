## Key Barriers to Digital Access for Deaf People

**Accessibility Issues:**
Barriers include inaccessible websites, lack of sign language options in digital tools, and limited availability of assistive devices. Deaf people may lack access because videos and podcasts lack captions or transcripts.

**Content and Platform Gaps:**
The d/Deaf and Hard of Hearing communities face obstacles including lack of captions and interpretations, low-quality audio, and social media inaccessibility. While TikTok took years to add automatic captioning, Clubhouse has yet to release any live captioning feature—essentially blocking the d/Deaf and Hard of Hearing communities from participating.

**Internet and Device Access:**
There is a gap of 16 percentage points between those with a disability and those without one in smartphone ownership (72% vs. 88%). Remote learning and work can be difficult due to limited internet access.

**Employment Impact:**
In 2017, only 53.3 percent of deaf working-age adults were employed, compared with 75.8 percent of hearing people—an employment gap of 22.5 percent that has not increased since 2008.

**Technology Mastery Challenges:**
Deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals face barriers to the mastery of digital technology tools, among which barriers to the use of smartphones, to the accessibility of online medical consultations, and to the presentation of health codes, were most noteworthy.
