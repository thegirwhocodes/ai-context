## World Bank Education Initiatives in Nigeria

The World Bank approved $1.08 billion in concessional financing for Nigeria in March 2025, including $500 million for the HOPE-EDU (Hope for Quality Basic Education for All) program. HOPE-EDU will support structured pedagogy approaches to foundational literacy and numeracy, create learning opportunities where school overcrowding impedes participation, and adopt decentralized allocation and management of education funds.

## Economic Impact and Skills Development

The HOPE-EDU program aims to equip Nigeria's fast-growing young population with foundational skills and knowledge necessary for rapid and inclusive economic growth. More broadly, globally, there is about a 10% increase in hourly earnings for every extra year of schooling, and this generation of children could lose a combined total of US$21 trillion in lifetime earnings in present value or the equivalent of 17% of today's global GDP without quality education.

## Nigeria's Literacy Status

Nigeria's adult literacy rate was 70.41% in 2024, though by 2024, Nigeria's literacy rate had increased to 86.3% for both sexes, with males at 90% and females at 82.7% according to another recent source, showing some variations in data.

## Broader African Context

Aid accounts for 5 percent of total education expenditure in Africa and is declining, highlighting the importance of World Bank financing for education on the continent.
