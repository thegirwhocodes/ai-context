## The Problem (the research question)

The most widely-cited figure for Nigeria's education crisis — **$40 billion/year lost** — comes from Milan & Burnett (2014), applied to Nigeria by CSEA/IDRC. But it has two critical weaknesses:

1. **It uses the wrong wage data.** Milan & Burnett used Psacharopoulos & Patrinos (2011) global wage premiums — 30% return to primary education. But Aromolaran (2004), using Nigeria's General Household Survey, found Nigeria's actual primary return is only **~2.5%/year (~15% total for 6 years)** — half the global estimate. If Nigeria's true returns are lower, the $40B is overstated.

2. **It only counts out-of-school children (OOSC).** It completely ignores the **23+ million enrolled children who cannot read** (70%, World Bank 2022) — children who attend school but gain almost no human capital. This population may actually be a bigger economic problem than OOSC.

---

## What You're Doing (the paper)

**Title:** *"Updating the Economic Cost of Out-of-School Children in Nigeria: A Country-Specific Estimation Using Nigerian Wage Return Data"*
**For:** Wesleyan Economics coursework/thesis | Author: Naomi Ivie

**Three research questions:**
1. What is the OOSC economic cost using Aromolaran's Nigerian wage data instead of global estimates?
2. What is the cost of in-school non-learning (the 70% who attend but can't read)? ← novel contribution, no existing study has done this
3. What is the TOTAL education crisis cost (OOSC + non-learners combined), and does it cross-validate against the World Bank HCI+ 2026?

**Methodology:**
- Replicate Milan & Burnett's equation using Aromolaran's 15% primary wage premium (vs their 30%)
- Extend the framework to in-school non-learners using: [% enrolled who can't read] × [full wage premium − actual wage premium received]
- Cross-validate against World Bank HCI+ 2026 derived estimate ($40–58B from education deficits)

**Preliminary numbers already calculated:**
- OOSC cost (Aromolaran): ~$16.8B (~5.88% of GDP) — vs Milan & Burnett's $40B
- In-school non-learning: ~$6–11B (preliminary, 2–4% of GDP)
- **Total: ~$23–28B**, with the remaining gap to HCI+ ($40–58B) likely reflecting macro externalities not captured by the micro wage approach

---

## The End Goal

Three layered purposes:

1. **Academic** — A genuine first: no study has ever estimated Nigeria's OOSC cost using Nigerian wage data, or estimated the non-learner cost at all. It's a publishable, citable contribution.

2. **E4E/Sabi's economic case** — The thesis becomes the most rigorous possible evidence base for grant applications and government pitches. Right now E4E cites SBM Intelligence/TheCable's $40B; after this paper, E4E can cite a peer-reviewed, country-specific analysis.

3. **Dual framing** — If Aromolaran is right and the true OOSC cost is $17B (not $40B), that's actually a stronger argument for Sabi: the problem is so bad even with lower wage returns, and adding the 23M non-learners shows the total crisis ($23–28B) is much bigger than any single published estimate.
