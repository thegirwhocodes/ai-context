## Key Findings:

**Moral Hazard and Risk-Taking:**
By providing explicit deposit guarantees and implicit guarantees, such as backing uninsured deposits, FDIC may create incentives for banks to engage in riskier behavior, such as investing in riskier assets or increasing risky lending, according to literature and academics. Additionally, the systemic risk exception may reduce depositor monitoring of their bank's activities, encouraging banks to take on excessive risk, as deposit insurance expansion may cause depositors to be less careful in selecting and monitoring their banks, and if depositors feel less afraid of losing their deposits, they may be less likely to withdraw funds from risk-taking banks.

**SVB Rapid Expansion:**
Prior research found that between 2019 and 2021, SVB and Signature Bank's total assets grew by 198 percent and 134 percent respectively, compared to a median growth of 33 percent among a group of 19 peer banks.

**Systemic Risk Exception and SVB:**
The Treasury secretary invoked the systemic risk exception for two banks that failed in March 2023, receiving recommendations from the FDIC and the Federal Reserve before making this decision, and GAO's analysis found that this decision likely helped prevent further financial instability.

**Policy Concerns:**
By using the systemic risk exception, policymakers have signaled that banks and their uninsured depositors need be less concerned about risk taking going forward.
