## Key Approaches for Small Datasets

**Few-Shot Learning with Geometry-Aware Methods:**
A geometry-aware approach proposes using 20 inter-joint angles computed from MediaPipe static hand keypoints, which are provably invariant to rotation, translation, and isotropic scaling. On multiple datasets including LIBRAS and Arabic, the angle representation paired with an MLP encoder consistently achieves the highest accuracy, with the largest gains over normalized raw coordinates appearing on Arabic (+25.3 percentage points at 5-shot) and LIBRAS (+12.9 percentage points).

**Temporal Models for Dynamic Signs:**
MediaPipe keypoints can be extracted and used to train an LSTM-based custom model called MP-GestLSTM, which achieved validation accuracy of 93.75% and testing accuracy of 94.38%. RNN models such as GRU, LSTM, and Bi-directional LSTM address the issue of frame dependency in sign movement.

**Transformer-Based Approaches:**
Recent Transformer-based pipelines address challenges through dynamic onset trimming to remove irrelevant idle frames at the beginning of video sequences and horizontal flip augmentation, which effectively mitigates handedness bias and significantly boosts recognition accuracy.

## Data Augmentation & Preprocessing

The approach for processing sign language videos includes keypoint extraction using MediaPipe, normalization of these keypoints, data augmentation techniques, and the development of the sign language translation model.

These methods suggest that for small datasets, using geometric representations (angles rather than raw coordinates) combined with few-shot learning frameworks like Prototypical Networks shows promise for achieving strong performance with limited training data.
