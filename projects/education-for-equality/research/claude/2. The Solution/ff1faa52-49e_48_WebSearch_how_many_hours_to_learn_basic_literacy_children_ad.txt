Based on my search results, I found some information about the hours required for literacy instruction, though the data varies depending on the context and approach:

## For Adults

It is estimated that 400 hours of instructional time is required to achieve basic literacy, though programme hours range from 250 hours (in Pakistan) to 450 hours (in Afghanistan).

## For Children

The research on children's literacy instruction is more granular, breaking down by specific skills:

**Phonemic Awareness (Pre-K through 1st Grade):**
Research found that for phonemic awareness instruction, students became better at the skill compared to a control group, but only up to a certain point: 10.2 hours total. However, when phonemic awareness instruction includes letters, intervention groups continued to show better phonemic awareness skills than control groups after 16 hours of instruction.

**First Grade Reading Development:**
The research suggests that foundational reading skills develop gradually throughout kindergarten and the primary grades. During one program, students received an average of eight hours of one-on-one individualized instruction and made over three weeks of progress in reading skills for every week in the program.

It's important to note that for children, foundational literacy skills develop over multiple years (kindergarten through 3rd grade), and the specific hours needed for individual components like phonemic awareness are much less than the total instructional time needed to achieve full literacy proficiency. Adult programs, by contrast, typically focus on achieving basic reading and writing skills in a more concentrated timeframe.
