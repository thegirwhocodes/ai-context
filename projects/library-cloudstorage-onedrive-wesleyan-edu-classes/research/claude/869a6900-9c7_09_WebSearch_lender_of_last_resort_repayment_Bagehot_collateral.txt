## Bagehot's Core Principles

Bagehot's dictum for the lender of last resort during a credit crunch is: Lend freely. At a high rate of interest. On good banking securities. Bagehot's reasoning for charging penalty rates (higher rates than are available in the market) was that it would really make the lender of last resort the very last resort and encourage prompt repayment of the debt.

## Collateral and Solvency

The lender of last resort should lend at a high interest rate against good quality collateral. The main purpose of the collateral requirement was to ensure that the central bank lent only to banks that were illiquid, not insolvent.

## Interest Rate Considerations

The penalty rate is intended to limit the demand for credit by institutions that are not in trouble—it can be interpreted as one of the ways of dealing with borrower moral hazard, the risk that financial institutions will take excessive risks in normal times secure in the knowledge that they will be able to borrow cheaply in tough times.

## Modern Deviations

Flannery and others mention that the Fed has neither asked for good collateral nor charged rates above the market, in recent years. The modern and much more comprehensive Lender of Last Resort function includes provision of liquidity and collateral, lowering interest rates and expansionary monetary policy, loosening collateral standards, and becoming market maker of last resort and buyer of last resort.
