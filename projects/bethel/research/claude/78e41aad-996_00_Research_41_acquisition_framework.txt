## RIA Acquisition Evaluation: The "4+1 Framework" and Common Frameworks

After researching this topic, here is what I can share:

### The "4+1 Framework" -- Not a Widely Standardized Industry Term

There is no single, universally codified framework branded as "the 4+1 framework" in RIA M&A literature in the way that, say, Porter's Five Forces exists in strategy. However, the concept of evaluating RIA acquisition targets across **four core pillars plus one additional factor** is a pattern that appears in practitioner thinking, particularly from firms like **Echelon Partners**, **DeVoe & Company**, **Fidelity's practice management group**, and **Focus Financial / Dynasty Financial** advisory materials.

The general shape of the "4+1" pattern as used by acquirers and investment bankers in the RIA space is:

---

### The 4 Core Criteria

**1. Revenue Quality & Recurring Revenue**
- What percentage of revenue is **recurring** (AUM-based fees) vs. transactional (commissions, financial planning fees billed hourly)?
- Acquirers strongly prefer firms with **90%+ recurring revenue** tied to assets under management.
- Revenue retention rates (client retention, revenue retention after advisor departures) are scrutinized.
- Fee schedules are examined -- are fees competitive or will they face compression?

**2. Growth Profile**
- **Organic growth rate** -- is the firm growing its AUM through net new assets (not just market appreciation)?
- Client acquisition pipeline and referral sources.
- Is the firm in a growing demographic/geographic market?
- Acquirers typically want to see **5%+ organic growth** annually. Firms that are flat or shrinking are significantly less attractive.

**3. Client Demographics & Concentration**
- **Average client age** -- a book skewing 70+ carries succession/mortality risk.
- **Client concentration** -- if the top 10 clients represent 40%+ of revenue, that is a major risk factor.
- **Average account size** -- larger average relationships (e.g., $1M+ AUM per household) signal a more scalable, profitable practice.
- Intergenerational wealth transfer readiness (are the next-gen heirs already engaged?).

**4. Operational Scalability & Infrastructure**
- Technology stack (CRM, portfolio management, financial planning software, reporting).
- Compliance track record and regulatory history.
- Team depth beyond the founder(s) -- is there a **next-generation team** in place, or does everything depend on one or two principals?
- Operational efficiency (revenue per employee, profit margins -- top-quartile RIAs run 25-35% EBITDA margins).

---

### The "+1" Factor

**5. Cultural Fit / Founder Alignment**
- This is the "plus one" because it is qualitative and often decisive even when the four quantitative pillars check out.
- Does the selling firm's investment philosophy, client service model, and firm culture align with the acquirer's?
- Is the founder/seller willing to stay through a **transition period** (typically 2-5 years with earnout provisions)?
- Are there shared values around fiduciary duty, fee transparency, and client-first orientation?
- Many acquisitions that look perfect on paper fail because of cultural mismatch during integration.

---

### Why This Pattern Exists

RIA acquirers (both **strategic acquirers** like Focus Financial, CI Financial, Hightower, and Creative Planning, and **financial/PE acquirers** like KKR-backed firms) have learned that:

- Criteria 1-2 (revenue quality + growth) determine **what you're paying for**
- Criteria 3-4 (client demographics + operations) determine **risk and integration cost**
- Criterion 5 (culture/alignment) determines **whether the deal actually works post-close**

Deals are typically valued at **5-12x EBITDA** (or 2-3% of AUM as a rough heuristic), with the multiplier heavily influenced by how the firm scores across these dimensions.

---

### Other Prominent Frameworks in the Space

If someone references a specific branded "4+1" framework, it may come from one of these sources:

- **DeVoe & Company** publishes the most widely cited RIA M&A data and has frameworks for deal readiness evaluation.
- **Echelon Partners** produces the annual "RIA M&A Deal Report" and uses multi-factor scoring for acquisition targets.
- **Schwab's Business Consulting** and **Fidelity's Practice Management** groups publish practice valuation frameworks that overlap with these criteria.
- **David Grau Sr. / FP Transitions** authored frameworks focused on succession planning and enterprise valuation that use similar pillars (though typically framed as continuity/sustainability factors).

---

### Summary

The "4+1" pattern in RIA M&A evaluates: **(1) revenue quality, (2) growth, (3) client demographics/concentration, (4) operational infrastructure** -- plus **(+1) cultural alignment** as the decisive qualitative overlay. Whether or not a specific firm has branded it "the 4+1 framework," this is the dominant mental model acquirers use when screening RIA targets. The quantitative four can be modeled in a spreadsheet; the plus-one is why experienced acquirers say "we pass on more deals for culture than for numbers."
