## Key Research Findings

**Prevalence and Demographics:**
Preventable emergency care visits occur at higher rates among patients who are lower-income, less educated, unemployed, uninsured, lack internet access, or don't have access to a car. More specifically, individuals in lower-income households visit emergency rooms for preventable reasons roughly 2.5 times as often as those with higher incomes.

**Scale of the Problem:**
An estimated 13% to 27% of emergency department visits in the United States could be managed in physician offices, clinics, and urgent care centers at a savings of $4.4 billion annually. In 2010, nearly 41% of emergency department visits in Houston/Harris County hospitals were primary care-related, with uninsured and Medicaid patients accounting for more than 53% of all such visits.

**Effective Interventions:**
Research has identified several promising approaches. Community health worker programs that connect Medicaid and uninsured patients with medical homes promote appropriate preventive and primary care utilization and reduce unnecessary costs associated with preventable ED use. Additionally, interventions targeting frequent ED users appear to decrease ED visits and may improve stable housing.

The search results reveal substantial research documenting how low-income patients disproportionately use emergency departments for preventable conditions and demonstrating various effective intervention strategies.
