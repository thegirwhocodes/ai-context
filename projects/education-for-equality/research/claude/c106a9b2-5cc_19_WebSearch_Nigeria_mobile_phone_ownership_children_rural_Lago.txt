Of the calls that were answered by parties other than the intended respondents, 82.7% (67/81) were people who knew the participants, revealing the extent to which mobile phones are shared in households.

This finding comes from a cross-sectional study on the reliability of self-reported mobile phone ownership in rural north-central Nigeria. The researchers dialed 349 numbers: 110 (31.5%) were answered by participants who self-reported ownership of the mobile phone; 123 (35.2%) of the phone numbers did not ring at all; 28 (8.0%) rang but were not answered; and 88 (25.2%) were answered by someone other than the participant.

The key finding was that self-reported access to mobile phones in rural and low-income areas in north-central Nigeria is higher than actual access. The high percentage of calls answered by people other than the phone's reported owner demonstrates that mobile phone sharing is a common practice in these rural Nigerian communities.
